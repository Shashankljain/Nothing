# Congenital Dislocation of the Hip (CDH / Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip - DDH) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Congenital Dislocation of the Hip (CDH)**, now universally termed **Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH)**, is a spectrum of congenital and developmental hip joint instabilities ranging from mild acetabular dysplasia and subluxation to frank complete dislocation of the femoral head from the acetabulum.
- **Incidence**: Approximately **1 in 1000 live births**.
- **Gender Predilection**: **Females > Males** (**5:1 to 8:1** ratio) due to ligamentous sensitivity to maternal relaxin.
- **Laterality**: **Left hip > Right hip** (Left side involved in >60% of cases; bilateral in **20%** of cases).
- **High-Risk Association**: Most frequently seen in **1st born female children** presenting with **breech malposition**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Hormonal Joint Laxity**: Maternal **relaxin** crosses the placental barrier in late gestation, producing hyper-laxity of infant hip joint capsule and ligaments.
- **Genetic Predisposition**: Family history of generalized joint hypermobility or familial CDH.
- **Intrauterine Mechanical Factors**:
    - **Breech Presentation**: Increases incidence by **10-fold** due to forced hip flexion and knee extension in utero.
    - Oligohydramnios, primigravida rigid uterus, and associated congenital torticollis or CTEV.
- **Post-Natal Environmental Factors**: Swaddling infant legs together in tight extension and adduction (common in certain traditional cultures).

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Teratologic DDH**: Severe irreducible dislocation occurring early in intrauterine life, associated with genetic syndromes (e.g., Arthrogryposis Multiplex Congenita, Myelomeningocele).
2. **Typical DDH**: Occurs around birth in otherwise normal infants; divided into:
    - _Acetabular Dysplasia_: Inadequate coverage of femoral head by shallow acetabular roof.
    - _Subluxation_: Femoral head partially displaced but remains in contact with acetabulum.
    - _Dislocation_: Complete loss of contact between femoral head and acetabular cavity.

---

### 4. Pathophysiology & Structural Changes [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acetabulum**: Shallow, sloping roof with an increased acetabular index (>30°).
- **Femoral Head & Neck**: Femoral head is displaced **superolaterally**; delayed ossification of femoral head epiphysis; increased **femoral neck anteversion**.
- **Soft Tissue Obstacles to Reduction**:
    - **Inverted Limbus**: Fibrocartilaginous labrum folded inward into the acetabular cavity.
    - **Pulvinar**: Hypertrophied fibro-fatty tissue occupying the acetabular floor.
    - **Hourglass Capsule**: Constriction of the joint capsule by the overlying tight **iliopsoas tendon**.
    - **Ligamentum Teres**: Hypertrophied and elongated.
    - **Adaptive Muscle Shortening**: Contracture of hip adductors and iliopsoas muscles.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Examination Signs in Newborns & Infants (< 6 Months)

- **Barlow's Test (Provocative Dislocation Test)**:
    - _Method_: Infant supine, hip flexed to 90° and fully adducted. Gentle backward/posterior pressure applied along the femoral shaft.
    - _Result_: Unstable femoral head slips out posteriorly over the acetabular rim with a palpable 'clunk'. (**Mnemonic: BAD = Barlow Adduction Dislocates**).
- **Ortolani's Test (Reduction Test)**:
    - _Method_: Hips flexed 90°, gently abducted while fingers over greater trochanter exert forward pressure.
    - _Result_: Dislocated femoral head rolls back into the acetabulum with a palpable and audible 'clunk'.
- **Asymmetry**: Asymmetrical thigh and groin skin creases; restricted hip abduction.

#### B. Clinical Signs in Older Infants & Walking Children (> 6 Months)

- **Galeazzi's / Allis Sign**: Child supine with hips and knees flexed to 90°. Knee level on the dislocated side is markedly lower due to femoral shortening.
- **Telescopy Test (Piston Sign)**: Pushing and pulling along the long axis of the flexed thigh produces abnormal up-and-down mobility.
- **Vascular Sign of Narath**: Femoral artery pulse is weak or unpalpable under the mid-inguinal point because the femoral head is displaced away and fails to support the artery from behind.
- **Trendelenburg Test & Gait**:
    - _Unilateral CDH_: Positive Trendelenburg sign; child walks with a **lurching gait** towards the affected side.
    - _Bilateral CDH_: **Waddling gait** ("duck-like gait") with exaggerated lumbar lordosis.

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Ultrasonography (USG)

- **Investigation of Choice in Infants < 6 Months**: Standard diagnostic modality prior to appearance of the femoral head ossification center (assesses alpha and beta acetabular angles).

#### B. Radiography (Plain X-Ray Pelvis with Both Hips AP View)

Radiological evaluation in children > 6 months relies on classical diagnostic lines (See Diagram 1):

1. **Hilgenreiner's Line**: Horizontal line passing through the centers of both triradiate cartilages.
2. **Perkins' Line**: Vertical line drawn perpendicular to Hilgenreiner's line through the outermost lateral margin of the acetabulum.
3. **Quadrant Localization**:
    - _Normal_: Femoral head/ossification center lies in the **lower-inner (inferomedial) quadrant**.
    - _CDH / DDH_: Femoral head lies displaced in the **upper-outer (superolateral) quadrant**.
4. **Shenton's Line**: Continuous smooth semi-circular arc drawn along the medial border of the femoral neck to the upper margin of the obturator foramen; **broken/disrupted in CDH**.
5. **Von Rosen's Line**: Line drawn along the shaft of an abducted (45°) and internally rotated femur; normally passes through the acetabulum, but in CDH it projects above the ASIS.
6. **Delayed Ossification**: Delayed appearance of the capital femoral epiphysis (normally appears at 1 year).

#### C. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

- **Best overall imaging modality** to confirm soft tissue reduction obstacles, assess concentric reduction, and evaluate avascular necrosis.

#### A. Age-Based Management Protocol

- **Birth to 6 Months**:
    - **Dynamic Abduction Splintage**: **Pavlik Harness** (maintains hips flexed >90° and abducts within safe zone), **Von Rosen Splint**, or **Frog-leg / Lorenz plaster cast**.
- **6 Months to 18 Months**:
    - **Closed Manipulation**: Under general anesthesia ± percutaneous adductor tenotomy, followed by **Hip Spica Cast** (Frog-leg or Bachelor cast) for 3 months.
    - **Open Reduction**: Indicated if closed reduction fails due to soft tissue interposition (limbus, pulvinar, tight iliopsoas).
- **18 Months to 3 Years**:
    - **Open Reduction + Femoral Derotation Shortening Osteotomy (VDRO)**: Shortening the femur reduces joint tension and protects femoral head blood supply.
- **3 Years to 8 Years**:
    - **Open Reduction + Pelvic / Acetabular Reconstruction Osteotomies**:
        - **Salter's Redirection Osteotomy**: Transverse iliac osteotomy above acetabulum rotated forward and laterally.
        - **Pemberton's Pericapsular Osteotomy**: Curved osteotomy through ilium to triradiate cartilage.
        - **Chiari's Osteotomy**: Medial displacement osteotomy providing a shelf over the femoral head.
- **> 10 Years & Adults**:
    - Untreated neglected cases or painful secondary osteoarthritis managed by **Total Hip Replacement (THR)**after skeletal maturity.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Avascular Necrosis (AVN) of Femoral Head**: Most catastrophic complication; caused by forceful reduction in extreme abduction compressing medial circumflex femoral vessels.
- **Relapse / Redislocation**: Secondary to inadequate acetabular coverage or residual femoral anteversion.
- **Joint Stiffness & Flexion Contracture**: Secondary to prolonged spica immobilization.
- **Early Secondary Osteoarthritis**: Develops in early adulthood due to persistent acetabular dysplasia.

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: C-D-H-P-A-V

- **C** - **C**lunk of Ortolani / **C**reases asymmetrical
- **D** - **D**isplaced to upper-outer quadrant (Perkins line)
- **H** - **H**ilgenreiner's line horizontal reference
- **P** - **P**avlik harness dynamic splint (<6 months)
- **A** - **A**cetabular dysplasia / **A**llis sign positive
- **V** - **V**on Rosen line & **V**ascular sign of Narath

#### Memory Palace: The Baby Nursery

1. **At the Crib**: A baby girl born in breech position with extra folds on her left thigh (**1st born female, breech, left side**).
2. **On the Examination Table**: The doctor flexes and adducts the hip and feels a **clunk out** (**Barlow test**), then flexes and abducts to feel a **clunk in** (**Ortolani test**).
3. **At the X-Ray Board**: A red grid drawn across the pelvis shows the femoral ball floating high up in the **upper-outer box** with a **broken curved Shenton arch**.
4. **In the Orthotic Room**: The infant is placed into a red chest harness with leg stirrups holding the hips flexed like a frog (**Pavlik Harness**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- **Barlow's Test** tests if a reduced hip is _dislocatable_ (BAD = Barlow Adduction Dislocates), whereas **Ortolani's Test** tests if a dislocated hip is _reducible_ upon abduction.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Misinterpreting **Shenton's line** or the quadrant position of the femoral head on plain X-rays in an infant under 6 months.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: The capital femoral epiphysis does not ossify until **1 year of age**; therefore, plain X-rays are difficult to interpret in early infancy, making **Ultrasound (USG)** the true diagnostic modality of choice in infants under 6 months.

### Diagram Guide

### Diagram 1: Radiological Lines in CDH / DDH (Hilgenreiner & Perkins Lines)

- **Type**: Labeled radiological schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Infant pelvis showing both acetabula and proximal femurs.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Hilgenreiner's Line**: Horizontal line passing through triradiate cartilages.
    2. **Perkins' Line**: Vertical line perpendicular to Hilgenreiner's through lateral acetabular margin.
    3. **Normal Side (Lower-Inner Quadrant)**: Femoral head epiphysis located inferomedially.
    4. **CDH Side (Upper-Outer Quadrant)**: Displaced femoral head located superolaterally.
    5. **Disrupted Shenton's Line**: Broken arc on affected side vs smooth continuous arc on normal side.
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: "In CDH, the femoral ossification center shifts to the upper-outer quadrant and Shenton's line is broken".

### Diagram 2: Pavlik Harness / Splint

- **Type**: Orthotic appliance schematic.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Infant fitted with chest strap, shoulder straps, and leg stirrups.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Chest Strap**: Positioned at nipple line.
    2. **Anterior Leg Straps**: Maintains hip **flexion > 90°**.
    3. **Posterior Leg Straps**: Restricts adduction, maintaining gentle abduction.
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: "First-line dynamic splint for infants 0-6 months of age".

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Superolateral dislocation of femoral head with shallow acetabular roof.
- **Newborn Screening Tests**: **Barlow's test** (dislocates) & **Ortolani's test** (reduces).
- **Walking Child Findings**: **Galeazzi sign**, positive **Telescopy**, positive **Trendelenburg test/gait**.
- **X-Ray Hallmark**: Femoral head in **upper-outer quadrant** (Perkins line) + **broken Shenton's line**.
- **Age-Based Therapy**: **Pavlik Harness (<6m)** → **Closed Reduction + Spica (6-18m)** → **Open Reduction + VDRO (18-36m)** → **Pelvic Osteotomy (3-8y)**.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Congenital dislocation of the hip (Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip - DDH) is a spectrum of congenital hip instability characterized by superolateral displacement of the femoral head from a shallow acetabulum, clinically diagnosed by Barlow's and Ortolani's tests in infants, radiologically confirmed by disruption of Shenton's line, and managed by age-specific protocols starting with Pavlik harness splintage.**
