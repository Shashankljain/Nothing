# Chondroma (Enchondroma) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Chondroma (Enchondroma)** is defined as a benign, cartilage-forming bone tumor composed of a lobulated mass of mature hyaline cartilage encapsulated by fibrous tissue, located within the medullary canal of bone.

### Key Points

- **Site & Age Predilection**: Most common primary bone tumor affecting the small short tubular bones of the hands and feet (phalanges and metacarpals); most frequently seen in young adults aged 15–30 years.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Presents as a slow-growing, painless, hard swelling of a digit, or manifests for the first time as an acute pathological fracture following minor trivial trauma.
- **Radiological Appearance (X-Ray)**: Demonstrates a well-circumscribed, central radiolucent (lytic) metaphyseal/diaphyseal lesion with cortical thinning and characteristic "popcorn" or "ring-and-arc" matrix calcifications.
- **Associated Syndromes**: Multiple enchondromas occur in **Ollier's disease** (multiple enchondromatosis) and **Maffucci's syndrome** (multiple enchondromas combined with soft tissue cavernous hemangiomas).
- **Management**: Definitively treated by **extended curettage** of the cartilaginous cavity followed by **autologous bone grafting**.

---

### Clinical Pearl

- Enchondroma is the single most common primary tumor involving the small bones of the hand; any central lytic lesion in a phalanx with matrix calcifications in a young adult is an enchondroma until proven otherwise.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Ollier's disease** with **Maffucci's syndrome**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Both feature multiple enchondromas, but **Maffucci's syndrome** uniquely includes co-existing **soft tissue cavernous hemangiomas and lymphangiomas**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Nature & Site**: Benign cartilaginous tumor; most common in small tubular bones of hands/feet.
- **X-Ray Hallmark**: Central lytic lesion with "popcorn" matrix calcification.
- **Syndromes**: Ollier's disease (multiple enchondromas) and Maffucci's syndrome (+ hemangiomas).
- **Treatment**: Extended curettage + bone grafting.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Chondroma (enchondroma) is a benign cartilaginous neoplasm arising within the medullary cavity of the short tubular bones of the hand and foot, radiologically characterized by central lytic bone destruction with popcorn calcifications, and treated by extended curettage with bone grafting.**
