# Simple Bone Cyst (Unicameral Bone Cyst / SBC) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)**, also known as **Unicameral Bone Cyst (UBC)**, is defined as a **benign, fluid-filled, single-cavity (unicameral) lesion** of bone lined by a thin fibrous membrane, containing clear, straw-colored or serosanguinous fluid.
- **Pathological Feature**: It is considered the **only true cyst of bone** (unlike aneurysmal bone cysts or neoplastic lytic lesions which contain solid or vascular tissue).
- **Age Incidence**: Most commonly affects children and young adolescents between **10 and 20 years of age** (80% of cases occur in skeletally immature individuals <20 years).
- **Gender Ratio**: **Males > Females** (2:1 ratio).
- **Predilection Sites**:
    - **Proximal Humerus** (**most common site overall**, ~50%–60% of cases).
    - **Proximal Femur** (second most common site, ~25%–30%).
    - **Proximal Tibia** and **Calcanium**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Etiology**: Exact etiology remains unknown (idiopathic).
- **Pathogenetic Hypotheses**:
    - **Venous Obstruction Theory**: Localized interstitial fluid pressure elevation due to venous drainage blockage at the metaphyseal growth plate region.
    - **Metaphyseal Remodeling Defect**: Localized failure of normal intramedullary osteogenesis and bone remodeling during rapid skeletal growth.
    - **Traumatic Hematoma Theory**: Enclosed intramedullary micro-hematoma undergoing cystic degeneration.

---

### 3. Classification (Based on Growth Plate Proximity) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Active Cyst**:
    - Abuts or lies in direct contact with the epiphyseal growth plate (distance <0.5 cm).
    - Continues to enlarge, exhibits high intra-cystic pressure, and carries a high rate of recurrence post-treatment.
2. **Latent (Passive) Cyst**:
    - Separated from the epiphyseal growth plate by a band of normal diaphyseal bone as the long bone grows in length.
    - Quiescent lesion with low intra-cystic pressure and lower risk of recurrence.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Asymptomatic Presentation**: Most simple bone cysts are indolent and pain-free, discovered incidentally on plain X-rays ordered for unrelated minor injuries.
- **Pathological Fracture (Most Common Presentation)**: Up to **70%–80% of patients** present for the first time with acute pain, swelling, and inability to use the limb following a **pathological fracture** triggered by trivial trauma.
- **Dull Ache**: Mild local discomfort may precede the fracture due to cortical thinning and micro-stress fractures.

#### B. Signs on Physical Examination

- **Inspection & Palpation**:
    - No visible external deformity or soft tissue mass prior to fracture.
    - Local tenderness present at the metaphyseal fracture site.
    - Absence of warmth, erythema, or dilated superficial veins (differentiates from malignant lesions).

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Plain Radiography (X-Ray - AP & Lateral Views) — Diagnostic Investigation

- **Centric Metaphyseal Location**: Centrally located in the metaphysis along the longitudinal axis of the bone.
- **Symmetrical Cortical Expansion**: Symmetrical "thinning" and mild expansion of the overlying bone cortex.
- **Maximum Width Rule**: The **maximum transverse width of the cyst is strictly LESS than the width of the adjacent epiphyseal growth plate** (crucial sign differentiating SBC from Aneurysmal Bone Cyst).
- **Unilocular & Radiolucent**: Well-circumscribed, homogeneous, uniloculated lytic lesion without internal trabeculation or calcification.
- **Pathognomonic Signs Post-Fracture**:
    1. **Fallen-Leaf Sign (Fallen-Fragment Sign)**: A broken cortical bone fragment sinks through the low-viscosity liquid to settle at the dependent bottom of the fluid-filled cyst cavity.
    2. **Trap-Door Sign**: A hinged cortical fracture fragment attached at one margin projecting into the cyst cavity.

#### B. Advanced Imaging (CT / MRI)

- **MRI**: Confirms a fluid-filled, unilocular cavity without internal nodularity, soft tissue mass, or multiple fluid-fluid levels.
- **Aspiration**: Yields clear, yellow/straw-colored or serosanguinous fluid, confirming unicameral cystic nature.

#### A. Conservative / Non-Operative Management

- **Observation / Cast Immobilization**:
    - When presenting with a **pathological fracture**, conservative immobilization in a PoP cast (e.g., hanging cast or U-slab for proximal humerus) often allows the fracture callus to consolidate and **spontaneously obliterate the cyst cavity** (~15%–25% spontaneous cure rate).

#### B. Interventional Pharmacological Management

- **Percutaneous Aspiration & Intralesional Steroid Injection**:
    - **Method**: Under fluoroscopic guidance, two large-bore needles are inserted into the cyst, fluid is drained, and a depot corticosteroid is injected.
    - **Mechanism**: Steroids inhibit local osteoclast-activating prostaglandins and decrease intra-cystic hydrostatic pressure.

#### C. Surgical Management

- **Indications**:
    - Lesions in weight-bearing bones (e.g., **Proximal Femur**) with high risk of pathological fracture.
    - Active cysts failing repeated steroid injections.
- **Procedure**:
    - **Curettage & Bone Grafting**: Deroofing of the cyst wall, thorough curettage of the lining membrane, and packing the cavity with autologous bone graft (from iliac crest) or bone substitutes (calcium phosphate / bone cement).

---

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pathological Fracture**: The most frequent primary complication and presenting event.
- **Local Recurrence**: Occurs in **15%–30% of cases**, particularly in young children with active cysts close to the growth plate.
- **Limb Length Discrepancy / Growth Arrest**: Occurs if aggressive curettage or thermal ablation damages the adjacent epiphyseal growth plate.

---

### 11. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: SBC is a non-neoplastic, self-limiting developmental lesion that typically stops expanding and undergoes spontaneous ossification following skeletal maturity. Zero risk of malignant transformation.

- **L** - **L**ocation: Centric metaphysis (Proximal humerus > Proximal femur)
- **E** - **E**xpansion width strictly LESS than epiphyseal growth plate
- **A** - **A**spiration yields clear straw-colored fluid (Methylprednisolone injection = DOC)
- **F** - **F**allen-leaf sign on X-ray post-fracture

#### Memory Palace: The Straw-Fluid Well

1. **At the Well (Proximal Humerus Metaphysis)**: A 12-year-old boy leaning against a dry wooden well (**SBC, 10–20 yrs, Proximal humerus**).
2. **Looking Down the Well**: A single, clear chamber containing bright **yellow straw-colored water** (**Unicameral clear fluid cavity**).
3. **Dropping a Leaf**: A dry cortical tree leaf falls straight down to the bottom of the water (**Fallen-leaf sign**).
4. **The Doctor's Syringe**: Injecting a large white steroid dose directly into the well water (**Intralesional Methylprednisolone injection**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- Always check the width of a metaphyseal lytic lesion relative to the growth plate: if the maximum width of the lesion is **less than the width of the growth plate**, it is a **Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)**; if it **distends and balloons beyond the width of the growth plate**, it is an **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)** with **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)** on plain X-rays.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **SBC**: **Centric** metaphyseal lesion, width **< growth plate**, contains **clear straw-colored fluid**, exhibits **fallen-leaf sign** after fracture.
    - **ABC**: **Eccentric** metaphyseal lesion, width **> growth plate** (ballooning out), contains **blood-filled vascular spaces** showing fluid-fluid levels on MRI.

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Benign fluid-filled unicameral cavity in the metaphysis (**Proximal humerus > Proximal femur**).
- **Demographics**: Children aged **10–20 years**; Males > Females.
- **Key X-Ray Sign**: **Fallen-leaf sign** (cortical fragment floating/sinking to dependent bottom after fracture).
- **Width Hallmark**: Maximum cyst width is **strictly less than the epiphyseal plate width**.
- **Treatment Standard**: Conservative casting for fractures; **Intralesional Methylprednisolone injection** or **Curettage + Bone Grafting** for unfractured/high-risk lesions.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Simple bone cyst (unicameral bone cyst) is a benign, fluid-filled, single-cavity lesion located centrally in the metaphysis of long bones in children, characteristically presenting with a fallen-leaf sign on X-ray following a pathological fracture, and managed by intralesional steroid injection or curettage with bone grafting.**
