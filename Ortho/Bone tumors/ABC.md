# Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)**: A benign, highly vascular, locally aggressive, expansile osteolytic "tumor-like" lesion of bone.
- **Morphological Nature**: Composed of blood-filled, non-endothelialized cavernous vascular spaces separated by fibrous connective tissue septa containing multinucleated osteoclast-like giant cells and reactive woven bone.
- **Etymology**: Named "aneurysmal" due to its characteristic outward "ballooning" or "blow-out" distension of the overlying bone cortex, resembling a vascular aneurysm.
- **Age Distribution**: Predominantly affects children and young adults between **10 and 20 years of age** (>75% of cases occur <20 years).
- **Sex Ratio**: Slightly **Females > Males** (1.3:1 ratio).
- **Predilection Sites**:
    - **Metaphysis of Long Bones**: Lower limb long bones around the knee (**Distal femur > Proximal tibia**) and upper limb (**Proximal humerus**).
    - **Spine**: Posterior elements of vertebrae (pedicle, lamina, and spinous process).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Primary Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (Idiopathic - 70%)**: Arises de novo due to a primary vascular malformation or somatic chromosomal translocation **t(16;17)(q22;p13)** causing upregulation of the **USP6** oncogene.
- **Secondary Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (30%)**: Develops secondary to a pre-existing primary bone lesion that induces local vascular disturbance.
    - Associated primary precursor lesions: **Giant Cell Tumor (GCT)** (most common), **Osteoblastoma**, **Chondroblastoma**, **Fibrous Dysplasia**, and **Non-Ossifying Fibroma**.

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Primary ABC**: True neoplastic lesion with USP6 gene rearrangement.
2. **Secondary ABC**: Reactive vascular phenomenon secondary to another primary bone lesion.
3. **Campanacci Morphological Staging**:

- **Inactive / Latent**: Intact periosteum, well-defined sclerotic margins.
- **Active**: Thinning of cortex, progressive expansion with incomplete sclerotic rim.
- **Aggressive**: Cortical destruction, soft tissue extension, ill-defined margins.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Local Pain**: Insidious, progressive, dull aching pain at the site of involvement, gradually worsening over weeks to months.
- **Swelling**: Rapidly expanding, tender local mass near a joint or over the spine.
- **Pathological Fracture**: Sudden onset of acute severe pain and disability following minor trivial trauma due to extreme cortical thinning.
- **Neurological Deficits**: Vertebral lesions presenting with radicular pain, spinal cord compression, or paraparesis.

#### B. Examination Signs

- **Inspection**: Asymmetrical, eccentric swelling located at the metaphyseal end of a long bone.
- **Palpation**:
    - Local temperature elevated (**warm to touch**).
    - Marked localized bone tenderness.
    - **Eggshell Crackling**: Thin, expanded subperiosteal bone shell yields under firm pressure with a characteristic crackling sensation.
- **Vascular Features**: ABC is a **pulsatile bone tumor**; a distinct vascular thrill or auditory bruit may be appreciated on palpation/auscultation.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Plain Radiography (X-Ray - AP & Lateral Views) — Primary Screening

- **Anatomical Site**: **Eccentric**, **metaphyseal** location in long bones.
- **Ballooning Appearance**: Asymmetrical, expansile, "blow-out" lytic lesion distending the overlying cortex.
- **Growth Plate Relationship**: The maximum transverse width of the lesion is **GREATER than the width of the adjacent epiphyseal growth plate** (crucial feature differentiating ABC from Simple Bone Cyst).
- **Internal Trabeculation**: Multiloculated "soap-bubble" or "honeycomb" appearance created by delicate internal osseous septa.
- **Periosteal Reaction**: Thin periosteal shell ("eggshell border") enclosing the lesion.

#### B. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) — Investigation of Choice (IOC)

- **Pathognomonic Sign**: **Multiple fluid-fluid levels** within multiloculated cystic spaces.
- **Mechanism**: Caused by gravitational settling and layering of un-clotted blood components of varying cell densities (heavy red blood cells at the bottom, light serum at the top).

#### C. Biopsy & Histopathology

- **Tissue Confirmation**: Mandatory to rule out secondary pre-existing lesions or malignant telangiectatic osteosarcoma.
- **Microscopy**: Cavernous blood-filled spaces lined by fibrous walls containing spindle cells, multinucleated osteoclast-like giant cells, and reactive osteoid trabeculae.

#### A. Surgical Management (Primary Standard)

1. **Extended Curettage & Bone Grafting / Cementing**:
    - **Method of Choice**: Thorough intralesional curettage of the cyst cavity using a high-speed burr to break down internal septa.
    - **Local Adjuvants**: Application of liquid nitrogen (cryotherapy), phenol, or argon beam coagulation to destroy microscopic residual tumor cells at margins.
    - **Cavity Reconstruction**: Packed with autologous bone graft, allograft, or Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement.
2. **En-Bloc Wide Excision**:
    - Indicated for massive, recurrent lesions or lesions located in expendable bones (e.g., proximal fibula, ribs, distal ulna).

#### B. Non-Surgical / Interventional Management

1. **Percutaneous Image-Guided Intralesional Sclerotherapy**:
    - Injection of fibrosing agent (**Polidocanol** or **Alcoholic solution of Zein / Ethibloc**) under CT/fluoroscopic guidance; induces thrombosis and sclerosis.
2. **Selective Arterial Embolization (SAE)**:
    - Primary treatment of choice for surgically inaccessible lesions in the spine or pelvis to reduce tumor volume and prevent intra-operative hemorrhage.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Local Recurrence**: Occurs in **20%–25% of cases** following simple curettage; usually manifests within the first 2 years post-treatment.
- **Pathological Fracture**: Frequent initial complication due to severe subperiosteal cortical erosion.
- **Massive Intra-operative Hemorrhage**: Profuse bleeding during curettage due to hypervascular arteriovenous pooling.
- **Growth Plate Arrest**: Limb length discrepancy or angular deformity if the lesion crosses the adjacent epiphyseal growth plate.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Good**: Benign lesion with zero risk of direct distant metastasis.
- High cure rates achieved when extended curettage with local adjuvants or sclerotherapy is properly executed.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: BLOOD-CYST

- **B** - **B**low-out / "Ballooning" expansile lytic lesion on X-ray
- **L** - **L**ocation: Metaphysis (eccentric) & posterior spinal elements
- **O** - **O**verlying cortex shows "eggshell crackling"
- **O** - **O**nset in children/adolescents (10–20 years)
- **D** - **D**iagnostic MRI shows **multiple fluid-fluid levels**
- **C** - **C**avernous blood-filled spaces on histology
- **Y** - **Y**ields profuse bleeding on open biopsy
- **S** - **S**clerotherapy / Extended curettage is management of choice
- **T** - **t(16;17)** translocation with USP6 gene upregulation

#### Memory Palace: The Ballooning Red Cavern

1. **At the Cavern Entrance (Metaphysis)**: A 15-year-old girl holding a large red balloon that is blowing up out of the side of a long bone shaft (**10–20 yrs, Eccentric Metaphyseal Ballooning**).
2. **Inside the Balloon**: Cavernous pools filled with settled blood layering into light and dark liquids (**Multiple fluid-fluid levels on MRI**).
3. **On the Outer Wall**: Squeezing the thin wall makes an **eggshell cracking sound** (**Eggshell crackling sign**).
4. **At the Treatment Table**: A radiologist injecting a clearing foam (**Polidocanol sclerotherapy**) to shrink the balloon completely.

---

### Clinical Pearl

- When evaluating an expansile lytic lesion with multiple fluid-fluid levels on MRI, always examine the solid non-cystic portions or septa on tissue biopsy: a solid component containing osteoid-producing malignant spindle cells indicates a **Telangiectatic Osteosarcoma**, whereas a solid component with multinucleated giant cells and USP6 translocation confirms a benign **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)** with **Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)** on X-rays.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **ABC**: **Eccentric** position, width **GREATER than growth plate**, contains **blood**, shows **fluid-fluid levels on MRI**.
    - **SBC**: **Centric** position, width **LESS than growth plate**, contains **clear straw-colored fluid**, shows **fallen-leaf sign** post-fracture.

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Benign blood-filled cavernous spaces expanding the metaphysis (**Eccentric**).
- **Demographics**: Children/young adults aged **10–20 years**; Females > Males.
- **Pathognomonic MRI Sign**: **Multiple fluid-fluid levels**.
- **Width Hallmark**: Maximum lesion width **distends beyond the width of the epiphyseal plate**.
- **Genetic Marker**: **t(16;17)** translocation creating **USP6** gene fusion.
- **Treatment Standard**: Extended curettage + bone grafting/cementing OR intralesional **Polidocanol sclerotherapy**.
