# Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)** is defined as an entrapment neuropathy resulting from the compression of the **median nerve** as it passes through the fibro-osseous carpal tunnel beneath the **flexor retinaculum** (transverse carpal ligament) at the wrist.
- **Epidemiology**: It is the **most common entrapment neuropathy** in the human body.
- **Sex Predilection**: **Females > Males** (most frequently presenting in middle-aged women).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Any space-occupying lesion or condition that increases pressure inside the confined fibro-osseous carpal tunnel leads to median nerve compression:

- **Idiopathic**: The **most common cause** overall.
- **Inflammatory Conditions**: **Rheumatoid arthritis** (tenosynovitis of flexor tendons) and wrist osteoarthritis.
- **Post-Traumatic Causes**: Bone thickening or bony prominence after a **Colles' fracture** (fracture hematoma/callus), and lunate bone dislocation.
- **Endocrine & Metabolic Disorders**: **Hypothyroidism (Myxoedema)**, **Acromegaly**, **Diabetes mellitus**, Amyloidosis, Gout, and fluid retention during **Pregnancy**.
- **Occupational / Repetitive Stress**: Activities causing continuous wrist compression or repetitive flexion-extension (e.g., prolonged typing, manual labor).

- **Micro-Anatomy of Nerve Sparing**: In CTS, the **deep motor and sensory branches** passing within the tunnel are compressed, while the **superficial palmar cutaneous branch** arises proximal to the flexor retinaculum and passes over it, leaving sensation over the proximal palm intact.

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Paresthesias & Sensory Disturbance**: Tingling, numbness, or "pins-and-needles" sensation over the **lateral 3.5 fingers** (thumb, index, middle, and radial half of ring finger) along the median nerve distribution.
- **Nocturnal Exacerbation (Night Pain)**: Symptoms are **maximum at night**, frequently awakening the patient from sleep.
- **Relieving Maneuvers**: Pain and numbness are characteristically relieved by **shaking the affected hand** ("flick sign") or hanging the hand over the edge of the bed.
- **Clumsiness & Motor Loss**: Feeling of clumsiness or weakness when performing fine motor tasks, such as buttoning a shirt, holding a needle, or picking up small objects.

#### B. Signs on Physical Examination

- **Thenar Muscle Wasting**: Atrophy of the **thenar eminence** (specifically Abductor Pollicis Brevis, Opponens Pollicis, and Flexor Pollicis Brevis) in long-standing cases.
- **Loss of Thumb Opposition**: Impaired thumb opposition due to paralysis of the **opponens pollicis** muscle (supplied by the deep recurrent branch of the median nerve).
- **Intact Palmar Sensation**: Sensation over the thenar skin of the palm is preserved because the palmar cutaneous branch passes superficial to the flexor retinaculum.

#### C. Provocative Clinical Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Durkan's Test (Carpal Compression Test)**:
    - _Procedure_: The examiner exerts direct manual pressure over the median nerve between the thenar and hypothenar eminences at the wrist for **30 seconds**.
    - _Result_: **Best and most sensitive clinical test**; reproduces paresthesia and numbness along the median nerve distribution.
2. **Phalen's Test**:
    - _Procedure_: The patient holds both wrists in complete forced palmar flexion (dorsa of hands pressed together) for **1 minute**.
    - _Result_: Positive if dull aching pain, numbness, or tingling is elicited in the lateral 3.5 fingers.
3. **Reverse Phalen's Test (Prayer Test)**:
    - _Procedure_: The patient holds both wrists in maximum extension (palms pressed together in a prayer position) for **1 minute**.
    - _Result_: Reproduces paresthesias along the median nerve territory.
4. **Tinel's Sign**:
    - _Procedure_: Percussing gently over the flexor retinaculum on the volar aspect of the wrist.
    - _Result_: Elicits a tingling or electric shock-like sensation radiating into the tips of the lateral 3.5 digits.

---

### 5. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS) — Investigation of Choice (IOC)**:
    - Confirms diagnosis by measuring nerve conduction velocity across the wrist.
    - Demonstrates **decreased conduction velocity** and delayed sensory/motor distal latencies of the median nerve across the flexor retinaculum.
- **Electromyography (EMG)**: Shows denervation potentials (fibrillations and positive sharp waves) in the thenar muscles in severe cases.
- **Plain Radiographs (X-Ray Wrist)**: Useful to rule out pre-existing bony abnormalities, such as an old malunited Colles' fracture or lunate dislocation.

#### A. Non-Pharmacological / Conservative Management

- **Wrist Splintage**: Application of a **wrist immobilizer / neutral volar splint** (worn particularly at night) to prevent wrist flexion and minimize intra-tunnel pressure.
- **Activity Modification**: Avoiding repetitive wrist flexion, heavy lifting, or ergonomic strain (e.g., keyboard modifications).

#### B. Pharmacological Treatment

#### C. Surgical Management

- **Indication**:
    1. Failure of conservative treatment after 4–6 weeks.
    2. Presence of progressive thenar muscle wasting or severe sensory loss.
    3. CTS secondary to structural lesions (e.g., callus, bone spur).
- **Surgical Procedure**: **Carpal Tunnel Release (Decompression)**.
    - Done via open or endoscopic approach by complete **longitudinal division/sectioning of the flexor retinaculum** (transverse carpal ligament) to decompress the median nerve.

---

### 8. Complications

- **Permanent Thenar Motor Paralysis**: Irreversible muscle atrophy if decompression is excessively delayed.
- **Trophic Skin Changes**: Dry, shiny, thin skin and ridged nails in the median nerve distribution.
- **Iatrogenic Nerve Injury**: Damage to the palmar cutaneous branch or recurrent motor branch of the median nerve during surgical release.

---

### 9. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Most patients experience complete symptom resolution following early conservative care or timely surgical release of the flexor retinaculum.

### Clinical Pearl

- In Carpal Tunnel Syndrome, **sensation over the thenar eminence of the palm is characteristically normal**. This is because the palmar cutaneous branch of the median nerve arises 5 cm proximal to the wrist joint and passes **superficial to the flexor retinaculum**, thereby escaping compression inside the carpal tunnel.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome** with **Pronator Teres Syndrome** (both involve median nerve entrapment).
- **Distinguishing Fact**: In **Pronator Teres Syndrome**, compression occurs high in the forearm, leading to **loss of palmar cutaneous sensation** over the thenar eminence. In **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome**, compression occurs at the wrist, and **palmar cutaneous sensation is strictly spared**.

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Compression of **median nerve** beneath the **flexor retinaculum** at the wrist.
- **Classic Presentation**: Nocturnal tingling/pain in **lateral 3.5 fingers**, relieved by hand shaking.
- **Best Clinical Test**: **Durkan's carpal compression test** (30 seconds pressure).
- **Investigation of Choice**: **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)** showing delayed distal latency.
- **Definitive Surgery**: Open or endoscopic **surgical division of the flexor retinaculum**.

### ### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Carpal tunnel syndrome is the most common entrapment neuropathy of the upper limb, characterized by median nerve compression beneath the flexor retinaculum at the wrist, presenting with nocturnal paresthesias in the lateral 3.5 digits and thenar muscle wasting, diagnosed by nerve conduction studies, and managed by volar splinting or surgical flexor retinaculum release.**
