# Triple Deformity of Knee (Triple Subluxation) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Triple Deformity of the Knee** (also referred to as **Triple Subluxation** or **Triple Displacement**): A complex multi-planar joint displacement characterized by three distinct spatial deformities of the tibia relative to the femoral condyles:
    1. **Flexion** at the knee joint.
    2. **Posterior subluxation** of the tibia on the femur.
    3. **External (lateral) rotation** of the tibia.
- It represents an advanced end-stage complication of severe joint destruction, chronic synovial hypertrophy, capsular/ligamentous laxity, and uncorrected muscle spasm.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Tuberculosis of Knee Joint (TB Knee)**: The most common infectious cause leading to late-stage triple displacement due to cold abscesses, synovial hypertrophy, and ligament destruction.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**: Chronic systemic inflammatory polyarthritis causing pannus formation, cartilage erosion, and late triple subluxation.
- **Neglected Septic Arthritis**: Severe acute pyogenic infection causing rapid destruction of articular cartilage and secondary joint subluxation/dislocation.
- **Post-Polio Residual Paralysis (PPRP)**: Severe neuromuscular muscle imbalance between weak quadriceps and spastic hamstrings.
- **Advanced Osteoarthritis / Haemophilic Arthritis**: End-stage cartilage breakdown, capsular stretching, and joint instability.

---

### 3. Classification / Components [⭐ High-Yield]

The deformity consists of three interconnected spatial displacements (Mnemonic: **PER** or **PERF**):

|Component|Joint Level Involved|Primary Patho-Mechanical Cause|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1. Flexion Deformity**|Tibiofemoral Joint|Protective hamstring muscle spasm overriding weak quadriceps.|
|**2. Posterior Subluxation**|Tibiofemoral Joint|Destruction/laxity of cruciate ligaments and continuous backward flexor pull.|
|**3. External Rotation**|Tibia on Femoral Condyles|Dominant lateral pull of **biceps femoris** on the fibular head and lateral tibia.|

---

### 4. Pathophysiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- Inflammatory or infectious processes (TB / RA / Septic arthritis) cause **synovial hypertrophy**, effusion, and pannus formation.
- The hypertrophied synovium and proteolytic enzymes erode articular cartilage and underlying subchondral bone.
- **Ligamentous Laxity**: Softening, stretching, and eventual destruction of crucial supporting structures—capsule, **Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL)**, and **Posterior Cruciate Ligament (PCL)**.
- **Muscle Spasm & Imbalance**:
    - Initial protective spasm of the hamstring muscles holds the knee in **flexion**.
    - Persistent flexor pull causes the proximal end of the tibia to slip backward behind the femoral condyles → **Posterior subluxation**.
    - The lateral hamstring (**biceps femoris**) exerts a stronger force than medial hamstrings, pulling the tibia outward into **external rotation**.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Symptoms**:
    - Severe knee pain on attempted movement or weight-bearing.
    - Marked joint stiffness and severe limitation of range of motion.
    - Limp / antalgic gait progressing to inability to bear weight on the affected limb.
    - Apparent limb shortening.
- **Signs on Examination**:
    - **Inspection**:
        - Fixed flexion posture of the knee (attitude of knee in 5°–30° flexion or more).
        - Anterior prominence of femoral condyles with a posterior step-off depression below the patella.
        - Tibial tuberosity and foot oriented laterally (**external rotation**).
    - **Palpation**: Synovial thickening, joint effusion (patellar tap sign), or cold abscess/sinus in TB knee.
    - **Muscle Wasting**: Severe unexplained atrophy of quadriceps and thigh muscles.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Imaging (Radiographs - AP & Lateral Views)**:
    - **Lateral View (Diagnostic)**: Demonstrates posterior offset of the upper end of the tibia behind the line of the femoral condyles.
    - **AP View**: Shows reduction of joint space, marginal bony erosions, and diffuse peri-articular osteoporosis.
- **MRI Scan (Investigation of Choice for Soft Tissues)**: Visualizes degree of synovitis, soft tissue edema, cartilage loss, and cruciate ligament destruction.
- **Biopsy / Synovial Fluid Aspiration**: Gold standard for confirming etiology (e.g., AFB/caseating granulomas in TB or inflammatory exudate).

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

|Feature|TB Knee|Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)|Septic Arthritis|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Onset**|Insidious, chronic course|Chronic, remitting-relapsing|Acute, fulminant|
|**Involvement**|Monoarticular (usually knee)|Symmetrical polyarticular|Monoarticular|
|**Constitutional Signs**|Low-grade fever, weight loss, night cries|Systemic vasculitis, nodule, anemia|High-grade fever with chills, tachycardia|
|**Synovial Pathology**|Caseating granuloma with Langhans giant cells|Non-caseating Pannus formation|Purulent exudate with polymorphs|
|**End Sequence**|**Fibrous ankylosis** in deformed position|**Fibrous or Bony ankylosis**|**Bony ankylosis**|

#### A. Non-Pharmacological / Conservative Management

- **Continuous Traction**: Below-knee skin traction or POP slab to relieve painful muscle spasm and correct mild flexor posture in early cases.
- **Immobilization**: Rest in an above-knee cast/splint during acute disease phase.

#### C. Operative Management

1. **Early / Synovial Stage**:
    - **Synovectomy**: Removal of hypertrophied diseased synovium when articular cartilage is preserved.
2. **Advanced Stage with Established Triple Deformity**:
    - **Knee Arthrodesis (Joint Fusion)**:
        - **Procedure**: **Charnley's Compression Arthrodesis**.
        - **Position**: Fused in **5°–10° flexion** and neutral rotation.
        - **Indication**: Active or previous severe TB infection, young active manual laborers needing a stable joint.
    - **Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA / Joint Replacement)**:
        - **Indication**: Burned-out quiescent disease (e.g., healed TB after 10–15 years or advanced RA) in older patients to restore joint mobility.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fibrous / Bony Ankylosis**: Knee fixed permanently in a severely deformed position.
- **Structural Limb Shortening**: Marked gait disability due to cartilage erosion and backward tibial displacement.
- **Secondary Osteoarthritis**: Degeneration of adjacent ipsilateral hip and opposite knee joints due to altered gait mechanics.
- **Peroneal Nerve Injury**: Traction injury to the common peroneal nerve during unmonitored forced manual correction of external rotation and flexion.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Early Stage (Synovial)**: Good outcome with complete disease control and preservation of joint function using ATT/DMARDs and traction.
- **Late Stage (Established Triple Subluxation)**: Requires surgical reconstruction (arthrodesis or arthroplasty) for a stable, painless limb.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: PERF

- **P** - **P**osterior subluxation of tibia
- **E** - **E**xternal rotation of tibia
- **R** - **R**estricted joint movements / destruction
- **F** - **F**lexion deformity at knee joint

#### Memory Palace: The 3-Legged Stool in the Ward

1. **Leg 1 (Bent Joint)**: A wooden stool leg bent backwards (**Flexion deformity**).
2. **Leg 2 (Pushed Back)**: The stool top slipped backwards off its base (**Posterior subluxation**).
3. **Leg 3 (Twisted Outer)**: The foot of the stool twisted outwards to the side (**External rotation**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- When examining a knee with suspected triple deformity, always feel for the anterior prominence of the femoral condyles and the posterior sag of the tibial tuberosity; never attempt forceful rapid manual extension, as it risks stretching the common peroneal nerve or tearing popliteal vessels.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Triple Deformity of Knee** with **Triple Arthrodesis** or **O'Donoghue's Triad**.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **Triple Deformity (Knee)**: Pathological displacement of knee (**Flexion + Posterior subluxation + External rotation**).
    - **Triple Arthrodesis (Foot)**: Surgical fusion of 3 tarsal joints (**Subtalar + Talonavicular + Calcaneocuboid**).
    - **O'Donoghue's Triad**: Sports injury of knee (**ACL tear + MCL tear + Medial Meniscus tear**).

### Quick Revision

- **Triple Deformity Components**: **Flexion + Posterior subluxation + External rotation of tibia**.
- **Most Common Causes**: **TB Knee** (infectious) and **Rheumatoid Arthritis** (inflammatory).
- **Key Muscle Pathoanatomy**: Spasm of **biceps femoris** pulls tibia laterally and posteriorly.
- **Diagnostic Investigation**: **Lateral X-ray** showing posterior offset of tibia; **MRI** for soft tissues.
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: **Charnley's Compression Arthrodesis** (young/active) or **TKA** (healed quiescent cases).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Triple deformity of the knee is an end-stage multi-planar joint displacement characterized by flexion, posterior subluxation, and external rotation of the tibia relative to the femur, occurring secondary to extensive articular and ligamentous destruction in TB knee or rheumatoid arthritis.**
