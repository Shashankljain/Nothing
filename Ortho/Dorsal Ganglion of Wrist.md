# Ganglion (Dorsal Ganglion of Wrist) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Ganglion (Dorsal Ganglion of Wrist)**: Defined as a non-neoplastic, benign, fluid-filled unilocular or multiloculated cystic swelling arising from the mucoid degeneration of a joint capsule (most commonly the scapholunate joint capsule) or tendon sheath over the dorsum of the wrist.

---

### 2. Key Clinical & Management Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Site & Demographics**: Most common cystic swelling of the wrist and hand (dorsum > ventral), occurring predominantly in young adults (females > males).
- **Pathology & Contents**: Contains clear, thick, gelatinous **mucinous fluid** rich in hyaluronic acid, enclosed within a fibrous pseudocyst wall devoid of a true epithelial or synovial lining.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Smooth, tense, firm-to-bony-hard painless swelling on the dorsum of the wrist that characteristically shows **brilliant positive transillumination**.
- **Interventional Management**: Aspiration of thick mucinous fluid followed by local intra-cystic injection of **steroids or hyaluronidase (Hyalase)**.
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: Complete **en-bloc surgical excision** of the cyst along with its narrow pedicle and underlying capsular base (to prevent high recurrence rates).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- A dorsal wrist ganglion is often so extremely tense under internal fluid pressure that it feels **bony-hard** on palpation, frequently mimicking an exostosis or bone tumor; a **positive transillumination test** easily confirms its fluid-filled cystic nature.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing a simple **Dorsal Wrist Ganglion** with a **Compound Palmar Ganglion**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: A **Dorsal Wrist Ganglion** is a benign unilocular/multiloculated cyst containing clear mucinous fluid (transillumination positive), whereas a **Compound Palmar Ganglion** is chronic tuberculous or rheumatoid tenosynovitis of the flexor sheaths causing an hourglass swelling across the flexor retinaculum containing **rice bodies**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Nature**: Most common cystic swelling of the wrist (dorsum > ventral).
- **Pathology**: **Mucoid degeneration** of joint capsule/tendon sheath; filled with clear mucinous fluid.
- **Diagnostic Hallmark**: Tense swelling with **positive transillumination**.
- **Treatment**: Aspiration + steroid/hyalase injection → Surgical excision with capsular base if recurrent.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **A dorsal ganglion of the wrist is the most common benign cystic swelling of the hand arising from mucoid degeneration of the joint capsule or tendon sheath, characteristically presenting as a tense, brilliantly transilluminant swelling containing clear mucinous fluid, and managed by aspiration with steroid injection or surgical excision with its capsular base.**
