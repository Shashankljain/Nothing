# Ganglion (Ganglion Cyst of Wrist) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Ganglion (Ganglion Cyst)**: Defined as a non-neoplastic, benign, fluid-filled unilocular or multiloculated cystic swelling arising from the mucoid degeneration of the peri-articular fibrous tissue of a joint capsule or tendon sheath.
- **Incidence & Site**: It is the **most common cystic swelling of the wrist and hand**.
- **Predilection Site**: **Dorsum of the wrist** (arising from the scapholunate ligament/capsule) > **Ventral aspect of the wrist** (arising near the flexor carpi radialis tendon).
- **Age & Sex**: Most frequently affects young adults between **20 and 40 years of age**, with a higher predilection in **females (F > M)**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mucoid Degeneration (Primary Mechanism)**: Results from focal collagenous breakdown and mucoid degeneration of the dense fibrous connective tissue of a joint capsule or synovial tendon sheath.
- **Repetitive Microtrauma & Stress**: Chronic mechanical strain or repetitive wrist extension/flexion activities (e.g., gymnasts, typists, manual workers) leading to synovial fluid leakage through a capsular defect.
- **Synovial Origin**: Originates from the underlying synovium or joint capsule attachments.

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Anatomical Classification of True Ganglion Cysts

1. **Dorsal Wrist Ganglion** (Most common, ~70%): Arises from the scapholunate joint capsule on the dorsum of the wrist.
2. **Volar (Palmar) Wrist Ganglion** (~20%): Arises over the radial aspect of the volar wrist between the flexor carpi radialis and radial artery.
3. **Flexor Tendon Sheath Ganglion (Retinacular Cyst)**: Small, pea-sized, tender cyst at the A1 pulley at the base of the digit.
4. **Mucous Cyst**: Digital ganglion arising over the dorsal aspect of the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint, associated with osteoarthritis.

#### B. Related Pathological Entity: Compound Palmar Ganglion

- **Compound Palmar Ganglion**: A historical misnomer; it is **NOT a true ganglion**, but rather a chronic inflammatory tenosynovitis of the flexor tendon sheaths of the wrist and hand.
- **Etiology**: Tubercular tenosynovitis (most common in developing countries) or Rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Classic Presentation**: Hourglass-shaped swelling above and below the flexor retinaculum of the wrist due to fluid accumulation in the radial and ulnar bursae.

- **Histopathology**:
    - Wall consists of compressed, dense fibrous collagenous tissue **devoid of a true epithelial or synovial lining**(hence termed a pseudocyst).
    - Contents: Filled with thick, clear, jelly-like **mucinous fluid** rich in hyaluronic acid and glucosamine.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Painless Swelling (Most Common)**: Ordinarily, patients present strictly with a slow-growing, painless swelling over the dorsum or volar aspect of the wrist.
- **Mild Discomfort / Pain**: Experienced occasionally during extremes of wrist dorsiflexion or heavy weight-bearing on the hand.
- **Weakness of Grip**: Mild weakness or sense of instability in the wrist.
- **Nerve Compression Symptoms**: Volar ganglions may compress the median nerve (carpal tunnel syndrome symptoms) or ulnar nerve (Guyon's canal symptoms), causing paresthesia in the fingers.

#### B. Signs on Examination

- **Inspection**: Smooth, well-defined, globular, or oval swelling over the dorsum of the wrist (or volar radial aspect).
- **Palpation**:
    - Firm, tense, elastic to bony-hard consistency (the cyst is often so tense under pressure that it mimics a solid bone or tendon tumor).
    - Fluctuant in large or soft cysts, but fluctuation may be difficult to elicit in small, tense cysts.
    - Attached to the underlying joint capsule or tendon sheath, but overlying skin is completely free and mobile.
- **Transillumination Test**: **Positive** (shining a bright light through the cyst in a darkened room produces brilliant transillumination due to clear mucinous fluid).

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: Primary diagnosis is established by location (Dorsal > Ventral wrist), tense consistency, mobility over underlying structures, and a positive **transillumination test**.
- **Diagnostic Aspiration**: Aspiration yields thick, clear, gelatinous, straw-colored **mucinous fluid**.
- **Ultrasonography (USG) of Wrist**:
    - Confirms a well-defined unilocular or multiloculated anechoic cystic lesion.
    - Identifies the narrow stalk/pedicle connecting the ganglion base to the joint capsule.
    - Evaluates proximity to the radial artery in volar wrist ganglions.
- **Plain Radiography (X-Ray Wrist AP & Lateral)**:
    - Demonstrates normal bony architecture; rules out underlying radiocarpal osteoarthritis, carpal bone pathology, or osteochondroma.
- **MRI Wrist**: Reserved for occult intra-osseous or deep unpalpable wrist ganglions causing unexplained wrist pain.

#### A. Conservative Management

- **Observation & Reassurance**: Asymptomatic small ganglions require no active surgical intervention; up to **40%–50% undergo spontaneous regression** over time.
- **Historical Rest Method**: Application of a firm crepe bandage or temporary wrist splint. _(Note: The traditional practice of smashing the cyst with a heavy book is obsolete and strongly condemned due to risk of tissue injury)._

#### B. Pharmacological & Interventional Management

#### C. Surgical Management

- **Indication**:
    1. Recurrent ganglion following aspiration and steroid/hyalase therapy.
    2. Severe persistent pain or restriction of wrist range of motion.
    3. Neurological compression (median or ulnar nerve compression).
    4. Patient preference for cosmetic deformity.
- **Procedure (Surgical Excision)**:
    - Performed under regional/general anesthesia using a tourniquet for a bloodless field.
    - Complete **en-bloc excision of the ganglion cyst** along with its duct, neck, and a generous cuff of the underlying joint capsule or tendon sheath pedicle.
    - _Crucial Step_: Complete excision of the capsular base is essential to prevent local recurrence.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Recurrence**: Most frequent complication (**10%–20% following surgery**; **> 50% following aspiration alone**), occurring if the capsular attachment or pedicle is incompletely excised.
- **Radial Artery Injury**: Risk during surgical release of volar wrist ganglions situated adjacent to the radial artery.
- **Scapholunate Instability**: Occurs if excessive capsular resection is performed at the scapholunate ligament base.
- **Post-Operative Stiffness & Scar Tenderness**.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Good**: Excellent functional outcome following complete surgical excision including the capsular pedicle, although patients must be warned about potential recurrence.

- **G** - **G**elatinous / Mucinous thick fluid contents
- **A** - **A**spiration + **H**yalase / Steroid injection as first-line intervention

#### Memory Palace: The Wrist Crystal Sphere

1. **At the Desk**: A 25-year-old female typist holding her right wrist, noticing a smooth marble-like bump on the back of her wrist (**Young female, dorsum of wrist**).
2. **Under the Torchlight**: The doctor shines a bright penlight through the marble, making it glow like a clear glass bulb (**Positive Transillumination test**).
3. **At the Aspiration Tray**: A needle draws out clear, thick honey-like gel (**Mucinous fluid contents**).
4. **In the Operating Room**: A surgeon carefully dissects the marble down to its roots in the joint capsule, cutting out the stem along with the bulb (**En-bloc excision with capsular base to prevent recurrence**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- A wrist ganglion often palpaes as **bony-hard** on clinical examination because the mucinous fluid is trapped under extreme pressure within a non-yielding fibrous sac; never mistake a very tense dorsal wrist ganglion for an osteoma, exostosis, or bone tumor. Always perform a **transillumination test**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing a simple **Wrist Ganglion** with a **Compound Palmar Ganglion**.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **Simple Wrist Ganglion**: Benign unilocular cyst from mucoid degeneration over the dorsal or volar wrist; contains clear mucinous fluid; transillumination positive.
    - **Compound Palmar Ganglion**: Chronic **Tuberculous or Rheumatoid tenosynovitis** of flexor tendon sheaths forming an hourglass swelling across the flexor retinaculum; contains **rice bodies** and turbid fluid; transillumination negative.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Cause**: Most common wrist cyst resulting from **mucoid degeneration of capsule/tendon sheath**.
- **Common Site**: **Dorsum of wrist** (scapholunate joint) > Volar wrist.
- **Diagnostic Hallmark**: **Positive Transillumination test** & clear mucinous fluid aspiration.
- **Conservative Treatment**: Aspiration + **Hyalase / Triamcinolone steroid injection**.
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: **En-bloc surgical excision including pedicle and capsular base** to prevent recurrence.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **A ganglion is the most common benign cystic swelling of the wrist, arising from mucoid degeneration of the joint capsule or tendon sheath, characteristically presenting as a tense, brilliantly transilluminant swelling on the dorsum of the wrist, and managed by aspiration with steroid/hyalase injection or complete surgical excision of the cyst with its capsular base.**
