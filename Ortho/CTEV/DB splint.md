# Denis-Browne Splint & CTEV Shoe [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition & Description [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Denis-Browne Splint (DB Splint)**: Also referred to as a **Foot Abduction Orthosis (FAO)**, it is a specialized orthotic appliance designed to maintain correction in **Congenital Talipes Equinovarus (CTEV / Clubfoot)**following serial manipulation and casting.
- **Components**: Consists of **two padded open-toed shoes or footplates** attached to a **transverse adjustable metal bar** set in external rotation.
- **CTEV Shoes**: Specially modified leather walking shoes prescribed once the child begins independent walking, used during daytime to maintain foot alignment.

---

### 2. Indications & Purpose [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primary Indication**: Maintenance of corrected foot position in **idiopathic CTEV** after complete correction via the **Ponseti method** (serial casting and percutaneous tendoachilles tenotomy).
- **Secondary Indications**: Prevention of deformity relapse in postural or neurogenic clubfoot following surgical or manipulative correction.
- **Core Purpose**: Prevents recurrence of the primary CTEV deformities—**Equinus**, **Varus**, and **Adduction**.

---

### 3. Mechanism of Action & Biomechanics [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Dynamic Stretching**: As the infant kicks or flexes and extends the knees, the rigid connecting bar enforces abduction and external rotation of both feet.
- **Soft Tissue Elongation**: Provides continuous dynamic stretching of the contracted **medial and posterior soft tissue structures** (e.g., **Tibialis posterior**, **Tendoachilles**, **spring ligament**).
- **Maintenance of Talocalcaneal Abduction**: Holds the calcaneo-cuboid-navicular complex in abduction under the talar head, maintaining the corrected **Kite's angle**.

### 4. Protocol & Application Schedule [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pre-Walking Phase (Infants < 1 Year)**:
    - **Full-Time Wear**: Applied immediately after final post-tenotomy cast removal.
    - **Duration**: Worn for **23 hours per day** for the first **3 months**.
- **Walking Phase (Children Age 1 to 5 Years)**:
    - **Night-Time Wear**: DB splint worn for **12–14 hours per day** (during night sleep and afternoon naps).
    - **Day-Time Wear**: Replaced by **CTEV shoes** during active walking hours.
    - **Total Treatment Duration**: Maintained strictly until **3 to 5 years of age**.

---

### 5. CTEV Shoe Modifications (Comparison Table) [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature / Modification|Structural Design|Target Deformity Prevented|Biomechanical Rationale|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**1. Straight Inner Border**|Inner edge of sole is kept completely straight (no medial forefoot curve)|**Forefoot Adduction**|Prevents medial deviation of forefoot at midtarsal joints.|
|**2. Outer Shoe Raise**|Lateral border of sole and heel raised by **1/8 to 1/4 inch**|**Heel / Foot Varus (Inversion)**|Forces foot into eversion, maintaining subtalar joint in neutral/valgus.|
|**3. Absence of Heel (No Heel)**|Flat sole with complete removal of elevated heel|**Ankle Equinus (Plantarflexion)**|Promotes continuous dorsiflexion and prevents Achilles tendon re-tightening.|

---

### 6. Disadvantages & Complications

- **Low Patient / Parental Compliance**: The most frequent cause of CTEV relapse; parents find rigid bar splintage difficult to enforce in irritable infants.
- **Blisters & Pressure Sores**: Occurs over the heel or dorsum of foot if footwear is ill-fitting or loosely strapped.
- **Slipped Heel**: Heel pulls out of the boot, leading to incomplete correction of equinus.
- **Deformity Recurrence**: Recovers rapidly if splint protocol is stopped prematurely before age 3–5 years (**equinus is the first deformity to recur**).

### Clinical Pearl

- Strict adherence to the DB splint protocol until **4–5 years of age** is the single most critical determinant of long-term success in clubfoot management; over 80% of relapses are directly caused by non-compliance with night-time splinting.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Swapping the purpose of **DB Splint** (maintains corrected foot alignment) with **Ponseti POP Casts**(actively corrects the deformity), or confusing CTEV shoe modifications.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **Casts vs. DB Splint**: Serial POP casts **correct** the deformities; DB splint **maintains** the correction.
    - **CTEV Shoe Modifications**: **Outer raise** (not inner) prevents varus; **No heel** (not raised heel) prevents equinus.

### Quick Revision

- **DB Splint**: Foot abduction orthosis consisting of footplates attached to a transverse metal bar.
- **Indication**: **Maintains correction** achieved by Ponseti serial casting in CTEV.
- **Wear Schedule**: **23 hours/day for 3 months** → **Night-time DB splint + Daytime CTEV shoes till 3–5 years**.
- **CTEV Shoe Modifications**: **Straight inner border** (prevents adduction), **Outer raise** (prevents varus), **No heel**(prevents equinus).
- **Primary Complication**: **Poor compliance** leading to rapid deformity relapse.
