# Prolapse of Lumbar Intervertebral Disc (PIVD / Clubbed Disc) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Prolapse of Intervertebral Disc (PIVD)**: Defined as the herniation, protrusion, or extrusion of the gelatinous **nucleus pulposus** through a rupture or tear in the surrounding **annulus fibrosus**.
- **Most Common Age Group**: Adults aged **20–40 years** with sedentary jobs or heavy manual labour.
- **Sex Ratio**: **Males > Females**.
- **Most Common Levels**:
    1. **L₄–L₅ level** (compresses traversing **L₅ root**) — **Most common overall**.
    2. **L₅–S₁ level** (compresses traversing **S₁ root**).
    3. **L₃–L₄ level** (uncommon, compresses **L₄ root**).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Primary Degeneration**: Age-related dehydration, fragmentation, and loss of proteoglycans in the nucleus pulposus (Type II collagen) and weakening of the annulus fibrosus (Type I collagen).
- **Precipitating Factors**:
    - Sudden heavy weight lifting while in a flexed posture.
    - Bending forward, twisting strain, or sudden vehicular jerk.
    - Chronic occupational lumbar strain (surgeons, drivers, computer professionals).
    - Postural insufficiency and lack of physical exercise.

---

### 3. Classification / Stages of Disc Prolapse [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Morphological Stages of Prolapse

1. **Stage 1 (Nucleus Degeneration / Bulging)**: Softening, disintegration, and central weakening of posterior annulus without displacement.
2. **Stage 2 (Protrusion)**: Nucleus pulposus bulges posterolaterally through incomplete annular rent ("disc bulge").
3. **Stage 3 (Extrusion)**: Nucleus pulposus ruptures completely through annulus fibrosus and lies under posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL).
4. **Stage 4 (Sequestration)**: Extruded disc fragment breaks completely free from parent disc and lies free inside the spinal canal.

#### B. Anatomical Location Classification

- **Paracentral / Posterolateral Prolapse** (**Most common**): Impinges on the **traversing nerve root** entering the spinal canal below.
- **Far Lateral Prolapse**: Impinges on the **exiting nerve root** at the same foraminal level.
- **Central / Posterior-Midline Prolapse**: Causes compression of the entire cauda equina or dural sac.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Low Backache**: Acute onset following exertion/lifting; severe, dull, aggravated by forward bending, sitting, coughing, or sneezing.
- **Sciatica**: Sharp, burning pain radiating from the gluteal region down the posterior/postero-lateral thigh, leg, and foot along the distribution of the compressed nerve root.
- **Paresthesias**: "Pins and needles" sensation or numbness in the dermatome corresponding to the affected nerve root.

#### B. Examination Signs

- **Posture & Gait**: Rigid, flattened lumbar spine with loss of normal lumbar lordosis; **Sciatic Tilt / Scoliosis** towards or away from the side of pain.
- **Spinal Movements**: Forward bending is severely restricted and initiates paraspinal muscle spasm.
- **Local Tenderness**: Diffuse tenderness in the lumbosacral region and localized tenderness over the affected spinous process / paraspinal space.
- **Nerve Root Tension Tests**:
    - **Straight Leg Raising Test (SLRT)**: Patient supine; leg raised with knee straight. Pain produced at `< 40°`indicates root compression.
    - **Bragard's Sign**: Ankle dorsiflexion at the limit of SLRT accentuates pain.
    - **Contralateral SLRT**: Raising the un-affected leg reproduces pain on the affected side (**highly specific for disc prolapse**).
    - **Lasegue Test**: Flexing hip to 90° with knee flexed, then extending the knee reproduces sciatic pain.

#### C. Root-Level Neurological Deficits (Table 29.1)

|Disc Level|Affected Nerve Root|Motor Weakness|Sensory Loss|Reflex Alteration|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Imaging Modalities

1. **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Scan) — Investigation of Choice (IOC)**:
    - Accurately demonstrates disc herniation, canal stenosis, dural sac indentation, and root compression.
    - Shows posterior disc bulge, disc extrusion, or sequestered fragments clearly on axial and sagittal T2-weighted views.
2. **Plain Radiograph (X-Ray Lumbosacral Spine AP & Lateral)**:
    - Usually normal in acute disc prolapse; excludes bony lesions (spondylolisthesis, fracture, infection, or tumor).
    - In chronic cases, demonstrates narrowing of intervertebral disc space, loss of lumbar lordosis, and posterior osteophytes.
3. **CT Scan**: Used when MRI is contraindicated or unavailable; delineates bony canal structure and disc herniation.
4. **Electromyography (EMG)**: Confirms specific root denervation.

#### A. Conservative Management (Successful in > 90% Cases)

- **Rest**: Absolute rest on a firm bed in semi-Fowler position (supine with hips and knees flexed 30°–40°) for a short period (2–4 days).
- **Physiotherapy & Physical Modalities**:
    - Hot fomentation, short-wave diathermy (SWD), and TENS for acute muscle spasm relief.
    - **Pelvic / Lumbar Traction**: Relieves paraspinal spasm and reduces intradiscal pressure.
    - **Lumbar Corset / Belt**: Temporary immobilization during acute ambulatory phase.

#### B. Pharmacological Management

### Dosage Rule

|Name|Mechanism of Action|Dose|Dosage / Route|Frequency|Features|Side Effects|When to Start / Stop|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Naproxen**|Reversible inhibition of COX-1 & COX-2 enzymes → ↓ Prostaglandins|**250–500 mg**|Oral|Twice daily (12-hourly)|First-line anti-inflammatory for acute disc radiculopathy|Gastric irritation, peptic ulceration, fluid retention|Start immediately at onset; stop after 2–3 weeks of pain resolution|
|**Thiocolchicoside**|Selective GABA-A receptor agonist & glycinergic muscle relaxant|**4–8 mg**|Oral|Twice daily|Relieves severe secondary paraspinal muscle spasm|Somnolence, mild GI upset, dizziness|Start during acute spastic phase; stop after 5–7 days|
|**Pregabalin**|Binds α₂-δ subunit of voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels → ↓ glutamate/substance P|**75–150 mg**|Oral|Nightly or twice daily|Specific for neuropathic radicular sciatic pain|Sedation, dizziness, weight gain, peripheral edema|Start if radicular pain persists; taper off after 4–6 weeks|

#### C. Operative Management

- **Indications for Surgery**:
    1. **Cauda Equina Syndrome** (Saddle anesthesia, bowel/bladder incontinence) — **EMERGENCY SURGERY**.
    2. Failure of conservative management after **3–6 weeks** of strict therapy.
    3. Progressive or severe motor weakness (e.g., evolving foot drop).
- **Surgical Techniques**:
    - **Microdiscectomy / Endoscopic Discectomy**: Gold standard minimally invasive removal of herniated disc material.
    - **Fenestration / Laminotomy & Discectomy**: Excision of ligamentum flavum and a margin of lamina to excise herniated disc fragment.
    - **Laminectomy & Discectomy**: Reserved for large central discs causing cauda equina compression.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cauda Equina Syndrome**: Massive central disc prolapse compressing multiple sacral roots leading to saddle anesthesia, flaccid lower limb paralysis, and urinary retention / fecal incontinence.
- **Foot Drop**: Paralysis of ankle dorsiflexors due to severe \(L_4\) or \(L_5\) root compression.
- **Chronic Backache & Failed Back Syndrome**: Post-operative epidural fibrosis or secondary facet joint osteoarthritis.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Over 90% of patients recover completely with conservative non-surgical management.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: DISC-SLRT

- **D** - **D**orsiflexion weakness of big toe (\(L_5\) root / EHL)
- **I** - **I**nvestigation of choice = MRI scan
- **S** - **S**ciatica & **S**LRT positive at `< 40°`
- **C** - **C**entral disc causing Cauda Equina Syndrome
- **S** - **S**emi-Fowler rest position + NSAIDs
- **L** - **L₄–L₅ & L₅–S₁** most common levels
- **R** - **R**oot compression (Paracentral affects traversing root)
- **T** - **T**reatment is conservative in >90% cases

#### Memory Palace: The Lumbar Spine Workshop

1. **At the Workshop Door**: A 30-year-old weightlifter bends down to pick up a heavy box, feeling a sudden pop in his back (**Young adult, L₄–L₅ disc prolapse, flexion strain**).
2. **Along the Floor**: A long electrical cable (sciatic nerve) sparks pain all the way down his outer leg to his big toe (**Sciatica, L₅ dermatome**).
3. **In the Examination Room**: A doctor lifts his leg straight up to 30°, causing him to wince in pain (**Positive SLRT**).
4. **At the Scanning Station**: A high-tech MRI scanner displaying a red bulging gel cushion pressing on the nerve root (**MRI as Investigation of Choice**).
5. **In the Recovery Area**: The patient rests in bed with knees bent, taking a tablet of Naproxen (**Conservative Semi-Fowler rest & NSAIDs**).

### Clinical Pearl

- **Contralateral SLRT (Fajersztajn's Sign)**: Raising the unaffected leg produces severe pain on the affected side. This test is far more specific for a true extruded or sequestered lumbar disc prolapse than a ipsilateral SLRT.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing the nerve root affected in a **paracentral \(L_4-L_5\) disc prolapse**.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: A paracentral disc prolapse at \(L_4-L_5\) compresses the **traversing \(L_5\) nerve root**(exiting canal below), NOT the exiting \(L_4\) root. The \(L_4\) root is compressed only by a rare _far lateral_ \(L_4-L_5\) disc prolapse.

### Diagram Guide

### Diagram 1: Stages of Disc Prolapse (Pathoanatomy)

- **Type**: Labeled sagittal schematic line diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Intervertebral disc between two lumbar vertebral bodies.
- **Labels in order (A to D)**:
    1. **A - Degeneration**: Intact annulus with central nuclear softening.
    2. **B - Protrusion**: Bulging nucleus against weakened inner annulus.
    3. **C - Extrusion**: Nucleus breaching annulus, lying under posterior longitudinal ligament.
    4. **D - Sequestration**: Free disc fragment detached inside the spinal canal.
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: "Paracentral herniation impinges on the traversing nerve root of the lower vertebra".

### Diagram 2: Straight Leg Raising Test (SLRT) & Nerve Root Stretch

- **Type**: Clinical examination schematic.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Patient in supine position with leg elevated by examiner.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Supine Position**: Patient lying flat on a firm examination couch.
    2. **Angle of Elevation**: Sciatic pain produced between **20°–40°** elevation.
    3. **Bragard's Reinforcement**: Additional ankle dorsiflexion accentuates root pain.
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: "Positive SLRT indicates mechanical nerve root compression at L₄, L₅, or S₁ levels".

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Site**: Protrusion of nucleus pulposus through annulus fibrosus; most common at **\(L_4-L_5\) and \(L_5-S_1\)**.
- **Classic Clinical Triad**: Low backache + **Sciatica** + Positive **SLRT** (`< 40°`).
- **Key Motor Sign (\(L_5\) Root)**: Weakness of **Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL)** / big toe dorsiflexion.
- **Investigation of Choice**: **MRI Scan** of lumbosacral spine.
- **Treatment**: **Conservative rest + NSAIDs + Traction** for > 90% cases; **Microdiscectomy** for failed conservative care or cauda equina emergency.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Prolapse of lumbar intervertebral disc (PIVD) is a common spinal disorder in young adults characterized by the herniation of the nucleus pulposus through the annulus fibrosus at the \(L_4-L_5\) or \(L_5-S_1\) level, presenting with low backache, sciatica, and a positive straight leg raising test, and diagnosed definitively by MRI.**
