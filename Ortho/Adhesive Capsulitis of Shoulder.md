# Adhesive Capsulitis of Shoulder (Frozen Shoulder / Periarthritis) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Adhesive Capsulitis**, commonly referred to as **Frozen Shoulder** or **Periarthritis of the Shoulder**, is defined as a self-limiting condition of unknown etiology characterized by painful and progressive global restriction of both active and passive glenohumeral joint movements.
- **Pathological Feature**: Loss of resilience, chronic inflammation, thickening, fibrosis, and contracture of the glenohumeral joint capsule, accompanied by adhesions between its folds.
- **Bilateral Involvement**: Occurs bilaterally in approximately **30% of cases**.
- **Sex & Age Predilection**: Most frequently affects individuals between **40 and 60 years of age**, with a higher incidence in females.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Primary / Idiopathic**: Most common form, arising spontaneously without any identifiable antecedent injury.
- **Precipitating Systemic Conditions**:
    - **Diabetes Mellitus**: Strongest clinical association (uncontrolled hyperglycemia increases risk by 5-fold).
    - **Thyroid Disorders**: Both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism.
    - **Cardiovascular Disease & Post-Stroke Hemiplegia**.
- **Secondary / Post-Traumatic Causes**:
    - History of preceding minor shoulder trauma or contusion.
    - Prolonged shoulder immobilization following distal upper limb fractures (e.g., Colles' fracture, humeral shaft fracture).
    - Post-rotator cuff tendinitis or subacromial bursitis.

---

### 3. Classification & Stages [⭐ High-Yield]

Adhesive capsulitis characteristically evolves through **three clinical stages** over a period of 6 months to 2 years:

|Stage|Name of Stage|Patho-Mechanism|Pain Severity|Stiffness Severity|Duration|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Stage I**|**Freezing Stage**|Acute hypervascular capsulitis & synovial inflammation|**Severe** (worst at night)|Minimal / Absent|2–9 months|
|**Stage II**|**Frozen Stage**|Capsular thickening, intra-articular adhesions & contracture|Subceding / Decreased|**Maximum** (global ROM loss)|4–12 months|
|**Stage III**|**Thawing Stage**|Capsular remodeling & spontaneous resolution|Minimal / Absent|Progressive improvement|5–24 months|

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Pain**:
    - Insidious onset of dull, aching pain around the deltoid insertion.
    - Characteristically **worst at night**, disturbing sleep when rolling onto the affected side in early stages.
    - In later stages, pain becomes constant and is elicited by any attempted movement.
- **Stiffness**: Progressive inability to perform daily functional tasks (e.g., combing hair, reaching into a back pocket, fastening clothing behind the back).

#### B. Examination Signs

- **Restriction of Movements (Global ROM Loss)**:
    - Both **active AND passive movements** of the shoulder are severely restricted in all planes.
    - **Earliest & Most Severely Restricted Movement**: **External Rotation > Abduction > Internal Rotation**.
- **Tenderness**: Diffuse tenderness over the anterior and posterior glenohumeral joint line and subacromial space.
- **Muscle Atrophy**: Disuse wasting of the deltoid, supraspinatus, and infraspinatus muscles in long-standing cases.
- **Scapulothoracic Compensation**: Humerus moves in unison with the scapula ("shrugging" of shoulder during attempted elevation).

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: Adhesive capsulitis is primarily a **clinical diagnosis** based on global active and passive ROM limitation.
- **Plain Radiography (X-Ray Shoulder AP & Lateral Views)**:
    - **Usually Normal** in primary adhesive capsulitis.
    - May demonstrate disuse osteopenia (juxta-articular rarefaction) of the humeral head in chronic cases.
    - Primary utility is to rule out bony pathology (e.g., calcific tendinitis, osteoarthritis, old fracture, or dislocation).
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Scan)**:
    - Demonstrates **thickening of the joint capsule and coracohumeral ligament** (> 4 mm) in the axillary recess and rotator interval, alongside joint effusion/capsulitis.
- **Blood Investigations**: Mandatory screening for **fasting blood glucose / HbA1c** to detect underlying diabetes mellitus.

---

#### A. Conservative / Non-Operative Management (First-Line)

- **Patient Education**: Reassure the patient that the condition is self-limiting (usually resolving over 6–9 months).
- **Physiotherapy & Exercise Therapy**:
    - **Codman's Pendulum Exercises**: Gentle gravity-assisted pendulum swings to maintain joint mobility.
    - Active-assisted stretching exercises using a wand or pulley system targeting external rotation and abduction.
- **Physical Modalities**: Local moist heat, infra-red radiation, or short-wave diathermy (SWD) prior to stretching to relieve secondary muscle spasm.

#### B. Pharmacological Treatment

|**Hydrocortisone Acetate**|Potent anti-inflammatory steroid suppressing capsular fibroblastic proliferation [Maheshwari p. 80]|**25–50 mg**|Intra-articular glenohumeral injection|Single dose (may repeat once at 4 weeks)|Speeds up pain relief and functional recovery significantly|Local skin depigmentation, transient flare reaction|Indicated in severe night pain unresponsive to oral NSAIDs.|

#### C. Operative / Interventional Management (Resistant Cases)

- **Manipulation Under Anesthesia (MUA)**: Controlled passive manipulation of the shoulder into abduction and external rotation under general anesthesia to rupture tight capsular adhesions.
- **Arthroscopic Capsular Release**: Procedure of choice for refractory cases; selective division of the thickened anterior capsule, coracohumeral ligament, and axillary recess under direct vision.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Residual Shoulder Stiffness**: Persistent mild restriction of terminal abduction or internal rotation.
- **Iatrogenic Fracture**: Humeral neck fracture or glenoid labral tear during over-aggressive Manipulation Under Anesthesia (MUA).
- **Reflex Sympathetic Dystrophy (Sudeck's Atrophy)**: Complex regional pain syndrome involving the hand and wrist on the affected side.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Good**: Self-limiting condition in the majority of cases; > 90% of patients achieve functional recovery with conservative management and glycemic control.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: FROZEN

- **F** - **F**emale predilection & **F**reezing stage (pain worst at night)
- **R** - **R**estriction of active and passive movements globally
- **O** - **O**utstanding loss of **External Rotation** (earliest movement lost)
- **Z** - **Z**ero bone changes on plain X-ray (normal radiograph)
- **E** - **E**tiology: Diabetes mellitus & thyroid disorders strongly linked
- **N** - **N**SAIDs, Steroid injection, and **N**ight pendulum exercises for treatment

#### Memory Palace: The Icy Shoulder Ward

1. **The Frozen Door**: Entering a room covered in ice with a diabetic patient clutching her shoulder in pain at midnight (**Diabetic association, Freezing stage, night pain**).
2. **The Examination Arm**: Trying to rotate the frozen arm outward, but it feels locked solid like a statue (**Restriction of External Rotation**).
3. **The Clear X-Ray View**: Looking at an X-ray film on the light box that appears completely normal (**Normal X-ray**).
4. **The Thawing Fireplace**: Applying warm heating pads and injecting a clear steroid solution into the joint, allowing the ice to melt (**Heat therapy, intra-articular steroid, thawing phase**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- The pathognomonic clinical feature that differentiates **Adhesive Capsulitis** from all other soft tissue shoulder conditions (such as rotator cuff tears or subacromial bursitis) is the **equal restriction of both ACTIVE and PASSIVE range of motion in all planes**, with **external rotation being the earliest and most severely affected**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing **Adhesive Capsulitis** with a **Rotator Cuff Tear**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: In a **Rotator Cuff Tear**, passive range of motion is completely normal (the examiner can move the arm freely), but active movement is painful or weak. In **Adhesive Capsulitis**, both active and passive ROM are strictly limited due to capsular contracture.

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and contracture of the glenohumeral joint capsule.
- **Key Associations**: **Diabetes Mellitus** (uncontrolled hyperglycemia) and thyroid disorders.
- **Hallmark Clinical Sign**: **Global loss of both active and passive ROM** (External rotation most restricted).
- **Radiology**: **Normal plain X-ray**; MRI shows capsular thickening in axillary recess.
- **Treatment Standard**: Glycemic control + NSAIDs + Intra-articular hydrocortisone + Pendulum exercises.
