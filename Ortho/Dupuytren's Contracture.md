# Dupuytren's Contracture (Contracture of Palmar Aponeurosis) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Dupuytren's Contracture**: A progressive fibro-proliferative condition characterized by abnormal cord-like fibrosis, thickening, and shortening of the palmar aponeurosis (palmar fascia), leading to a fixed flexion deformity of one or more fingers.

### Key Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology & Demographics**: Most commonly idiopathic with a strong hereditary predisposition; more frequent in **males than females (M > F)** and often bilateral. Strongly associated with **diabetes mellitus**, **alcoholism / hepatic cirrhosis**, and long-term use of **sodium hydantoin (phenytoin)** in epileptics.
- **Anatomy & Predilection Sites**: The **ring finger** is the most commonly affected digit, followed by the little finger, with the **metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint** being the most frequently involved joint (along with the proximal interphalangeal [PIP] joint).
- **Ectopic Manifestations**: May be associated with extra-palmar fibromatosis in other body sites, such as **Ledderhose disease** (plantar fascia of the soles of feet) and **Peyronie's disease** (penile fascia).
- **Clinical Presentation & Differential**: Begins as painless nodular thickening at the bases of the fingers, progressing to fibrotic flexion cords; differentiated from flexor tendon contractures because the **distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint remains extended** in Dupuytren's contracture.
- **Management Protocol**: Mild, non-disabling contractures in elderly patients require no active intervention. Treatment options include conservative observation, intralesional **collagenase injections**, or surgical **fasciectomy / subtotal excision of the palmar aponeurosis** (indicated if MCP joint contracture is >30° or PIP joint contracture is >15°).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- In Dupuytren's contracture, the flexion deformity involves only the **MCP and PIP joints** while the **DIP joint is characteristically spared** (remains extended), whereas flexor tendon contractures produce flexion deformity across **all three joints (MCP, PIP, and DIP)**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Dupuytren's Contracture** with **Trigger Finger**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Dupuytren's contracture is a progressive, fixed palmar fascial cord contracture without joint locking or snapping, whereas Trigger Finger is an A1 pulley flexor tendon sheath constriction that produces a characteristic pistol-like snap-release during extension.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Fibrous thickening and shortening of the **palmar aponeurosis**.
- **Demographics & Risk**: **Males > Females**; linked to **diabetes**, **alcoholism/cirrhosis**, and **sodium hydantoin**.
- **Most Common Site**: **Ring finger**; **metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint**.
- **Associated Conditions**: **Ledderhose disease** (soles) & **Peyronie's disease** (penis).
- **Treatment Standard**: **Subtotal fasciectomy** or **collagenase injection** if MCP contracture >30° or PIP contracture >15°.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Dupuytren's contracture is a progressive fibro-proliferative disorder of the palmar aponeurosis strongly associated with diabetes, alcoholism, and hereditary factors, presenting with a flexion deformity of the ring and little fingers at the MCP and PIP joints with characteristic sparing of the DIP joint, and treated by subtotal fasciectomy or collagenase injection.**
