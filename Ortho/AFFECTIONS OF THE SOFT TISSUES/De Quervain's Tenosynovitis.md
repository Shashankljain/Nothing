# De Quervain's Tenosynovitis (De Quervain's Disease) [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **De Quervain's Tenosynovitis** (also known as **De Quervain's Stenosing Tenosynovitis** or **De Quervain's Disease**): A painful non-infectious inflammatory condition characterized by stenosis and thickening of the common synovial sheath enclosing the **Abductor Pollicis Longus (APL)** and **Extensor Pollicis Brevis (EPB)** tendons.
- **Anatomical Site**: Located within the **first dorsal osteofibrous compartment** of the wrist over the groove of the **radial styloid process**.
- **Sex Predilection**: Significantly more common in **females than males (F > M)**, particularly women in the age group of 30–50 years and young mothers (due to repetitive lifting of infants).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Repetitive Microtrauma / Overuse (Most Common)**: Frequent repetitive wrist and thumb movements involving combined thumb abduction and ulnar deviation of the wrist (e.g., typing, twisting, wringing clothes, lifting babies, gardening, racquet sports).
- **Inflammatory Systemic Conditions**: **Rheumatoid arthritis** causing chronic proliferative tenosynovitis.
- **Post-Traumatic**: Direct blunt blow or fracture callus over the radial styloid process creating mechanical friction.

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Radial Wrist Pain**: Insidious onset of aching or sharp pain over the radial styloid process at the base of the thumb.
- **Aggravating Factors**: Pain is characteristically exacerbated by flexing the thumb, pinching, grasping objects, and turning the wrist (ulnar deviation).
- **Radiation**: Pain may radiate proximally into the dorsolateral forearm or distally into the thumb.

#### B. Examination Signs

- **Localized Tenderness**: Exquisite point tenderness strictly localized over the **radial styloid process**.
- **Local Swelling & Thickening**: Visible or palpable fusiform thickening and firmness over the first dorsal compartment tendon sheath.
- **Crepitus**: Fine crepitus felt on active motion of the thumb in severe acute cases.

#### C. Special Provocative Clinical Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Finkelstein's Test**:
    - _Procedure_: The patient flexes the thumb across the palm, tucks it under the folded fingers to form a fist, and the examiner forcefully deviates the wrist toward the ulnar side.
    - _Positive Result_: Sharp, severe pain reproduced directly over the radial styloid process.
2. **Eichhoff's Test**:
    - _Procedure_: Similar maneuver where the patient actively flexes the thumb into the palm, closes the fingers tightly over it, and actively bends the wrist in ulnar deviation.
    - _Positive Result_: Intense localized pain over the first dorsal compartment.

---

### 5. Diagnosis / Investigations

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: De Quervain's disease is primarily diagnosed clinically based on localized radial styloid tenderness and a positive **Finkelstein's test**.
- **Plain Radiography (X-Ray Wrist AP & Lateral Views)**:
    - Typically normal; ordered to exclude bony pathology such as scaphoid fracture, radial styloid osteophytes, or osteoarthritis of the first carpometacarpal (CMC) joint.
- **Ultrasonography (USG) / MRI**: Demonstrates hypoechoic halo, synovial thickening, and fluid accumulation surrounding the APL and EPB tendons within the first dorsal compartment.

#### A. Non-Pharmacological / Conservative Management

- **Rest & Immobilization**: Immobilization of the wrist and thumb using a **thumb spica splint**, plaster slab, or firm crepe bandage to prevent thumb abduction and ulnar deviation.
- **Physical Modalities**: **Ultrasonic radiation therapy** and local ice pack application to diminish acute inflammatory edema.
- **Activity Modification**: Avoidance of repetitive pinching, wringing, or forceful thumb abduction activities.

#### C. Surgical Management

- **Indications**: Chronic, resistant, or recurrent cases failing conservative therapy and steroid infiltration.
- **Procedure**: **Surgical Release of First Dorsal Compartment**.
    - Under local anesthesia, a longitudinal or transverse incision is made over the radial styloid.
    - The tight fibrous retinaculum of the first dorsal compartment is **longitudinally split and partially excised**, freeing the APL and EPB tendons completely.
    - _Surgical Precaution_: Care is taken to identify and protect the superficial branch of the radial nerve and look for sub-compartments/separate slips of APL/EPB tendons.

---

### 8. Complications

- **Recurrence**: Occurs if a separate sub-compartment of EPB or an aberrant slip of APL is missed during surgical release.
- **Radial Nerve Neuritis / Neuroma**: Injury or traction on the superficial radial nerve causing burning paresthesia over the 1st web space.
- **Tendon Subluxation**: Volar subluxation of APL/EPB tendons following overly generous excision of the retinacular sheath.

---

### 9. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Over 80%–90% of early cases resolve with conservative splinting and local steroid injection. Surgical release yields near 100% cure in refractory cases.

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: FINKEL

- **F** - **F**inkelstein's test positive (pain on thumb flexion + ulnar deviation)
- **I** - **I**nflammation of common sheath of APL & EPB tendons
- **N** - **N**SAIDs, rest, and thumb spica splint for conservative care
- **K** - **K**eep off thumb overuse (repetitive microtrauma / F > M)
- **E** - **E**ichhoff's test secondary evaluation
- **L** - **L**ocal hydrocortisone infiltration or surgical sheath release

#### Memory Palace: The Radial Styloid Gate

1. **At the Gate**: A 35-year-old mother holding her newborn baby, wincing in pain at her outer wrist (**Females > Males, overuse lifting baby**).
2. **On the Gate Post (Radial Styloid)**: Two ropes labelled **APL** and **EPB** rubbing against a rough wooden groove (**Abductor pollicis longus & Extensor pollicis brevis in 1st dorsal compartment**).
3. **The Examination Lock**: Tucking the thumb into the palm and pulling the hand downward causes a sharp snap of pain (**Finkelstein's test**).
4. **The Treatment Box**: A thumb spica splint holding the thumb still, accompanied by a syringe injecting white steroid liquid into the tight rope sheath (**Thumb spica splint, hydrocortisone infiltration, surgical sheath release**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- Always check for **anatomical variations and sub-compartments** during surgical release of De Quervain's disease: the Extensor Pollicis Brevis (EPB) tendon often lies in its own separate osseofibrous sub-compartment within the first dorsal tunnel; failure to identify and release this septum is the single most common cause of persistent pain after surgery.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the tendons involved in **De Quervain's Disease** with those in **Trigger Thumb** or **Intersection Syndrome**.
- **Distinguishing Facts**:
    - **De Quervain's Disease**: Involves **Abductor Pollicis Longus (APL)** and **Extensor Pollicis Brevis (EPB)** at the **first dorsal compartment** over the radial styloid process.
    - **Trigger Thumb**: Involves **Flexor Pollicis Longus (FPL)** at the **A1 pulley** on the volar side.
    - **Intersection Syndrome**: Involves ECRL/ECRB crossed by APL/EPB **4–6 cm proximal** to the wrist joint on the dorsal forearm.

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Stenosing tenosynovitis of **APL** and **EPB** in the **1st dorsal compartment** over the radial styloid.
- **Demographics**: **Females > Males**; associated with overuse and rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Hallmark Clinical Test**: **Finkelstein's test** (pain on thumb flexion + ulnar deviation).
- **First-Line Conservative Treatment**: Rest in **thumb spica splint** + NSAIDs + local ultrasound.
- **Definitive Invasives**: **Local hydrocortisone infiltration** → **Surgical longitudinal release** of 1st dorsal sheath.
