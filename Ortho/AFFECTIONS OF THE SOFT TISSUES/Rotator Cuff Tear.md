# Rotator Cuff Tear [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rotator Cuff Tear**: A tear involving one or more of the musculotendinous units forming the dynamic musculotendinous shroud of the glenohumeral joint.
- **Rotator Cuff Anatomy**: Comprises four intrinsic muscles—**Supraspinatus**, **Infraspinatus**, **Teres Minor**, and **Subscapularis** (Mnemonic: **SITS**).
    - _Supraspinatus_: Located superiorly; initiates abduction (0°–15°).
    - _Subscapularis_: Located anteriorly; internal rotator ("forgotten tendon").
    - _Infraspinatus & Teres Minor_: Located posteriorly; external rotators.
- **Most Common Tendon Torn**: **Supraspinatus tendon** is the most common tendon to tear in the body (Supraspinatus > Tendoachilles).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Degenerative Tears (Elderly)**: Most common cause; age-related hypovascularity and repetitive microtrauma within the hypovascular "critical zone" of the supraspinatus tendon near its insertion.
- **Traumatic Tears (Young Adults)**: Acute high-energy trauma, shoulder dislocation, or heavy lifting against resistance.
- **Subacromial Impingement**: Chronic impingement of the supraspinatus tendon beneath the coracoacromial arch (acromial spur, subacromial bursitis).

---

### 3. Classification

- **By Degree of Thickness**:
    1. _Partial-Thickness Tear_: Involves bursal or articular surface without full disruption.
    2. _Full-Thickness Tear_: Complete anatomical disruption extending from articular to bursal surface.
- **By Etiology**: Degenerative (elderly) vs Traumatic (young individuals).
- **By Tendon Involved**: Single-tendon (Supraspinatus most common) vs Multi-tendon / Massive rotator cuff tear.

- **Patho-Mechanism**: Disruption of the rotator cuff compromises the dynamic shroud stabilizing the glenohumeral joint, allowing the deltoid muscle to pull the humeral head superiorly against the acromion.
- **Biceps Tendon Secondary Rupture**: Chronic cuff deficiency places excessive stress on the long head of the biceps tendon, resulting in degeneration, rupture, and distal muscle belly retraction (**Popeye Sign**).

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Shoulder Pain**: Dull, aching pain localized over the anterolateral aspect of the shoulder and deltoid region, exaggerated by overhead activities and active movement.
- **Night Pain**: Inability to sleep on the affected side due to exacerbated nocturnal pain.
- **Weakness & Decreased ROM**: Inability to elevate or actively abduct the arm; feeling of shoulder instability or "dead arm".

#### B. Muscle-Specific Clinical Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

|Muscle|Primary Action|Diagnostic Clinical Tests|Key Test Findings|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Supraspinatus**|Abduction (0°–15°)|**Jobe's Test (Empty Can Test)** / Beer Can Test / Drop Arm Test|Arm abducted 90°, flexed 30°, internally rotated (thumbs down); pain/weakness on downward resistance.|
|**Infraspinatus**|External Rotation|**Horn Blower's Test** / External Rotation Lag Sign|Pain or weakness on resisted external rotation with arm at side.|
|**Teres Minor**|External Rotation|**Horn Blower's Test**|Weakness of external rotation with shoulder in 90° abduction.|
|**Subscapularis**("Forgotten Tendon")|Internal Rotation|**Lift-Off Test** / Belly Press Test / Bear Hug Test|Inability to lift the hand away from the lumbar spine against resistance.|

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Plain Radiography (X-Ray Shoulder AP View)**:
    - Typically **normal** in acute or early tears.
    - In chronic massive tears: Shows narrowing of the acromiohumeral distance (<7 mm), superior migration of the humeral head, and subacromial sclerosis.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) — Investigation of Choice (IOC)**:
    - Accurately demonstrates tendon discontinuity, gap size, muscle belly atrophy, and fatty infiltration.
- **Ultrasonography (USG Shoulder)**: Dynamic, cost-effective evaluation of tendon continuity.
- **Diagnostic Arthroscopy**: **Gold Standard** diagnostic and therapeutic modality.

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

|Feature|Rotator Cuff Tear|Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder)|Subacromial Impingement|Axillary Nerve Palsy|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Passive ROM**|**Normal / Preserved**|**Globally Restricted**(Active & Passive)|Normal|Normal|
|**Active ROM**|Restricted / Weak (Abduction)|Globally Restricted|Painful Arc (60°–120°)|Loss of active abduction|
|**Pathognomonic Sign**|**Positive Jobe's / Lift-off / Popeye sign**|Equal active & passive loss|Neer / Hawkins test positive|**Regimental badge sensory loss**|
|**MRI Findings**|**Tendon discontinuity / gap**|Capsular thickening in axillary recess|Subacromial bursitis|Denervation of Deltoid & Teres minor|

#### A. Conservative Management (First-Line for Degenerative / Partial Tears)

- **Activity Modification**: Avoidance of painful overhead lifting and repetitive shoulder activities.
- **Physiotherapy**: Deltoid and intact cuff muscle strengthening exercises; gentle ROM maintenance.
- **Pharmacotherapy**:

#### B. Surgical Management

- **Indications**: Acute full-thickness traumatic tears in young patients, failed conservative treatment (3–6 months), or progressive motor weakness.
- **Surgical Procedure**: **Arthroscopic Rotator Cuff Repair**.
    - Tendons are mobilized and re-attached directly to their anatomical footprint on the greater/lesser tuberosities of the humerus using **suture anchors** (small metallic/bioabsorbable screws with attached sutures).

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rotator Cuff Tear Arthropathy**: Advanced secondary glenohumeral joint destruction following chronic massive un-repaired tears.
- **Rupture of Long Head of Biceps**: Secondary degeneration leading to distal muscle belly retraction (**Popeye Sign**).
- **Persistent Stiffness & Re-tear**: Post-operative anchor pull-out or repair failure.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- Excellent functional recovery with early arthroscopic suture anchor repair in acute traumatic tears.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: SITS-JOBE

- **S** - **S**upraspinatus (most common tear; tested by Jobe's test)
- **I** - **I**nfraspinatus (external rotation; Horn blower's test)
- **T** - **T**eres minor (external rotation)
- **S** - **S**ubscapularis ("forgotten tendon"; internal rotation; Lift-off test)
- **J** - **J**obe's empty can test for supraspinatus
- **O** - **O**verhead activity avoidance + NSAIDs for conservative care
- **B** - **B**iceps long head rupture producing **Popeye sign**
- **E** - **E**valuation IOC = MRI; Gold Standard = Arthroscopy

#### Memory Palace: The Arm Gym & The Popeye Sailor

1. **At the Entrance**: A gym-goer attempting to lift a dumbbell sideways, wincing as his arm drops (**Supraspinatus tear, Drop arm test**).
2. **At the Soda Machine**: He holds a soda can turned upside down (**Empty Can / Jobe's test**).
3. **Behind the Machine**: He tries to lift his hand off his lower back (**Lift-Off test for Subscapularis**).
4. **At the Mirror**: His arm displays a large bulge like **Popeye the Sailor** due to a snapped biceps tendon (**Popeye Sign from chronic cuff tear**).
5. **In the Operating Room**: A surgeon fixing the torn tendon back onto the bone footprint using tiny screw anchors (**Arthroscopic suture anchor repair**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

- In Rotator Cuff Tears, **passive range of motion is normal**, whereas active movement is limited or weak. If **both active AND passive ROM are restricted**, suspect **Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder)** instead.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Misidentifying **Subscapularis** as an external rotator or missing its specific diagnostic tests.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Subscapularis is the **only internal rotator** of the rotator cuff ("the forgotten tendon"); it is specifically tested by the **Lift-Off test**, **Belly Press test**, and **Bear Hug test**, NOT the Horn blower's test.

### Quick Revision

- **Cuff Components**: **Supraspinatus** (m/c tear), **Infraspinatus**, **Teres Minor**, **Subscapularis** ("forgotten tendon").
- **Key Tests**: **Jobe's Empty Can test** (Supraspinatus), **Horn Blower's test** (Infraspinatus/Teres minor), **Lift-Off test**(Subscapularis).
- **Associated Sign**: **Popeye sign** (rupture of long head of biceps in chronic tears).
- **Investigation of Choice**: **MRI**; Gold standard: **Arthroscopy**.
- **Definitive Surgery**: **Arthroscopic Rotator Cuff Repair using Suture Anchors**.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Rotator cuff tear is a common cause of shoulder pain and disability characterized by disruption of one or more of the SITS tendons (most commonly the supraspinatus), presenting with weakness in active abduction, positive Jobe's empty can test, diagnosed by MRI, and managed by conservative rehabilitation or arthroscopic suture anchor repair.**
