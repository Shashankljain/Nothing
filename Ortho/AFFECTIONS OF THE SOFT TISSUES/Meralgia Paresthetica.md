# Meralgia Paresthetica [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Meralgia Paresthetica**: An entrapment neuropathy characterized by a feeling of tingling, burning, and numbness in the skin along the anterolateral aspect of the thigh, caused by compression or entrapment of the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh** as it passes beneath the fascia/inguinal ligament just medial to the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS).

### Key Clinical & Management Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology & Risk Factors**: Caused by mechanical compression under tight clothing around the hip or a tight belt, obesity/overweight, or iatrogenic nerve stretch during hyperflexion maneuvers (e.g., McRobert's procedure for delivery in shoulder dystocia).
- **Clinical Symptoms**: Purely sensory presentation consisting of localized burning pain, tingling, and paresthesia over the anterolateral territory of the thigh.
- **Clinical Examination Signs**: Altered sensation (hypoesthesia/hyperesthesia) over the distribution of the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh with completely normal motor power and normal deep tendon reflexes.
- **Conservative Treatment**: First-line non-specific management including oral analgesics, elimination of mechanical compression (avoiding tight belts/clothing, weight loss), and local hydrocortisone infiltration.
- **Surgical Intervention**: Indicated in chronic, severe, or treatment-resistant cases, consisting of **surgical decompression** of the nerve at its passage under the inguinal ligament.

---

### Clinical Pearl

- Meralgia paresthetica is a **purely sensory entrapment neuropathy**; because the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh contains zero motor fibers, patients present with isolated burning paresthesia over the lateral thigh while quadriceps motor power and the knee jerk reflex remain completely intact.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Meralgia Paresthetica** with **L₃ / L₄ Lumbar Radiculopathy** or **Femoral Nerve Palsy**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Meralgia paresthetica** produces purely sensory symptoms over the anterolateral thigh with **normal quadriceps motor strength and an intact knee jerk**, whereas L₃/L₄ radiculopathy or femoral nerve lesions cause quadriceps muscle weakness and a diminished or absent knee jerk reflex.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Entrapped Nerve**: **Lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh**.
- **Site of Entrapment**: Under the **inguinal ligament** medial to the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS).
- **Common Triggers**: Obesity, tight belts/clothing, or McRobert's delivery maneuver.
- **Clinical Feature**: Burning paresthesia over anterolateral thigh with **zero motor deficit**.
- **Treatment**: Analgesics, local **hydrocortisone infiltration**, or surgical nerve decompression.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Meralgia paresthetica is an entrapment neuropathy of the lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh as it passes beneath the inguinal ligament, clinically characterized by burning pain and numbness over the anterolateral aspect of the thigh without motor weakness, and managed conservatively with analgesics, local hydrocortisone injections, or surgical decompression.**
