# Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)**: A painful condition characterized by non-specific inflammation and micro-tearing at the common extensor origin on the lateral epicondyle of the humerus, most commonly affecting the **Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB)** tendon.

### Key Clinical & Management Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology**: Triggered by repetitive wrist extension and forearm pronation strain (e.g., tennis players, squeezing/wringing clothes, carrying heavy suitcases).
- **Clinical Presentation**: Point tenderness precisely localized to the lateral epicondyle of the humerus, with completely normal elbow joint range of motion and normal plain X-rays.
- **Special Diagnostic Tests**:
    - **Cozen's Test**: Pain reproduced at the lateral epicondyle on extending the wrist against manual resistance (or palmar-flexing the wrist with the forearm pronated and fist clenched).
    - **Wringing Test**: Severe pain elicited at the lateral epicondyle when asking the patient to wring a towel.
- **Treatment Protocol**:
    - **Conservative**: Activity modification, oral NSAIDs, and wearing a **counterforce brace** (dampens force transmission to the tendon origin).
    - **Interventional**: Local hydrocortisone or orthobiologic (Platelet-Rich Plasma / PRP) injection at the point of maximum tenderness for persistent cases.

---

### Clinical Pearl

- The single muscle earliest and most frequently involved in tennis elbow is the **Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB)**, rather than the extensor digitorum or ECRL.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Tennis Elbow** with **Golfer's Elbow**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Tennis Elbow** is _lateral epicondylitis_ affecting the common _extensor_ origin (ECRB), whereas **Golfer's Elbow** is _medial epicondylitis_ affecting the common _flexor_ origin (Flexor Carpi Radialis).

---

### Quick Revision

- **Site & Pathology**: Common extensor origin (**ECRB > ECRL**) at the lateral epicondyle.
- **Key Examination**: Point tenderness over the **lateral epicondyle** with pain on stretching extensors.
- **Special Tests**: **Cozen's test** (resisted wrist extension) and **Wringing test**.
- **Management**: Counterforce brace, NSAIDs, and local hydrocortisone/PRP injection.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) is an overuse tendinopathy at the common extensor origin of the lateral humeral epicondyle—primarily involving the Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB) tendon—clinically diagnosed by localized point tenderness and a positive Cozen's test, and managed conservatively with counterforce bracing, NSAIDs, and local injections.**
