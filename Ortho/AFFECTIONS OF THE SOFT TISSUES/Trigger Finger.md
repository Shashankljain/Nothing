# Trigger Finger / Trigger Thumb [⭐ High-Yield]
[[C36 - AFFECTIONS OF THE SOFT TISSUES]]
---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Trigger Finger / Trigger Thumb** (Stenosing Flexor Tenosynovitis) is a painful condition resulting from the constriction and fibrous stenosis of the first annular (A1) pulley of the digital sheath over the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint, preventing free gliding of the contained flexor tendon.

---

### Key Clinical & Pathological Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology & Demographics**: Primarily caused by repetitive micro-trauma or associated with rheumatoid arthritis; seen more commonly in **females > males**, most frequently involving the ring finger and thumb.
- **Patho-mechanism**: Continuous friction leads to fibrous thickening of the A1 pulley sheath and secondary nodular swelling of the flexor tendon (Flexor Digitorum Profundus / Flexor Pollicis Longus) proximal to the constriction.
- **Clinical Presentation & Locking**: Characterized by localized pain and tenderness at the palmar base of the digit over the MCP joint line, progressing to mechanical **'locking' of the finger in a flexed posture**.
- **Triggering Snap**: Attempting to straighten the flexed digit requires a strong effort, causing the swollen tendon nodule to pass through the tight sheath with a painful, sudden **snap-like trigger of a pistol** (or requires passive extension with the opposite hand).
- **Management Protocol**:
    - _Conservative_: Rest, activity modification, and local ultrasonic therapy in early stages.
    - _Interventional / Surgical_: Local infiltration of **hydrocortisone** into the tendon sheath for long-standing pain; **surgical release (longitudinal splitting)** of the tight A1 pulley sheath in chronic resistant cases.

---

### Clinical Pearl

- The pathognomonic mechanical hallmark of trigger finger/thumb is the painful inability of the swollen tendon nodule to enter the tight A1 pulley during extension, followed by a sudden snap-like release upon forceful extension.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Confusion**: Confusing **Trigger Finger** with **Dupuytren's Contracture**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Trigger Finger** involves A1 pulley stenosis with a snap-like 'trigger' release during extension, whereas **Dupuytren's Contracture** is a fixed progressive flexion contracture caused by palmar aponeurosis fibrosis without any snap-like release.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Core Pathology**: Stenosis of **A1 pulley** at the MCP joint level.
- **Key Symptom**: **Locking of digit in flexion** with pistol-like snap on extension.
- **Demographics**: **Females > Males**; ring finger and thumb most commonly involved.
- **First-Line Treatment**: Ultrasonic therapy and **local hydrocortisone injection** into sheath.
- **Definitive Surgery**: **Surgical splitting/release of the A1 pulley**.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Trigger finger or trigger thumb is a stenosing flexor tenosynovitis caused by constriction of the A1 pulley sheath at the metacarpophalangeal joint, presenting clinically with pain, localized nodular tenderness, and mechanical locking of the digit that releases with a characteristic pistol-like snap.**
