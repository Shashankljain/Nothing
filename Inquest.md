[[legal procedure]]
### **Inquest**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
An **inquest** is defined as a legal inquiry into the cause of a suspicious, sudden, or unnatural death.

**Introduction**  
Whenever a person dies under circumstances that are not natural (e.g., murder, accident, suicide), the state must investigate the cause and manner of death before the body can be disposed of. This preliminary investigation is called an inquest.

**Objectives**  
*   To determine the apparent cause of death.
*   To note any injuries present on the body.
*   To determine if there is any suspicion of foul play.
*   To establish the identity of the deceased.

---

#### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Foundation of Investigation**
*   **Criminal Investigation:** The inquest report (Panchanama) is the first formal document in a death investigation. It helps the police decide if a **medico-legal autopsy** is necessary.
*   **Legal Evidence:** The findings noted at the scene and on the body serve as crucial evidence in court under the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**.
*   **Civil Role:** Necessary for processing insurance claims and inheritance in cases of sudden death.

---

#### **3. Relevant Law (BNSS, 2023)**

Under the new criminal codes effective from 2024, the sections for inquest have been updated:

| **Type of Inquest** | **Old Law (CrPC)** | **New Law (BNSS, 2023)** | **Medico-Legal Significance** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Police Inquest** | Section 174 | **Section 194** | Conducted by police for most unnatural deaths. |
| **Magistrate Inquest** | Section 176 | **Section 196** | Conducted by a Magistrate in specific sensitive cases. |

---

#### **4. Classification of Inquest**

There are two types of inquests practised in India:

##### **I. Police Inquest (Section 194 BNSS)**  
This is the most common type of inquest in India.
*   **Who conducts it:** The police officer in charge of a station or a police officer specially empowered (Minimum rank: **Senior Head Constable**).
*   **Circumstances:** Conducted in cases of suicide, homicide, accidents, or deaths where there is a reasonable suspicion of an offence.

##### **II. Magistrate’s Inquest (Section 196 BNSS)**  
A superior inquiry conducted by a District Magistrate, Sub-divisional Magistrate, or an Executive Magistrate.
*   **Circumstances (Mandatory Cases):**
    *   **Custodial Deaths:** Death in police lock-up, prison, or police custody.
    *   **Police Violence:** Deaths due to police firing or beating.
    *   **Dowry Death:** Death of a woman within **7 years of marriage**.
    *   **Sexual Offence:** Rape committed on a woman in police custody.
    *   **Exhumation:** Lawful digging out of a buried body for examination.
    *   **Death in Psychiatric Hospital:** Deaths occurring in mental asylums.

---

#### **5. Procedure of Inquest**

**Flowchart: The Inquest Process**
1.  **Information:** Police receive info about a suspicious death.
2.  **Notification:** Police officer informs the nearest Magistrate.
3.  **Visit:** Investigating Officer (IO) visits the scene of death.
4.  **Observation:** IO examines the dead body and the surroundings in the presence of two or more **‘Panchas’** (respectable local witnesses).
5.  **Documentation:** An **Inquest Report (Panchanama)** is prepared.
6.  **Decision:**
    *   *If no foul play:* Body is handed over to relatives.
    *   *If foul play is suspected:* Body is sent to a doctor for a **medico-legal autopsy**.

**The Inquest Report (Panchanama) Contents:**
*   Name, age, sex, and marital status of the deceased.
*   Time, date, and place where the corpse was found.
*   Condition of the body (fresh or decomposed) and posture.
*   List of injuries and the apparent cause of those injuries.
*   Signatures of the IO and the Panchas.

---

#### **6. Comparison of Inquest Systems**

| **Feature** | **Police Inquest** | **Magistrate Inquest** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Authority** | Police Officer (IO) | Executive Magistrate |
| **BNSS Section** | 194 | 196 |
| **Prevalence** | Most common in India | Done in special/sensitive cases |
| **Superiority** | Inferior | **Superior** (can be done instead of or in addition to police inquest) |

*Note: Systems like the **Coroner’s Inquest** (UK/USA) and **Medical Examiner’s System** (USA) are not practised in India, though the Medical Examiner system is considered scientifically superior as a doctor leads the scene investigation.*

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**
*   ★ **Panchanama:** The common name for the Inquest Report.
*   ★ **7 Years:** The duration of marriage within which a woman's death requires a Magistrate Inquest.
*   ★ **Section 196 BNSS:** The new section for Magistrate Inquests (formerly 176 CrPC).
*   ★ **Exhumation:** Requires a written order from a Magistrate, never the police.

---

#### **Viva Questions**
1.  **What is a ‘Pancha’?**  
    *   *Ans:* A respectable person from the locality who witnesses the inquest and signs the report.
2.  **Who can order exhumation in India?**  
    *   *Ans:* Only a Magistrate (Executive or Judicial First Class); the police have no power to order it.
3.  **Why is a Magistrate Inquest done in dowry deaths?**  
    *   *Ans:* To ensure a transparent and unbiased inquiry into sensitive cases involving potential domestic cruelty.

---

#### **Quick Revision Summary**
*   **Inquest:** Legal inquiry into unnatural death.
*   **Police Inquest (Sec 194 BNSS):** Regular cases (accidents, suicides).
*   **Magistrate Inquest (Sec 196 BNSS):** Sensitive cases (custody, dowry, exhumation).
*   **Procedure:** Scene visit $\rightarrow$ Panchas $\rightarrow$ Panchanama $\rightarrow$ Decision for Autopsy.
*   **Outcome:** Reserved opinion until Chemical Examiner's report if poisoning is suspected.
