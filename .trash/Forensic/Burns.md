**Classification of Causes of Death**

University exams require this classification into **Immediate, Early, and Delayed** categories.

**1. Immediate Causes (Minutes to Hours)**

- **Neurogenic Shock (Primary Shock):** Resulting from intense ==pain== and sudden fear/fright. This causes a sudden ==drop in blood pressure and heart failure==.
- **Asphyxia:** The most common cause of immediate death in fires. This occurs due to:
    - Inhalation of **smoke** and carbon particles.
    - Depletion of oxygen in the environment.
    - **Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning:** CO binds to hemoglobin, forming carboxyhemoglobin (bright red blood/staining), leading to ==anemic anoxia.==
- **Vagal Inhibition:** Sudden death occurring within seconds due to minor trauma or peripheral stimulation (reflex cardiac arrest).

**2. Early Causes (24 to 48 Hours)**

- **Hypovolemic Shock (Secondary Shock):** Caused by massive ==loss of plasma== from the burnt surfaces, leading to reduced blood volume and circulatory collapse.
- **Laryngeal Edema:** Inhalation of hot air and gases causes ==swelling of the glottis== and ==larynx==, leading to rapid airway obstruction and asphyxia.
- **Toxemia:** Absorption of ==toxic== products from the destroyed tissues into the bloodstream.

**3. Delayed Causes (After 48 Hours)**

- **Septicemia (Sepsis):** The **==most common cause==** of delayed death. The burnt skin loses its protective barrier, allowing saprophytic microorganisms to invade and cause systemic infection.
- **Curling’s Ulcer:** Acute stress ulcers occurring in the **==first part of the duodenum==**. These can lead to fatal perforation or gastrointestinal hemorrhage.
- **Renal Failure:** Acute tubular necrosis (==ATN==) due to low blood pressure (shock) and the ==excretion== of ==hemoglobin==/myoglobin through the kidneys.
- **Pulmonary Complications:** Bronchopneumonia or pulmonary embolism resulting from prolonged immobility and lung injury.

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **★ Important Exam Point: Antemortem vs. Postmortem Burns.** To prove a person was alive when burnt, doctors look for **soot particles in the trachea** and **redness (vital reaction)** at the margins of the burn.
- **Identification:** Tattoos may become more visible after the epidermis is burnt/peeled off, aiding in identifying unknown bodies.
- **Differentiating Manner of Death:** While most burns are accidental, "Necklacing" is a form of homicidal burn.
