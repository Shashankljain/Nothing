[[Mechanical injuries]]
**Puppe’s Rule** is a fundamental principle in forensic traumatology used to determine the chronological sequence of multiple impacts on the skull.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

- **Definition:** Puppe’s rule states that when two or more fracture lines intersect on the skull, the fracture line from a subsequent (later) impact will never cross a pre-existing fracture line from an earlier impact.
- **Mechanism:** When the first impact occurs, it creates a fracture line that relieves the stress in the bone. When a second impact occurs, the resulting stress wave travels through the bone but stops (terminates) at the existing gap or discontinuity created by the first fracture, as there is ==no continuous medium to carry the energy further.==

---

### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Determining Sequence of Events:** It is the primary method used by forensic experts to identify which injury occurred first and which followed.
- **Firearm Injuries:** In cases where a victim has been shot multiple times in the head, Puppe’s rule helps the pathologist determine the **sequence of shots**.
- **Blunt Force Trauma:** The rule is equally applicable to multiple blunt force impacts (e.g., blows from a hammer or rod) to determine the order of the strikes.
- **Court Evidence:** This evidence is vital in reconstructing a crime scene and can be presented in court under **Section 39 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**, as part of an expert medical opinion.

---

### **3. Clinical Correlation (Example)**

If a skull shows two intersecting linear fractures:

- **Fracture A** is a long, continuous line.
- **Fracture B** runs toward Fracture A but stops exactly where it meets it.
- **Conclusion:** Fracture A occurred first, and Fracture B occurred second.

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Important Exam Point:** Puppe’s rule is specifically used for **cranial (skull) fractures**.
- ★ **Important Exam Point:** The second fracture line **terminates** at the first; it does not "jump" or continue on the other side.
- ★ **Common Examiner Favorite:** "How do you determine which of two bullet holes in a skull was made first?" (Answer: Use Puppe's Rule by observing the intersection of radiating fracture lines).

---

### **Viva Questions**

**Q: Does Puppe’s rule apply to long bones?** **Answer:** While the physical principle of stress termination exists, Puppe’s rule is most reliably and traditionally applied to the **flat bones of the skull** in forensic practice.

**Q: Can a second fracture line ever cross the first?** **Answer:** No. According to the rule, a subsequent fracture line will always stop at the pre-existing one because the continuity of the bone is broken.
