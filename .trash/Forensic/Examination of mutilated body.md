[[Autopsy]]
The examination of **mutilated human remains** is one of the most challenging tasks for a forensic expert. It involves the study of bodies that have been extensively disfigured, deprived of limbs, or recovered as fragments for the purpose of identification and determining the circumstances of death.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definitions**

- **Mutilated Bodies:** These are bodies that are ==extensively disfigured== and ==deprived== of a limb or part of the body, though soft tissues, muscles, and skin are still attached to the bones.
- **Fragmentary Remains:** These include only specific fragments of the body, such as the head, trunk, or a single limb.
- **Charred Bone:** Bone that is black in colour, representing carbonised skeletal material in direct contact with heat/flames.

**Introduction** Mutilation can be **homicidal** (to destroy identity or facilitate disposal), **accidental** (e.g., propeller injuries, plane crashes), or **postmortem** (by animals or birds). The primary principle is to reassemble the fragments to prove **Corpus Delicti**—the fact that a crime actually occurred.

---

### **2. Relevant Law (2023)**

**Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- **Section 194 BNSS:** Relates to the Police Inquest. The police officer investigates the scene and requisitions the medical officer to examine the recovered fragments.
- **Section 196 BNSS:** Magistrate Inquest. Mandatory in specific cases like custodial deaths or when fragments are recovered during an official exhumation.

**Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 103 BNS:** Punishment for murder. Mutilation is often done to hide a murder.
- **Section 238 BNS:** Causing disappearance of evidence of offence (formerly IPC 201). Mutilating a body to hide identity falls under this.

**Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 39 BSA:** Expert Opinion. The report of the forensic expert on fragmented remains is admissible as evidence in court.

---

### **3. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Establish Identity:** The foremost objective in any mutilated case.
- **Determine Cause of Death (COD):** Identifying fatal injuries to vital organs or major vessels even in fragments.
- **Manner of Death (MOD):** Differentiating between homicidal dismemberment and accidental mutilation.
- **Time Since Death (TSD):** Using decomposition changes and entomological evidence (maggots) on the fragments.

★ **Important Exam Point:** In India, identification is essential before a sentence is passed in murder trials involving mutilated remains to prevent false charges.

---

### **4. Step-by-Step Procedure of Examination**

| **Step**           | **Procedure**                                                                            | **Medico-Legal Significance**                                   |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Preparation** | Arrange fragments in anatomical order on a table.                                        | Helps determine if fragments belong to one or more bodies.      |
| **2. Cleaning**    | Soften earth/debris with weak ==carbolic acid== (lysol); use ==acetone== for tight dirt. | Reveals hidden injuries or identifying marks.                   |
| **3. Photography** | Take 20–25 coloured photographs, including close-ups of injuries and labels.             | Essential for "chain of evidence" and court presentation.       |
| **4. X-ray/CT**    | Mandatory to locate foreign bodies (bullets) and detect fractures.                       | Helps identify implants or old healed fractures for comparison. |
| **5. Sampling**    | Collect 100g of muscle (if fluids unavailable) and bone marrow for DNA.                  | DNA is the most reliable method for final confirmation.         |

### **5. Key Medico-Legal Questions**

**A. Is it Human or Animal?**

- If only muscle is available: Use the **==Precipitin== ==test==** or anti-globulin inhibition test.
- If bone is available: Examine the **Haversian system** microscopically.

**B. Does it belong to one or more bodies?**

- Check for disparity in size or duplicated parts (e.g., two right femurs).
- Use **UV light**; different skeletons may fluoresce differently due to varying mineral/organic content.

**C. What is the Sex?**

- **Prostate and Uterus** are the last organs to decompose and are highly reliable.
- Skull (supraorbital ridges, mastoid) and Pelvis (greater sciatic notch, pre-auricular sulcus) are used if bones are present.

**D. What is the Age and Stature?**

- **Age:** Estimated from teeth eruption, skull sutures, and ossification centers.
- **Stature:** Calculated from long bones (Femur, Humerus) using **Karl Pearson’s formula**.

---

### **6. High-Yield Exam Points**

- **Corpus Delicti:** The "Body of Offence." In dismemberment, the identity of the victim must be established to satisfy this principle.
- **Rule of 5:** Multiplying the length of the humerus by 5 gives a quick estimation of height.
- **Last Organs to Putrefy:** Prostate in males and non-gravid uterus in females.
- **Pre-auricular Sulcus:** A characteristic feature of the female pelvis used in sex determination.

### **7. Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: What is the "Jigsaw Murders" case?** **Model Answer:** The 1935 Buck Ruxton case, where a team of experts reassembled mutilated fragments using anthropology, fingerprinting, and the first-ever use of entomology to solve a murder.

**Q2: Which bone is most reliable for stature estimation?** **Model Answer:** The **Femur** is considered the most reliable long bone for estimating stature.

**Q3: How do you differentiate antemortem vs. postmortem mutilation?** **Model Answer:** By looking for **vital reactions** (haemorrhage, inflammation) at the margins of the injured parts. If the margins are dry and bloodless, it is likely postmortem.

### **8. Model University Answer (5 Marks)**

**Q: Enumerate the objectives and procedure for the examination of mutilated remains.**

1. **Definition:** Define mutilated and fragmentary remains.
2. **Objectives:** Identification, COD, TSD, MOD, and proving Corpus Delicti.
3. **Procedure:**
    - Anatomical arrangement and skeletal charting.
    - Cleaning with lysol/acetone and X-ray examination.
    - Photography of all fragments with autopsy numbers.
4. **Determination of Individuality:** Mention specific tests for Race (Cephalic index), Sex (Pelvis/Skull), and Age (Teeth/Ossification).
5. **Legal Conclusion:** Mention the role of BNSS 194/196 and DNA analysis for final confirmation.
