[[legal procedure]]
### **Conduct Money (Diet Money)**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Conduct money** (also known as **Diet money**) is the sum of money paid to a witness to meet their expenses for travel, food, and stay while attending court to give evidence.

**Introduction**  
In the legal system, it is recognized that a witness should not be financially burdened for performing their civic duty of testifying in court. Therefore, the party requiring the witness's presence is often responsible for covering their reasonable expenses.

---

#### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Attendance Guarantee:** In civil cases, if conduct money is not paid at the time the summons is served, the medical practitioner can legally refuse to attend court until the payment is made.
- **Professional Rights:** It ensures that medical experts are compensated for their time and travel, especially when they have to travel to different districts or states.
- **Prevention of Bribery:** Providing a standardized "conduct money" prevents the perception that a witness is being paid for their _testimony_ rather than their _expenses_.

---

#### **3. Relevant Law and Procedure**

Under the updated criminal codes (**BNSS, 2023**), the rules for witness attendance and compensation remain structured:

|**Type of Case**|**Who Pays?**|**Legal Rule**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Civil Case**|**The Party** calling the witness (Plaintiff or Defendant).|The money must be offered when the **Summons** is served. If not offered, the doctor may decline the summons.|
|**Criminal Case**|**Generally Not Paid**(Common Witness).|It is considered a public duty toward the State. No private party pays.|
|**Criminal Case (Expert)**|**The Court/Government**.|★ **Important Exception:** As a doctor is an **Expert Witness (Section 39 BSA)**, the court provides compensation for their expenses.|

---

#### **4. Guidelines for Medical Practitioners**

1. **Refusal in Civil Cases:** If a doctor receives a summons for a civil case without conduct money, they should inform the court in writing that they cannot attend due to non-payment of travel expenses.
2. **Acceptance:** Once conduct money is accepted, the doctor is **legally bound** to attend. Failure to attend after accepting the money can lead to a charge of **Contempt of Court** or penalties under **Section 208 BNS**.
3. **Rates:** The amount of money is usually based on a fixed scale determined by the court, considering the witness's professional status and distance travelled.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Alternate Name:** Diet Money.
- ★ **Civil vs. Criminal:** In civil cases, it is a right; in criminal cases, it is usually provided only for experts.
- ★ **Expert Witness Status:** Under **Section 39 BSA**, doctors are entitled to special professional fees/expenses in many jurisdictions.
- ★ **Summons Connection:** Payment is linked to the receipt of a **Subpoena Ad Testificandum** [Previous Context].

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Can a doctor refuse a summons in a criminal case because they weren't paid travel money?**
    - _Ans:_ Generally, no. In criminal cases, attendance is a state duty. However, for expert testimony, they are usually compensated by the court after the appearance.
2. **What happens if you accept conduct money in a civil case but don't show up?**
    - _Ans:_ You can be held in contempt of court and may be prosecuted under **Section 208 BNS** for non-attendance [1, 62; 3].
3. **Is conduct money the same as a professional fee?**
    - _Ans:_ No. Conduct money is specifically for _expenses_ (travel/food). A professional fee for expert opinion is a separate consideration.

---

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Purpose:** To cover travel/food/stay expenses.
- **Civil:** Paid by the party; mandatory for attendance.
- **Criminal:** Paid by the court for **expert witnesses** only.
- **Refusal:** Only allowed in civil cases if not paid during summons.
- **Section 208 BNS:** Penalty for non-attendance after accepting summons/money.
