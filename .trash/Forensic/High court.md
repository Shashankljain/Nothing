[[legal procedure]]
### **High Court**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** The **High Court** is the highest judicial authority and tribunal within a State in India. It serves as a superior court of record with administrative and judicial superintendence over all subordinate courts within its jurisdiction.

**Introduction** In the hierarchy of the Indian legal system, the High Court sits at the **State level**, positioned above the Sessions Courts and below the Supreme Court of India. Each State generally has its own High Court, though some High Courts have jurisdiction over multiple States or Union Territories.

**Objectives**

- To hear appeals from subordinate courts.
- To protect the fundamental rights of citizens through writ jurisdiction.
- To confirm or commute major sentences (like death penalties) awarded by lower courts.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Confirmation of Death Sentence (★ Important Exam Point):** A Sessions Court can award a death sentence, but it **cannot be executed** until it is confirmed by the High Court [1, 61; 6, 6].
- **Commutation of Sentence:** The High Court has the power to confirm, change, or commute a death sentence. Specifically, if a woman sentenced to death is found to be pregnant, the High Court **must** commute the sentence to life imprisonment under **Section 456 BNSS**.
- **Summons Priority:** For a medical practitioner, a summons from a High Court takes precedence over summons from any lower court (Sessions or Magistrate) for the same date and time.
- **Appellate Role:** It is the primary venue where medical negligence cases or criminal appeals involving medical evidence are reviewed after a lower court verdict.

#### **Commutation of Death Sentence: The Process**

**Flowchart: Post-verdict Procedure**

1. **Sessions Court:** Passes a death sentence.
2. **Forwarding:** Case is sent to the **High Court** for mandatory confirmation.
3. **HC Decision:**
    - **Confirm:** The sentence is upheld.
    - **Commute/Change:** HC reduces the sentence (e.g., to life imprisonment).
    - **Cancel:** Verdict is overturned.
4. **Further Appeal:** If confirmed, the convict can appeal to the **Supreme Court** or file a **Mercy Petition** to the President or Governor.

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Section 456 BNSS:** Specific protection for pregnant women (Death $\rightarrow$ Life Imprisonment).
- ★ **Death Sentence Confirmation:** No death sentence by a Sessions Judge is final without High Court approval.
- ★ **Unlimited Fine:** Unlike Magistrate courts, High Courts can impose any amount of fine.
- ★ **Top of State Hierarchy:** It is the "Biggest in the state".

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Can a Sessions Judge execute a death sentence immediately?**
    - _Ans:_ No. It must be forwarded to and confirmed by the High Court first [1, 61; 6, 6].
2. **What is the legal duty of the High Court if a condemned prisoner is pregnant?**
    - _Ans:_ Under Section 456 BNSS, the High Court must commute the death sentence to life imprisonment.
3. **Which court is the immediate level of appeal for the High Court?**
    - _Ans:_ The Supreme Court of India.
4. **What is the maximum fine a High Court can impose?**
    - _Ans:_ There is no limit; it is "Unlimited".
