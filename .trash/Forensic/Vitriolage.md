# 🧪🔥 **VITRIOLAGE (VITRIOL THROWING / ACID ATTACK)**

---

### **Basic Concepts** 📚

#### **Definition**

**Vitriolage (Vitriol throwing)** is defined as the act of throwing any corrosive substance onto a person with malicious intent.

#### **Introduction**

- **Why is it called Vitriolage?** Although any highly corrosive chemical can be used, **Sulphuric acid (commercially known as "Oil of Vitriol")** is the most commonly used agent for this crime, hence the term "vitriolage".
- **Target:** The chemical is almost always thrown directly at the victim's **face**.
- **Objective:** The primary motive of the assailant is to destroy the victim's vision, cause permanent facial disfigurement, and inflict severe psychological and physical trauma.

#### **Classification of Substances Used in Vitriolage**

Assailants utilize easily accessible household or industrial corrosives:

|**Class of Corrosive**|**Chemical Agents Used**|
|:--|:--|
|**Mineral/Inorganic Acids**|• **Sulphuric acid** (Oil of Vitriol - _most common_)• Nitric acid (Aqua fortis)• Hydrochloric acid (HCI)|
|**Organic Acids**|• Carbolic acid (Phenol)|
|**Alkalis / Caustics**|• Caustic soda (Sodium hydroxide)• Caustic potash (Potassium hydroxide)|
|**Other Corrosives & Organic Irritants**|• Iodine• Marking nut juice (_Semecarpus anacardium_)• Calotropis latex|

### **Clinical Features & Characteristics of Burns** 🩺

The burns produced by vitriol throwing have highly distinctive features that differentiate them from thermal (fire) burns:

- ★ **Important Exam Point (The ==Painless Paradox==):** Unlike thermal burns, the local lesions in vitriolage are **completely painless**. This is because the strong corrosive chemical rapidly penetrates the skin layers and destroys the cutaneous sensory nerve endings.
- **Absence of Vesication:** There is an **absence of blisters (vesication)** because the acid coagulates tissue proteins immediately rather than causing fluid exudation.
- **No Red Line of Demarcation:** There is no inflammatory red zone surrounding the burned area.
- **Discoloration and Staining:**
    - **Sulphuric acid:** Leaves **brown or black** stains on the skin and clothing.
    - **Nitric acid:** Leaves a characteristic **yellow** stain due to the _xanthoproteic reaction_ with tissue proteins.
- **Trickle Marks:** Liquid acids run down the face and neck due to gravity, leaving clear, downward-running streaks or **trickle marks** of corroded skin.
- **Slow Repair and Contractures:** Chemical burns heal exceptionally slowly, producing thick, dense, fibrous scar tissue that leads to severe **contractures** and permanent facial deformities.

## **Relevant Law (Latest BNS, 2023)** ⚖️

Under the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, acid attacks are treated with severe penalties:

- ★ **Section 124(1) BNS (formerly Sec 326-A IPC):** Punishes voluntarily causing **grievous hurt by the use of acid**.
    - _Punishment:_ Minimum of **10 years imprisonment to life imprisonment**, along with a fine paid directly to the victim to cover medical expenses.
- ★ **Section 124(2) BNS (formerly Sec 326-B IPC):** Punishes **throwing or attempting to throw acid**.
    - _Punishment:_ Imprisonment for **5 to 7 years** and a fine.
- **Classification of Hurt:** Facial disfigurement and the permanent loss of sight in one or both eyes are legally classified as **grievous hurt**.

## **Treatment & Emergency Management** 🏥

Immediate clinical decontamination must be executed to limit tissue damage:

```
                            [VITRIOLAGE EMERGENCY]
                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                         ▼
  [Skin Decontamination]                                    [Eye Management]
• Wash with plenty of water & soap.                       • Anesthetize with                                                                 Proparacaine.
• Apply thick paste of MgO / Carbonate.                   • Flush with water                                                                  for 15 mins.
• Cover with antibiotic ointment.                         • Wash with 0.9%                                                                 saline to pH 7.0.
                                                          • Instill steroid +                                                                antibiotic drops.
```

1. **Skin Care:** Flush with copious amounts of **water and soap**. Neutralize any remaining acid with a thick paste of **Magnesium Oxide (MgO)** or carbonate. Apply local **antibiotic ointment** to prevent infection.
2. **Ocular Emergency:** Instill topical anesthetic drops (e.g., **proparacaine**). Irrigate the eyes with water for **at least 15 minutes** while holding the eyelids open. Continue irrigation with **0.9% normal saline** until the ocular surface pH returns to **7.0**. Instill **antibiotic and steroid eye drops**.
