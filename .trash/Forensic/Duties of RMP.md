[[Medical law]]

**Definition of RMP** A Registered Medical Practitioner is a person whose name is enrolled in the **National Medical Register** or a **State Medical Register** maintained by the **National Medical Commission (NMC)** or **State Medical Council (SMC)**.

**Medical Jurisprudence vs. Medical Ethics**

- **Medical Jurisprudence:** The application of medical knowledge to legal matters (Law for medicine).
- **Medical Ethics:** Moral principles guiding the doctor-patient relationship and conduct (Medicine for law).
- **Medical Etiquette:** Conventional laws of courtesy followed between members of the medical profession.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

Understanding these duties is critical to:

1. Providing standard patient care.
2. Avoiding charges of **Medical Negligence (Sec 106 BNS)**.
3. Preventing **Professional Misconduct (Infamous Conduct)**, which can lead to **Penal Erasure** (permanent removal from the register).
4. Fulfilling legal obligations toward the State during criminal investigations.

★ **Important Exam Point:** A doctor’s duty of care begins the moment they accept a patient for treatment.

### **Classification of Duties**

#### **1. General Duties**

- **Maintenance of Records:** Doctors must keep indoor patient records for **3 years** and medico-legal registers for **10 years**.
- **The 72-Hour Rule:** A doctor must provide medical records to the patient or authorized authorities within **72 hours**of a request.
- **Registration Numbers:** Must display the unique registration number provided by the SMC/NMC in the clinic and on all prescriptions/certificates.
- **Continuous Learning:** Doctors should participate in CME programs for at least 30 hours every 5 years.

#### **2. Duties toward Patients (★ Important University Question)**

- **Exercise Reasonable Skill and Care:** A practitioner (MBBS) is expected to exercise a "reasonable" degree of skill; they are liable if they attempt a procedure beyond their competence.
- **Informed Consent:** Must obtain written informed consent for major procedures, explaining risks and alternatives (BNS 25-30).
- **Attendance:** Once a patient is accepted, the doctor is obligated to attend to them as long as required. Withdrawal requires reasonable notice.
- **Professional Secrecy:** Doctors are obliged to keep patient information ==confidential==. Breach of secrecy can lead to legal action.
- **Emergency Cases:** Doctors have a moral and ethical duty to provide life-saving treatment in emergencies, even without immediate consent (Sec 30 BNS).

#### **3. Duties toward the State**

- **Reporting Crimes:** Under **Section 33 BNSS**, a doctor must inform the nearest police or Magistrate about suspected homicidal poisoning or crimes.
- **Notifiable Diseases:** Doctors must notify public health authorities about cases like COVID-19, TB, or Cholera.
- **Court Attendance:** Must obey court summons punctually and act as an **Expert Witness** under Section 39 BSA.
- **Certifying Death:** Mandatory to issue the Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCD) if they attended the last illness.

#### **4. Duties in Consultation**

- **Patient Benefit:** Consultation should be sought whenever the case is obscure or requires specialized skills.
- **Substitute Duty:** If attending for another physician, the doctor should maintain the patient's confidence and return the patient to the original doctor once they return.

---

### **Privileged Communication**

**Definition:** A communication made in ==good faith== on a subject in which the doctor has a duty (legal, social, or moral) to protect the ==interests== of the community or the State.

**Exceptions to Professional Secrecy:**

1. **Suspected Crime:** Reporting an assault,rape,murder,or poisoning (Sec 33 BNSS).
2. **Civic Benefit:** Informing authorities about a pilot with ==color blindness== or a driver with ==epilepsy==.
3. **Infectious Diseases:** Notifying partners in case of venereal disease or health departments in case of TB/Cholera,STD.
4. **Court Orders:** Disclosing information when ordered by a judge.
