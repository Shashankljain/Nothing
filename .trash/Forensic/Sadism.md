[[Sexual offence]]
**Sadism** is a sexual perversion in which an individual derives sexual gratification or satisfaction by **inflicting pain**, ill-treatment, or humiliation on a partner.

**Introduction**

- **Term Origin:** The term is derived from **Marquis de Sade**, a French nobleman who described such behavior in his writings [Standard Forensic Knowledge].
- **Synonym:** Active algolagnia.
- **Algolagnia:** A broader term for pleasure derived from pain (encompasses both sadism and masochism).
- **Bondage:** A practice where one partner is restrained or bound; it is essentially a combination of **Sadism + Masochism**.

---

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Criminal Investigation (Lust Murder):** In extreme cases, a sadist may kill their partner to achieve maximum sexual arousal, known as **Lust Murder** (_Lustmord_).
- **Overkilling:** Sadistic homicides are characterized by **==massive, multiple injuries==** on the body that are far more than required to cause death (e.g., dozens of stab wounds or burns), reflecting the perpetrator's rage or pleasure.
- **Physical Evidence:** Injuries such as bites, cuts, or cigarette burns found on a victim's body (often in "accessible parts" or sexual areas) can point towards a sadistic perpetrator.
- **Divorce:** Habitual sadistic behavior is a strong ground for **divorce** in civil law as it constitutes mental and physical cruelty.

---

**Relevant Law (BNS, 2023)**

Under the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, the legal handling of sadistic acts depends on consent and the severity of the injury:

- **Hurt and Grievous Hurt:** Inflicting physical pain is punishable under **Section 115 BNS** (Hurt) or **Section 117 BNS** (Grievous Hurt). Grievous hurt includes permanent disfigurement or injuries endangering life.
- **Murder:** If the act results in death, the perpetrator is charged under **Section 101/103 BNS**.
- **Non-Consensual Acts:** If sadistic acts are performed without consent, they are prosecuted as **Sexual Assault** (Section 74 BNS) or **Rape** (Section 63 BNS).
- **Protection of Children:** If a child (<18 years) is involved, the **POCSO Act, 2012** and **Section 65 BNS** apply, where consent is legally irrelevant.
