[[Infanticide]]

- **Precipitate Labour:** A rapid, hyperactive form of childbirth where the entire process of labour—from the onset of regular uterine contractions to the complete expulsion of the fetus—is completed in **less than 3 hours** [FM 3.19].
- **Normal Labour (For Comparison):** Typically lasts **12 to 18 hours** in primigravidae (first-time mothers) and **8 to 12 hours** in multigravidae.
- **Physiological Etiology:** It occurs due to a combination of **strong, hypertonic, coordinate uterine contractions** and **extremely low maternal soft-tissue resistance** in the cervix and birth canal. It is highly common in **multiparous women** (women who have had multiple previous deliveries) due to pre-stretched pelvic tissues.

**Topic-Specific Explanation: Maternal and Fetal Signs** 🔬

When a newborn is brought for autopsy, or a mother is examined, the forensic pathologist must seek specific biological signatures to verify a claim of precipitate labour:

```
                            [PRECIPITATE LABOUR SIGNATURES]
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                   ▼
   [Maternal Signs]                                              [Fetal Signs]
 • Extensive perineal/vulval tears.     • **Absent/Minimal Caput Succedaneum** 
 • Uterine inversion or postpartum hemorrhage   • Torn umbilical cord with                                                                    ragged edges.
 • Lack of normal maternal exhaustion.        • Linear skull fractures (from.                                                           falling) [3].
```

**1. Signs in the Mother** 👩

- **Vulval and Perineal Tears:** Due to the rapid, forceful stretch of unyielding tissues, there are often **extensive lacerations** of the perineum, vulva, or vagina.
- **Retained Placenta & Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH):** Uterine muscles may fail to contract uniformly after such a rapid evacuation, leading to retained placental fragments and severe bleeding.
- **Uterine Inversion or Rupture:** Rarely, the sudden expulsion pulls the fundus down, causing acute inversion.
- **Absence of Exhaustion:** Unlike normal prolonged labour, the mother does not show signs of physical exhaustion or prolonged distress.

---

**2. Signs in the Fetus / Newborn** 👶

- ★ **Caput Succedaneum is Absent or Minimal:** Caput succedaneum (scalp edema) requires prolonged pressure of the fetal head against the cervix. Because passage through the birth canal is rapid in precipitate labour, **caput succedaneum does not have time to form**.
- ★ **Torn Umbilical Cord:** As the baby is expelled suddenly while the mother is standing or sitting on a toilet, the child falls, stretching and **tearing the umbilical cord** under its own weight.
- **Head Injuries (Fissure Fractures):** The infant’s head may strike a hard floor or the toilet pan, resulting in **linear, fissure fractures** of the parietal or frontal bones and localized subdural/epidural hemorrhage.
- **Contaminants on the Body:** Finding toilet water, sand, mud, or fecal matter on the baby’s skin, inside the respiratory passage, or stomach helps corroborate an unusual site of delivery.

---

**Medico-Legal Importance (MLI)** ⚖️🩺

Precipitate labour is one of the most critical topics in forensic obstetrics because it is frequently used as a defense shield in cases of suspected infanticide.

**1. The "Toilet Pan Delivery" Defense (Accidental Infanticide)**

- An unmarried mother wishing to conceal her pregnancy may claim that she was sitting on a toilet pan to relieve constipation when the baby was suddenly expelled, fell into the pan, and died of **accidental drowning** or **head impact**.
- ★ **The Forensic Task:** The pathologist must determine if the findings support this claim or if they point toward **active homicidal smothering, drowning, or mechanical head bashing**.
