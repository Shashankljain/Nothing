### **Model University Answer: Chelating Agents** 🧲💊

---

#### **1. Definition & Mechanism** ⚙️

- **Definition:** **Chelating agents** are specialized physiological antidotes that bind with heavy metal ions using polar coordinate groups (such as sulfhydryl \(-\text{SH}\) groups) to form stable, non-toxic, ring-like, and water-soluble complexes.
- **Mechanism:** They compete with vital cellular enzymes for heavy metal binding. The resulting metal-chelate complex is inert and easily excreted from the body via **urine** or **bile**.

---

#### **2. Key Examples & Clinical Indications** 📋

|**Chelating Agent**|**Route**|**Primary Indication (Heavy Metal)**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**BAL (Dimercaprol)**|viscid IM|Acute **Arsenic (As)**, **Mercury (Hg)**, and **Lead (Pb)** poisoning (often combined with EDTA).|
|**CaNa₂ EDTA**|IV infusion|**Lead (Pb) poisoning / Plumbism**.|
|**Penicillamine**|Oral|**Copper (Cu)** poisoning (Wilson's disease), Lead, and Mercury.|
|**Deferoxamine**|IV / IM|Acute **Iron (Fe)** poisoning (excreted as "vin-rose" reddish-pink urine).|
|**Succimer (DMSA)**|Oral|Safer, water-soluble BAL-derivative for **Lead, Mercury, and Arsenic**.|

---

#### **3. ★ Important Exam Point (Contraindications)** ⚠️

- **BAL Contraindication:** BAL is strictly **contraindicated in Iron (Fe) and Cadmium (Cd) poisoning** because the resulting BAL-metal complexes are highly unstable and dissociate in the renal tubules, causing severe nephrotoxicity. It is also avoided in **G-6-PD deficiency**.
- **Legitimacy:** All suspected cases of homicidal heavy metal poisoning must be immediately reported to the police under **Section 33 BNSS, 2023** (formerly Sec 39 CrPC).
