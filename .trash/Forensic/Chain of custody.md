[[legal procedure]]
### **Chain of Custody (Chain of Evidence)**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Chain of Custody** refers to the continuous, documented, and chronological record of the custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of physical or electronic evidence.

**Introduction**  
In forensic practice, it is not enough to simply collect evidence; the prosecution must prove in court that the evidence presented is the same as that collected at the crime scene or during an autopsy, and that it has not been tampered with, substituted, or contaminated.

**Objectives**

- To maintain the **integrity** and **authenticity** of evidence.
- To ensure the evidence is admissible in a court of law.
- To provide a transparent trail of everyone who handled the specimen.

---

#### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Admissibility:** If the chain of custody is broken (e.g., a seal is found tampered with or a signature is missing), the court may reject the evidence entirely, potentially leading to the acquittal of the accused.
- **Establishment of Corpus Delicti:** It ensures that items like the victim's clothing, weapons, or bloodstains are correctly linked to the specific crime.
- **Role in Investigation:** It helps forensic scientists reconstruct how evidence fits into the timeline of a crime.
- **Importance for Medical Practitioners:** Doctors are responsible for the first link in the chain during autopsies or clinical examinations (e.g., rape cases or poisoning). Any lapse in documentation is considered professional negligence.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The "only thing worse than no autopsy is a partial autopsy" or an autopsy where the chain of evidence is compromised.

#### **4. Procedure for Maintaining Chain of Custody**

**Step 1: Collection and Preservation**

- Evidence must be placed in appropriate containers (e.g., glass jars for viscera, paper bags for dried bloodstains).
- **Note:** Never use ==plastic bags== for wet clothing as it encourages putrefaction.

**Step 2: Labeling (★ High-Yield)** Every sample must be labeled immediately with the following details:

1. Autopsy/MLC number.
2. Name of the deceased/patient.
3. Nature of the sample (e.g., "Stomach and its contents").
4. Date, time, and place of collection.
5. **Signature of the Medical Officer.**

**Step 3: Sealing**

- Bottles should be well-fitted and the stoppers tied with string.
- A **departmental seal** must be applied. A specimen of the seal used should be sent in a separate sealed envelope for comparison by the laboratory.

**Step 4: Documentation and Dispatch**

- A list of all articles removed (jewelry, bullets, clothes) must be made.
- Evidence is handed over to the police constable/Investigating Officer (IO) only after obtaining a **receipt**.
- **Supreme Court Ruling:** In suspected poisoning, viscera must be sent to the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL) **immediately** after the postmortem.

---

#### **5. Specimen-Specific Requirements**

|**Specimen**|**Container/Preservative**|**Special Requirement**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Viscera**|Glass/Polypropylene Jars|Fill only 2/3rd of the jar to allow for gas expansion.|
|**Blood**|EDTA Vials (4°C)|Do not shake the tube; preserve with sodium fluoride if alcohol is suspected.|
|**Clothing**|Paper Bags|Must be air-dried in shade before packing.|
|**Bullets**|Cardboard Box|Pick up with gloved hands or rubber-tipped forceps to avoid secondary scratches.|

---

#### **6. High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Receipt:** Always obtain a written receipt when handing evidence to the police.
- ★ **Seal Integrity:** The FSL will reject a sample if the seal does not match the specimen seal provided.
- ★ **Digital Autopsy:** Uses 3D-optical measuring and imaging to create a digital chain of evidence that is hard to tamper with.
- ★ **Immediate Dispatch:** The Supreme Court mandates immediate transfer to FSL in poisoning cases.

#### **7. Viva Questions**

1. **What happens if you use a plastic bag to pack a wet bloodstained shirt?**
    - _Ans:_ It traps moisture, leading to fungal growth and DNA degradation, breaking the biological chain of evidence.
2. **Who is responsible for the chain of custody during an autopsy?**
    - _Ans:_ The autopsy surgeon is responsible until the specimens are sealed and handed over to the police.
3. **Why is a specimen seal sent separately?**
    - _Ans:_ To allow the laboratory to verify that the seal on the container has not been broken and replaced by an unauthorized person.

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Chain of Custody:** Chronological documentation of evidence handling.
- **Goal:** Prove no tampering occurred.
- **Steps:** Collect $\rightarrow$ Label (5 essentials) $\rightarrow$ Seal (Dept. Seal) $\rightarrow$ Handover $\rightarrow$ Receipt.
- **Failure:** Leads to legal "inadmissibility" and possible prosecution of the doctor under **Section 238 BNS** [Slide 7, Source 3].
