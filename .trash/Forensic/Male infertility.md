[[Impotence and sterility]]
### **Basic Concepts** 🧬

#### **Definitions**

- **Sterility (Infertility):** The absolute or relative inability of a male to have children (produce offspring) due to a defect in semen quality, sperm production, or genetic transmission [Page 134].
- **Impotence (Erectile Dysfunction):** The physical inability of a male to perform the sexual act (coitus) by initiating or maintaining a penile erection [Page 134].
- ★ **Important Exam Point:** While **impotence** makes a marriage voidable/null in court, **sterility/infertility is NOT a ground for divorce** under Indian personal laws (e.g., Hindu Marriage Act) [Page 134].

### **Causes of Male Infertility & Impotence** 🔍

For university examinations, the causes of male reproductive failure are classified into **Impotence-related (Functional)**and **Sterility-related (Biological/Semen defects)**:

#### **1. Causes of Male Impotence (Erectile Dysfunction)** [Page 134]

- **Psychological (Most Common):** Performance anxiety, depression, emotional stress, and fear [Page 134].
    - _Honeymoon Impotence:_ A transient form of impotence caused by temporary anxiety on the wedding night [Page 134].
- **Vasculogenic (Most Important Organic Cause):** Conditions ==compromising== pelvic blood flow, classically **hypertension** (which is the 2nd most common cause of impotence overall) [Page 134].
- **Congenital Deformities:** Microphallus, severe curvature, or penile developmental anomalies [Page 134].
- **Neurogenic Causes:** Damage to the ==sacral== ==parasympathetic== nerves (nervi erigentes) due to pelvic surgery, spinal trauma, or diabetic neuropathy [Page 134].
- **Acquired Diseases:** Local ==infections== (e.g., orchitis, urethritis) or **==malignancies==** of the male genitalia [Page 134].

#### **2. Causes of Male Sterility (Infertility)** [Page 52, 61, 136]

- **Oligospermia (Low Sperm Count):** Semen containing a sub-normal concentration of spermatozoa (normal is **17–192 million sperm/mL** with \(\ge\)60% motility) [Page 242].
- **Azoospermia (Complete Absence of Sperm):** Failure of spermatogenesis, which can be due to:
    - _Genetic/Chromosomal disorders:_ **==Klinefelter== ==Syndrome== (47,XXY)**, characterized by testicular atrophy, hyalinization/fibrosis of seminiferous tubules, and azoospermia.
    - _Acquired Testicular Damage:_ Secondary to viral orchitis (**==mumps==**), cytotoxic chemotherapy drugs, or therapeutic pelvic irradiation.
- **Congenital Anatomical Defects preventing Semen Deposition:**
    - **Hypospadias:** The urethral opening is located on the ==ventral== aspect of the penis [Page 136].
    - **Epispadias:** The urethral opening is located on the ==dorsal== aspect of the penis [Page 136].
- **Hereditary/Genetic Transmissible Disorders:** Conditions like hemophilia, cystic fibrosis, or Huntington's chorea where the husband carries a risk of passing severe disease to the child [Page 136, 283].
- **Rh Incompatibility:** Where the husband is Rh-positive (homozygous) and the wife is Rh-negative, threatening subsequent pregnancies with erythroblastosis fetalis [Page 136].

### **Methods of Overcoming Each Cause (Assisted Reproductive Techniques - ART)** 🏥

Each specific cause is managed utilizing a tailored **Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)** method, as categorized under modern forensic and clinical guidelines [Page 135]:

|**S.No.**|**Specific Male Cause**|**Diagnostic/Pathological Feature**|**Method of Overcoming**[Page 135, 136]|**Clinical Mechanism / Protocol** [Page 135, 136]|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**1**|**Erectile Dysfunction (Impotence)**|Psychological, vasculogenic, or neurological inability to maintain erection [Page 134].|**AIH (Artificial Insemination Husband / Homologous)**|Semen is collected from the husband manually, processed, and artificially introduced into the wife’s vaginal/cervical canal [Page 135, 136].|
|**2**|**Congenital Defects (Hypospadias / Epispadias)**|Inability to naturally deposit semen into the upper vagina [Page 136].|**AIH (Artificial Insemination Husband / Homologous)**|Direct collection of semen and direct mechanical deposition bypasses the anatomical defect [Page 136].|
|**3**|**Oligospermia (Mild to Moderate)**|Mildly reduced sperm count or low motility [Page 136].|**AIH** or **Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)**|The husband’s semen is centrifuged to concentrate the sperm count before insemination [Page 135, 136].|
|**4**|**Oligospermia (Severe)**|Extremely low sperm count or poor motility [Page 135].|**ICSI (Intra-Cytoplasmic Sperm Injection)**|A single, viable spermatozoon is micro-injected directly into the cytoplasm of a harvested maternal oocyte _in vitro_ [Page 135].|
|**5**|**Azoospermia / Absolute Sterility**|Zero sperm production (e.g., Klinefelter Syndrome, post-chemotherapy).|**AID (Artificial Insemination Donor)**|Semen obtained from a thoroughly screened, healthy, anonymous donor is inseminated [Page 136].|
|**6**|**Hereditary Diseases in Husband**|Risk of passing down fatal/debilitating genetic anomalies [Page 136].|**AID (Artificial Insemination Donor)**|Bypasses the husband's genetic lineage completely by utilizing anonymous donor semen [Page 136].|
|**7**|**Severe Rh Incompatibility**|Homozygous Rh-positive husband + Rh-negative wife [Page 136].|**AID (Artificial Insemination Donor)**|Bypasses fetal hemolysis by using semen from an Rh-negative compatible donor [Page 136].|

### **Relevant Law & Legal Guidelines (2023 Framework)** ⚖️

- ★ **Section 116 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:** Relates to the **Presumption of Legitimacy**. A child born during the subsistence of a valid marriage via consensual **AIH** is legally presumed legitimate [Page 135, 136]. If **AID** is performed, the child is legally legitimate, though formal adoption procedures may be pursued depending on local personal laws [Page 136].
- **Adultery Status:** Consensual AI (performed with the written consent of both spouses) **does not amount to adultery**, as there is no physical sexual union or coitus with the donor [Page 136].
- **Cruelty and Divorce:** If a wife undergoes **AID without her husband’s written consent**, it constitutes mental cruelty, giving the husband valid grounds to file for **divorce** [Page 136].
- **Surrogacy (Regulation) Acts:** Banned commercial surrogacy in India [Page 136]. Only **altruistic surrogacy** (no monetary exchange except medical expenses/insurance for up to 3 years) is permitted for Indian married couples with a proven 5-year history of infertility [Page 136, 137].

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Vasculogenic Impotence:** Hypertension is the most common organic vasculogenic cause of male impotence [Page 134].
- ★ **Legitimacy Clause:** Presumption of child legitimacy falls under **Section 116 BSA, 2023** (formerly Section 112 IEA) [Page 135].
- ★ **The Quadruple Consent Rule:** Performing AID requires the signed written consent of **four parties**: the Wife, Husband, Donor, and the Donor's Wife [Page 136].
- ★ **The Single-Donor Limit:** To prevent accidental consanguinity/incest, semen from a single donor is restricted to a **maximum of 10 children** [Page 136].
