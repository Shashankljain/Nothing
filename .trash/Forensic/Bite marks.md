[[Identification]]

### **Bite Marks**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Forensic Odontology** is the application of dental science to legal matters. A **Bite Mark** is a representative pattern left by the teeth of a human or animal on a substance (skin, food, etc.). In forensic terms, a bite mark is classified as an **imprint abrasion** or **pressure abrasion**.

**Introduction**  
Bite marks are highly individualistic due to variations in dental alignment, missing teeth, and dental work. They are frequently encountered in violent crimes, particularly sexual assault and child abuse.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Why are Bite Marks Significant?**

1. **Identification of the Assailant:** By comparing the bite mark on a victim with the dental impressions of a suspect, the perpetrator can be identified or excluded.
2. **Sexual Assault Evidence:** Bite marks are often found on the breasts, neck, thighs, or pubis of victims, providing physical proof of sexual contact.
3. **Child Abuse Indicator:** Repeated bite marks on various parts of the body are a common finding in **Battered Baby Syndrome**.
4. **Saliva Swabbing (Secretor Status):** Saliva found on the bite mark can reveal the assailant's blood group if they are a "secretor" (80% of the population).
5. **Corpus Delicti:** Establishes the "body of the crime" by linking the suspect's teeth to the injury on the victim.

#### **Classification and Characteristics**

**1. Human vs. Animal Bites**

- **Human Bite:** Characteristically **semicircular or crescentic**. It consists of a gap on either side due to the separation of the upper and lower jaws.
- **Animal Bite:** Usually a **deep parabolic arch** or **U-shaped**.

**2. Types of Scenarios**

- **Sexual Assault:** Often includes "sucking" action, which produces ==multiple petechial hemorrhages== in the center due to negative pressure (==Hickey==).
- **Self-Inflicted:** Found on accessible parts like ==shoulders or arms==; often seen in psychiatric patients or fabricated cases.
- **Accidental:** May occur during fits (tongue/lip biting) or falls.

---

#### **Clinical Features (Examination Findings)**

- **Appearance:** Comprised of a "crop" of punctate hemorrhages (petechiae) and larger bruises (ecchymoses) that may merge into a central bruise.
- **Dimensions:** The distance between the canines (inter-canine distance) is typically **2.5 to 4 cm** in human bites.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Examination Procedure**

★ **Important Exam Point: Bite Mark Investigation Steps**

1. **Photography:** The bite mark must be photographed from multiple angles and with a measurement scale (standard practice).
2. **Saliva Swabbing:** Swab the area immediately to identify the assailant's ABO blood group antigens (secretor status).
3. **Impressions:** Use silicone-based material or Plaster of Paris to create a permanent negative cast of the lesion.
4. **Autopsy Preservation:** In deceased victims, the skin carrying the bite mark is removed and preserved in **formalin**for further analysis.
5. **Comparison:** The victim's bite mark is compared with the **dental charts** or impressions of the suspect.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Pressure Abrasion:** The technical injury classification for a bite mark.
- ★ **Section 117 BNS:** Grievous hurt if a tooth is fractured/dislocated.
- ★ **Secretors:** ABO antigens are found in the saliva of 80% of individuals.
- ★ **Hickey:** Multiple petechial hemorrhages due to rupture of small capillaries and venules.

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is a hickey?**
    - _Ans:_ Multiple petechial hemorrhages in the center of a bite mark caused by the negative pressure of sucking.
2. **How do you differentiate a human bite from an animal bite?**
    - _Ans:_ Human bites are semicircular/crescentic with a jaw gap; animal bites are deep parabolic or U-shaped.
3. **What can you find in the saliva of a bite mark?**
    - _Ans:_ ABO blood group antigens (if they are a secretor) and the enzyme $\alpha$-amylase.
4. **Can bite mark evidence be the sole basis for conviction?**
    - _Ans:_ While it is strong corroborative evidence, many DNA exonerations have occurred for individuals previously convicted based on erroneous bite mark IDs.

---

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Definition:** Forensic dental evidence of contact.
- **Injury Type:** Imprint/Pressure abrasion.
- **Key Law:** Section 117 BNS (Grievous hurt if tooth lost).
- **Key Evidence:** Saliva (DNA/Blood group) and dental pattern (identity).
- **Main Goal:** Identifying the assailant in sexual assault or child abuse.
