[[Sodomy]]
In cases involving an alleged child victim of **Sodomy** (anal intercourse), the medical practitioner must handle the situation with extreme clinical sensitivity and legal precision. The legal framework has recently transitioned to the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, and the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**, alongside the **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012**.

---

### **Basic Concepts and Legal Framework**

- **Definition:** Sodomy refers to sexual intercourse _per anum_ (through the anus).
- **Child Victim:** Under the POCSO Act, any person below **18 years** of age is a child.
- **Legal Classification:** Under **Section 63 BNS**, the non-consensual penetration of the **anal canal** with a penis or any object is defined as **Rape**.
- **★ Important Exam Point:** If the victim is a child (<18 years), the act is also classified as **Penetrative Sexual Assault** under the POCSO Act. Consent is legally irrelevant for any person under 18 years of age (Statutory Rape).

---

### **a. Procedure of Examination**

The examination must be conducted following the guidelines laid out in **Section 184 BNSS** (formerly Sec 164A CrPC).

#### **1. Prerequisites and Legal Duties**

- **Mandatory Reporting:** Any healthcare provider who suspects child abuse must inform the police under the POCSO Act; failure to do so is a punishable offence.
- **Authorization:** A written request from a police officer (not below Sub-Inspector) is required.
- **Consent:** Informed consent must be obtained from the parent or legal guardian.
- **Mandatory Female Doctor:** For a girl child, the examination **must** be conducted by a female Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP).
- **Timeline:** The victim should be examined within **24 hours** of the police receiving information.

#### **2. History**

- Record the child's own words regarding the incident.
- Note the date, time, and place of the alleged act.
- Enquire about any symptoms like pain, bleeding, or difficulty in walking/defecation.

#### **3. Physical and Local Examination**

- **Position:** The subject is usually examined in the **Knee-Elbow position** or the Lithotomy position to allow for proper visualization of the perianal area.
- **General Physical:** Look for general signs of struggle, such as bruises on the thighs or "butterfly bruises".
- **Local Anal Examination:**
    - **Acute (Non-Habitual) Signs:** Look for fresh tears, bleeding, or the **Tyre sign** (an anal hematoma/bruise). The sphincter may be spasmodic due to pain.
    - **Chronic (Habitual) Signs:** If the abuse is repeated, look for a funnel-shaped anus, loss of mucosal rugosity (folds), and **Paradoxical Dilatation** (the sphincter opens instead of closing when the buttocks are pulled apart).
- **★ Important Exam Point:** The **Toluidine-blue test** can be used to detect fresh micro-injuries in the perianal skin that may not be visible to the naked eye.

---

### **b. Evidence to be Collected**

Evidence collection must be meticulous and follow the **Chain of Custody** using a **SAFE Kit** (Sexual Assault Forensic Evidence Kit).

|**Material**|**Source/Method**|**Forensic Significance**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Anal Swabs**|**At least 4 swabs** taken from the anal canal.|Used for detecting **intact spermatozoa**, DNA profiling, and chemical tests like p30 (Prostate Specific Antigen).|
|**Clothing**|All garments worn during or after the incident.|To detect blood, semen, mud, or the assailant’s hair.|
|**Nail Scrapings**|Using a nail pick to collect debris from under the child’s nails.|May contain the assailant's skin cells or blood if the child struggled.|
|**Pubic/Head Hair**|Plucking hair with roots.|To compare with hair found on the suspect or at the crime scene.|
|**Other Swabs**|Swabs from the thighs, buttocks, or any visible stains.|Detection of dried seminal fluid or saliva.|
|**Control Samples**|Blood or buccal swabs from the child.|For DNA comparison to exclude the victim's own profile from mixed samples.|

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **BNSS 184:** Governs the medical examination of sexual assault victims.
- ★ **BSA 120:** If the victim states in court they did not consent, the court **shall presume** absence of consent.
- ★ **Tyre Sign:** A sign of forceful anal penetration in a non-habitual agent.
- ★ **SAFE Kit:** The standard tool for evidence collection in sexual assault cases.

---

### **Quick Revision Summary**

1. **Law:** BNS 63 (Rape), POCSO (Child protection), BNSS 184 (Exam procedure).
2. **Examination:** Knee-elbow position; look for tears, "Tyre sign," or "Paradoxical dilatation".
3. **Mandatory:** Female doctor for girls, inform police immediately, parent's consent.
4. **Evidence:** 4 anal swabs, clothing, nail scrapings, and hair.
5. **Chain of Custody:** Document every transfer of evidence to prevent tampering.
