[[Mechanical injuries]]
# 🛡️⚔️ **DEFENSE WOUNDS (3-MARK MODEL ANSWER)** ⚔️🛡️

---

### **1. Definition** 📚

- ★ **Defense Wounds (Defense Injuries):** Wounds of the extremities (hands and arms) that result from the **immediate, instinctive, and automatic reaction** of a victim trying to ward off an attack or shield themselves from a weapon.

---

### **2. Classification & Common Sites** 📊

Defense wounds are classified into two distinct types based on the victim’s response [slide]:

```
                            [TYPES OF DEFENSE WOUNDS]
                                        │
         ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                             ▼
   [ACTIVE DEFENSE]                                     [PASSIVE DEFENSE]
 • Occurs when grabbing the weapon.        • Occurs when shielding vital areas.
 • **M/C Site:** Palmar surface of hand  • **M/C Site:** Medial or ulnar aspect
   and 1st web space [slide].                of the forearm, or extensor aspect
 • If weapon is sharp, produces cuts     • If weapon is blunt, produces bruises
   on palm or bends of fingers.                           and abrasions.
```

- **Active Defense Wounds:**
    - The victim actively **grasps or grabs the weapon** [slide].
    - ★ **Typical injury:** Cuts inside the **palmar surface** and the **first web space** [slide].
    - _Sharp weapon features:_ A single-edged knife produces a single cut on the palm/fingers, while a double-edged weapon produces cuts on both sides. These cuts are usually irregular/ragged because skin tension is loose when gripping.
- **Passive Defense Wounds:**
    - The victim raises their arms to **deflect or shield** vital regions like the head and neck [415, slide].
    - ★ **Typical injury:** Located on the **medial/ulnar aspect of the forearm** or the **extensor aspect of the forearm/wrist** [slide]. Blunt weapons produce bruises and abrasions here.

---

### **3. Situations Where Defense Wounds are Absent** 🚫

★ **Important Exam Point:** Examiners frequently ask when defense wounds are missing during a homicide. They are absent if the victim is:

- **U**nconscious [417, slide]
- **T**aken completely by surprise [417, slide]
- **A**ttacked from behind [417, slide]
- **A**sleep [slide]
- **U**nder the influence of alcohol or drugs [417, slide]

---

### **4. Medico-Legal Importance (MLI)** ⚖️🩺

- ★ **Proof of Homicide:** The presence of defense wounds is strong, almost diagnostic evidence of **homicide** or a violent physical struggle [slide].
- **Conscious State:** It proves the victim was **conscious, alert, and capable of anticipating** the attack [417, slide].
- **Excludes Suicide:** Suicide victims do not exhibit defense wounds.
