[[Autopsy]]
In university examinations, **Preservation of Viscera** is a high-yield topic, frequently asked as a 5-mark short note or as part of a 10-mark long question on poisoning.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition** Viscera preservation is the systematic collection and storage of internal organs and body fluids during a postmortem examination to detect, identify, and quantify poisons through chemical analysis.

**Introduction** In cases of suspected poisoning, the gross findings at autopsy may be inconclusive (Obscure Autopsy) or completely absent (Negative Autopsy). In such scenarios, the final diagnosis depends entirely on the report from the **Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL)** or the **Chemical Examiner**.

**Objectives**

- To establish the **Cause of Death** in suspected poisoning.
- To identify the ==specific poison== and its quantity.
- To determine the ==route of administration== (e.g., finding unabsorbed poison in the stomach).
- To establish a legal "Chain of Custody" for evidence.

---

### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Criminal Investigation:** Provides scientific proof of homicide, suicide, or accidental poisoning.
- **Civil Cases:** Crucial for insurance claims, inheritance disputes, and workmen's compensation.
- **Medical Negligence:** Helps differentiate between a ==fatal dose of poison== and a medical mishap.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The Supreme Court has ruled that in all cases of suspected poisoning, the viscera **must** be sent to the FSL immediately after the postmortem.

---

### **3. Relevant Law**

**Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- **Section 194 BNSS:** Police Inquest. The police officer requisitions the autopsy and chemical analysis in unnatural deaths.
- **Section 196 BNSS:** Magistrate Inquest. Mandatory for custodial deaths or suspicious deaths within 7 years of marriage.

**Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 286 BNS:** Relates to negligence regarding poisonous substances.
- **Section 123 BNS:** Causing hurt by means of poison with intent to commit an offence.

**Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 26 BSA:** Relates to the admissibility of a **Dying Declaration** (if recorded before the victim's death).
- **Chain of Custody:** Under BSA, every person who handles the viscera from the doctor to the chemist must be documented to prevent evidence tampering.

---

### **4. Routine Specimen Collection (Quantity & Procedure)**

During a routine poisoning autopsy, the following specimens are collected in separate containers:

| **Specimen**               | **Quantity**                 | **Medico-Legal Significance**                                       |
| :------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Stomach & its contents** | **Whole**                    | To find ==unabsorbed poison== and ==route== of intake.              |
| **Upper Small Intestine**  | **30 cm**                    | Detects poison that has ==passed== the pylorus.                     |
| **Liver**                  | **500 g** (with Gallbladder) | The primary site of metabolism/detoxification.                      |
| **Kidney**                 | **Half of each**             | To detect ==excreted== poisons and their metabolites.               |
| **Blood**                  | **10–100 mL**                | Most useful for relating ==toxin== levels to physiological effects. |
| **Urine**                  | **100 mL**                   | Useful if blood is unavailable or toxin is extensively metabolized. |

---

### **5. Additional Specimens for Specific Poisons**

| **Suspected Poison**                    | **Specimen to Collect**                           |
| :-------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| **Inhaled Gases** (CO, HCN), Chloroform | **Lungs** (tied at bronchus).                     |
| **Heavy Metals** (Arsenic, Lead)        | **Long bone (Femur), Hair, and Nails**.           |
| **Strychnine**                          | **Spinal Cord**.                                  |
| **OPC / Cerebral poisons**              | **Brain**.                                        |
| **Alcohol** (in putrefied bodies)       | **Vitreous Humor** or CSF.                        |
| **Injected Poisons**                    | **Skin** (area of injection mark with a control). |
| **Putrefied Bodies**                    | **Skeletal Muscle** (from thigh/buttock).         |

---

### **6. Preservatives**

Preservatives are used to prevent ==putrefaction== and ==autolysis==, which can destroy the chemical structure of the poison.

**1. Saturated Solution of Common Salt**

- **Most commonly used** in India.
- Cheap, easily available, and effective.

**2. Rectified Spirit (95% Ethanol)**

- **Ideal preservative** for most `organic and metallic poisons.`
- **Contraindicated** in certain poisonings (see Mnemonic).

**3. Specific Preservatives**

- **Blood:** Sodium Fluoride (anti-glycolytic) + Potassium Oxalate (anti-coagulant).
- **Urine:** Toluene or Thymol.
- **Vitreous Humor:** Sodium Fluoride.

---

### **7. Mnemonics**

#### **A. Contraindications for Rectified Spirit**

Use this to remember when **NOT** to use spirit: **Mnemonic: "P-A-P-P-A don't consume alcohol"**

- **P** – **P**henol (Carbolic acid)
- **A** – **A**lcohol (Ethanol/Methanol)
- **P** – **P**ara-aldehyde
- **P** – **P**hosphorus
- **A** – **A**cetic acid (or Corrosive acids/alkalis)

#### **B. Preservation of Bile**

**Mnemonic: "GB ON Alcohol"**

- **G**all **B**ladder and bile are preserved in **Alcohol**.

---

### **8. Procedure for Packing and Dispatch**

1. **Container:** 1-liter wide-mouthed transparent glass jars or hard plastic (polypropylene) jars.
2. **Filling Rule:** The jar should be filled **2/3rds** with the specimen and preservative, leaving **1/3rd space** for gases to evolve.
3. **Labelling:** Each jar must have the deceased's name, age, sex, date/time of autopsy, and the doctor's signature.
4. **Sealing:** The lid is closed, covered with cloth, and tied with twine. A **departmental seal** is applied using wax.
5. **Chain of Custody:** The sealed jars are placed in a "Viscera Box," which is also sealed and handed over to the **Police Constable/Investigating Officer** with a proper receipt.
6. **Documents Sent to FSL:**
    - Copy of the Inquest papers.
    - Copy of the Autopsy Report.
    - Requisition letter to the Chemical Examiner.
    - Sample of the preservative used (in a small vial) to rule out contamination.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Formalin** is **NEVER** used as a preservative for chemical analysis because it makes poison extraction difficult.
- ★ **Sodium Fluoride** is used in blood samples to inhibit the enzyme **enolase** and prevent glycolysis/bacterial growth.
- ★ **Femoral Vein** is the best site for postmortem blood collection.
- ★ **Magenstrasse Effect:** In acid poisoning, the stomach should be opened along the **greater curvature** to preserve the damage along the lesser curvature.

---

### **Last-Minute Revision Summary**

- **Routine:** Stomach, Intestine, Liver, Kidneys, Blood, Urine.
- **Containers:** 1-liter Wide-mouthed jars.
- **Space:** 1/3rd empty for gases.
- **Preservatives:** Salt (Routine), Spirit (Ideal), NaF/Oxalate (Blood).
- **BNS/BNSS:** Procedure governed by 194/196 BNSS.
- **Handover:** Return evidence to **Police**, not family.

---
