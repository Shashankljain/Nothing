[[Identification]]

### **Superimposition (Skull-Photo Superimposition)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** 
**Superimposition** is a forensic technique used to determine whether a recovered skull matches the face of a person in a specific photograph. It is primarily a **screening test** used in the identification of skeletal remains.

**Introduction** In cases where a body is skeletonised and only the skull is available, forensic experts compare the morphology of the skull with an antemortem (before death) photograph of a missing person.

**Principles** The technique relies on the anatomical consistency between the bony landmarks of the skull and the soft tissue features of the face.

---

#### **Procedure of Superimposition**

Technologically, this method has evolved through three distinct phases: **Photographic, Video, and Computer-assisted**techniques.

**1. Prerequisites and Requirements**

- **Complete Skull:** A complete skull, including the **mandible**, is essential. Positive identification is nearly impossible without the mandible, even if the rest of the skull appears to match.
- **Antemortem Photograph:** A clear photograph of the suspected individual in a known orientation (front or profile view).

**2. The Photographic Method (Traditional)**

- The comparison photograph is enlarged to the life-size dimensions of the unknown skull.
- The skull is then placed on a stand and positioned in the exact same orientation and angle as the face in the photograph.

**3. The Computer-Assisted Method (Modern)**

- Both the skull and the facial photograph are **digitised** using video cameras and computer software.
- The two images are superimposed electronically. The software allows for the adjustment of transparency levels to see how well the bony structures (like the eye sockets or teeth) align with the facial features in the photo.

**4. Points of Comparison (Anatomical Landmarks)** Experts look for consistency at specific anthropometric points:

- **Eyes:** Alignment of the eye with the bony orbit.
- **Eyebrows:** Relationship to the supraorbital margin.
- **Nose:** Alignment of the nose with the nasal aperture.
- **Mouth/Teeth:** Coincidence of dentition, especially if incisors or canines are visible in a "smiling" photograph.
- **Ears:** Alignment of the ear with the external auditory meatus.
- **Facial Tissue Thickness:** Ensuring the thickness of the soft tissue at various points is anatomically plausible.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Identification of Unknown Remains:** It is a vital tool for identifying skeletal remains in criminal investigations (e.g., homicide) or mass disasters.
- **Negative Value (Exclusion):** The test has a **high negative value**. If the skull and photograph do not match, it can be stated with certainty that they are **not** the same person.
- **Corroborative Value (Inclusion):** If the images match perfectly, the expert can only state that the skull **could be**that of the photographed person. It is usually considered a presumptive match that requires further confirmation (e.g., DNA).
- **Corpus Delicti:** It helps establish the identity of the victim, which is a core component of the "Body of Crime" (Corpus Delicti).

---

#### **Landmark Cases**

- **The Ruxton Case (Jigsaw Murders, 1935):** One of the first successful uses of photographic superimposition to identify two mutilated victims.
- **The Sheena Bora Case (2012):** Modern digital superimposition was used to match a recovered skull and mandible with antemortem photographs of the victim, which was later confirmed by DNA.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Presumptive Test:** Superimposition is a screening test; DNA is the confirmatory test.
- ★ **Mandible Necessity:** Positive identification is not possible without the mandible.
- ★ **Negative vs. Positive:** It is more reliable for **excluding** a person than for a 100% positive identification.
- ★ **Anatomical Landmarks:** Know the key points (Orbit, Supraorbital margin, Nasal aperture).

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Is superimposition a confirmatory test?**
    - _Ans:_ No, it is a screening/presumptive test. DNA profiling is the confirmatory method.
2. **Can you identify a person if the mandible is missing?**
    - _Ans:_ No. A complete skull with the mandible is required for positive identification.
3. **What is the "negative value" of this test?**
    - _Ans:_ It means the test is better at proving the skull does _not_ belong to the person in the photo than proving it _does_.
4. **In which famous Indian case was this used recently?**
    - _Ans:_ The Sheena Bora murder case.

---

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Objective:** Matching skull to antemortem photo.
- **Phases:** Photo $\rightarrow$ Video $\rightarrow$ Computer.
- **Key Requirement:** Mandible must be present.
- **Landmarks:** Orbit, teeth (incisors/canines), and nasal aperture.
- **Legal Standing:** Strong exclusionary value; presumptive for inclusion.
