[[Asphyxia]]
For an MBBS student, **Lynching** is a specific category within the study of **homicidal hanging** and is often tested in the context of classifications of asphyxial deaths.

---

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** **Lynching** is a form of **homicidal hanging** where a group of people (a mob) acting jointly and illegally overpowers a suspect, an accused, or an enemy and hangs them by means of a rope from a tree or a similar object.

**Historical Background** The practice was historically prevalent in North America. It represents extrajudicial killing performed without legal authorization or due process.

**Mechanism**

1. **Overpowering:** Several persons act together to physically subdue the victim.
2. **Suspension:** The victim is forcefully suspended, typically from a branch or beam.
3. **Cause of Death:** As in other forms of homicidal hanging, death usually results from a combination of **asphyxia**, venous congestion, and cerebral ischemia.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point:** Lynching is always classified as **homicidal**.

- **Signs of Struggle:** Because the victim is overpowered by a mob, the body usually shows extensive **signs of struggle**, including abrasions, contusions, and defensive injuries that are generally absent in suicidal hanging.
- **Crime Scene:** Unlike suicidal hanging, which occurs in secluded places, lynching typically involves signs of a disturbance at the scene and footprints of multiple individuals.
- **Postmortem Suspension:** Doctors must differentiate between true lynching (hanging while alive) and "postmortem suspension," where a body is murdered by other means and then hung to simulate suicide or to display the body.

---

### **Relevant Law (2023)**

As a form of homicidal hanging, lynching falls under the following provisions of the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**:

- **Section 100 BNS:** Definition of **Culpable Homicide**.
- **Section 101 BNS:** Definition of **Murder**.
- **Section 103 BNS:** Punishment for murder, which can include the death sentence or life imprisonment.
- **BNSS Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer must document the injuries and the nature of the ligature mark to assist the Investigating Officer (IO) in establishing that the hanging was not suicidal.

---

### **Differentiating Lynching (Homicidal) from Suicidal Hanging**

|**Feature**|**Suicidal Hanging**|**Lynching (Homicidal Hanging)**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Point of Suspension**|Reachable by the victim.|Usually high/inaccessible to a lone victim.|
|**Signs of Struggle**|Usually absent.|Present (Bruises, torn clothes).|
|**Ligature Mark**|Oblique and non-continuous.|May be circular/horizontal if dragged.|
|**Saliva Dribbling**|Present (Antemortem sign).|Present only if alive during suspension.|
|**Suicide Note**|Often present.|Not present.|

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Category:** Lynching is a sub-type of **homicidal hanging**.
- ★ **Key Indicator:** Presence of multiple injuries and signs of a struggle indicates mob involvement.
- ★ **Cause of Death:** Usually a combination of Asphyxia and Venous Congestion.

---

### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **What?** Illegal mob hanging of an enemy or suspect.
- **Why?** Extrajudicial punishment or revenge.
- **Findings:** Signs of struggle, multiple assailants' footprints, high suspension point.
- **Legal:** Murder (Section 101 BNS).
- **Significance:** Must be differentiated from suicidal hanging during autopsy.
