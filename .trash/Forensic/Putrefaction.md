[[PM Changes]]
**Basic Concepts**

**Definition** Putrefaction is the process by which ==complex organic== body tissues break down into **==simpler inorganic==** compounds or elements due to the action of **saprophytic microorganisms** (mainly bacteria) or **autolysis**.

**Introduction** Putrefaction is classified as a **late change after death**. It usually follows the disappearance of rigor mortis. In tropical climates like India, external signs typically commence within 12–24 hours during summer.

**Microorganisms Involved** The most important bacterium responsible for putrefaction is **Clostridium perfringens** (which produces lecithinase). Other involved organisms include _Staphylococcus_, _Streptococcus_, and _Proteus_.

---

**Mechanism of Putrefaction**

The breakdown occurs via two parallel processes:

1. **Autolysis:** "Self-dissolution" where the body’s own digestive enzymes (proteolytic, glycolytic, and lipolytic) break down cells immediately after death.
2. **Bacterial Action:** Bacteria from the intestinal tract (where they are most abundant in the ileocecal zone) ==invade== the blood vessels and tissues once the body's immune defense is lost.

**Gases Produced:** As tissues break down, various gases are released, including ==**Hydrogen Sulphide (**H2​S**)**, Ammonia (NH3​), Carbon Dioxide (CO2​), and Methane (CH4​).==

---

**Postmortem Changes (The '4 Ds')**

Decomposition is traditionally described through four major changes:

1. **Discoloration:**
    - **★ Important Exam Point: The First External Sign:** A greenish discoloration appears over the **Right Iliac Fossa (RIF)**.
    - **Why RIF?** The caecum (full of bacteria and fluid) lies superficially here.
    - **Internal Sign:** The first internal sign is discoloration of the **==aortic intima**==.
2. **Distension (Bloating):**
    - Gases accumulate in the hollow viscera and tissues.
    - **Face:** Becomes bloated, eyelids swell, and the tongue protrudes ("==fish-mouth"== appearance).
    - **Abdomen:** Becomes tense and distended.
    - **Genitalia:** Scrotum and penis in males or labia in females become markedly swollen.
3. **Degradation (Skin and Hair Changes):**
    - **Skin Slippage:** The epidermis becomes loose and can be peeled off easily.
    - **Degloving:** Skin of the hands and feet may come off entirely like a ==glove or stocking.==
    - **Postmortem Purge:** Blood-stained ==frothy fluid== escapes from the mouth and nostrils due to gas pressure in the abdomen.
4. **Dissolution (Liquefaction):**
    - Tissues eventually turn into a ==thick==, ==semisolid== ==black== mass (Colliquative putrefaction) and eventually skeletonize.

---

**Special Phenomona**

- **Marbling of Skin:** Linear, branching, greenish-brown patterns appear along the superficial veins (resembling a "road map"). This is due to the reaction of H2​S gas with hemoglobin to form **sulfhemoglobin**.
- **"Swiss Cheese" / Honeycomb Liver:** The liver becomes soft and full of gas bubbles, appearing porous on cut section.
- **Tête de négre:** In putrefied drowning victims, the head and neck appear very dark/bronze due to the settling of blood in these dependent parts (from conversation history and).

---

**Order of Putrefaction**

Examiners frequently ask about the sequence in which organs decompose:

|**Sequence**|**Organs**|
|---|---|
|**Early**|Larynx, Trachea, Stomach, Intestines, Spleen, Liver.|
|**Late**|Heart, Lungs, Kidneys, Bladder.|
|**Last**|**Prostate** (males) and **Non-gravid Uterus** (females).|

★ **Medico-Legal Significance:** Because the prostate and uterus resist putrefaction the longest, they are vital for identifying the sex of a highly decomposed body.

---

**Factors Affecting the Rate of Putrefaction**

The speed of decay depends on the environment, summarized by **Casper’s Dictum**:

- **Casper's Dictum:** The rate of putrefaction in **Air : Water : Soil** occurs in a ratio of **1 : 2 : 8**.
    - _Meaning:_ A body decomposes 2 times slower in water and 8 times slower in soil than in air.
- **Temperature:** Optimum temperature is **21–38°C**. Decomposition stops below 0°C or above 48°C.
- **Moisture:** Humidity promotes bacterial growth.
- **State of Body:** Emaciated bodies decompose slower than well-nourished/obese bodies.

---

**Medico-Legal Importance**

1. **Estimation of Time Since Death (TSD):** Provides a range of hours/days based on the stages of decay.
2. **Identification:** Advanced putrefaction makes visual identification and fingerprinting difficult.
3. **Cause of Death:** Usually cannot be determined in advanced cases, except for poisoning (heavy metals), fractures, or firearm injuries.
4. **Rule out Foul Play:** Putrefaction can hide minor bruises, but it cannot hide a "Swiss cheese" liver or major skeletal trauma.

---

**Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023, Section 2(6):** Defines **Death** as the death of a human being.
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer must note signs of putrefaction during the autopsy to assist the Investigating Officer (IO) in establishing the TSD.

---

**High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **First External Sign:** Greenish spot in the **Right Iliac Fossa**.
- ★ **First Internal Sign:** Discoloration of **Aortic Intima**.
- ★ **Last Organ to Putrefy:** **Prostate/Uterus**.
- ★ **Casper’s Dictum Ratio:** **1 (Air) : 2 (Water) : 8 (Soil)**.
- ★ **Diatoms:** They resist putrefaction and are the "gold standard" for diagnosing drowning in decomposed bodies.

---

**Quick Revision Summary**

1. **What?** Tissue breakdown by bacteria (_C. perfringens_) and autolysis.
2. **Early Signs:** Green spot in RIF (12–24h), Marbling (36–48h).
3. **Late Signs:** Bloating, Skin slippage, Purge fluid, Liquefaction.
4. **Factors:** Heat and moisture hasten it; cold and dryness retard it.
5. **Significance:** Tells us **when** the person died and helps identify **sex** in advanced decay.
