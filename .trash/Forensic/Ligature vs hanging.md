[[Asphyxia]]
In Forensic Medicine, the examination of the **ligature mark** (also known as the **furrow**) is the most crucial part of an autopsy in deaths involving neck compression. For a university examination, you must be able to describe and differentiate the marks seen in **hanging** and **ligature strangulation**.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition of Ligature Mark** A ligature mark is a **pressure abrasion** produced on the neck by the application of a constricting ==material== (rope, wire, cloth). It bears the ==imprint== of the material used and provides vital clues about the manner of death.

**Significance in Medico-Legal Practice**

- **Manner of Death:** Helps distinguish between suicide (common in hanging) and homicide (common in strangulation).
- **Proof of Life:** The presence of a "vital reaction" in the mark proves the person was alive when the ligature was applied.
- **Identification of Weapon:** Patterned marks can link the injury to a specific rope or object.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The ligature mark is a **process of drying**, not just an injury. A fresh mark may be pale and soft, but it becomes tough, yellowish-brown, and "parchment-like" over time as the skin dehydrates.

---

### **2. Ligature Mark in Hanging**

**Mechanism:** The neck is constricted by a loop, and the force is provided by the **weight of the body**.

#### **Characteristics of the Mark (The Furrow)**

- **Course:** **Oblique** (angled upwards). It follows the line of the jaw towards the point of suspension (the knot).
- **Continuity:** **Non-continuous**. The mark is interrupted at the site of the knot or where hair/clothing intervened between the rope and the skin.
- **Level:** Usually high in the neck, **above the thyroid cartilage** (hyoid level).
- **Appearance:** Depressed, pale, and becomes **parchment-like** (hard and leathery) after death.
- **Abrasion:** "Rope burns" or small abrasions may be seen at the edges.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The presence of **dribbling of saliva** from the angle of the mouth opposite the knot is the surest sign that the hanging was antemortem.

---

### **3. Ligature Mark in Strangulation**

**Mechanism:** The neck is constricted by a ligature where the force is applied by an **external agency** (e.g., an assailant) other than the body weight.

#### **Characteristics of the Mark**

- **Course:** **Transverse** (horizontal) and level. It encircles the neck like a collar.
- **Continuity:** **Continuous**. It usually completely encircles the neck as it is often tied with force.
- **Level:** Usually at or **below the level of the thyroid cartilage**.
- **Appearance:** The base is often **soft, reddish, and bruised** rather than dry and parchment-like (unless a thin wire was used).
- **Injuries:** Scratches and fingernail marks (from the victim trying to pull the rope away) are frequently seen around the mark.

### **5. Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer must document the ligature mark's position, continuity, and appearance to help the Investigating Officer (IO) determine if the death was suicidal or homicidal.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, Section 39:** Relates to **Expert Opinion**. A doctor’s interpretation of the ligature mark (e.g., "The transverse course indicates homicidal strangulation") is admissible as expert evidence in court.
- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023, Section 238:** Relates to the **disappearance of evidence**. Tampering with a ligature mark or moving a body to simulate hanging (Postmortem Suspension) is punishable under this section [derived from legal context].

### **6. Medico-Legal Examination & Documentation**

When examining a ligature mark, follow this flowchart:

1. **Photography:** Photograph with the ligature in situ and after removal.
2. **Removal:** **NEVER untie the knot**. Cut the ligature away from the knot and join the ends with tape to preserve the knot's structure.
3. **Description:** Note the level, course, width, and pattern (e.g., "weave of the rope").
4. **Internal Dissection:** Perform a **bloodless neck dissection** (open the skull first) to look for internal bruising, intimal tears in carotid arteries (**Amussat's Sign**), or **Hyoid bone fractures**.

---

### **7. High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Amussat's Sign:** Transverse tears in the inner lining (intima) of the carotid arteries; seen in judicial hanging or long drops.
- ★ **Simon’s Sign:** Hemorrhages on the lumbar intervertebral discs; a vital sign of antemortem hanging.
- ★ **La Facie Sympathique:** One eye remains open and the pupil dilated due to pressure on the cervical sympathetic chain; indicates antemortem hanging.
- ★ **Pseudo-strangulation:** Skin folds in infants or obese individuals can mimic a ligature mark; distinguished by the absence of a vital reaction.

---

### **8. Viva Questions**

**Q1: Why is the mark in hanging often non-continuous?** **Answer:** Because the knot or the hair/clothing prevents the ligature from touching the skin at the point of suspension.

**Q2: What is "Postmortem Suspension"?** **Answer:** It is a homicidal artefact where a person is murdered (e.g., by poisoning or stabbing) and then hanged to simulate suicide. It is identified by the absence of vital reactions like saliva or Simon's sign.

**Q3: Can a soft ligature (like a silk scarf) leave a mark?** **Answer:** Yes, but it will be faint, less depressed, and may lack parchmentization compared to a rough rope.

### **Model University Answer (5 Marks)**

**Q: Describe the ligature mark in homicidal strangulation.**

1. **Definition:** It is a pressure abrasion collar around the neck caused by an assailant using a cord or cloth.
2. **Course:** Horizontal/Transverse, completely encircling the neck.
3. **Level:** Located at or below the thyroid cartilage (lower neck).
4. **Appearance:** Base is reddish, soft, and shows bruising (ecchymosis).
5. **Associated Findings:** Presence of fingernail abrasions, signs of struggle, and cyanosis of the face.
6. **MLI:** Differentiates it from suicidal hanging; admissible under BSA Sec 39.
