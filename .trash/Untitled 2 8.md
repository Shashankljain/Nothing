# Gallstone Disease (Cholelithiasis)

### 1. Definition
- **Gallstone disease (Cholelithiasis)** refers to the formation and presence of solid calculi within the **gallbladder** or **biliary tree**, characteristically demonstrated on **abdominal ultrasound** by **acoustic shadowing**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Disruption of Enterohepatic Circulation**: Impairment of bile acid reabsorption (e.g., following **ileal disease or resection**) disrupts the **enterohepatic circulation**, resulting in gallstone formation in **45%** of patients.
- **Cystic Fibrosis**: Disruption of secretions leads to **gallstones** as a major systemic gastrointestinal complication in **25%** of adult patients.
- **Severe Immune Suppression / HIV**: Associated with **HIV cholangiopathy**, which predisposes patients to **papillary stenosis** and **acalculous cholecystitis**.

---

### 3. Classification
- **Cholelithiasis**: Calculi located within the **gallbladder lumen**, displaying **posterior acoustic shadowing**.
- **Choledocholithiasis**: Calculi located within the **common bile duct**, causing **biliary tract dilatation** and extrahepatic obstruction.
- **Acalculous Cholecystitis**: Gallbladder inflammation occurring without calculi, commonly complicating **HIV cholangiopathy**.

---

### 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Hormonal Regulation**: **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** regulates pancreatic and biliary exocrine secretions.
- **Bile Acid Depletion**: Interruption of **enterohepatic circulation** (e.g., terminal ileal resection) depletes bile salts, promoting cholesterol supersaturation and crystallization.
- **Biliary Obstruction**: Calculi migrating into the **common bile duct** cause obstruction and **biliary tract dilatation**.

### 5. Clinical Features
- **Signs**:
  - **Jaundice**: Indicates extrahepatic obstruction of the **common bile duct**.
  - **Palpable Gallbladder**: Associated with biliary obstruction or distension on abdominal examination.
  - **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) Tenderness & Guarding**: Signs of localized peritonitis or acute inflammation.
- **Symptoms**:
  - **Epigastric / RUQ Pain**: Episodic or severe postprandial biliary pain.
  - **Pleuritic Chest Pain**: Subdiaphragmatic biliary inflammation in **cholecystitis** or **ascending cholangitis** can mimic pneumonia.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Lab Findings**:
  - **Serum Amylase & Lipase**: Checked to diagnose complicating **acute pancreatitis**.
- **Imaging**:
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound (USG)**: Initial investigation of choice; identifies **gallstones** with **acoustic shadowing** and evaluates **biliary tract dilatation**.
  - **Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)**: Replaces diagnostic ERCP as the non-invasive imaging test of choice to evaluate the **biliary tree**, **common bile duct**, and **pancreatic duct**.
  - **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: High-resolution tool for evaluating **common bile duct stones**, **ampullary lesions**, and **cholangiocarcinoma**.
  - **Plain Abdominal X-Ray**: Detects radiopaque calcified **gallstones**.
  - **Computed Tomography (CT)**: Used for staging and identifying abdominal collections.
- **Endoscopic Procedures**:
  - **Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)**: Reserved for therapeutic interventions, including **removal of common bile duct calculi**, **sphincterotomy**, **basket retrieval**, and **biliary stent insertion**.

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

| Feature | Biliary Colic / Cholelithiasis | Acute Cholecystitis | Choledocholithiasis / Ascending Cholangitis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site of Impaction** | Gallbladder lumen | Cystic duct / Gallbladder wall | Common Bile Duct (CBD) |
| **Fever & Pleuritic Pain** | Absent | Present (fever, pleuritic RUQ pain) | Present with high fever & rigors |
| **Jaundice** | Absent | Absent | Present (Obstructive Jaundice) |
| **Diagnostic Tool** | Abdominal **Ultrasound** | Abdominal **Ultrasound** | **MRCP** (Diagnostic) |
| **Definitive Treatment** | Elective **Cholecystectomy** | **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** | **Therapeutic ERCP** stone clearance + **Cholecystectomy** |

---

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Non-Pharmacological**:
  - Conservative monitoring for asymptomatic gallstones.
- **Pharmacological**:
  - Supportive hydration and pain control during acute episodes.
- **Surgical & Interventional**:
  - **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**: Preferred procedure in fit individuals requiring gallbladder removal.
  - **Therapeutic ERCP**: Indicated for **common bile duct calculi removal**, **sphincterotomy**, **basket retrieval**, and **stent placement**.
  - **Papillary Cautery via ERCP**: Amenable therapeutic option for **papillary stenosis** in **HIV cholangiopathy**.

---

### 9. Complications
- **Acute Cholecystitis**
- **Ascending Cholangitis**
- **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis**
- **Obstructive Jaundice**
- **Acalculous Cholecystitis**

---

### 10. Prognosis
- Excellent long-term outcomes following successful **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** or ductal stone clearance via **ERCP**.

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace
- **Mnemonic: GALLSTONES**
  - **G** — **Gallbladder**: Primary anatomical site of cholelithiasis.
  - **A** — **Acoustic Shadowing**: Characteristic sign on **ultrasound**.
  - **L** — **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**: Gold-standard surgical cure.
  - **L** — **Loss of Bile Acids**: **Ileal resection** causes gallstones in **45%** of cases.
  - **S** — **Sphincterotomy**: Therapeutic technique performed during **ERCP**.
  - **T** — **Therapeutic ERCP**: For **common bile duct calculi removal** and **basket retrieval**.
  - **O** — **Obstructive Jaundice**: Complication of CBD obstruction.
  - **N** — **Non-invasive MRCP**: Replaces diagnostic ERCP for viewing the biliary tree.
  - **E** — **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: For high-resolution detection of ductal stones.
  - **S** — **Severe Complications**: **Cholangitis**, **cholecystitis**, and acute pancreatitis.

---

### Clinical Pearl
- Perform non-invasive **MRCP** or **EUS** to evaluate the biliary tree and CBD, reserving **ERCP** strictly for therapeutic procedures (**sphincterotomy**, **stone removal**, or **stenting**) due to ERCP-related complication risks (**5–10%**, including **acute pancreatitis 1–3%**).

---

### Exam Trap
- **The Mix-up**: Performing diagnostic **ERCP** for routine biliary evaluation.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **MRCP** is completely non-invasive and purely diagnostic with zero risk of pancreatitis, whereas **ERCP** is an invasive therapeutic procedure carrying a **30-day mortality of 0.5–1%**.

---

### Dosage Rule

| Procedure / Intervention | Modality | Target Indication | Frequency / Timing | Key Caution / Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** | Surgical excision | Preferred in fit individuals requiring gallbladder removal | Single procedure | Standard operative risks |
| **Therapeutic ERCP** | Endoscopic **sphincterotomy** & **basket retrieval** | **Common bile duct calculi** removal | Single procedure | Complication risk **5–10%** (**post-ERCP pancreatitis 1–3%**, bleeding) |
| **Papillary Cautery via ERCP** | Endoscopic cauterization | **Papillary stenosis** in **HIV cholangiopathy** | As needed | Perform only in severe immune suppression |

### Quick Revision
- **Abdominal Ultrasound** is the initial diagnostic test of choice for detecting **gallstones** (showing **acoustic shadowing**).
- **MRCP** is the diagnostic imaging modality of choice for biliary evaluation, whereas **ERCP** is strictly therapeutic.
- **Ileal resection / short bowel syndrome** disrupts the **enterohepatic circulation**, resulting in gallstones in **45%** of cases.
- **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** is the definitive surgical management in fit individuals.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener
- **Gallstone disease (cholelithiasis)** is a biliary disorder diagnosed initially via **abdominal ultrasound** showing **acoustic shadowing** and definitively managed with **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** or therapeutic **ERCP**.
