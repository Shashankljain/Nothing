### **I. Introduction and Definitions** ★ Important Exam Point

To write a high-scoring answer in university examinations, you must clearly distinguish between different categories of compromised medical products. Do not use these terms interchangeably.

| **Term**                                                                   | **Official Definition (WHO)**                                                                                                                            | **Key Characteristics**                                                                                                                |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Counterfeit Medicines**                                                  | Medicines deliberately and fraudulently produced with an intention to cheat.                                                                             | May involve mis-labelling, faked expiry dates, false packaging, or faked brand identity. Applies to both generic and branded products. |
| **Sub-standard Medicines** _(also called "out of specification" products)_ | Authorized medical products that fail to meet their established ==quality standards or chemical specifications==, or both.                               | This is typically due to manufacturing errors, poor quality control, or degradation during improper storage.                           |
| **Falsified Medical Products**                                             | Products that deliberately/fraudulently misrepresent their identity, composition, or source.                                                             | May contain no active pharmaceutical ingredient (API), the wrong API, or incorrect amounts of the correct API.                         |
| **Unregistered / Unlicensed Medicines**                                    | Medical products that have ==not== undergone evaluation or approval by the competent National or Regional Regulatory Authority for that specific market. | Distributed ==illegally== outside formal regulatory pathways.                                                                          |

### **II. Public Health Significance & Epidemiology** ★ Important Exam Point

- **Disease Burden:** It is estimated that **10% of medical products** in low- and middle-income countries are either sub-standard or falsified.
- **Most Affected Drug Classes:** **Anti-malarials** and **antibiotics** are the most commonly reported ==sub-standard== and ==falsified== products.
- **Consequences of Bad Drugs:**
    1. **Therapeutic Failure:** Leads to prolonged illness, complications, and death due to lack of active ingredients.
    2. **Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):** Sub-therapeutic doses of antibiotics directly select for resistant mutants, contributing heavily to drug-resistant infections globally.
    3. **Toxicity & Death:** Falsified drugs are commonly found to contain fillers like **corn starch, potato starch, or chalk**. Worse, some contain highly toxic chemicals or fatal levels of the wrong active ingredients.

### **III. Channels of Distribution (How Do They Reach Consumers?)**

With modern technology, the routes of exposure have expanded beyond traditional physical pharmacies:

- **Unregulated Websites:** Direct-to-consumer online platforms offering drugs without prescriptions.
- **Social Media Platforms:** Direct advertisements targeting vulnerable patients.
- **Smartphone Applications:** Unlicensed digital portals bypassing local pharmaceutical regulations.
- **Spam Emails:** Direct unsolicited marketing campaigns advertising cheap medicines.

### **IV. Identification of Falsified and Sub-standard Products** ★ Important Exam Point

While some falsified products are visually identical to genuine ones, healthcare professionals and consumers can detect many through systematic inspection:

```
[Inspect Packaging] ──> Spelling/grammar errors? Inner/outer batch & expiry dates match?
        │
        ▼
[Inspect Medicine]  ──> Discolouration? Physical degradation? Unusual smell?
        │
        ▼
[Assess Efficacy]   ──> Patient not responding as expected? Unusual adverse events?
        │
        ▼
[Take Action]       ──> Report immediately to National Medicines Regulatory Authority
```

1. **Packaging Assessment:** Check for poor print quality, damaged seals, spelling mistakes, or grammatical errors on labels.
2. **Date Verification:** Cross-check the manufacture and expiry dates on the outer box to ensure they match exactly with the details printed on the inner blister pack/vial.
3. **Physical Properties:** Verify that the pill, liquid, or cream does not show discoloration, signs of chemical degradation, or a strange, unusual smell.
4. **Clinical Monitoring:** If a patient suddenly fails to respond to a previously effective treatment or experiences an unusual adverse reaction, suspect product failure.
5. **Reporting:** Suspected products should be reported immediately to the pharmacy, doctor, and the **National Medicines Regulatory Authority**.

### **V. Regulatory Quality Control in India (CDSCO)** ★ Important Exam Point

In India, the quality control of marketed drugs is strictly regulated under the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and the **Drugs & Cosmetics Rules, 1945**.

#### **The Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO)**

- **Leadership:** Headed at the central level by the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)** under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- **Structure:**
    - Central level: CDSCO.
    - State level: State Drug Control Organizations, generally headed by **State Drug Controllers** appointed by State Governments.
    - CDSCO maintains a network of **6 Zonal Offices** (Mumbai, Ghaziabad, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, and Chennai), 6 sub-zonal offices, 16 port offices, and 7 testing laboratories.
- **Core Functions of CDSCO:** ★
     C 1. **Import Control:** Ensuring the quality of all medical products imported into India through its 16 port offices.
    S 2. **State Coordination:** Coordinating the regulatory activities of various State/UT Drug Control Authorities.
    D 3. **New Drug Approvals:** Evaluating and approving new drugs proposed for import or domestic manufacture.
    S 4.**Standards Formulation:** Laying down drug standards and regulatory measures.
    C 5. **Central Licensing Approving Authority:** Acting as the primary authority for licensing high-risk biologicals, including:
        - Whole human blood and blood-derived products.
        - Large volume parenterals (IV fluids).
        - Sera and vaccines.
        - Recombinant DNA (rDNA) products.
    O 6. **Joint Inspections:** Zonal officers carry out inspections of manufacturing units, blood banks, and testing laboratories jointly with State authorities to identify deficiencies.

### **VI. Global and National Countermeasures**

To protect public health, several structural measures have been put in place:

- **IMPACT (2006):** WHO launched the **International Medical Products Anti-Counterfeiting Task Force** to coordinate global responses.
- **Global Surveillance and Monitoring System (2013):** Launched by WHO to encourage structured, systematic reporting of substandard and falsified products.
- **Authenticity Markers:** Developing simple, cheap, and easily interpretable chemical or visual markers (e.g., security holograms or scratch codes) to verify genuine products.
- **Intersectoral Collaboration:** Promoting cooperation between drug regulatory authorities, customs departments, police forces, and the judiciary to disrupt illegal supply chains.
- **Education:** Training healthcare professionals, pharmacists, and educating patients on the risks of buying from unregulated online sources.

### **Frequently Asked Viva Questions**

- **Q: What is the main difference between a counterfeit drug and a substandard drug?**
    - **A:** A counterfeit drug is made with a **deliberate intent to cheat/defraud** (e.g., faked branding, faked label). A substandard drug is an authorized product that simply fails to meet quality standards, often due to poor manufacturing or bad storage conditions.
- **Q: What are the typical inert ingredients found in falsified pills?**
    - **A:** Corn starch, potato starch, and chalk.
- **Q: What are the dangers of utilizing sub-standard antibiotics?**
    - **A:** They fail to cure the disease and deliver sub-therapeutic doses of the drug to the pathogen, directly promoting **antimicrobial resistance (AMR)**.
- **Q: Where are the zonal offices of CDSCO located?**
    - **A:** Mumbai, Ghaziabad, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, and Chennai.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **Confusing CDSCO and State jurisdiction:** Thinking that the Central government regulates all retail drug shops. Retail sale, local distribution, and licensing of standard drug shops are handled by State Drug Control Organizations, while CDSCO handles imports, new drug approvals, and high-risk licenses.
- **Vague definitions:** Defining "counterfeit" merely as "bad quality". You must use the keyword **"produced with an intention to cheat"** to get full marks.
- **Ignoring the link to AMR:** Forgetting to mention that substandard/falsified drugs are major drivers of drug-resistant infections.
