[[Genetics]]

The basic principles of genetics were established by **Gregor Mendel** (and Galton) towards the end of the 19th century. Mendel’s work involved crossing different races of the common pea (_Pisum sativum_), which led to the formulation of the fundamental laws of inheritance.

### **Mendel's Laws of Inheritance** ★ Important Exam Point

Mendel formulated three primary laws based on his observations of how traits are passed from parents to offspring.

#### **1. Law of Unit Characters**

- **Definition:** This law states that ==all inherited== characters are controlled by ==discrete units== (originally called "factors," now known as **genes**).
- **Mechanism:** These genes control the expression of specific traits ==during== the development of the organism and are passed intact from one generation to the next.

#### **2. Law of Dominance**

- **Definition:** Genes occur in pairs. In a cross between two organisms with contrasting traits, one factor in the pair may mask or suppress the expression of the other.
- **Dominant vs. Recessive:**
    - The trait that expresses itself in the first filial (F1) generation is called **dominant**.
    - The trait that remains hidden in the F1 generation but reappears in the F2 generation is called **recessive**.
- **Example:** When Mendel crossed tall and short pea plants, the F1 generation consisted entirely of tall plants, meaning "tallness" was dominant.

#### **3. Law of Segregation**

- **Definition:** Also known as the "Law of Purity of Gametes," it states that when germ cells (sperms and ova) are formed, the pair of factors for a trait separate (segregate) so that each gamete carries only **one** factor of the pair, never both.
- **Significance:** This ensures that the offspring receive one factor from each parent, restoring the pair upon fertilisation.

---

### **Mendelian Ratios (The 3:1 Rule)**

Mendel observed specific patterns when the F1 generation (all tall plants) was inbred:

- **Phenotypic Ratio:** The F2 generation showed a ratio of **3:1** (three-fourths tall and one-fourth short).
- **Genotypic Ratio:** Further analysis showed a **1:2:1** ratio (one-fourth pure tall, one-half mixed tall, and one-fourth pure short).

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points** ★

- **Mendelian Diseases:** These are unifactorial (single gene) diseases inherited strictly according to these laws, such as haemophilia or sickle cell anaemia.
- **Point Mutation:** If a mutation is confined to a single gene (Mendelian inheritance), it is referred to as a "point" mutation.
- **Genotype vs. Phenotype:** Genotype is the fixed genetic constitution (the "clay"), while phenotype is the outward expression (the "mould").

### **Frequently Asked Viva Questions**

- **Q: What plant did Mendel use for his experiments?**
    - **A:** The common pea plant, _Pisum sativum_.
- **Q: What is the phenotypic ratio in a Mendelian F2 generation?**
    - **A:** 3:1 (Dominant:Recessive).
- **Q: How does the Law of Segregation apply to human genetics?**
    - **A:** It explains how alleles separate during meiosis, ensuring gametes have the haploid number (23 chromosomes) before fertilisation restores the diploid number (46).
