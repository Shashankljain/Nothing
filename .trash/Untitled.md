● The scrotal skin rubs against the medial surface of the thighs

3. Irritating local applications

D. Fluctuation test

Finding: The swelling is fluctuant.

> [!question] Mentor: What is fluctuation? Fluctuation is the presence  
> of a transmitted impulse in two planes at right angles to each other.

What does fluctuation indicate?

It indicates that the swelling contains fluid.

How is the fluctuation test performed?

1. First, fix the swelling.

2. Hold and steady the swelling between the ring and middle fingers of  
both hands.

3. Place the thumbs and index fingers of both hands at the upper and  
lower poles of the swelling.

4. Apply pressure with one finger.

5. Feel for transmission of the impulse at the diagonally opposite  
finger.

6. Repeat the process in the perpendicular plane.

> [!question] Mentor: Why is the scrotum fixed during the test? The  
> scrotum is freely mobile. If it is not fixed, the entire swelling may  
> move during examination, giving a false impression of fluctuation.

> [!tip] Examination point Always fix the swelling before performing  
> fluctuation testing in scrotal swellings.

  

9. Transillumination test (Diaphanoscopy)

How is the test performed?

1. Pull the scrotal skin over the swelling with the left hand.

2. Take a flashlight in the right hand.

3. First place the flashlight against the normal, healthy side of the  
scrotum.

4. Observe the normal translucency of the skin.

5. Then place the flashlight against the lateral surface of the  
swelling.

6. Observe the appearance of the transmitted light.

Finding in this patient:

A red glow is seen throughout the swelling. This indicates that the  
swelling is translucent.

If the glow is doubtful or absent:

● Perform the test in a completely dark room.

● Alternatively, observe through rolled black paper.

> [!important] Mentor’s examination instruction A roll of black paper  
> may be provided in the examination room. Use it if asked to  
> demonstrate diaphanoscopy.

Why should normal skin translucency be checked first?

Translucency varies from person to person depending on:

1. Thickness of the skin.

2. Power of the flashlight.

3. Lighting conditions in the examination room.

Thin skin or a powerful flashlight may produce a false impression of  
positive transillumination.

Interpretation of positive transillumination

The student concludes:

● The swelling contains clear fluid.

● The wall of the sac is not thickened.

● The testicle cannot be separated from the swelling because the fluid  
surrounds it.

Positive transillumination is described as a characteristic diagnostic  
finding in hydrocele of the tunica vaginalis.

Limitations and errors of transillumination

A. False-negative transillumination

A hydrocele may show negative transillumination in the following  
situations:

1. Cloudy fluid within the sac.

2. Thickened sac wall due to infection.

3. Calcification of the sac wall.

4. Elephantiasis of the scrotum.

Technical errors causing false-negative results:

● Performing the test in a brightly illuminated room.

● Holding the flashlight from behind the swelling.

The testicle lies posteriorly and is attached to the tunica vaginalis  
from behind. It may block the light.

B. False-positive transillumination

May occur in:

1. Congenital hernia in infants

● Thin-walled intestine.

● Thin overlying skin.

2. Normal translucency of scrotal skin

● Particularly in fair-skinned individuals with thin skin.

This is why the normal side must be checked first.

Transillumination in epididymal cysts

> [!question] Mentor: What is the characteristic appearance of  
> epididymal cysts on transillumination?

● A single epididymal cyst shows bright transillumination.

● Multiple small cysts produce a mosaic appearance.

This mosaic appearance is called the Chinese lantern appearance.

Which scrotal swellings show transillumination?

1. All types of hydrocele.

2. Epididymal cyst.

3. Hydatid cyst of Morgagni.

4. Sometimes spermatocele.

Which other swellings in the body show bright transillumination?

1. Thin-walled hydrocele.

2. Epididymal cyst.

3. Cystic hygroma of the neck.

4. Ranula (cystic swelling in the floor of the mouth).

5. Thin-walled meningocele.

15. Definition of hydrocele

> [!question] Mentor: What is a hydrocele? A hydrocele is an abnormal  
> accumulation of fluid in a part of the processus vaginalis, usually  
> within the tunica vaginalis, where it is called a vaginal hydrocele.

Composition of hydrocele fluid

According to the discussion, hydrocele fluid contains:

1. Water.

2. Inorganic salts.

3. Proteins:

● Approximately 6% albumin.

● Fibrinogen.

4. Cholesterol crystals.

5. Sometimes tyrosine crystals.

Characteristics of hydrocele fluid

● Clear fluid.

● Pale yellow or amber in colour.

● Has a shimmering appearance due to cholesterol crystals.

● Coagulates when a drop of blood is added because of its high  
fibrinogen content.
