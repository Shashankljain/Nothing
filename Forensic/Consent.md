### **PART A: Consent in Medical Practice**

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

- **Definition:** Consent is an agreement, permission, or compliance given by a person to allow a medical examination or procedure.
- **Legal Basis:** It is governed by **Section 13 of the Indian Contract Act**, which defines it as two or more persons agreeing upon the same thing in the same sense.
- **Significance:** Any medical examination or treatment performed without valid consent is considered **Assault** and is punishable under **Section 131 BNS**.

#### **2. Relevant Law: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

|**Section**|**Provision**|**Medico-Legal Significance**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**25 BNS**|Consent for Non-fatal Acts|Acts not intended to cause death/grievous hurt done with consent (Age >18) are not offences.|
|**26 BNS**|Good Faith Benefit|Acts done in good faith for a patient's benefit (e.g., risky surgery) with consent.|
|**27 BNS**|Guardian Consent|For children **<12 years** or mentally ill persons, consent is given by the guardian.|
|**28 BNS**|**Invalid Consent**|Consent obtained through fear, misconception, or from persons mentally unsound/intoxicated is **not valid**.|
|**30 BNS**|**Doctrine of Emergency**|No consent is required for acts done in good faith for the benefit of a person in a life-threatening emergency.|

#### **3. Types of Consent**
![[Pasted image 20260802124618.png]]
1. **Implied Consent:** Deduced from the patient's gestures or body language (e.g., a patient sitting in the dental chair or offering an arm for a BP check).
2. **Expressed Consent:** Given in words, either **Verbal** or **Written**. Written consent is always legally superior.
3. **Informed Consent (★ Important Exam Point):** The doctor must explain the nature of the disease, the procedure, risks involved, and alternatives. The patient must have all information to make a choice.
4. **Surrogate/Proxy Consent:** Given by a parent or guardian when the patient is unable to give consent (child/unconscious).
5. **Blanket/Open Consent:** A single consent for all possible procedures during admission. This is **invalid in India**.

#### **4. Age Limits for Consent**

- **General Physical Examination:** Valid at **>12 years**.
- **Surgery/Major Procedures:** Mandatory at **>18 years**.
- **Note:** A child **<12 years** cannot give valid consent for any act that may cause harm, even if intended for their benefit.

#### **5. Special Scenarios**

- **Rape Victim:** Consent is **mandatory** for examination (Section 184 BNSS).
- **Rape Accused:** Consent is **not mandatory**; the police can request a medical examination using reasonable force if necessary (Section 52 BNSS).
- **Medico-legal Autopsy:** No consent is required from relatives as it is a legal requirement.

### **PART B: Consumer Protection Act (CPA/COPRA) 2019**

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

- **Introduction:** The Consumer Protection Act was originally passed in 1986 and was replaced by the **2019 Act**, which came into effect on **July 20, 2020**.
- **Objective:** 
	- To protect the interests of consumers 
	- to establish authorities for timely and effective settlement of consumer disputes.
- **Applicability:** Medical services provided for a fee fall under "services" as defined by the act.

#### **2. Redressal Commission Hierarchy (Pecuniary Jurisdiction)**

|**Level**|**Monetary Limit for Compensation**|
|:--|:--|
|**District Commission**|Up to **₹1 Crore**.|
|**State Commission**|**₹1 Crore to ₹10 Crore**.|
|**National Commission**|**>₹10 Crore**.|
![[image 9.jpg]]
#### **3. Key Features and Time Limits**

- **Filing a Case:** A consumer can file a complaint within **2 years** of the incident (Res Indicata).
- **Appeals:**
    - District $\rightarrow$ State: Within **45 days**.
    - State $\rightarrow$ National: Within **30 days**.
    - National $\rightarrow$ **Supreme Court**: Final level of appeal.
- **Modern Provisions:** The 2019 Act introduced **E-filing** of complaints and provisions against **misleading advertisements**.
