### **Methyl Alcohol (Methanol) Poisoning**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Introduction**  
**Methyl Alcohol (Methanol)**, also known as **Wood Alcohol, Wood Naphtha, or Wood Spirits**, is a colorless, volatile liquid with a burning taste and an odor similar to ethyl alcohol. It is widely used as an **==industrial solvent==** and is a frequent constituent of **==antifreeze==**, duplicating fluids, and cleaning materials.

**Toxicokinetics (Metabolism)**

- **Absorption:** It is rapidly absorbed through the gastrointestinal tract (GIT), lungs (inhalation), and skin.
- **Pathophysiology:** Methanol is a **==cumulative poison==** because its oxidation occurs at only 15% the rate of ethyl alcohol.
**The Toxicological Pathway (The Metabolic Cascade)**

```
                      [METHYL ALCOHOL (METHANOL)] 
                                  │
                                  ▼ (Slow Zero-Order Rate: 8.5–20 mg/dL/h) [6]
                      [Alcohol Dehydrogenase (ADH)] ◄─── blocked by Ethanol / Fomepizole [13, 14]
                                  │
                                  ▼ (Highly Toxic Intermediate)
                       [FORMALDEHYDE (HCHO)]
                                  │
                                  ▼
                     [Aldehyde Dehydrogenase (ALDH)]
                                  │
                                  ▼ (Systemic Accumulator)
                        [FORMIC ACID (HCOOH)] 
                                  │
         ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                 ▼
 [Metabolic Acidosis]                             [Ocular Neurotoxicity]
• Profound depletion of HCO3-                 • Binds to Cytochrome c Oxidase 
• High Anion-Gap Acidosis [7]                   • Halts axonal transport [10]
         │                                    • Optic neuritis & Blindness [10]
         └────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                  ▼
                     [Metabolized via Folate Pathway] ◄─── enhanced by Folate Therapy [13]
                                  │
                                  ▼
                         [CO2 + H2O Excretion]
```

1. **Zero-Order Elimination:** Methanol undergoes zero-order elimination at a slow rate of **8.5 to 20 mg/dL/h** when metabolizing without therapeutic competition6.
2. **Formaldehyde Generation:** In the liver, the enzyme **alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH)** oxidizes methanol into **formaldehyde (HCHO)**610. Formaldehyde is highly reactive but is quickly converted by **aldehyde dehydrogenase** into formic acid610.
3. **Formic Acid Accumulation:** **Formic acid (HCOOH)** accumulates systemically610. Formate directly inhibits mitochondrial **cytochrome c oxidase** (Complex IV of the electron transport chain), halting ATP production at the cellular level and inducing **cellular hypoxia**710.
4. **Acidosis:** Unbound formic acid triggers a profound **High Anion-Gap Metabolic Acidosis** by depleting serum bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$)713.

**Fatal Dose and Period**

- **Fatal Dose:** **60–140 mL** (blood levels >150 mg/dL are usually fatal).
- **Fatal Period:** **24–36 hours**.

---

#### **2. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Hooch Tragedies:** Most cases are **accidental**, resulting from the consumption of adulterated illicit liquor where methanol is used as a cheap substitute for ethanol.
- **Suicide/Homicide:** These are rare but can occur.
- **Occupational Hazard:** Risk exists for workers in industries using methanol as a solvent.

★ **Important Exam Point: Mandatory Reporting**  
Under **Section 33 BNSS (2023)**, it is a doctor's duty to inform the police of any suspected poisoning case attended in the hospital.

#### **4. Clinical Features (Symptoms and Signs)**

Symptoms typically appear after a latent period of **12–24 hours**.

**I. Gastrointestinal (GIT)**

- Nausea, vomiting, and severe abdominal cramps.
- A *==spirit-like odor==* may be perceived in the breath.
- ★ **Pancreatitis:** Acute hemorrhagic or edematous pancreatitis is a common, highly painful complication of methanol ingestion.
- 
**II. Ocular (Hallmark Feature) ★**

- **"Snowfield Vision"**: Misty or blurred vision, as if looking through a snowstorm. -Retina involvement.
- 'Halo vision' (bright circles around lights).
- **Signs:** Pupils are fixed and **dilated (mydriasis)**; optic neuritis and atrophy can lead to permanent blindness.

**III. Central Nervous System (CNS)**

- Headache, dizziness, vertigo, restlessness, and delirium.
- Severe cases lead to coma and convulsions.

**IV. Systemic/Renal**

- **High Anion-Gap Metabolic Acidosis:** Characterized by rapid, deep breathing ***==(Kussmaul breathing).==***
- Scanty urine output.

5.**Clinical Management & Antidote Protocol** 🏥

The main goal of treatment is to halt the metabolic oxidation of methanol while correcting the life-threatening metabolic acidosis:

```
                        [METHANOL EMERGENCY PROTOCOL]
                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                            ▼                            ▼
  [ADH Blockade]             [Acidosis Correction]       [Folate Acceleration] Administer Fomepizole   Administer IV Bicarbonate   Administer Calcium Folinate
  (Loading: 15 mg/kg)    (Keep systemic pH >7.2).      (50–75 mg IV q6h)
• Or **Ethanol** (IV/Oral)                           • Stimulates conversion of
  (Target: 100–150 mg/dL)                             formic acid to CO2 + H2O.
         │                            │                            │
         └────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                                      ▼
                        [★ IMMEDIATE HEMODIALYSIS]
                     (The gold-standard clearance therapy)
```

**1. Decontamination**

- ★ **Gastric Lavage:** Performed using a **5% Sodium Bicarbonate solution**, leaving approximately 500 mL of the fluid within the stomach cavity to assist in systemic alkalization. It is most useful if initiated within **1 to 2 hours of ingestion**.

**2. Systemic Alkalization**

- ★ **IV Sodium Bicarbonate:** Crucial to treat the severe metabolic acidosis. Administer IV bicarbonate to keep the arterial pH above 7.2.
    - _Mechanism:_ Correcting the systemic pH keeps formic acid in its ionized (formate) state, **preventing it from crossing the blood-brain barrier** or entering ocular tissues, thereby protecting the eyes and brain.

**3. Competitive ADH Enzyme Blockade (The Specific Antidotes)**

To prevent ADH from converting methanol into its toxic metabolites, we must competitively block the enzyme:

- **Option A: Fomepizole (4-Methylpyrazole) - The Preferred Antidote** (15,10,15)
    - _Mechanism:_ A highly specific, potent competitive inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH).
    - _Dosing:_ Loading dose of **15 mg/kg IV over 30 minutes**, followed by **10 mg/kg IV every 12 hours** for 4 doses, and then **15 mg/kg IV every 12 hours** until methanol levels fall below 20 mg/dL.
- **Option B: Ethanol (Ethyl Alcohol) - The Competitive Alternative**
    - _Mechanism:_ ADH has a **10-to-20 times higher affinity for ethanol** than methanol. Introducing ethanol completely monopolizes the ADH enzyme, allowing parent methanol to be safely excreted unchanged via the lungs and kidneys.
    - _Dosing:_
        - _Oral:_ Loading dose of **0.8–1.0 mL/kg of 95% ethanol** in 200 mL of orange juice.
        - _Intravenous:_ Loading dose of **7.6–10.0 mL/kg of 10% ethanol** in D5W over 30 minutes.
        - _Maintenance:_ IV maintenance dose of **1.4 mL/kg/h** to maintain a target serum ethanol concentration of **100 to 150 mg/dL**.

**4. Folate Acceleration Therapy**

- ★ **Calcium Folinate (Leucovorin):** Administered at **50 to 75 mg IV every 6 hours**.
    - _Mechanism:_ Folate acts as a metabolic ==co==-==factor== that directly **accelerates the oxidation of toxic formic acid** into non-toxic carbon dioxide and water.
    - _Adjuncts:_ Thiamine and pyridoxine may be added as ancillary co-factors.

**5. Extracorporeal Clearance**

- ★ **Immediate Hemodialysis:** Highly indicated and extremely effective. Methanol and formic acid are small, water-soluble, unbound molecules with low volumes of distribution, making them **highly dialyzable**. Dialysis is started immediately if the patient presents with visual impairment, severe acidosis (pH < 7.1), or a serum methanol level > 50 mg/dL.


#### **6. Autopsy Findings (Internal & External)**

**I. Dissection Protocol: Cranium Opened FIRST**

- ★ **The Cranium Opened FIRST Rule:** In all suspected cases of volatile chemical or alcohol deaths, the autopsy surgeon **must open the cranial cavity first**.
    - _Forensic Rationale:_ This allows the volatile, ==spirit==-like odor of methanol to be detected and documented from the brain tissue before it is masked by the putrefactive gases released when opening the abdominal or thoracic cavities.

---

**II. External Findings**

- **Signs of Asphyxia:** Marked cyanosis of the lips, fingertips, and ear lobules.
- ★ **==Purple Postmortem Lividity==:** Postmortem staining (hypostasis) is characteristically **deep purple or bluish-violet** in color and highly prominent over dependent parts [683, Image 24, `input_file_23.png`].
- **Froth:** Fine white or ==pinkish==-white frothy discharge may be present about the mouth and nostrils.
- **The Denaturant Color:** If the victim consumed denatured spirit containing **Pyridine**, the skin and tissues may show a **distinct purple discoloration**.

---

**III. Internal Findings**

- ★ **The Brain (Pathognomonic Target):**
    - The brain is severely edematous with flattened gyri and narrowed sulci.
    - ★ **Bilateral Putaminal Necrosis:** Coronal sections of the brain demonstrate highly characteristic **bilateral, symmetrical ischemic and hemorrhagic necrosis of the putamen (basal ganglia)** and subcortical white matter.
- **Gastrointestinal Tract:** The stomach and duodenum show intense acute gastritis with heavily congested, swollen mucosa, covered in thick mucus, and scattered with **small petechial hemorrhages (submucosal bleeding)**.
- **Lungs:** Voluminous, heavy, intensely congested, and filled with frothy, edematous fluid (**pulmonary edema**).
- **Liver & Kidneys:** Swollen and congested, with histopathological evidence of **necrobiosis and fatty degeneration in the liver**, and **proximal tubular degeneration** in the kidneys.
- **Urinary Bladder:** Intensely congested mucosal lining.
- **Blood:** Characteristically **dark, fluid, and completely uncoagulated**.
