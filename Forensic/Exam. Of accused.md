[[Sexual offence]]
**Definition** The medico-legal examination of an accused involves a thorough physical and genital evaluation by a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) to collect biological evidence and document injuries that may have occurred during the alleged act.

**Objectives**

- To identify the accused.
- To find evidence of recent sexual activity.
- To document signs of a struggle (injuries).
- To collect trace evidence (hair, blood, vaginal cells).
- To assess the physical capacity (potency) of the accused to perform the act.

---

**Relevant Law (2023)**

★ **Important Exam Point:** The procedural law has shifted from the CrPC to the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**.

- **Section 52 BNSS (formerly Sec 53A CrPC):** This section specifically provides for the medical examination of a person accused of rape by a medical practitioner at the request of a police officer.
- **Powers of Police:** The request must be made by a police officer **not below the rank of Sub-Inspector**.
- **Reasonable Force:** Under this section, the doctor is legally permitted to use **"reasonable force"** to conduct the examination if the accused resists.
- **Consent:** Unlike the victim, the **consent of the accused is not required** for a medico-legal examination requested under Section 52 BNSS.

---

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Corroborative Evidence:** Finding the victim's DNA or vaginal cells on the accused's penis provides strong scientific links.
- **Defense of Potency:** Accused persons often falsely claim to be impotent as a defense. The doctor must evaluate this claim.
- **Reconstructing the Struggle:** Defensive injuries on the accused (like bite marks or scratches) help prove the victim resisted.

---

**Procedure of Examination**

**1. Prerequisites**

- **Authorization:** Ensure a written requisition is received from a police officer (Sub-Inspector or above).
- **Identification:** Record the name, age, address, and two permanent identification marks (moles/scars).

**2. History**

- History of the alleged incident.
- Recent sexual history (to account for any findings).
- History of any illness affecting **potency** (diabetes, spinal injuries, or drug use).

**3. General Physical Examination**

- **Behavior:** Note if he is excited, calm, or intoxicated.
- **Clothes:** Examine for stains (blood, semen, mud) or tears. These must be preserved.
- **Injuries:** Look for scratches, bite marks, or bruises sustained during a struggle.

**4. Genital Examination**
    - Pubic hair
    - Development of genital organs
- **Smegma:** Check the corona glandis (under the foreskin).
    - ★ **Important Exam Point:** The presence of a thick layer of smegma usually indicates that the person has **not had sexual intercourse in the last 24 hours**, as the friction of sex normally rubs it off.
- **Penis and Scrotum:** Look for fresh injuries, tears in the frenulum, or signs of STDs.

**5. Detection of Vaginal Cells (The Lugol's Iodine Test)**

- Vaginal epithelial cells are rich in glycogen.
- **Procedure:** A swab is taken from the glans penis and exposed to **Lugol’s iodine** or Papanicolaou’s stain.
- **Finding:** If vaginal cells are present, the swab turns **brown** due to the reaction with glycogen.
- **Timeline:** This test can be effective for up to **4 days** after the alleged rape.

---

**Specimen Collection (The SAFE Kit)**

All evidentiary materials must be collected using the **Sexual Assault Forensic Evidence (SAFE) Kit**.

|Specimen|Container/Method|Purpose|
|---|---|---|
|**Clothes**|Paper bags (after air drying)|Blood, semen, or victim’s hair.|
|**Penile Swabs**|Sterile swabs/Glass slides|To detect vaginal cells/DNA.|
|**Pubic Hair**|Plucking (with roots)|Comparison with victim's hair.|
|**Nail Scrapings**|Nail pick/Envelopes|To find the victim's skin/blood from struggle.|
|**Blood/Saliva**|EDTA Vials/Cards|For DNA profiling of the accused.|
