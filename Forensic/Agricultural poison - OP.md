### **Part I: Classification of Poisons**

Poisons are generally classified based on their **mode of action** on the human body.

|**Class**|**Description**|**Examples**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1. Corrosives**|Cause chemical destruction and ulceration of tissues on contact.|**Acids:** Sulphuric, Nitric. **Alkalis:** Caustic soda.|
|**2. Irritants**|Cause inflammation, irritation, or ulceration; symptoms appear slowly.|**Inorganic:** Arsenic, Lead, Mercury, Phosphorus. **Organic:** Castor, Snake venom, Scorpions. **Mechanical:** Powdered glass, needles.|
|**3. Neurotics**|Act primarily on the Central Nervous System (CNS).|**Cerebral:** Alcohol (Inebriant), Opium (Somniferous), Dhatura (Deliriant). **Spinal:** Nux vomica (Strychnine). **Peripheral:** Conium.|
|**4. Cardiac**|Act directly on the heart or its nerve supply.|Digitalis, Oleander, Aconite, Nicotine.|
|**5. Asphyxiants**|Deprive the body of oxygen.|Carbon monoxide (CO), Cyanide (HCN).|
|**6. Miscellaneous**|Includes various agrochemicals and drugs of dependence.|**Organophosphates**, Kerosene, Paracetamol.|

# 🧪🌾 **ORGANOPHOSPHATE INSECTICIDE POISONING**

---

### **Basic Concepts** 📚

#### **Definition**

**Organophosphate compounds (OPCs)** are organic esters of phosphoric acid widely utilized as agricultural pesticides, insecticides, and chemical warfare nerve agents.

```
                [ORGANOPHOSPHATE INSECTICIDE]
                             │
                             ▼ (Covalent Binding)
              [Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)]
                             │
                             ▼ (Enzyme Inactivation)
           [Excess Accumulation of Acetylcholine]
                             │
                             ▼ (Persistent Synaptic Overstimulation)
         [Cholinergic Autonomic Storm (SLUDGE/DUMBBELS)]
```

#### **Introduction**

- **The Suicidal Poison of Choice:** Organophosphates are classified as **Ideal Suicidal Poisons** in forensic toxicology due to their cheap cost, wide household/agricultural availability, and high toxicity [Image 9].
- **Exposure Routes:** Ingestion (suicidal), dermal contact (occupational during spraying), and inhalation of pesticide fumes.

#### **Principles of Action (Toxicodynamics)**

- **Enzyme Inactivation:** OPCs bind covalently to the active esteratic site of the enzyme **acetylcholinesterase (AChE)**, rendering it inactive.
- **Acetylcholine Accumulation:** The inactivation of AChE prevents the breakdown of the neurotransmitter **acetylcholine (ACh)**.
- **Synaptic Flood:** This leads to a massive, toxic accumulation of ACh at cholinergic synapses (muscarinic, nicotinic receptors, and the CNS), resulting in persistent depolarization and hyper-stimulation of effector organs.
- **Aging of the Enzyme:** Over time, the phosphorylated AChE complex loses an alkyl group (called "aging"). Once aged, the bond becomes completely irreversible and cannot be reactivated by oximes.

---

### **Clinical Manifestations (Signs & Symptoms)** 🩺

The clinical features are dominated by the **Cholinergic Toxidrome** and can be systematically classified into **Muscarinic**, **Nicotinic**, and **CNS** effects:

#### **★ The Muscarinic "DUMBBELS" Mnemonic** 🧠

- **D** – **D**iarrhea / Defecation
- **U** – **U**rination / Urinary incontinence
- **M** – **M**iosis (**Pinpoint constricted pupils**)
- **B** – **B**ronchospasm & **B**ronchorrhea (excessive watery lung secretions)
- **B** – **B**radycardia (slow heart rate)
- **E** – **E**mesis (vomiting)
- **L** – **L**acrimation (watering of eyes)
- **S** – **S**alivation (excessive drooling)

#### **Nicotinic Effects (Neuromuscular Junction)**

- Muscle twitching, fine **fasciculations**, muscle cramps, generalized weakness, and eventually **flaccid paralysis** of skeletal and respiratory muscles (diaphragm/intercostals).

#### **CNS Effects**

- Headache, restlessness, confusion, convulsions, respiratory depression, and terminal coma.

#### **Pathognomonic Odors** 👃

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Kerosene-like Odor:** Commercial organophosphates are formulated in hydrocarbon solvents, giving the patient's breath, vomitus, and gastric contents a highly characteristic **kerosene-like smell** [366, 388, Image 11]. Raw, pure compounds (like parathion or malathion) may present with a **garlic-like smell**.

---

### **Laboratory Investigations & Diagnosis** 🔬

1. **Clinical Triad:** Diagnosed clinically by the presence of **pinpoint pupils (miosis)**, **kerosene-like breath odor**, and **muscle fasciculations**.
2. **Cholinesterase Assays (Gold Standard):**
    - **Plasma Cholinesterase (Butyrylcholinesterase):** Rapidly declines but is less specific.
    - **Red Blood Cell Acetylcholinesterase (True AChE):** Highly specific; a reduction below 30% of baseline levels confirms severe poisoning.
3. **Chemical Screening:**
    - ★ **Furfuraldehyde Test:** Performed as a bedside screen on gastric aspirates to detect the presence of carbamates or organophosphates.
    - Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC) and GC-MS are used to identify and quantify the specific compound.

---

### **Management & Treatment Protocol** 🏥

#### **I. Immediate Resuscitation & Decontamination**

1. **Airway & Breathing (Highest Priority):** Secure a patent airway. Continuous suctioning of excessive bronchorrhea is vital before initiating mechanical ventilation.
2. **Dermal Decontamination:** Strip all clothes immediately. Wash the skin thoroughly with soap and water to prevent further dermal absorption.
3. **Gastric Lavage:** Perform cautious gastric lavage within **1 hour of ingestion** using a **5% Sodium Bicarbonate**solution or tap water to clear unabsorbed poison [346, 348, Image 12].
4. **Activated Charcoal:** Administer **Activated Charcoal (1 g/kg)** orally to adsorb remaining gut toxins.

#### **II. Specific Antidote Therapy**

```
                         [SPECIFIC OPC ANTIDOTES]
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                       ▼
 [ATROPINE (Muscarinic Blocker)]                         [OXIMES (Reactivators)]
• Competitively blocks ACh at receptors.                 • Breaks covalent phosphorus bond.
• Dilates pupils, dries secretions.                      • Restores active AChE enzymes.
• Dose: 2-5 mg IV repeating for Atropinisation.          • Dose: Pralidoxime (PAM) 1-2 g IV.
```

1. ★ **Atropine (Pharmacological Antagonist):** [Image 13]
    - _Mechanism:_ Competitively blocks acetylcholine at **muscarinic receptors** to dry life-threatening bronchial secretions. It has no effect on nicotinic neuromuscular paralysis.
    - _Dose:_ Give **2 to 5 mg IV bolus** initially, repeating every 5–10 minutes until **Atropinisation** is achieved (indicated by: clearing of lung crepitations, dry skin, dry tongue, heart rate >80 bpm, and mid-dilated pupils). Once achieved, maintain on a continuous infusion.
2. ★ **Oximes (Cholinesterase Reactivators - e.g., Pralidoxime / PAM):**
    - _Mechanism:_ Attaches to the alkyl phosphate radical of the inhibitor, breaking the covalent phosphorus bond with AChE and reactivating the enzyme before "aging" occurs.
    - _Dose:_ **1 to 2 g slow IV** over 10–15 minutes, followed by a continuous infusion of 500 mg/hour.

#### **★ Critical Contraindications** ⚠️

- ★ **No Dialysis:** Hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis are **NOT useful** in organophosphate poisoning because of the toxin's large volume of distribution.
- ★ **No Oily/Fatty Demulcents:** Avoid castor oil or milk; since pesticides are lipid-soluble, fats will accelerate their absorption.

### **Postmortem & Autopsy Findings** 🔍🩺

#### **I. External Findings**

- **Odor:** Prominent **kerosene-like or garlic-like odor** emanating from the mouth, nostrils, and internally upon opening the body [366, 388, Image 11].
- ★ **Tenacious Froth:** Copious, fine, white **leathery and tenacious mucoid froth** at the mouth and nostrils (due to intense bronchorrhea and hypersecretion) [Image 7, Image 12].
- **Miosis:** Constricted pinpoint pupils.
- **Cyanosis:** Bluish discoloration of the lips, fingertips, and face.

#### **II. Internal Findings**

- ★ **The Cranium Opened FIRST:** The skull must be opened first to detect and document the volatile kerosene/garlic toxic odor from the brain before it is masked by abdominal odors [Image 1].
- **Stomach:** Swollen, heavily congested mucosa with a strong kerosene odor.
    - _Autopsy Dissection Rule:_ Isolated using the **Double Ligature Method** and incised strictly along the **greater curvature** to preserve lesser curvature pathology [46, Image 2].
- **Lungs:** Extremely voluminous, heavy, and **edematous** (foamy fluid oozes on sectioning).
- **Other Organs:** Passive venous congestion of all parenchymatous organs (liver, spleen, kidneys).

#### **III. Viscera Preservation**

- Preserve routine viscera in **Saturated solution of common salt**.
- ★ **Important Exam Point (PAPPA Spirit Contraindication):** Under the **PAPPA** rule, do **NOT** use Rectified Spirit or Absolute Alcohol because volatile organic solvents (kerosene/petroleum base) are highly soluble in alcohol, which ruins the FSL analysis.

### **Differentiating Organophosphates from Carbamates**

★ **Important Exam Point:** Differentiating these two common agricultural cholinesterase inhibitors is a frequent university question.

|**Feature**|**Organophosphate Poisoning**|**Carbamate Poisoning**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Enzyme Binding**|Covalent, highly stable, and undergoes **aging**.|Reversible, transient carbamylation (no aging).|
|**Use of Oximes (PAM)**|**Highly indicated** if given early.|**Contraindicated** (oximes may worsen toxicity).|
|**Duration of Action**|Prolonged (days to weeks).|Short-lived (hours).|

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: Why is the cranium opened first during an autopsy in suspected poisoning cases?**  
**Answer:** The cranium must be opened first to detect and document unique **volatile toxic odors** (like the kerosene-like or garlic-like smell of OPC) from the brain tissue before they are masked by the putrefactive odors of the abdomen [Image 1].

**Q2: Why are oximes ineffective once "aging" of the enzyme occurs?**  
**Answer:** Aging involves the loss of an alkyl group from the phosphorylated AChE complex. This strengthens the covalent bond, making it extremely stable and completely resistant to nucleophilic attack by reactivators like Pralidoxime.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

- **Writing "PAM is used for all cholinergic poisons":** Students often forget that oximes are **contraindicated in carbamate poisoning** because they can worsen the carbamate-AChE complex blockade.
- **Using Spirit to Preserve Viscera:** Organic solvents in OPC are highly soluble in alcohol, which ruins the quantitative analysis. Saturated common salt must be used.
