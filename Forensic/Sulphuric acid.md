# 🧪💀 **SULPHURIC ACID POISONING: UNIVERSITY EXAMINATION GUIDE** 💀🧪

---

### **Basic Concepts** 📘

#### **Definition**

**Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\))**, commercially known as **"Oil of Vitriol"**, is a highly powerful, inorganic/mineral corrosive acid. It acts locally by rapidly destroying tissues on contact through dehydration and protein coagulation.

#### **Introduction & Physical Properties**

- **Physical Properties:** Concentrated Sulphuric Acid is a **colorless, odorless, oily, non-fuming liquid** with a highly burning taste.

---

### **Topic-Specific Explanation: Pathophysiology & Mechanisms** 🔬

```
                       [SULPHURIC ACID CONTACT]
                                  │
         ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                 ▼
[Rapid Extraction of Water]                       [Protein Coagulation]
         │                                                 │
         ▼                                                 ▼
 [Carbonization / Charring]                       [Hard, Dry Eschar]
         │                                                 │
         └────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                  ▼
                     [Coagulative Necrosis]
```

1. **Coagulative Necrosis:** On tissue contact, sulphuric acid precipitates and coagulates cellular proteins. This forms a **hard, dry eschar**, which limits further deep lateral spread of the acid.
2. **Carbonization (Dehydration):** Due to its intense affinity for water, \(H_2SO_4\) chars tissues, converting organic molecules into carbon, which turns the stomach and surrounding tissues **black**.

---

### **Clinical Features** 🩺

- **Oropharyngeal:** Intense, immediate burning pain in the mouth, throat, and epigastrium.
- **Trickle Marks:** Downward-running corridors of blackened, excoriated skin at the angles of the mouth, cheeks, chin, and chest.
- **The Teeth:** **Chalky white and brittle** (due to the calcium-stripping action of the acid).
- **Vomiting:** Persistent vomiting of strongly acidic, **blackish, charred fluid containing mucous shreds and altered blood** ("coffee-ground" appearance).
- **Respiratory Distress:** Glottic edema, laryngeal spasm, hoarseness, dysphagia, dysphonia, and dyspnea.
- **Abdominal Signs:** Extreme tenderness, guarding, and ==board-like rigidity== due to stomach perforation and subsequent ==chemical peritonitis==.
- **Systemic Shock:** A rapid, feeble pulse, cold clammy skin, and profound circulatory collapse.

### **Fatal Dose & Fatal Period** ⏱️

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Fatal Dose:** **5 to 10 mL** of concentrated acid.
- ★ **Important Exam Point: Fatal Period:** **12 to 18 hours**.

### **Clinical Management & Treatment** 🏥

#### **I. Immediate Emergency Protocol**

```
                       [ACUTE H2SO4 INGESTION]
                                  │
                                  ▼
                  [Assess Airway, Vitals, & Shock]
                                  │
         ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                 ▼
 [Airway Obstruction]                               [Airway Stable]
  • Perform immediate                             • Avoid Lavage/Emetics!
    Tracheostomy                                  • Cautious dilution
                                                    (Milk or Water)
                                                  • Give Demulcents
                                                  • Morphine for Pain
                                                  • IV Fluids & Steroids
```

1. **Airway Management:** If glottic edema causes stridor or asphyxia, perform an immediate **Tracheostomy**.
2. **Decontamination (Strict Contraindications):**
    - ★ **Important Exam Point: Avoid Gastric Lavage and Emetics:** Performing lavage or inducing vomiting is **absolutely contraindicated** due to the high risk of esophageal/gastric perforation [269, 331, Image 24].
    - ★ **Important Exam Point: Avoid Carbonates/Bicarbonates:** Never administer sodium bicarbonate or other carbonate neutralizing agents. Their chemical reaction releases massive amounts of Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\)) gas, causing sudden gastric distension and **==catastrophic stomach rupture==**.
3. **Dilution & Demulcents:**
    - Dilute the acid immediately by giving a glass of **milk or water** to drink.
    - Administer **aluminum hydroxide gel** (4 tablespoonfuls) to coat mucosal surfaces.
    - Give **demulcents** such as olive oil, milk, egg white, starch water, or butter to protect raw areas.
4. **Systemic Support:**
    - Administer **==Morphine==** to alleviate agonizing pain and combat neurogenic shock.
    - Start **systemic corticosteroids (Prednisolone 60 mg/day)** in divided doses to minimize the chronic complication of ==esophageal stricture==.
    - Maintain the patient **Nothing by Mouth (NPO)**, administering all nutrients and fluids intravenously for at least one week.
    - Wash any external skin burns with copious amounts of water for **at least 20 minutes**.

### **Autopsy & Postmortem Findings** 🔍🩺

#### **I. External Findings**

- Corrosive ==burns==, skin ==excoriation==, and **==blackened trickle marks==** running down the cheeks, chin, and chest.
- The lips are swollen and blackened, and the teeth appear **chalky white and brittle**.

#### **II. Internal Findings**

- **Blackish Charring of the Stomach:** The gastric mucosa is intensely blackened and extensively destroyed, presenting a characteristic **"==peppery feel"==** on palpation.
- **Toxic Swelling:** Spleen, liver, and kidneys are heavily congested and may show toxic swelling.
- ★ **Important Exam Point: ==The Magenstrasse Effect==:** Ingested liquid acid flows rapidly along the lesser curvature of the stomach (forming a path known as _Magenstrasse_) and pools in the pyloric antrum due to acid-induced pylorospasm. This leads to localized, severe corrosion and **stomach perforation** at the pyloric region.
- ★ **Incision Protocol:** To preserve and demonstrate this pathognomonic _Magenstrasse_ pathology, the autopsy surgeon must apply double ligatures at the cardiac and pyloric ends, excise the stomach, and **open it strictly along the greater curvature** [31, 333, Image 24].

#### **III. Viscera Preservation**

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Preservative Selection:** Harvested viscera and skin in sulphuric acid deaths must be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit** [333, Image 22]. (Do _not_ use the general saturated salt solution, as spirit is specifically preferred for mineral acid toxicities).

### **Relevant Law (BNS, BNSS, & BSA, 2023 Update)** ⚖️

- ★ **Section 124(1) BNS (formerly Sec 326-A IPC) - Grievous Hurt by Acid:** Voluntarily causing permanent or partial damage, deformity, burns, or disfigurement by throwing acid (Vitriolage). Punishable by **10 years to Life Imprisonment** and a fine paid directly to the victim.
- ★ **Section 124(2) BNS (formerly Sec 326-B IPC) - Attempted Acid Attack:** Voluntarily throwing or attempting to throw acid on a person. Punishable by **5 to 7 years imprisonment** and a fine.
- **Section 200 BNS - Non-treatment of Victim:** Declares the failure or refusal of any hospital or doctor to treat an acid attack victim immediately as a punishable offense [Image 24].
- **Section 397 BNSS - Free Medical Treatment:** Lays down the statutory mandate that all acid attack victims must receive **immediate, free medical treatment** at any government or private hospital [Image 24].
- **Section 33 BNSS (formerly Sec 39 CrPC) - Duty of Doctor:** Mandates that every Registered Medical Practitioner must immediately notify the police of any suspected homicidal or suspicious poisoning case [251, Image 22].

#### **2 Marks: Define Magenstrasse and its forensic importance.**

Magenstrasse ("street of the stomach") refers to the physiological path along the lesser curvature of the stomach. Ingested liquid corrosive acids slide along this curvature and pool at the pylorus, causing severe localized necrosis and perforation. To preserve this characteristic pathology during autopsy, the stomach must be ligate-isolated and opened along the **greater curvature** [31, 333, Image 24].

#### **5 Marks: Describe the postmortem findings in a fatal case of Sulphuric Acid poisoning.**

1. **External:** Excoriation, burns, and **blackened trickle marks** running down the mouth, chin, and chest. Lips are swollen/blackened, and the teeth are **chalky white and brittle**.
2. **Internal Gastrointestinal:** Blackish charring and carbonization of the stomach wall, giving a **"peppery feel"** on touch. Extensive mucosal desquamation and perforation at the pyloric region are common due to **Magenstrasse pooling**.
3. **Internal Viscera:** Lungs are heavily congested with subpleural ecchymoses; liver and kidneys show toxic swelling and congestion.
4. **Preservation:** Viscera must be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit** [333, Image 22].
