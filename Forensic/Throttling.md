[[Asphyxia]]
For an MBBS student, the study of **Violent Asphyxial Deaths** is high-yield for university examinations, particularly the forensic differentiation between hanging and various types of strangulation like throttling.

---

### **1. Enumeration of Violent Asphyxial Deaths**

Violent or **Mechanical Asphyxia** is caused by physical interference with the passage of air into the respiratory tract. These are classified based on the site and mechanism of obstruction:

|**Category**|**Specific Types**|
|:--|:--|
|**Obstruction of Orifices**|**Smothering** (closing nose and mouth).|
|**Obstruction of Air Passages**|**Hanging**, **Strangulation** (Ligature or Manual/Throttling).|
|**Internal Obstruction**|**Choking** (foreign body in airway), **Gagging** (cloth/paper in mouth).|
|**Occlusion by Fluid**|**Drowning**.|
|**Pressure on Chest**|**Traumatic Asphyxia** (e.g., stampedes, building collapse), **Burking** (combination of smothering and traumatic asphyxia).|
|**Positional Asphyxia**|Death due to abnormal body position (e.g., **Jack-knife position**).|

---

### **2. Throttling (Manual Strangulation)**

**Definition** **Throttling** is a form of violent asphyxia produced by the compression of the neck by human hands.

★ **Important Exam Point:** Throttling is **almost always homicidal**. Self-throttling is impossible because once unconsciousness sets in, the hands relax and the grip is released.

#### **Postmortem Findings in Throttling**

**External Findings**

1. **Face:** Typically congested, swollen, and deeply cyanosed.
2. **Tardieu’s Spots:** Pin-point petechial hemorrhages are common in the conjunctiva, face, and eyelids.
3. **Neck Marks:**
    - **Bruises (Six-penny bruises):** Round or discoid bruises (1.5–2 cm) produced by the pressure of fingertips.
    - **Abrasions (Fingernail marks):** Crescentic or semilunar marks caused by the nails of the assailant.
    - ★ **Note:** Bruises from the **thumb tips** are usually more prominent than other fingers.
4. **Tongue:** Often swollen, dark, and may protrude or show bite marks.
5. **Orifices:** Bloodstained frothy fluid and mucus may escape from the mouth and nostrils; bleeding from ears or nose may occur.

**Internal Findings**

1. **Extravasation of Blood:** Extensive bleeding in the subcutaneous tissues and neck muscles (platysma/sternomastoid) is the **most significant internal sign**.
2. **Larynx and Pharynx:** Mucous membranes show hyperemia and petechial hemorrhages.
3. **Skeletal Fractures:**
    - **Hyoid Bone:** **Inward compression fracture** (adduction fracture) is a highly diagnostic finding.
    - **Thyroid Cartilage:** Fracture of the superior horns is common.
    - **Cricoid Cartilage:** Rare, but if present, it is pathognomonic of throttling.
4. **Lungs:** Congested and edematous with subpleural petechiae.

---

### **3. Note on Hyoid Bone Fractures**

The hyoid bone is a horseshoe-shaped bone in the neck that is frequently fractured in asphyxial deaths involving neck compression.

- **Incidence:** Fractures occur in 50–70% of cases, typically in individuals **above 40 years of age** because the bone becomes calcified and brittle with age.
- **Proof of Life:** For a fracture to be considered **antemortem**, there must be evidence of a vital reaction, such as hemorrhage or contusion in the surrounding soft tissues.

#### **Classification of Hyoid Fractures**

|**Type of Fracture**|**Mechanism**|**Common Scenario**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Adduction Fracture**|**Inward compression**; the fingers squeeze the neck and push the greater cornua inward.|**Throttling**.|
|**Abduction Fracture**|**Anteroposterior compression**; the bone is driven backward, causing the greater cornua to diverge/tug outward.|**Hanging**.|
|**Avulsion/Traction**|Due to extreme hyperextension of the neck or muscular overactivity (e.g., tetanus).|Trauma or Disease.|

### **Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:** Throttling is prosecuted as **Murder** (Section 101/103) or **Culpable Homicide** (Section 100).
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer is required to meticulously document the "six-penny bruises" and "crescentic abrasions" to assist the investigation [derived from legal context 143].
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, Section 39:** The forensic expert's opinion on the **inward compression fracture** of the hyoid is vital evidence in court to prove homicidal throttling.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Throttling:** Always homicidal; characterized by fingertip bruises and nail marks.
- ★ **Hyoid Fracture in Throttling:** **Inward** (Adduction) fracture.
- ★ **Hyoid Fracture in Hanging:** **Outward** (Abduction/Tug) fracture.
- ★ **Nysten's Law:** Sequence of Rigor Mortis onset; helps in TSD estimation in asphyxia.
- ★ **Prinsloo & Gordon's Artefact:** Artificial neck hemorrhage that must be avoided by opening the cranium first.

### **Viva Questions**

**Q1: Can a person throttle themselves to death?** **Answer:** No. As the person loses consciousness, the muscle tone in the hands fails, the grip relaxes, and the airway is restored.

**Q2: Why are hyoid fractures rare in young victims?** **Answer:** In young people, the hyoid bone is cartilaginous and flexible. It only becomes brittle and prone to fracture after it calcifies, usually after age 40.

**Q3: What is the "Broken Neck Sign"?** **Answer:** It refers to the paralysis of neck-flexing muscles seen in neurotoxic snakebites (like cobra), which may mimic asphyxia.
