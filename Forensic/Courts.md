[[legal procedure]]
### **Courts in India and Their Powers**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Introduction** The Indian judicial system is a ==hierarchical structure== of courts that interpret and apply the law. For a medical practitioner, understanding this hierarchy is essential for knowing the legal weight of their testimony and the priority of court summons.

**Classification of Courts** Courts are primarily classified into:

1. **Criminal Courts:** Deal with offences against the state (e.g., murder, rape, theft).
2. **Civil Courts:** Deal with disputes between two private parties (e.g., divorce, property disputes).

---

#### **Hierarchy and Powers of Courts**

★ **Important Exam Point: The power of a court is defined by the maximum punishment and fine it can impose.**

|**Court Level**|**Jurisdiction**|**Maximum Punishment**|**Maximum Fine**|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Supreme Court**|Entire Country|Any sentence authorized by law|Unlimited.|
|**High Court**|Specific State|Any sentence authorized by law|Unlimited.|
|**Sessions Court / Additional Sessions Court**|District|Any sentence (Death sentence requires HC confirmation)|Unlimited.|
|**Assistant Sessions Court**|District|Up to **10 years** imprisonment|Unlimited.|
|**Chief Judicial Magistrate (CJM)**|District/Metro City|Up to **7 years** imprisonment|Unlimited.|
|**First Class Judicial Magistrate**|Sub-division|Up to **3 years** imprisonment|Up to **₹10,000**.|
|**Second Class Judicial Magistrate**|Local area|Up to **1 year** imprisonment|Up to **₹5,000**.|

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Confirmation of Death Sentence:** While a Sessions Court can award a death sentence, it is **not final** until it is confirmed by the High Court.
- **Priority of Attendance:** If a doctor receives multiple summons for the same time:
    1. Higher Court takes priority over a Lower Court (e.g., High Court > Sessions Court).
    2. Criminal cases take priority over Civil cases.
    3. If both are equal, the summons received first is attended.
- **Expert Witness:** Doctors depose in these courts as expert witnesses under **Section 39 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**.

---

#### **Specialized Courts**

**1. Juvenile Justice Board (Juvenile Magistrate)**

- **Who:** For children **<18 years** who have committed a crime.
- **Composition:** A First Class Judicial Magistrate and two social workers (at least one woman).
- **Purpose:** Focused on reformation rather than punishment. Heinous crimes by those aged 16–18 can be tried as adults after assessment.

**2. Consumer Redressal Commissions (COPRA)**

- Specifically for "deficiency in service" (medical negligence).
- **District Level:** Up to ₹1 Crore.
- **State Level:** ₹1 Crore to ₹10 Crore.
- **National Level:** >₹10 Crore.

---

#### **Commutation of the Death Sentence**

★ **Important Exam Point: The Flow of Mercy**

1. **Sessions Court:** Passes the sentence.
2. **High Court:** Must confirm the sentence. It has the **1st power of commutation** (can change death to life imprisonment).
    - _Note:_ If a woman is pregnant, the High Court **must** commute the death sentence to life imprisonment (**Section 456 BNSS**) [Source History].
3. **Supreme Court:** Final appellate power to confirm or change the sentence.
4. **President of India:** Holds the ultimate "Power of Amnesty" to forgive a death sentence.

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Unlimited Fine:** Only Courts of Session and above, CJM, and Assistant Sessions Courts can charge unlimited fines.
- ★ **Death Sentence Confirmation:** No death penalty can be executed without High Court approval.
- ★ **Magistrate Inquest (196 BNSS):** Done in custodial deaths, dowry deaths, or exhumations.
- ★ **Juvenile Age:** Under 18 years.

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is the maximum fine a 1st Class Judicial Magistrate can impose?**
    - _Ans:_ ₹10,000.
2. **Who can award a death sentence in a district?**
    - _Ans:_ Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge (subject to HC confirmation).
3. **What happens to a death sentence if the convict is pregnant?**
    - _Ans:_ It must be commuted to life imprisonment by the High Court under Section 456 BNSS [Source History].
4. **What is the difference between a Cognizable and Non-Cognizable offence?**
    - _Ans:_ In cognizable offences (e.g., murder), the police can arrest without a warrant. In non-cognizable (e.g., forgery), a warrant is required.

---

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Highest Court:** Supreme Court.
- **State Head:** High Court (Must confirm death sentences).
- **District Power:** Sessions Court (Can give any sentence authorized by law).
- **Magistrate Tiers:** CJM (7 yrs), 1st Class (3 yrs/10k), 2nd Class (1 yr/5k).
- **Legal Shift:** CrPC is now **BNSS**, IPC is now **BNS**, and IEA is now **BSA**.
