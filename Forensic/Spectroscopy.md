# 🔬🩸 **SPECTROSCOPY / SPECTROSCOPIC EXAMINATION (3-MARK MODEL ANSWER)** 🩸🔬

---

### **1. Definition & Core Principle** 📚

- **Definition:** Spectroscopy is a **delicate, highly reliable, and the most specific confirmatory physical test** used to detect the presence of blood in both recent and extremely old, weathered stains [191, Image 59, 60].
- **Biochemical Principle:** It is based on the light-absorbing properties of hemoglobin.
    - Hemoglobin and its various derivatives (pigments) absorb specific wavelengths of light.
    - When light passes through a blood solution, it produces characteristic **dark absorption bands** between **Fraunhofer’s lines (C, D, E, and F)** on the spectrum.

---

### **2. Laboratory Procedure** 🧪

1. **Step 1:** A small portion of the suspected bloodstain is dissolved in water or normal saline to create a clear solution.
2. **Step 2:** The solution is placed in a small test tube or cuvette.
3. **Step 3:** The tube is placed directly between a light source and a spectroscope to visualize the absorption bands.

---

### **3. Medico-Legal Importance (MLI)** ⚖️🩺

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Most Specific Confirmatory Test:** Unlike catalytic screening tests (e.g., Kastle-Meyer), spectroscopy is a definitive confirmatory test that directly identifies blood pigments with absolute specificity [Image 59, 61].
- ★ **Differentiation of Pigments:** Each hemoglobin derivative has a unique spectral signature. It can easily differentiate:
    - _Oxyhemoglobin_ (2 bands in yellow-green region) from _Carboxyhemoglobin_ (resembles oxyhemoglobin but does not change upon adding a reducing agent).
    - _Methemoglobin_, _Hematin_, or _Hemochromogen_ (from dried/old stains).
- **Efficacy on Aged Samples:** It remains highly effective even on old, dry, or partially putrefied stains where intact red blood cells can no longer be seen under a microscope.
