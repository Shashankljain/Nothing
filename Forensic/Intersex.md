### **Intersex (Disorders of Sexual Development)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Intersex** is defined as the intermingling of sexual characteristics of both sexes in one individual to varying degrees. This includes variations in physical form, reproductive organs, and sexual behaviour [4-p81].

**Introduction**  
In modern medical terminology, the term "Intersex" and "Pseudohermaphroditism" are replaced by the more comprehensive term **Disorders of Sexual Development (DSD)** [4-p84]. This change was made because the older terms were often considered stigmatising and were based only on identifying gonads, without considering if they were functional [4-p84].

![[image 69.jpg]]
**Terminology Shift** [4-p84]:  
The karyotype (chromosomal makeup) is now used as a prefix to define the category (e.g., 46,XY DSD instead of male pseudohermaphroditism).

#### **Major Intersex Conditions**

**1. Klinefelter Syndrome (47,XXY)** [4-p85, 4-p94]

- **Karyotype:** 47,XXY (extra X chromosome).
- **Clinical Features:** Anatomically male, tall stature, small/firm testes, gynecomastia (breast development), and absent/reduced facial and pubic hair.
- **Sex Chromatin:** Presence of a **Barr body** (False positive for a male).

**2. Turner Syndrome (45,XO)** [4-p85, 4-p98]

- **Karyotype:** 45,XO (missing one X chromosome).
- **Clinical Features:** Anatomically female, short stature, webbed neck, widely spaced nipples, and sexual infantilism (primary amenorrhea).
- **Sex Chromatin:** **No Barr body** (False negative for a female).

**3. True Hermaphroditism (Ovotesticular DSD)** [4-p82, 4-p84, 4-p89]

- **Definition:** An individual possessing both ovarian and testicular tissues.
- **Features:** External genitalia may resemble either sex, but internal gonads of both sexes are present.

**4. Pseudohermaphroditism (46,XY or 46,XX DSD)** [4-p86, 4-p100]

- **Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (46,XY DSD):** Genetically male (46,XY) but receptors are insensitive to testosterone. They are born and raised as girls with a female gender identity [4-p85].
- **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (46,XX DSD):** Genetically female (46,XX) but adrenal glands produce excessive testosterone, leading to masculinised external genitalia [4-p86, 4-p102].

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Why is sex determination vital?** [4-p76, 4-p82]

1. **Civil Rights:** To decide if an individual can exercise some civil rights restricted to one sex (e.g., specific government schemes).
2. **Family Law:** Determining questions of **marriage**, **divorce**, **legitimacy** of children, and **paternity/maternity**.
3. **Criminal Law:** Relevant in cases of **rape**, **sodomy**, or other sex-specific crimes.
4. **Sports:** Sex segregation in competitive sports is based on biological androgen exposure [4-p76].
5. **Acquired Intersex:** Forensic doctors must distinguish natural intersex from acquired states, such as "hijras" or "zenanas" who are castrated before or after puberty [4-p82].

#### **Medico-Legal Examination (Sex Determination)**

In doubtful cases or intersex conditions, a systematic approach is used [4-p79, 4-p82]:

1. **Physical Morphology:** External examination of secondary sexual characters (hair distribution, breasts, genitalia) [4-p79].
2. **Nuclear Sexing (Sex Chromatin):**
    - **Barr Body:** Found in buccal smears of females (20–80%) [4-p81, 4-p83].
    - **Davidson Body:** Found in neutrophils of females (about 3%) [3-S52, 4-p82].
3. **Gonadal Biopsy (★ Confirmatory):** The "Gold Standard" to identify actual internal gonads (testes or ovaries) [3-S51, 4-p80].
4. **Gene-based Tests:** Using **PCR** to detect the **SRY gene** on the Y-chromosome [4-p80].
