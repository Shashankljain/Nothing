[[PM Changes]]
**Algor mortis**, commonly known as the **chill of death**, is one of the most reliable early signs of death used by forensic experts to estimate the time since death (TSD) during the first 24 hours.

---

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** **Algor mortis** (Latin _algor_: coolness, _mortis_: death) is the progressive cooling of the body that occurs after death until it equilibrates with the temperature of its immediate environment.

**Introduction** In a living person, the body temperature is maintained around \(37^\circ\text{C}\) (\(98.6^\circ\text{F}\)) by the heat-regulating center in the brain. After somatic death, this center stops functioning, and energy production ceases, causing the body to lose heat.

**Principles of Cooling**

- The cooling process follows **Newton’s Law of Cooling**, which states that the rate of cooling is proportional to the difference in temperature between the body and its surroundings.
- The surface temperature falls more rapidly initially than the inner core temperature.

**Importance in Medico-Legal Practice**

1. **Sign of Death:** It is a definitive sign of molecular death.
2. **Estimation of TSD:** It is the most useful single indicator of the postmortem interval during the first 24 hours in temperate climates.

★ **Important Exam Point:** Algor mortis is a **process**, not an event. It typically takes **16–20 hours** for a body to reach the environmental temperature.

---

### **Relevant Law**

- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer is required to examine the body (including recording temperature) to help the investigating officer establish the timeline of death.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, Section 39:** Relates to **Expert Opinion**. A doctor’s estimate of the time since death based on algor mortis is admissible as expert evidence in court.

---

### **Mechanism: The Cooling Pattern**

The cooling of a cadaver does not occur at a constant rate. It follows a **sigmoid (inverted 'S' shaped)** or biexponential curve:

1. **Isothermic Phase (Initial Plateau):** For the first **1–2 hours**, there is little to no fall in the inner core (rectal) temperature. This is because the skin and subcutaneous fat act as insulators.
2. **Intermediate Phase:** A regular, linear, and constant pattern of rapid temperature fall occurs.
3. **Terminal Phase:** The rate of cooling slows down as the body temperature approaches the ambient (environmental) temperature.

---

### **Medico-Legal Examination (How to Measure)**

To ensure accuracy, the **inner core temperature** must be measured, as it is more reliable than skin temperature.

- **Preferred Site:** The **rectum** is the most common site.
- **Instrument:** A 10–12 inch chemical thermometer (range \(0–50^\circ\text{C}\)) or a digital probe thermometer.
- **Procedure:** The thermometer is introduced at least **10 cm** above the anus.
- **Other Sites:** Subhepatic (under the liver), external auditory meatus, or the nasal cavity.

---

### **Estimation of Time Since Death (TSD)**

**General Rule of Thumb:** In temperate climates, the temperature falls at an average rate of **\(1.5^\circ\text{F}\) per hour**.

**TSD Formula:** \[TSD \text{ (in hours)} = \frac{\text{Normal rectal temp} - \text{Measured rectal temp}}{\text{Rate of fall per hour}}\]

---

### **Factors Affecting Algor Mortis**

The rate of cooling is highly variable and depends on several factors:

|**Factor**|**Effect on Cooling Rate**|**Reason**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Environmental Temp**|Major factor; cooling is faster if the surroundings are very cold.|Greater temperature gradient.|
|**Medium**|**Water > Air > Soil** (Ratio 4:2:1).|Water is a better conductor of heat than air.|
|**Body Built**|**Lean** bodies cool faster than **Obese** bodies.|Fat is a poor conductor of heat (insulator).|
|**Age**|**Children/Elderly** cool faster.|Larger surface area-to-body volume ratio.|
|**Clothing**|**Naked** bodies cool faster than **clothed** ones.|Clothing acts as an insulator.|
|**Air Movement**|**Moving air** (wind) increases cooling.|Increases evaporation of body fluids.|
|**Mode of Death**|**Wasting illnesses** (e.g., Cancer) lead to faster cooling.|Reduced body mass and heat stores.|

---

### **Postmortem Caloricity**

In some specific conditions, the body temperature may **stay high or even rise** for the first 2 hours after death instead of falling.

**Causes of Postmortem Caloricity:**

- **Infectious diseases:** Septicemia, cholera, or tetanus.
- **Convulsant poisons:** Strychnine poisoning (due to excessive muscle contraction).
- **Brain conditions:** Pontine hemorrhage or heat stroke (disturbed heat regulation center).
- **Glycogenolysis:** A compulsory chemical process that produces up to 140 calories immediately after death.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Isothermic Phase:** The first 1–2 hours where no heat is lost from the core.
- ★ **Sigmoid Curve:** The inverted 'S' shape of the cooling graph.
- ★ **Medium Ratio:** Cooling in **Water : Air : Soil** occurs in a **4 : 2 : 1** ratio (Casper's Dictum principle).
- ★ **Standard Rate:** \(1.5^\circ\text{F}\) fall per hour.

---

### **Viva Questions**

**Q1: Where is the best place to record postmortem temperature?** **A:** The **rectum** (at least 10 cm above the anus) using a long chemical or digital probe thermometer.

**Q2: What is "Postmortem Caloricity"?** **A:** A condition where the body temperature rises after death due to factors like septicemia, heat stroke, or strychnine poisoning.

**Q3: Does a body cool faster in a humid environment?** **A:** Yes, because moist air is a better conductor of heat than dry air.

---

### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Definition:** Cooling of the body to reach ambient temperature.
- **TSD Indicator:** Best for the first 24 hours.
- **The Curve:** Sigmoid/Inverted 'S' (Plateau \(\rightarrow\) Linear \(\rightarrow\) Slow).
- **Rate:** Average \(0.4–0.7^\circ\text{C}\)/hr or \(1.5^\circ\text{F}\)/hr.
- **Measurement:** Rectal temperature is the gold standard.
- **Exceptions:** Postmortem Caloricity (temperature rise).
