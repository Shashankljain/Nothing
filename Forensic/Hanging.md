### **1. Basic Concepts & Definition**

**Definition** **Hanging** is a form of mechanical asphyxia caused by the suspension of the body by a **ligature** which encircles the neck, where the constricting force is the **weight of the body**.

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Manner of Death:** It is the most common method of suicide in India.
- **Alibi/Crime Scene:** It is often used for "postmortem suspension" to simulate suicide after a homicide.
- **Proof of Life:** The presence of specific signs (like saliva) proves the person was alive when suspended.

---

### **2. Classification / Types of Hanging**

Hanging is classified under three main categories:

#### **A. Based on the Degree of Suspension**

|**Type**|**Description**|**Constricting Force**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Complete**|No part of the body touches the ground.|**Entire body weight**.|
|**Incomplete (Partial)**|Lower part of the body (toes, knees, or buttocks) touches the ground.|**Weight of the head** (~4–5 kg).|

#### **B. Based on the Position of the Knot**

- **Typical Hanging:** The knot is placed at the **nape of the neck** (occiput).
- **Atypical Hanging:** The knot of the ligature is anywhere other than the occiput (e.g., side of the neck or under the chin).

#### **C. Based on Intent**

- **Suicidal:** Most common; usually in a secluded place with a reachable point of suspension.
- **Accidental:** Seen in ==children== playing or in cases of **==autoerotic== asphyxia**.
- **Homicidal (Lynching):** Rare; usually involves multiple assailants.

### **3. Postmortem (PM) Findings in Hanging**

#### **External Findings**
![[Pasted image 20260906201359.png]]

![[image 91.jpg]]

1. **Ligature Mark (The Furrow):**
    - **Course:** **Oblique** (angled), following the line of the mandible towards the knot.
    - **Continuity:** **Non-continuous** (interrupted by the knot or hair).
    - **Level:** High in the neck, usually **above the hyoid bone**.
    - **Skin Appearance:** Depressed, pale, and later becomes yellowish-brown, dry, and **parchment-like**.
2. **★ Dribbling of Saliva:** The **==surest== ==sign== of antemortem hanging**. It occurs due to pressure on the ==submandibular== glands and is found running from the angle of the mouth opposite the knot.
3. **Facial Signs:** Face may be pale or cyanosed; eyes are often half-open with **dilated pupils**.
4. **La Facie Sympathique:** If the knot presses on the cervical sympathetic chain, the eye on that side remains open and the pupil dilated.
5. **PM Staining:** Most marked in the lower limbs and hands, known as a **'==Glove== and ==Stocking=='** pattern.

#### **Internal Findings**

1. **Neck Tissues:** Subcutaneous tissue underneath the mark is **dry, white, firm, and glistening**.
2. **Hyoid Bone Fracture:** More common in victims over 40 years of age. It is typically an **abduction (Tug) fracture**, where the horns are driven outwards.
3. **Amussat's Sign:** Transverse tears in the **intima of the carotid arteries**, seen more in obese victims or long drops.
4. **Simon's Hemorrhage:** Hemorrhages on the anterior surface of the intervertebral discs in the lumbar spine; considered a vital sign of hanging.
5. **Lungs:** Voluminous, distended (emphysematous), showing **Tardieu's spots** (petechiae).

### **4. Enumeration of Causes of Death**

Death in hanging can occur due to one or a combination of the following:

1. **Asphyxia:** Compression of the airway.
2. **Venous Congestion:** Blockage of jugular veins.
3. **Cerebral Ischemia:** Occlusion of carotid arteries.
4. **Reflex Vagal Inhibition:** Sudden cardiac arrest due to pressure on the carotid sinus.
5. **Fracture/Dislocation of Cervical Vertebrae:** Common in judicial hanging.

---

### **5. Note on Judicial Hanging**

**Judicial hanging** is the legal execution of the death penalty.

- **Procedure:** The ligature is looped with the knot under the chin (**submental**). The victim is subjected to a "drop" of **5–7 feet**.
- **Mechanism of Death:** The forceful jerky impact causes a **Hangman's Fracture**.
- **Hangman’s Fracture:** Fracture-dislocation of the **C2 vertebra over the C3 vertebra**, leading to stretching or tearing of the spinal cord and brain stem damage.
- **Medico-Legal Role:** Under **Section 194 BNSS**, a medical officer must be present to certify the death [derived from legal context 143].

- ★ **Surest sign of AM hanging:** Dribbling of saliva.

- ★ **Simon’s sign:** Lumbar spine hemorrhages.

### **Viva Questions**

**Q: Can a dead person be hanged?** **A:** Yes, it is called **postmortem suspension**, used to hide murder. It is identified by the absence of vital signs like saliva and Simon's sign.

**Q: Why is the ligature mark in hanging non-continuous?** **A:** Because the knot or hair prevents the ligature from touching the skin at the point of suspension.
