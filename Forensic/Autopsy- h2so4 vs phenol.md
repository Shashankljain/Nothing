[[Corrosive]]
# 🧪💀 **AUTOPSY FINDINGS IN SULPHURIC ACID & CARBOLIC ACID POISONING** 💀🧪

---

### **Basic Concepts** 📚

#### **Definitions**

- **Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\)):** Commercially known as **"Oil of Vitriol"**, it is a highly powerful, ==inorganic==/mineral corrosive acid that acts locally on contact by extracting tissue water and coagulating proteins, resulting in severe tissue carbonisation.
- **Carbolic Acid (Phenol):** An ==organic== corrosive poison derived from coal tar. It has both a local corrosive action (coagulation necrosis) and a powerful systemic neurotoxic/nephrotoxic action once absorbed into the circulation.
- **Vitriolage:** The malicious act of throwing any corrosive substance (most commonly concentrated Sulphuric acid) onto a person's body, primarily the face, with the intent to disfigure, main, or blind.

---

### **The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

1. **What?** A critical postmortem evaluation of deaths caused by strong inorganic corrosive (Sulphuric acid) versus organic corrosive (Carbolic acid) ingestion.
2. **Why?** Corrosive substances are commonly ingested for suicide due to cheap household availability (e.g., toilet cleaners, disinfectants) or used criminally in acid throwing (Vitriolage).
3. **How?**
    - _Sulphuric Acid_ causes immediate death via **intense local corrosion**, rapid **coagulative necrosis**, and systemic **circulatory shock**.
    - _Carbolic Acid_ causes rapid death by systemic **paralysis of respiratory and cardiovascular centers** in the brain, along with severe renal damage.
4. **When?** Postmortem changes and corrosions are documented during the forensic autopsy, which should be conducted as soon as possible after death.
5. **Where?** Autopsy examinations must be performed in an authorised government mortuary, with all body cavities systematically opened.
6. **Who?** Conducted by a qualified forensic pathologist or autopsy surgeon under a legal mandate from a police officer or magistrate.

---

### **General Autopsy Protocol for Suspected Poisoning Deaths** 🔍🩺

★ **Important Exam Point: Order of Dissection** Before detailing individual findings, every MBBS student must know the standard operating procedures for a poisoning autopsy:

1. **Open the Cranium (Skull) FIRST:**
    - _Why?_ The head cavity must be opened before the thoracic and abdominal cavities to detect and document unique **volatile toxic odors** (such as the distinctive phenolic smell of Carbolic acid) before they are masked by the putrefactive smells of the abdominal organs.
2. **Double Ligature Method of Stomach Removal:**
    - The esophagus is tied with two ligatures at the cardiac end, and the duodenum is tied with two ligatures below the pyloric end.
    - The stomach is then removed by cutting between these double ligatures to prevent any loss or leakage of gastric contents.
3. **Incision along the Greater Curvature:**
    - The stomach must **always be opened along its greater curvature**.
    - _Why?_ Liquid corrosive acids naturally flow under gravity along the lesser curvature, pooling at the pyloric antrum due to reactive pylorospasm (**The Magenstrasse Effect**). Opening along the greater curvature leaves this pathognomonic lesser curvature pathology completely intact and visible for documentation.

### **I. Autopsy Findings in Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\)) Poisoning** 🧪🔥

#### **A. External Findings**

- **Trickle Marks (Rope Burns):** Characteristic downward-running corridors of ==blackened, corroded, and excoriated skin== at the angles of the mouth, cheeks, chin, and neck, outlining where the spilled liquid flowed due to gravity.
- **The Lips:** Swollen, charred, and intensely **blackened**.
- **The Teeth:** Classically **chalky white, dull, and brittle** because the acid strips away calcium.
- **Postmortem Lividity:** Typically dark purplish-black due to systemic asphyxia and profound deoxygenation.

#### **B. Internal Findings**

- **Upper GIT Mucosa:** The mouth, tongue, pharynx, and esophagus show intense inflammation, mucosal desquamation, and **black/charred staining**.
- **The Stomach:**
    - **Peppery Feel:** Widespread **blackish charring** of the gastric mucosa, giving it a rough, "peppery feel" on palpation due to severe carbonisation of tissue proteins.
    - **Magenstrasse Damage:** Concentrated, thinned corrosion and necrotic sloughing along the lesser curvature.
    - **High Perforation Rate:** Immediate or delayed **perforation of the stomach** is highly common. The acid escapes into the peritoneal cavity, extensively damaging the pancreas, spleen, and diaphragm, and leading to severe **==chemical peritonitis==**.
    - **Gastric Contents:** ==Strongly acidic, thick, gelatinous, and **blackish flu==id containing altered blood** and shredded mucosal remnants ("coffee-ground" appearance).
- **Respiratory System:** Extreme laryngeal edema, congestion, and desquamation of the tracheobronchial tree due to inhalation of fumes or accidental aspiration.
- **Other Viscera:** Widespread passive venous congestion with **toxic swelling of the liver and kidneys**.

#### **C. Viscera Preservation Guidelines**

- ★ **Preservative of Choice:** Harvested organs and skin must be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit (95% Ethanol)**.
- _Why?_ Saturated salt is not preferred for general mineral acids; alcohol acts as the ideal tissue stabilizer to lock in the inorganic acid ions.

---

### **II. Autopsy Findings in Carbolic Acid (Phenol) Poisoning (Carbolism)** 🧼💀

#### **A. External Findings**

- **Grayish-White Corrosions:** Soft, opaque, grayish-white or **buff-white sloughs** at the angles of the mouth, chin, and lips, which dry to leave a ==hard==, ==brown==, parchment-like stain.
- **Characteristic Odor:** A strong, unmistakable **==phenolic/medicinal odor==** emanating from the mouth and nose.
- **Retarded Putrefaction:** Because phenol is a powerful antiseptic/disinfectant, it destroys saprophytic bacteria, **significantly delaying the onset of putrefaction/decomposition** of the body.

#### **B. Internal Findings**

- **Esophagus:** Grayish-white or buff-white corrosion of the esophageal mucosa, with intervening areas appearing relatively normal.
- **The Stomach:**
    - **Leathery Stomach:** The gastric wall is thickened, hardened, tough, and **distinctly leathery**.
    - _Why?_ Phenol precipitates and coagulates mucosal proteins into a dense, protective, rubbery eschar. Because of this structural hardening, **perforation of the stomach is extremely rare** in Carbolic acid poisoning (unlike Sulphuric acid).
    - **Mucosal Color:** The mucosal folds appear swollen and **buff-white or grayish-white**, while the intervening mucosa remains a normal red color.
    - **Gastric Contents:** ==Viscous, slimy, grayish or brownish fluid== containing partially detached mucosal shreds with a strong phenolic smell.
- **Kidneys (Hemorrhagic Nephritis):** Swollen, pale, and intensely congested. Histopathology reveals acute proximal tubular necrosis (ATN) and glomerular degeneration.
- **Carboluria (Smoky Green Urine):** The urinary bladder contains urine that, upon exposure to air, oxidizes to ==hydroquinone and pyrocatechol==, turning **dark smoky green**.
- **Ochronosis:** A rare but pathognomonic finding in delayed or chronic exposure, showing **bluish-black pigmentation** of the sclera (between the cornea and canthi), the ear cartilages, joint cartilages, and the cornea.
- **Other Organs:** Diffuse cerebral edema and intense congestion of all internal viscera.

#### **C. Viscera Preservation Guidelines**

- ★ **Preservative of Choice:** Harvested organs must be preserved strictly in a **Saturated solution of common salt**.
- ★ **Important Exam Point: PAPPA Contraindication:** **Rectified spirit is strictly contraindicated** for Carbolic acid preservation under the **PAPPA** mnemonic (**P**henol, **A**lcohol, **P**araldehyde, **P**hosphorus, **A**cetic acid). Phenol is highly soluble in alcohol, meaning spirit will dissolve the poison out of the tissues and completely ruin the quantitative laboratory FSL analysis.

### **Relevant Law (Must Include) 📜**

#### **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- ★ **Section 124(1) BNS (formerly Sec 326-A IPC):** Deals with voluntarily causing **grievous hurt by the use of acid** (Vitriolage). Punishable by a minimum of 10 years to life imprisonment and a fine paid directly to the victim.
- ★ **Section 124(2) BNS (formerly Sec 326-B IPC):** Punishes **throwing or attempting to throw acid** with 5 to 7 years of imprisonment.
- **Section 123 BNS (formerly Sec 328 IPC):** Voluntarily causing **hurt by means of poison** with intent to commit an offence [123 BNS].
- **Section 238 BNS (formerly Sec 201 IPC):** Punishes **destruction of evidence** [238 BNS]. If an autopsy surgeon fails to preserve the viscera in suspected poisoning, they can be prosecuted under this section.

#### **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- ★ **Section 33 BNSS (formerly Sec 39 CrPC):** Mandates that every Registered Medical Practitioner **must report all suspected cases of homicidal or suspicious poisoning** to the nearest police officer [33 BNSS].

#### **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 39 BSA (formerly Sec 45 IEA):** Admits the FSL chemical analysis report of the preserved viscera as admissible **Expert Opinion** in judicial trials [39 BSA].
