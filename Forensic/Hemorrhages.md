[[Regional injuries]]
**Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH)** refers to the pathological extravasation and accumulation of blood within the cranial cavity. It can occur within the meningeal spaces (extradural, subdural, or subarachnoid) or directly within the brain parenchyma (intracerebral/intra-parenchymal), resulting in elevated intracranial pressure (ICP), brain compression, and potential brain herniation [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].

**Anatomical Layers of the Cranium** 🧫

To understand intracranial bleeds, a beginner must master the cranial layers from **outside to inside**: \text{Skull Vault (Cranial Bone)} \rightarrow \text{Dura Mater} \rightarrow \text{Arachnoid Mater} \rightarrow \text{Pia Mater} \rightarrow \text{Brain Tissue/Cerebrum} \text{ [`input_file_15.png`]}$$

---

**The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

1. **What?** Life-threatening accumulations of blood within the non-yielding bony confines of the skull [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
2. **Why?** Triggered by mechanical trauma (road traffic accidents, falls, physical assaults) or non-traumatic pathological crises (ruptured aneurysms, chronic hypertension) [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
3. **How?** Deceleration/acceleration forces shear delicate bridging veins, direct impacts fracture bones to lacerate arteries, or high systemic blood pressure ruptures microaneurysms [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
4. **When?** Manifests either immediately as an acute crisis (e.g., Subarachnoid Hemorrhage) or after a deceptive delay (e.g., the **Lucid Interval** in Epidural Hemorrhage or weeks later in Chronic Subdural Hemorrhage) [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
5. **Where?** Localized within the potential or real spaces of the meninges, or deep within vital brain structures such as the **putamen** or **brainstem** [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
6. **Who?** Commonly seen in motorcyclists (Hinge fractures), boxers (Dementia Pugilistica), infants (Shaken Baby Syndrome), and elderly individuals prone to falls [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].

**Topic-Specific Detailed Explanations** 🔬

**1. Epidural / Extradural Hemorrhage (EDH)** 🔴

```
                  [DIRECT BLUNT IMPACT TO PTERION]
                                 │
                                 ▼
               [Skull Fracture traversing MMA Grooves]
                                 │
                                 ▼
             [Tearing of the Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA)]
                                 │
                                 ▼
           [High-Pressure Arterial Bleeding strips Dura from Bone]
                                 │
                                 ▼
            [★ BICONVEX / LENTICULAR HEMATOMA ACCUMULATION]
```

- **Biomechanics:** Exclusively caused by **trauma** [`input_file_15.png`]. A direct blow to the thin temporal bone at the **Pterion** (junction of frontal, parietal, sphenoid, and temporal bones) fractures the bone and tears the underlying **Middle Meningeal Artery** [`input_file_15.png`].
- **The "D-Shape" Limitation:** Because the dura is tightly adhered to the skull sutures, the arterial blood cannot cross suture lines, giving the hematoma its characteristic **biconvex/lenticular shape** on Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Lethal Threshold:** A minimum volume of **100 mL** of blood in the extradural space is sufficient to compress the brain, leading to transtentorial herniation, respiratory failure, and death [`input_file_15.png`].

**The Lucid Interval** ⏳

- ★ **Important Exam Point:** The **Lucid Interval** is a temporary phase of normal consciousness and mental clarity sandwiched between **two distinct episodes of unconsciousness** [`input_file_15.png`]:
    1. **First Phase:** Unconsciousness occurs immediately after impact due to a **cerebral concussion** [`input_file_15.png`].
    2. **Clear Phase (The Lucid Interval):** The patient regains consciousness as the concussive effect resolves [`input_file_15.png`].
    3. **Second Phase:** As the arterial bleeding continues, it reaches the fatal threshold (>100 mL), compressing the brain and plunging the patient into a secondary, terminal coma [`input_file_15.png`].

---

**2. Subdural Hemorrhage (SDH)** 🔵

- **Biomechanics:** Most commonly caused by **trauma** involving acceleration/deceleration forces that tear the fragile **bridging veins** running from the cerebral cortex to the dural venous sinuses [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Non-Traumatic Risk Factors:** Highly common in **chronic alcoholics**, boxers, infants, and the elderly [`input_file_15.png`]. In these groups, cerebral atrophy stretches the bridging veins, making them highly vulnerable to rupture even after minor, unnoticed trauma [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Suture Boundaries:** Unlike EDH, the subdural space is a free potential space. The hematoma can spread widely over the entire cerebral hemisphere, yielding a **concavo-convex (sickle/crescent) shape** on CT [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Subdural Hygroma:** Occurs when a tear in the arachnoid membrane allows cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) to escape from the subarachnoid space and pool in the subdural space [`input_file_15.png`].

**Chronological Classification of SDH by Density** 📅

- **Acute SDH (0 to 3 days):** Appears **hyperdense** (bright white) on CT [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Sub-acute SDH (3 days to 3 weeks):** Appears **isodense** (grey, matching brain tissue) and is easily missed [`input_file_15.png`].
- **Chronic SDH (>3 weeks):** Appears **hypodense** (dark/black) [`input_file_15.png`].

---

**3. Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)** 🟡

- **Anatomy:** Bleeding directly into the subarachnoid space (between the arachnoid and pia mater), mixing with the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
- **Etiology:**
    - **Traumatic (Most Common Overall):** Rupture of the anterior communicating artery or other vessels of the **Circle of Willis** due to head trauma [`input_file_16.png`].
    - **Non-Traumatic (Spontaneous):** Rupture of a **berry aneurysm** (most commonly located at the bifurcations of the Circle of Willis) [`input_file_16.png`].
- **Clinical Presentation:** Characterized by an immediate, agonizing **"thunderclap headache"** (often described by the patient as _"the worst headache of my life"_), projectile vomiting, photophobia, meningismus (neck stiffness), and rapid loss of consciousness [`input_file_16.png`].
- **Diagnosis:**
    1. **NCCT:** Blood tracking within the cerebral sulci, fissures, and basal cisterns [`input_file_16.png`].
    2. **Lumbar Puncture:** If CT is inconclusive, a lumbar puncture reveals bloody CSF. After centrifuging, the supernatant shows **Xanthochromia** (a yellow discoloration caused by bilirubin from lysed RBCs, appearing 4–6 hours post-bleed) [`input_file_16.png`].

---

**4. Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH) / Intra-Parenchymal Hemorrhage** 🟢

- **Definition:** Bleeding directly within the functional brain parenchyma [`input_file_15.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
- **Etiology:**
    - **Non-Traumatic (Most Common):** Rupture of Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms on the **lenticulostriate branch of the Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA)** (historically termed **Charcot's Artery of Cerebral Hemorrhage**) due to chronic, uncontrolled **systemic hypertension** [`input_file_16.png`].
    - **Traumatic:** Deep contusions or shear injuries.
- **Most Common Site:** The **Putamen** of the basal ganglia [`input_file_16.png`].

**Pathological & Forensic Brain Syndromes** 🧠🥊

**1. Dementia Pugilistica (Punch-Drunk Syndrome)**

- **Pathogenesis:** A progressive, neurodegenerative disorder caused by chronic, repetitive mild head trauma (most commonly seen in **professional boxers** and contact-sport athletes) [`input_file_16.png`].
- **Pathology:** Repetitive acceleration/deceleration forces trigger recurrent micro-subdural hemorrhages and diffuse axonal injuries, resulting in diffuse **cerebral atrophy** and the accumulation of **neurofibrillary tangles** [`input_file_16.png`].
- **Clinical Triad:** Cognitive decline (short-term memory loss), psychiatric disturbances (mood swings, depression), and motor deficits (Parkinsonian tremors and muscle rigidity) [`input_file_16.png`].

**2. Shaken Baby Syndrome (Infantile Whiplash Syndrome)**

- **Mechanism:** A severe form of child abuse where an infant is forcefully and repeatedly shaken [`input_file_16.png`]. The rapid back-and-forth movement causes violent hyperextension and hyperflexion of the delicate infant neck [`input_file_16.png`].
- ★ **The Pathognomonic Diagnostic Triad:**
    1. **Subdural Hemorrhage (SDH)** [`input_file_16.png`].
    2. **Intraocular Bleeding** (Retinal dot-blot hemorrhages / Berlin's edema) [`input_file_16.png`].
    3. **Encephalopathy** (severe brain swelling and developmental delay) [`input_file_16.png`].

**Medico-Legal Importance & Autopsy Findings** 🔍💀

**I. Autopsy Protocol for Intracranial Bleeds**

1. ★ **Cranium Opened FIRST:** In all suspected head injury cases, **the skull must be opened first** before dissecting the thoracic or abdominal cavities [`input_file_7.7_C.png`, `input_file_82.png`, `input_file_83.png`].
    - _Forensic Rationale:_ Opening the body cavities first allows blood to drain from the large veins, which can collapse the intracranial vessels and distort the measurement of hematoma volumes.
2. ★ **The Autopsy "Water Test" for SDH vs. SAH:**
    - _Procedure:_ After reflecting the scalp and removing the skull cap, the dura is gently incised and a stream of running water is poured over the brain surface [`input_file_95.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
    - _Result:_
        - If the clotted blood **washes away easily** under running water, it is confirmed as a **Subdural Hemorrhage (SDH)** [`input_file_95.png`, `input_file_16.png`].
        - If the blood **cannot be washed away** (because it is locked beneath the arachnoid membrane within the CSF space), it is confirmed as a **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)** [`input_file_95.png`, `input_file_16.png`].

**II. Brainstem Markers & Internal Trauma**

- **Duret Hemorrhages:** Small, linear, pinpoint hemorrhages located in the **midbrain and pons** [`input_file_16.png`]. They represent the terminal stage of descending brain herniation where stretching and tearing of brainstem perforating vessels occur [`input_file_16.png`].
- **Kernohan's Notch:** A grooving or indentation of the contralateral cerebral peduncle against the free edge of the tentorium cerebelli due to massive hemispheric shift [`input_file_16.png`].
