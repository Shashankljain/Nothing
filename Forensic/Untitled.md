[[Mechanical injuries]]
Mechanical injuries are the most common topics in university forensic medicine examinations. Below is a comprehensive, exam-oriented guide to their classification and a detailed study of abrasions.

### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition of Injury** According to **Section 2(14) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023** (formerly Section 44 IPC), an injury is defined as "any harm, whatever illegally, caused to any person in body, mind, reputation or property".

**Definition of Wound** Clinically, a wound is a breach in the natural continuity of the skin or mucous membrane. In medico-legal practice, "injury" and "wound" are often used synonymously.

---

### **2. Classification of Mechanical Injuries**

Mechanical injuries are produced by physical violence. They are classified under several headings as follows:

#### **A. Based on the Causative Factor (The Weapon/Force)**

|**Category**|**Types of Injuries**|
|:--|:--|
|**Blunt Force Trauma**|Abrasion, Contusion (Bruise), Laceration, Fracture, and Dislocation.|
|**Sharp Force Trauma**|Incised wound, Stab (Punctured) wound, and Chop wound.|
|**Firearm Injuries**|Rifled weapon injuries and Smooth-bore (Shotgun) injuries.|

#### **B. Based on Legal Nature (Degree of Severity)**

- **Simple Hurt:** Injuries that heal rapidly and are not extensive.
- **Grievous Hurt:** Defined under **Section 116 BNS** (formerly Sec 320 IPC). It includes 8 specific clauses like emasculation, permanent loss of sight/hearing, and fractures.

#### **C. Based on Medico-Legal Manner**

- Suicidal, Homicidal, Accidental, Defense wounds, and Fabricated (self-inflicted) wounds.

#### **D. Based on Time of Infliction**

- **Antemortem:** Inflicted before death (show vital reactions like bleeding/scabs).
- **Postmortem:** Inflicted after death (appear pale and bloodless).

---

### **3. Abrasion: Detailed Study**

#### **Definition**

An abrasion is the removal of the superficial epithelial layer of the skin (usually the epidermis and papillary dermis) by friction against a rough surface.

#### **Classification of Abrasions**

Abrasions are classified based on the direction and nature of the force applied.

★ **Important Exam Mnemonic: "P-I-G-S"**

- **P** – **Pressure** Abrasion
- **I** – **Imprint** Abrasion
- **G** – **Graze** Abrasion
- **S** – **Scratch** Abrasion

|**Type**|**Mechanism**|**Examples / Features**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Scratch (Linear)**|Sharp/pointed object passing across the skin.|Pin, thorn, or fingernail scratches.|
|**Graze (Sliding)**|Horizontal friction between skin and rough surface.|"Gravel rash" or "Brush burn" in road accidents.|
|**Pressure (Crushing)**|Directed impact or linear pressure over the skin.|Ligature marks in hanging or strangulation.|
|**Imprint (Patterned)**|Force applied perpendicular to skin; cuticle is crushed.|Radiator grill patterns, tyre marks, or bite marks.|

---

### **4. Medico-Legal Importance of Abrasion**

Abrasions are often considered "minor" by students, but they are of **extremely high** medico-legal value to a forensic expert.

1. **Site of Impact:** Abrasions indicate the exact point where the force or weapon struck the body.
2. **Direction of Force:** In a graze abrasion, the epidermis is heaped up as an **epithelial tag** at the "tail end," showing the direction the body or object was moving.
3. **Identification of the Weapon:** Patterned (imprint) abrasions can help identify the object used, such as the pattern of a vehicle’s tyre or a radiator grill.
4. **Character and Manner of Injury:**
    - **Throttling:** Crescentic fingernail abrasions on the neck.
    - **Sexual Assault:** Abrasions on the breasts, inner thighs, or genitals.
    - **Smothering:** Abrasions around the mouth and nose.
5. **Age of Injury:** The color and nature of the scab help determine when the injury occurred (e.g., Bright red = Fresh; Brownish-black scab = 4–6 days; Scab falls off = 7+ days).
6. **Scene of Crime Evidence:** Dirt, gravel, or grease stuck to an open abrasion can link a victim to a specific crime scene (e.g., a specific type of road surface).
7. **Differentiation of Death:** Helping distinguish between antemortem (show vital reactions) and postmortem (pale, yellowish-white) injuries.

---

### **5. Relevant Law (BNS, 2023)**

- **Section 2(14) BNS:** Definition of Injury.
- **Section 114 BNS:** Punishment for voluntarily causing hurt (Abrasions are generally "Simple Hurt").
- **Section 116 BNS:** Grievous Hurt. **★ Important Exam Point:** Abrasions are usually simple, but an abrasion over the **cornea** that restricts vision permanently is considered **Grievous Hurt**.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Epithelial Tags:** Found at the tail end of a graze abrasion; used to determine the direction of force.
- ★ **Antemortem vs. Postmortem:** Antemortem abrasions have a bright red/brown appearance with vital reactions; postmortem ones are yellowish-white and lack a scab.
- ★ **Fingernail Marks:** Crescentic abrasions on the neck are pathognomonic findings in cases of manual strangulation (throttling).
