[[Thermal deaths]]
Thermal injuries refer to the local or general effects produced on the body by the application of heat (High Temperature) or cold (Low Temperature).

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Manner of Death:** Helps distinguish between accidental (most common), suicidal, or homicidal (e.g., dowry deaths or "necklacing").
- **Identification:** Burns can sometimes obscure features, but specific findings like tattoos or dental records may persist.
- **Assessment of Severity:** The fatality of a burn is primarily determined by the **percentage of body surface area** involved rather than the depth.

**Classification of Thermal Injuries**

Thermal injuries are classified based on the nature of the temperature source and whether the effect is local or systemic.

**A. Heat-Related Injuries**

1. **Local Effects:**
    - **Dry Heat (Burns):** Caused by flames, hot solids (e.g., heated iron), or radiant heat.
    - **Moist Heat (Scalds):** Caused by hot liquids (water, oil) or steam.
    - **Chemical/Corrosive Burns:** Caused by strong acids or alkalis (==Vitriolage==).
    - **Electrical Burns:** Caused by the passage of electric current through the skin (==Joule== burns).
2. **General (Systemic) Effects:**
    - **Heat Stroke:** Failure of the ==thermoregulatory== center (Core Temp >40.5°C).
    - **Heat Exhaustion:** Due to ==water== and ==electrolyte== depletion.
    - **Heat Cramps:** Painful muscle spasms in miners/firemen due to loss of ==salt==.

**B. Cold-Related Injuries**

1. **Local Effects:**
    - **Frostbite:** Freezing of tissues (usually peripheries like nose, ears, fingers).
    - **Trench Foot:** Non-freezing injury due to prolonged immersion in cold water.
2. **General (Systemic) Effects:**
    - **Hypothermia:** Core body temperature falling below 35°C.

**Calculation of Percentage of Burn Injuries**

Calculating the Total Body Surface Area (TBSA) is critical for determining prognosis and treatment. There are three main methods used:

**I. Rule of Nine (Wallace Rule)**

This is the most common method used for adults. The body is divided into areas representing 9% or multiples of 9%.

|**Body Part**|**Percentage (%)**|
|---|---|
|**Head and Neck**|9%|
|**Each Upper Limb**|9% (Total 18% for both)|
|**Front of Trunk**|18%|
|**Back of Trunk**|18%|
|**Each Lower Limb**|18% (Front 9%, Back 9%)|
|**Genitalia/Perineum**|1%|
|**Total**|**100%**|

**II. Rule of Palm**

- **Use:** Best for calculating the area of **patchy or scattered burns**.
- **Calculation:** The area of the patient's own palm (including fingers) is roughly equal to **1% of their total body surface area**.

**III. Lund and Browder Chart**

- **Use:** This is the most accurate method for **==children== (<15 years)**.
- **Reasoning:** In children, the ==head== occupies a much ==larger== proportion of the body surface area, and the legs are relatively smaller compared to adults. The percentages are adjusted based on the child's age.
