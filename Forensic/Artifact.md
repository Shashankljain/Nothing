For an MBBS student, the study of **Artefacts** is critical because these findings can mimic injuries or diseases, potentially leading to a "miscarriage of justice" if misinterpreted by the medical officer.

---

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** An **artefact** in forensic medicine is any change or feature introduced into the body after death (postmortem) or during the agonal period (the process of dying) that was ==not part of the natural disease== process or the original injury.

**Introduction** The word is derived from the Latin _ars_ (art) and _factum_ (made), essentially meaning "false findings". Recognising artefacts is vital to differentiate them from **antemortem** (before death) injuries that carry legal weight.

**Classification of Artefacts** Artefacts are classified based on when and how they are introduced:

|**Type**|**Description**|**Examples**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Agonal Artefacts**|Occur during the struggle for life just before death.|Aspiration of vomit, agonal thrombi.|
|**Postmortem Artefacts**|Occur after death due to decay, environment, or handling.|Putrefaction signs (Swiss cheese liver), insect marks, bloating.|
|**Iatrogenic Artefacts**|Introduced by medical professionals during treatment.|CPR rib fractures, injection marks, surgical alterations (Kennedy Phenomenon).|
|**Intentional Artefacts**|Created by someone to mislead the legal investigation.|Rayalaseema phenomenon (planting bullets), fabricated wounds.|

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Avoidance of Error:** Distinguishing an artefact from a **vital reaction** ensures the cause and manner of death are reported accurately.
- **Role in Criminal Investigation:** Prevents the police from investigating a "natural" death that appears "unnatural" due to postmortem changes.
- **★ Important Exam Point:** The presence of a **vital reaction** (e.g., bleeding, redness, swelling, or clotted blood that cannot be washed away) is the gold standard for identifying a true antemortem injury.
- **Court Evidence:** Under **Section 39 BSA, 2023**, the doctor must defend their findings as expert evidence and explain why a particular mark is an artefact.

---

### **Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023, Section 238:** Relates to the **disappearance of evidence** or giving false information. Creating intentional artefacts (like planting a bullet) to mislead an investigation is punishable under this section.
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** The medical officer conducting the autopsy must document findings meticulously to assist the **Police Inquest**.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, Section 39:** Relates to **Expert Opinion**; the doctor's ability to exclude artefacts is essential for their testimony to be admissible.

### **Common Forensic Artefacts**

#### **1. Asphyxial Artefacts (Prinsloo & Gordon's)**

- **Description:** Hemorrhage in the soft tissues of the neck discovered during autopsy.
- **Cause:** Postmortem draining of blood from neck vessels if the heart has stopped but the neck is dissected before the head.
- **Significance:** It can be mistaken for **throttling** or **strangulation**.
- **Prevention:** The **cranial cavity** should be opened first to decompress cerebral vessels and drain blood before the neck is examined.

#### **2. Thermal (Heat) Artefacts**

- **Pugilistic Attitude:** Muscles exposed to heat (>\(65^\circ\text{C}\)) undergo protein coagulation and stiffening, causing the limbs to flex like a "boxer". This is an artefact and not a sign of a struggle.
- **Heat Rupture:** Skin exposed to heat dries and splits; these can look like **incised wounds** but lack vital reactions and show intact nerves/vessels in the floor.
- **Heat Hematoma:** Blood in the extradural space following fire; it appears chocolate-brown and honeycomb-like, unlike the firm clotted blood of a traumatic **extradural hemorrhage**.

#### **3. Putrefactive Artefacts**

- **Swiss Cheese Liver:** Also called "Honeycomb liver," it is caused by gas-forming bacteria (_C. perfringens_) creating holes in the organ.
- **Postmortem Purge:** Oozing of blood-stained fluid from the nose/mouth due to gas pressure in the abdomen; often mistaken for internal injury or trauma.
- **Gas Stiffening:** Accumulation of gases causes the body to become rigid, mimicking **Rigor Mortis**.

#### **4. Iatrogenic (Medical) Artefacts**

- **Kennedy Phenomenon:** Surgical alteration of a gunshot wound (e.g., cleaning or suturing) by doctors, making it difficult for the forensic expert to identify "Entry" from "Exit" wounds.
- **Resuscitation Injuries:** Rib and sternal fractures caused by vigorous chest compressions (CPR).

---

### **Differentiating Injuries from Artefacts**

|**Feature**|**True Antemortem Injury**|**Postmortem Artefact**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Vital Reaction**|**Present** (Swelling, redness).|**Absent**.|
|**Color**|Red/Purple (due to hemoglobin).|Yellowish, pale, or brown.|
|**Bleeding**|**Clotted blood**; cannot be washed away.|Liquid blood; **washes away** easily.|
|**Microscopy**|Shows cellular infiltration/inflammation.|No signs of inflammation.|

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Prinsloo & Gordon's Artefact:** Mimics throttling; prevent by opening the skull first.
- ★ **Rayalaseema Phenomenon:** Planting a bullet in a stab wound to mislead the law.
- ★ **Embalming Artefact:** Dislodges thrombi and emboli, making them hard to diagnose.
- ★ **Washerwoman’s Hand:** A sign of immersion in water, not a definitive sign of drowning.

---

### **Viva Questions**

**Q: How do you tell a heat rupture from an incised wound?** **A:** Heat ruptures have irregular margins, show no vital reaction (bleeding/redness), and vital structures like nerves and vessels are often preserved across the gap.

**Q: Why is the "Swiss cheese liver" significant?** **A:** It is a postmortem change due to gas-producing bacteria and should not be mistaken for a disease or internal trauma.

### **Quick Revision Notes**

1. **Definition:** Changes after death that mimic injuries.
2. **Types:** Agonal, Postmortem, Iatrogenic, Intentional.
3. **Key Examples:** Prinsloo & Gordon (neck), Heat hematoma (skull), Kennedy phenomenon (surgical).
4. **Vital Reaction:** The main differentiator; if absent, it's likely an artefact.
5. **Legal:** BNS 238 covers tampering with evidence.
