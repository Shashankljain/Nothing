[[Mechanical injuries]]

### **1. Basic Concepts**

|Concept|Definition|Type of Firearm|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Rifling**|Spiral grooves cut into the internal surface (bore) of a barrel.|**Rifled Weapons** (Rifles, Pistols, Revolvers).|
|**Choking**|An interior constriction of the bore at the muzzle end.|**Smooth-bore Weapons** (Shotguns).|

---

### **2. Rifling: Detailed Study**

**Mechanism:** The internal surface of the barrel is scored with shallow spiral grooves. The projecting ridges between these grooves are called **lands**, and the depressions are called **grooves**. The distance between two opposite lands is known as the **Caliber**.

#### **Purpose of Rifling**

- **Gyroscopic Stability:** It imparts a rotational spin to the bullet along its longitudinal axis.
- **Accuracy:** The spin prevents the bullet from wobbling or tumbling end-over-end in the air.
- **Range:** It significantly increases the distance the projectile can travel.
- **Penetration:** The stability and spin allow for greater power of penetration upon hitting the target.

#### **Medico-Legal Importance of Rifling**

- **Bullet Fingerprinting (Individual Identification):**
    - **Primary Markings:** Rifling leaves "class characteristics" (number, width, and direction of twist of lands and grooves) that identify the **make and model** of the gun.
    - **Secondary Markings:** Imperfections inside the barrel (metallic fouling) produce unique "individual characteristics" on every bullet. These allow experts to link a specific bullet to a specific gun using a **Comparison Microscope**.
- **Direction of Fire:** The shape of the **Abrasion Collar** (eccentric vs. concentric) on the skin helps determine the angle at which the rifled bullet struck the body.
- **Identification of Entry Wound:** The presence of a **Grease Collar** (dirt/lubricant from the bullet) and an **Abrasion Collar** are definitive signs of an entry wound.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The number of lands and grooves is fixed for a specific brand of weapon, but the fine striations (secondary markings) are unique to one single gun, much like a human fingerprint.

### **3. Choking: Detailed Study**

**Mechanism:** Choking involves narrowing the diameter of the shotgun barrel near the muzzle. This constriction usually occurs in the last ==7–10 cm== of the barrel.

#### **Classification (Degrees of Choking)**

The range of a shotgun increases with the degree of choking:

1. **Full Choke:** Most constriction; effective range ~40 yards.
2. **Modified Choke:** Moderate constriction; range ~35 yards.
3. **Improved Cylinder:** Slight constriction; range ~30 yards.
4. **Cylinder Bore:** No constriction (unchoked); range ~25 yards.

#### **Purpose of Choking**

- **Control Dispersion:** It prevents the pellets from spreading too quickly after leaving the barrel.
- **Range Extension:** By keeping the pellet mass compact for a longer distance, it increases the effective range.
- **Increased Velocity:** It increases the explosive force and velocity of the pellets.

#### **Medico-Legal Importance of Choking**

- **Estimation of Range:** In smooth-bore weapons, the **spread of pellets** is used to calculate the distance. As a "==rule of thumb==," the diameter of the pellet spread in inches is approximately equal to the distance in yards (for a full choke).
- **Weapon Identification:** The type of **Wad** (felt, plastic, or cardboard) found in the body can indicate the bore and gauge of the shotgun used.
- **Paradox Gun:** This is a hybrid weapon—a shotgun with rifling near the muzzle to increase range and accuracy while still firing pellets.

### **4. Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Section 39 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:** Relates to **Expert Opinion**. The findings of a ballistic expert regarding bullet markings (rifling) or pellet spread (choking) are admissible as evidence in court.
- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:** Firearm injuries are classified as **Grievous Hurt** under **Section 116** if they endanger life or cause permanent disfigurement.

---

### **5. High-Yield Exam Points**

- **Puppe’s Rule:** Helps determine the sequence of shots in the skull; a second fracture line will never cross a pre-existing one.
- **Balling (Welding) of Shot:** Occurs in shotguns when pellets fuse into a single mass due to faulty ammunition, causing a large, circular "rat-hole" entry wound at a range where dispersion is expected.
- **Maltese Cross Abrasion:** A specific skin marking caused by the petals of a plastic wad striking the skin at close range (~1–2 feet).

### **6. Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: What is the "Medullary Index" in ballistics?** **Model Answer:** This is a distractor! The Medullary Index refers to the ratio of hair medulla to shaft diameter. In ballistics, the relevant term is **Caliber** (bore diameter).

**Q2: Can you perform "Bullet Fingerprinting" on a shotgun pellet?** **Model Answer:** No. Since shotgun pellets are fired through a smooth-bore barrel, they do not acquire the spiral markings (lands and grooves) necessary for fingerprinting.

**Q3: Why is a "Comparison Microscope" used?** **Model Answer:** It is used to compare a "crime bullet" recovered from a victim with a "test bullet" fired from a suspect weapon to see if the secondary markings match.

### **7. Quick Revision Summary**

- **Rifling** = Spin + Stability + Individual ID + Rifled Guns.
- **Choking** = Constriction + Pellet Control + Range + Shotguns.
- **Caliber** = Distance between opposite Lands.
- **Shotgun Rule:** Spread (inches) ≈ Range (yards).
- **Key Law:** BSA 2023 Section 39 (Expert Opinion).
