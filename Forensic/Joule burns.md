[[Thermal deaths]]
For an MBBS student, **Joule Burn** (also known as the **electric mark** or _endogenous burn_) is a highly high-yield topic in Forensic Thanatology and Thermal/Electrical Injuries. It is frequently tested as a short-note question or in viva examinations due to its diagnostic value in cases of suspected electrocution.

---

### **Basic Concepts**

#### **Definition**

A **Joule burn** is a specific, localized, endogenous tissue injury produced at the point of entry of an electrical current during **low-voltage electrocution with firm contact**.

#### **Introduction**

- **The Electric Mark:** It is the pathognomonic physical signature left by an electric current on the skin.
- **Mechanism (Pathophysiology):** When a person makes firm contact with a live, low-voltage electrical source, the current meets the resistance of the skin. According to Joule's law of heating, this resistance converts electrical energy into thermal energy, resulting in localized ==tissue coagulation and blister formation== from the inside out (hence "endogenous" burn).
- **Resistance Factor:** Skin resistance plays a major role; dry skin provides much higher resistance than wet or moist skin, which affects the severity of the local mark.

---

### **Classification of Electrical Burns**

Electrical burns are classified based on the voltage, contact type, and presence of an air gap:

|**Type of Burn**|**Voltage & Contact Type**|**Key Characteristics**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Joule Burn**|Low-voltage, **firm contact**|Endogenous burn; oval/round/crater shape; depressed central blister; raised, pale peripheral area.|
|**Crocodile Burn**|High-voltage, **loose contact**|"Arc dancing"; multiple pitted or punched-out lesions resembling crocodile skin.|
|**Flash / Spark Burn**|High-voltage, **loose contact with air gap**|Exogenous burn; superficial singing or flash marks.|

---

### **Clinical and Autopsy Features of a Joule Burn**

#### **External Genital/Somatic Examination**

- **Shape:** Typically **oval, round, or crater-like** in appearance.
- **Central Blister:** The center of the burn features a **depressed blister**.
- **Peripheral Margin:** The edge surrounding the blister is **raised, pale, and shows blanching** (loss of color due to local vascular spasm).

#### **Histopathology (Under the Microscope)**

If a biopsy of the Joule burn is taken, the following classic microscopic findings are observed:

1. **Coagulation of the dermis** due to thermal energy.
2. **Elongation of epidermal cells** (classically described as "streaming" or "palisading" of nuclei).

---

### **Other Postmortem Findings in Electrocution**

When performing an autopsy on a victim of electrocution, the medical officer should look for these systemic and localized findings:

- **Rigor Mortis:** Onset of rigor mortis is **unusually early**.
- **Muscle Injuries:** Localized hemorrhages and actual tears can be found in the skeletal muscles due to violent, tetanic contractions.
- **Hair:** May show **pit-like defects** and superficial arching/singeing.
- **Current Pearl:** Molten metal from the conductor may be driven deep into the soft tissues.
- **Bone Pearls / Wax Drippings:** Molten calcium phosphate forms bone pearls or "wax-like drippings" in the skeletal system, which are highly visible on radiological/X-ray investigations.
- **Myoglobinuria:** Severe skeletal muscle damage (rhabdomyolysis) leads to the excretion of myoglobin in the urine.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **★ Important Exam Point: Proof of Electrocution.** The Joule burn is the single most important piece of physical evidence to prove that a victim made contact with an electrical source while alive.
- **Determining Point of Entry:** Because it occurs at the point of contact, it maps the pathway of the current through the body.
- **Distinction from Natural Death:** Electrocution can cause sudden death mimicking a myocardial infarction. Finding a Joule burn on the hand (point of contact) and exit marks on the foot (point of grounding) rules out natural death.
- **Manner of Death:** While most cases are accidental (industrial/household), the location of the burn helps reconstruct whether the death was suicidal, homicidal, or accidental.

---

### **Relevant Law (BNS, 2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023, Section 106:** Death by negligence. Frequently applied in cases of electrocution due to poorly maintained public wiring or municipal negligence (punishment: up to 5 years imprisonment +/- fine).
- **BNS, 2023, Sections 115 & 117:** Voluntarily causing hurt or **grievous hurt** using dangerous means (including electricity).
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest** in unnatural deaths. The autopsy surgeon must document the exact entry (Joule burn) and exit points to assist the Investigating Officer (IO).
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023, Section 39:** The histopathological findings of the Joule burn (coagulated dermis and elongated epidermal cells) are admissible as **Expert Opinion** in court.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Joule Burn:** Endogenous burn from firm contact with low voltage.
- ★ **Crocodile Burns:** Pitted/punched-out marks characteristic of high-voltage arc dancing.
- ★ **Dangerous Circuit:** Left hand (entry) to right foot (exit) or vice-versa, which forces the current directly through the heart (causing ventricular fibrillation).
- ★ **Bone Pearls:** Radiologically visible postmortem marker of high-voltage electrocution.

### **Viva Questions**

**Q1: What is the cause of death in electrocution?**  
**Answer:** It depends on the path of the current:

1. **Heart:** Ventricular fibrillation or cardiac arrhythmia.
2. **Brainstem:** Respiratory center failure.
3. **Diaphragm:** Spasm of the diaphragm leading to asphyxia.

**Q2: What histopathological features confirm a Joule burn?**  
**Answer:** Coagulation of the dermis and elongation (nuclear streaming) of the epidermal cells.

**Q3: Why is the center of a Joule burn depressed?**  
**Answer:** Because the electric current causes sudden localized steam formation, tissue dehydration, and shrinkage of the central necrotic skin, pulling it downward.

### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Joule Burn =** Low-voltage + Firm contact + Endogenous burn.
- **Gross Appearance:** Oval/crater shape, depressed center, raised pale/blanched margin.
- **Microscopic:** Coagulation of dermis + epidermal cell elongation.
- **Systemic Autopsy Signs:** Early rigor mortis, rhabdomyolysis, bone pearls (radiology).
- **Legal:** Reported under **Section 194 BNSS**; negligence prosecuted under **Section 106 BNS**.
