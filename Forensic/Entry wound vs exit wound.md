[[Firearm]]

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

- **Rifled Firearm:** A weapon with spiral grooves inside the barrel that impart a spin to the bullet for stability.
- **Entry Wound:** The site where the projectile enters the body, typically characterized by "collars".
- **Exit Wound:** The site where the projectile leaves the body, often showing more tissue damage and everted margins.
- **Beveling:** A sloped or slanting edge created as a bullet passes through bone. In the skull, this is the most diagnostic feature for distinguishing entry from exit.

---

### **2. Difference Between Entry and Exit Wounds (Skull & Head)**

When a bullet strikes the skull, it produces characteristic changes on the two tables (layers) of the bone.

#### **A. Comparison of Bone Changes (The Skull)**

|**Feature**|**Entry Wound (Skull)**|**Exit Wound (Skull)**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Outer Table**|**"Punched-in" appearance**; clean-cut hole.|**Beveled** (sloped outwards).|
|**Inner Table**|**Beveled** (sloped inwards); appears larger than outer.|**"Punched-out" appearance**; clean-cut hole.|
|**Fracture Lines**|Fissured fractures may radiate from the hole.|Often more extensive shattering if the bullet has deformed.|

#### **B. Comparison of General Characteristics**

|**Feature**|**Entry Wound**|**Exit Wound**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Size**|Usually **smaller** than the bullet diameter (due to skin elasticity).|Usually **larger** than the entry wound and the bullet.|
|**Margins**|**Inverted** (tucked inward).|**Everted** (pushed outward) or puckered.|
|**Abrasion Collar**|**Present** (reddish-brown rim of abraded skin).|**Absent** (except in "Shored Exit" wounds).|
|**Grease/Dirt Collar**|**Present** (gray ring from bullet lubricant).|**Absent**.|
|**Bleeding**|Relatively **less**.|Relatively **more**.|
|**Fat/Soft Tissue**|No protrusion.|**Protrusion** may be present.|
|**Fibers of Clothes**|Turned **inward**.|Turned **outward**.|

★ **Important Exam Point (The Head):** While entry wounds are typically small, a **Contact Shot** to the head is often **large and stellate (star-shaped)** due to the "Blowback Phenomenon".

### **3. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Determination of Range:** The presence of secondary signs (burning, blackening, tattooing) on the entry wound helps estimate the distance of fire.
- **Direction of Fire:** The shape of the **eccentric abrasion collar** (wider on one side) indicates the angle at which the bullet struck the body.
- **Manner of Death:** Contact wounds at "sites of election" (temple, mouth) often suggest suicide, while distant or multiple wounds may suggest homicide.
- **Puppe’s Rule:** Helps determine the sequence of shots by analyzing intersecting fracture lines on the skull.

### **4. Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:**
    - **Section 2(14):** Defines **Injury**.
    - **Section 103:** Punishment for **Murder** (Homicide).
    - **Section 116:** Defines **Grievous Hurt** (Firearm injuries are often classified here if they cause permanent damage).
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023:**
    - **Section 194:** Relates to **Police Inquest** in firearm deaths.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:**
    - **Section 39:** Allows the **Expert Opinion** of the forensic pathologist and ballistics expert to be admissible in court.

### **5. High-Yield Exam Concepts**

1. **Blowback Phenomenon:** In a contact shot to the skull, gases expand between the scalp and bone, causing the skin to burst outward into a stellate wound.
2. **Shored Exit Wound:** If the skin at the exit site is supported by a firm object (e.g., a wall or tight belt), it will develop an abrasion collar, mimicking an entry wound.
3. **Tattooing (Stippling):** An **antemortem phenomenon**; it cannot be wiped off, proving the person was alive when shot.
4. **Cherry-Red Tissues:** Seen in the entry track due to the formation of carboxyhemoglobin (CO+Hb) from the discharge gases.

### **6. Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: What is "internal beveling"?** **Answer:** It is the inward sloping of the margins of a skull fracture seen at the site of bullet entry.

**Q2: Why is the exit wound usually larger than the entry wound?** **Answer:** The bullet often becomes deformed, tumbles, or picks up bone fragments (secondary missiles) as it traverses the body, causing more damage at the exit point.

**Q3: Can an exit wound have an abrasion collar?** **Answer:** Yes, but only in a **Shored Exit Wound**, where the skin is pressed against a firm surface at the moment of exit.

### **7. Mnemonics for Entry Wound Signs**

#### **"B-A-G-T"**

- **B** – **B**lackening (Soot - can be wiped off).
- **A** – **A**brasion Collar (Permanent).
- **G** – **G**rease Collar (Bullet Wipe).
- **T** – **T**attooing (Stippling - cannot be wiped off).

---

### **8. Quick Revision Notes**

- **Entry:** Inverted margins, collars present, beveled inner table.
- **Exit:** Everted margins, collars absent, beveled outer table.
- **Contact (Head):** Stellate shape, soot inside track, CO-Hb (cherry red).
- **Law:** BSA Sec 39 for Expert Opinion; BNS Sec 103 for Murder.
