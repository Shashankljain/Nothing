[[Trace evidence]]

legal dispute where the biological fatherhood of a child is questioned, requiring scientific and legal determination of parentage.

---

## **2. Indications (Why & When It Arises)** ❓

Disputed paternity cases typically arise in civil and criminal courts under four circumstances:

1. **Adultery & Divorce:** When a child is born during a lawful marriage, but the husband denies being the father and sues for divorce.
2. **Blackmail:** When an unmarried mother accuses a wealthy or specific man of fathering her child to extort money.
3. **Maintenance Claims:** When a mother claims financial support for an illegitimate child under the law.
4. **Suppositious Child (Property Fraud):** When a woman simulates pregnancy and presents a fraudulent/kidnapped child to claim a share in her deceased husband's estate.

## **3. Forensic Methods of Determination** 🔬

### **A. Blood Grouping (Paternity Exclusion)**

Traditional serology **can never definitively prove paternity**, but it can **100% exclude** a wrongly accused man. It relies on two rules:

- ★ **First-Order Exclusion:** The child possesses a blood group gene (antigen) that is completely absent in both the mother and the alleged father.
- ★ **Second-Order Exclusion:** The alleged father is homozygous for a gene, but that gene is completely absent in the child.

### **B. DNA Fingerprinting (The Gold Standard)** 🧬

- ★ **Absolute Determination:** DNA profiling (using STR or VNTR markers) is the **only method that provides 100% absolute certainty** for both inclusion and exclusion of paternity.
- _Principle:_ Every band in the child's DNA fingerprint must be present in either the biological mother's or the biological father's profile.

## **5. Relevant Law (Latest 2023 Forensic Codes)** ⚖️

- ★ **Section 116 BSA, 2023 (formerly Sec 112 IEA) – Presumption of Legitimacy:**
    - _The Law:_ A child born during a valid marriage or within **280 days of its dissolution** (the mother remaining unmarried) is considered **conclusive proof of legitimacy** [input_file_15.png].
    - _Scientific Rebuttal:_ This presumption can only be challenged if the husband proves "non-access" or if the court orders **DNA fingerprinting** to reveal the biological truth [input_file_15.png, 247, 260].
- ★ **Section 144 BNSS, 2023 (formerly Sec 125 CrPC) – Maintenance Claims:** Legally mandates a father to support his wife and children. Courts routinely order DNA profiling under this section to resolve parentage disputes before awarding maintenance.
