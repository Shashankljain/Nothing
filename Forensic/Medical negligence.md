[[Medical law]]
### **Medical Negligence (Professional Malpraxis)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Medical Negligence** is defined as the absence of reasonable skill and care, or the willful negligence by a medical practitioner during the treatment of a patient, which results in ill-health, injury, or death [1, 69; 2, 16]. 

Negligence can occur in two ways:
1.  **Omission:** Failing to do something that a reasonable and prudent doctor would do (e.g., not performing a sensitivity test before injecting penicillin).
2.  **Commission:** Doing something that a reasonable and prudent doctor would not do (e.g., performing a major surgery in a poorly equipped clinic).

**Importance in Medico-Legal Practice**  
★ **Important Exam Point:** For a doctor to be held liable, it must be proved that they owed a "Duty of Care" to the patient and that their actions directly led to the damage [1, 69; 2, 16].

---

#### **Ingredients of Medical Negligence (The 4 Ds)**

To prove a case of medical negligence in a court of law, the following four essential ingredients must be established [1, 69; 2, 16]:
![[image 63.jpg]]

1.  **Duty of Care:** A legal obligation exists once the doctor accepts a patient for treatment. The doctor cannot refuse treatment based on caste, religion, or creed.
2.  **Dereliction of Duty:** The doctor failed to meet the standard of care expected of a "reasonable" practitioner of similar qualifications. This is a breach of duty [1, 69; 2, 16].
3.  **Direct Causation:** The negligent act must be the **proximal cause** of the injury. The damage must not be an unavoidable complication of the disease itself [1, 69; 2, 16].
4.  **Damage:** The patient must have suffered actual harm, which may include monetary loss, pain/suffering, worsening of condition, disability, or death [1, 69; 2, 16].

#### **Doctrines of Medical Negligence**

*   **Res Ipsa Loquitur (★ High-Yield):** Means "The thing speaks for itself." It is applied when the negligence is so obvious that no expert witness is required to prove it (e.g., surgery on the wrong limb or wrong patient) [1, 70; 2, 17].
*   **Bolam Test:** A doctor is not negligent if they acted in accordance with a practice accepted as proper by a responsible body of medical men [1, 69; 2, 18].
*   **Bolitho Test:** A modification of the Bolam test stating that the body of medical opinion must be reasonable, responsible, and logical.
*   **Vicarious Liability:** An employer (hospital) is responsible for the negligent acts of their employees (doctors/nurses) committed during their employment [1, 71; 2, 17].

#### **Precautions for Doctors to Avoid Negligence**

**Precautions Against Litigation: "C-O-N-S-E-N-T"**

- **C** – **C**onfidentiality & **C**ounseling
- **O** – **O**rganized documentation (record-keeping)
- **N** – **N**otifying police (where legally bound) [24, 33 BNSS]
- **S** – **S**tandard clinical protocols
- **E** – **E**quipped setup for surgical interventions
- **N** – **N**ever exceed limits of training (timely referral)
- **T** – **T**aking written informed consent

1.  **Documentation:** Maintain meticulous medical records. The legal dictum is: **"If it is not in the record—it did not occur"**.
    *   Indoor records must be kept for **3 years**.
    *   Medico-legal registers must be kept for **10 years**.
2.  **Informed Consent:** Always obtain **written-informed consent** before major procedures, explaining the nature, risks, and alternatives [1, 73; 2, 15].
3.  **The 72-Hour Rule:** A doctor must provide a copy of medical records to the patient or their authorized representative within **72 hours** of a request.
4.  ** **confidentiality**/Professional Secrecy:** Maintain patient confidentiality except in cases of "Privileged Communication" (e.g., reporting a notifiable disease or suspected crime under **Section 33 BNSS**).
5.  **Standard Protocols:** Always follow standard, accepted clinical guidelines and do not attempt procedures beyond your competency [1, 69; 2, 18].

#### **Legal Defenses Against Negligence**

If a doctor is charged, they may use the following defenses [1, 71-72; 2, 17]:
*   **Denial:** Claiming there was no Duty of Care or that no Dereliction of Duty occurred [1, 71; 2, 17].
*   **Error of Judgment:** If the doctor chose one of many recognized treatment methods, but it failed, they are not held liable if they acted in good faith [1, 72; 2, 17].
*   **Therapeutic Misadventure:** An unintentional, accidental, and unavoidable mishap during treatment despite all precautions [1, 72; 2, 17].
*   **Contributory Negligence:** When the patient’s own negligence (e.g., not taking medicines or following instructions) contributed to the damage [1, 70; 2, 16].
*   **Res Judicata:** A person cannot be tried twice in the same court for the same offence [1, 71; 2, 17].
*   **Res Indicata (Limitation Period):** In India, a patient must file a civil negligence case within **2 years** of the incident.

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

*   ★ **Section 106 BNS:** Death by negligence (up to 2 years prison).
*   ★ **The 4 Ds:** Duty, Dereliction, Direct Cause, Damage.
*   ★ **Res Ipsa Loquitur:** 100% proof of negligence without expert testimony.
*   ★ **72 Hours:** The deadline to provide medical records to a patient.

#### **Viva Questions**

1.  **What is the "Last Clear Chance Doctrine"?**  
    *   *Ans:* It means the doctor had the final opportunity to prevent a complication but missed it, making them responsible [1, 70; 2, 16].
2.  **Can a doctor refuse a patient in an emergency?**  
    *   *Ans:* No. Under the Principle of Beneficence and Section 30 BNS, treatment should be provided immediately in life-threatening emergencies [1, 65; 2, 15].
3.  **What is "Penal Erasure"?**  
    *   *Ans:* It is the "professional death sentence" where a doctor's name is removed from the medical register due to serious misconduct [1, 65; 2, 13, 21].
