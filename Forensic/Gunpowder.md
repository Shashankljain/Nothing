# 🔫💨 **GUNPOWDER / PROPELLANT CHARGE (3-MARK MODEL ANSWER)** 💨🔫

---

### **1. Definition** 📚

- **Gunpowder (Propellant Charge):** A low-explosive chemical mixture loaded into a cartridge that, when ignited by the primer, burns rapidly to produce a massive volume of high-pressure gas, driving the projectile (bullet or pellets) out of the firearm barrel.

---

---

### **3. Fineness & Power (Grading)** ⚙️

- ★ **Important Exam Point (Mnemonic):** The size of the gunpowder grains is designated as **FG, FFG, or FFFG**[118, Slide 34].
- The relationship is: \[\text{FG} < \text{FFG} < \text{FFFG}\]
- **The Rule:** As the number of **"F"s increases**, the fineness of the powder increases, the surface area increases, and it **burns much faster** with greater explosive power [118, Slide 34].

---

### **4. Medico-Legal Importance (MLI)** ⚖️🩺

1. **Estimation of Range of Fire:** Combustion of gunpowder leaves distinctive residue on the skin or clothing:
    - **Blackening (Soot):** Caused by deposited carbon soot. Absent beyond \(30\text{ cm}\) in handguns.
    - **Tattooing (Stippling):** Caused by unburnt/partially burnt gunpowder grains embedding in the skin. Extends up to a maximum range of \(45\text{ to }60\text{ cm}\) (arm's length) in handguns.
2. **Differentiating Entry vs. Exit Wounds:** Gunpowder deposits (burning, blackening, tattooing) are **present only around entry wounds** (and inside the wound track in contact shots). They are strictly **absent in exit wounds**.
