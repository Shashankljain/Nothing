[[Identification]]
### **Tattoo Marks**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** **Tattoos** (derived from the Tahitian/Polynesian word _tatau_, meaning 'to mark or strike') are designs made in the skin by multiple small puncture wounds with needles dipped in coloring matter.

**Introduction** A tattoo is a form of permanent body modification. For a tattoo to be permanent, the pigment must be deposited into the **dermis** layer of the skin. If the pigment only involves the epidermis, it will disappear as the skin cells shed.

**Dyes Used** Various pigments are used to create different colors:

- **Indigo, Prussian blue, Cobalt:** Blue/Black shades.
- **Vermilion, Cinnabar:** Red.
- **Cadmium/Selenium:** Yellow/Orange.
- **Carbon/India Ink:** Black.

---

#### **Classification of Tattoos**

The American Academy of Dermatology distinguishes five main types of tattoos:

| **Type**                | **Description**                                                                                                                                              |
| :---------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Traumatic (Natural)** | Unintentional tattoos resulting from ==injuries== (e.g., road traffic accidents) or close-range firearm discharge (unburnt gunpowder/pencil lead).           |
| **Amateur**             | Applied at home by ==non-professionals== using a needle and a single color (usually carbon-based India ink) at varying depths.                               |
| **Professional**        | Created by trained ==artists== using modern machines and multiple colors, applied uniformly beneath the skin.                                                |
| **Cosmetic**            | Also known as "permanent makeup"; used to ==camouflage== skin discolorations, scars, or to tattoo "hair follicles" in bald areas.                            |
| **Medical**             | Used for medical purposes, such as indicating a blood group, identifying radiation fields for cancer therapy, or medical alert tattoos (e.g., for diabetes). |

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Why are Tattoos Significant?** Tattoos are considered "Primary Characteristics" of identification because they are often unique and remain identifiable even after death.

1. **Identification of Deceased:** Crucial for identifying unknown dead or decomposed bodies. They can reveal the person's name, spouse's name, or date of birth.
2. **Religion and Nationality:** Designs like the Cross (Christian) or Lord Krishna (Hindu) can indicate a person's religious background.
3. **Political and Social Affiliations:** Emblems like the "hammer and sickle" or specific gang emblems help determine political or group associations.
4. **Profession/Occupation:** Coal miners may have visible coal dust tattoos on their face/hands; some gangs have specific professional tattoos.
5. **Behavioral Characteristics:** Tattoos may be associated with high-risk behaviors, including drug use (e.g., "addict" type marks to conceal injection sites) or sexual fanatical designs.
6. **Place of Origin:** Certain designs are common to specific races or regions (e.g., chest tattooing common among the Japanese).

#### **Autopsy and Identification Findings**

**Identification in Decomposed Bodies**

- Tattoos are recognized even in ==badly== ==decomposed== bodies or those recovered from water where the epidermis is lost, as the pigment remains in the dermis.
- **★ Important Exam Point:** Since pigment migrates to the body's lymph nodes, identification can be made by examining the ==axillary lymph nodes== for pigmentation during an autopsy, even if the skin is missing.

**Identification of Faded Tattoos** A latent or faded tattoo can be made visible by:

- Rubbing the part and examining with a magnifying lens.
- High-contrast photography or UV/Infra-red photography.

---

#### **Erasure and Removal**

Tattoos can be removed through several methods, though they often leave a scar:

- **Surgical:** Dermabrasion (using salts like zinc chloride), complete excision, or skin grafting.
- **Laser:** **Q-switched Nd:YAG laser** is the modern standard; it vaporizes dye particles.
- **Chemical:** Caustic substances like a mixture of ==**papain in glycerin**== produce a superficial scar.

---
#### COMPLICATION- 
- Infection and sepsis
- Transmission of STD
- Regional lymph node can be involved

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Dermis:** The layer of skin where permanent tattoo pigment is deposited.
- ★ **Lymph Nodes:** Tattoos can be identified by pigment in regional lymph nodes during autopsy.
- ★ **Shark Arm Case (1935):** A famous forensic case where a tattoo of two boxers on a vomited-up human arm led to the identification of a murder victim.
- ★ **Gold/Platinum Chloride:** Used for chemical dyeing or "corneal tattooing" in medical practice.

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Why do tattoos persist after death?**
    - _Ans:_ Because the pigment is deposited in the dermis, which is more resistant to initial decomposition than the epidermis.
2. **How can you identify a tattoo if the skin has putrefied?**
    - _Ans:_ By looking for pigment in the regional lymph nodes (e.g., axillary nodes for arm tattoos).
3. **What is a "Traumatic Tattoo"?**
    - _Ans:_ A tattoo caused by foreign particles (like gravel or gunpowder) getting embedded in the skin during an accident or blast.
4. **Can a tattoo indicate a person's drug habit?**
    - _Ans:_ Yes, "addict" type tattoos are often used to conceal needle injection sites.

#### **Quick Revision Notes**

- **Permanency:** Needs pigment in **Dermis** + protected by clothing + specific colors.
- **Dyes:** Black (Carbon), Red (Vermilion), Blue (Indigo).
- **Key Use:** Absolute identification of unknown/decomposed remains.
- **Complications:** Septicemia, Hepatitis B, HIV, and Leprosy.
- **Identification tip:** Use **Infrared/UV rays** for faded marks.
