[[Gen toxicology]]
### **Basic Concepts** 🧪

#### **Definition**

**Gastric lavage** (commonly known as stomach washing, stomach pumping, or gastric irrigation) is the mechanical process of sequentially ==administering== and ==aspirating== fluid to empty the stomach of unabsorbed poisons.

#### **The Golden Rule: Timeline**

- ★ **Important Exam Point:** Gastric lavage is **most useful within 1 hour** of poison ingestion.
- It is generally not recommended if the patient presents **more than 2 hours** post-ingestion, if the poison/dose is non-life-threatening, or if the patient has already vomited.

---

### **Procedure & Methodology** 🏥

#### **Patient Positioning (Critical Step)**

To safely perform gastric lavage, the patient must be placed in **two specific positions**:

1. **Left Lateral Decubitus Position:** The patient lies on their left side.
    - _Why?_ In this orientation, the pylorus of the stomach points upward, which mechanically prevents the poison from passing through the pyloric sphincter into the small intestine during the wash.
2. **Trendelenburg Position:** The body is laid supine with the feet elevated higher than the head by 15–30 degrees.
    - _Why?_ This places the mouth at a lower level than the larynx, aiding respiratory drainage and preventing ==accidental aspiration== of stomach contents into the lungs.

```
                     [PATIENT ON LEFT SIDE & HEAD TILTED DOWN 15-30°]
                                            │
                                            ▼
                           [Pass Orogastric Tube into Stomach]
                                            │
                                            ▼
                       [Confirm Tube Placement via Bubbling Test]
                                            │
                                            ▼
                     [Sequentially Administer 5 mL Fluid/kg Body Wt]
                                            │
                                            ▼
                  [Aspirate: Save FIRST 500 mL Washout for FSL]
                                            │
                                            ▼
                   [Repeat Sequential Wash Until Fluid Runs Clear]
```

#### **Apparatus (Tubes Used)**

- **Ewald or Boas Tube:** A non-collapsible rubber tube of 1 cm diameter and 1.5 metres in length. It features a filter funnel at one end, a suction bulb in the middle to pump out contents, and a rounded tip with lateral openings.
- **Mouth Gag:** A wooden mouth gag is forcefully inserted to keep the mouth open in uncooperative or comatose patients.
- **Tube Sizes:** An orogastric tube of **36–40 French** is used for adults (**22–28 French** for children). Alternatively, a **Ryle's tube** can be inserted nasally.

#### **Lavage Fluids**

- **Tap water** is acceptable for adults; **normal saline** is mandatory for infants to prevent electrolyte imbalance.
- **Chemical neutralisers** left in the stomach after washing include:
    - _Potassium Permanganate (\(\text{KMnO}_4\) 1:5000):_ ==Oxidises== alkaloids (opium/morphine, strychnine, atropine).
    - _Sodium Bicarbonate (\(\text{NaHCO}_3\) 5%):_ Used to wash, but **contraindicated in corrosive acid poisoning** as it liberates \(\text{CO}_2\) gas, risking gastric rupture.
    - _Tannic Acid (4%):_ ==Precipitates== metals and alkaloids.
    - _Calcium Lactate (1–3%):_ Specifically precipitates oxalic acid as insoluble calcium oxalate.

#### **Confirmation of Tube Placement (To Prevent Lung Suffocation)**

Before infusing any fluid, the doctor must verify that the tube is in the stomach and not the trachea:

- **The Bubbling Test:** Force a little air down the tube using a syringe while listening through a stethoscope over the stomach. Bubbling sounds confirm gastric placement.
- **Tracheal warning signs:** If the tube enters the trachea, a ==hissing== ==noise== is heard at the outer end, conscious patients will cough reflexively, and air bubbles will escape if the outer end is dipped in water.

---

### **Contraindications (The "C" Rule)** ⚠️

★ **Important Exam Point:** Lavage is contraindicated in the following scenarios:

1. **C**orrosives (Strong mineral acids and alkalis).
    - _Reason:_ The esophagus and stomach are structurally weakened; inserting a tube risks immediate **mucosal perforation**.
    - ★ **THE CRITICAL EXCEPTION:** **Carbolic Acid (Phenol)**. Phenol causes coagulation necrosis which hardens and thickens the gastric wall (_"leathery stomach"_), making it structurally safe to perform a cautious gastric wash.
2. **C**onvulsant Poisons (e.g., Strychnine).
    - _Reason:_ The sensory stimulation of passing a tube can precipitate violent, fatal tetanic spasms.
3. **C**omatose / Unconscious Patients.
    - _Reason:_ The airway protective reflexes (cough, gag) are impaired, presenting an extreme risk of fatal aspiration.
    - _Protocol:_ Lavage can _only_ be performed if the airway is secured first with a cuffed **endotracheal tube**.
4. **C**o-ingestion of Volatile / Hydrocarbons (Kerosene, petrol, gasoline).
5. _Reason:_ These have extremely low viscosity; if the patient vomits or coughs during lavage, the liquid is easily aspirated, causing immediate chemical pneumonitis.
6. **Other medical factors:** Severe esophageal varices, hemorrhagic diathesis, or ingestion of hard foreign bodies/drug packets (risks package rupture).

---

### **Complications** 🚨

- **Aspiration Pneumonitis:** Most common complication, occurring in 10% of patients when gastric fluids flow into the lungs.
- **Perforation:** Esophageal or gastric tears (1% of cases).
- **Hypothermia:** From infusing massive volumes of cold water.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance** ⚖️

- **Evidence Collection:** The **first gastric aspirate (approximately 500 mL)** must be collected immediately, sealed, and sent to the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL). This sample is invaluable because it contains the highest concentration of unmetabolised parent poison.
- **Negligence & Evidence:** Under **Section 238 BNS, 2023** (formerly Sec 201 IPC), a doctor who intentionally fails to collect or preserve gastric lavage samples to shield an accused faces up to 7 years of imprisonment.
