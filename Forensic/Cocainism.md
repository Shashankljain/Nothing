### **Cocainism (Chronic Cocaine Poisoning)**
[[Deliriants]]
---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Cocainism**, also known as **Cocainomania** or **Cocainophagia**, is a state of chronic addiction resulting from the repeated use of cocaine, characterized by a morbid craving for the drug and progressive physical and mental deterioration.

**Introduction**  
Cocaine is a **deliriant cerebral neurotic poison** obtained from the dried leaves of the plant _**Erythroxylum coca**_. It is a colorless, odorless, crystalline substance with a bitter taste.

**Common Slang Names:**  
Snow, Coke, Crack, White Lady, Candy, and Bazooka.

**Toxicokinetics (Mechanism of Action)**

- **Hyperadrenergic State:** Cocaine blocks the reuptake of neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin), leading to their accumulation in the synaptic cleft.
- **The "Rush and Crush":** It produces an initial stimulatory effect (**"Rush"**) due to cortical stimulation, which is invariably followed by a period of depression (**"Crush"**).

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Aphrodisiac Myth:** It is mistakenly believed to increase libidinal drive and is used to prolong sexual acts by paralyzing sensory nerves in the genitalia.
- **Crime & Violence:** High association with binge drinking and violent crimes like homicide, assault, and robbery.
- **Body Packers/Mules:** Smugglers swallow packets of cocaine; packet rupture often leads to sudden lethal toxicity.
- **Stupefying Agent:** Occasionally used to facilitate robbery or kidnapping.

★ **Important Exam Point: Legal Responsibility**  
Under **Section 22 BNS (2023)**, involuntary intoxication provides a defense, but voluntary drunkenness is not an excuse for committing a crime.

---

#### **Relevant Law (BNS & BNSS, 2023)**

University examinations now require the application of the new criminal codes.

---

#### **Clinical Features of Chronic Cocainism**

Chronic users show a shifty gaze and a suspicious nature due to increasing paranoia.

**I. Physical Signs**

- **Nasal Septum Perforation:** A classic sign due to the powerful vasoconstrictive action of the drug when "snorted," leading to ischaemic necrosis.
- **The Tongue and Teeth:** Characteristically become **black**.
- **Ocular Signs:** Sunken eyes and dilated pupils (**Mydriasis**).
- **General:** Emaciation, anorexia (loss of weight), and digestive disturbances.

**II. Specific Syndromes**

1. **Magnan’s Syndrome (Cocaine Bugs):** ★ **High-Yield Point**. A tactile hallucination where the addict feels as if small insects or grains of sand are crawling under the skin (**formication**). This leads to self-inflicted scratches and cuts.
2. **Cocaine Washed-out Syndrome:** A state of deep lethargy or coma following binge use, caused by dopamine depletion.
3. **Crack Lung:** A hypersensitivity pneumonitis involving chest pain and cough after smoking cocaine.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Examination & Investigations**

**I. Physical Examination**

- Search for **track marks** (injection sites) in unusual areas like under the tongue or on the feet.
- Look for "crack pipe" burns on fingers or thumbs.

**II. Laboratory Analysis**

- **Scott Test:** A presumptive test where cobalt thiocyanate produces a **blue precipitate**.
- **Mandelin Test:** The reagent produces an **orange color**.
- **Urinalysis:** Cocaine metabolites (benzoylecgonine) can be detected for **2–4 days** post-exposure.

---

#### **Management**

★ **Important Exam Point: Management follows the ABCD protocol.**

1. **Stabilization:** Maintain airway and circulation.
2. **Control Agitation/Seizures:** Intravenous **Benzodiazepines** (Diazepam) are the drug of choice.
3. **Cardiac Care:** Propranolol or Esmolol for tachycardia and hypertension.
4. **Decontamination:** Gastric lavage with **Potassium Permanganate (1:5000)** if ingested.
5. **Body Packers:** Require immediate surgical intervention if there is leakage from ingested drug packets.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Magnan’s Syndrome:** Tactile hallucinations ("bugs under skin").
- ★ **Nasal Septum Perforation:** Result of chronic "snorting".
- ★ **Antidote:** No specific physiological antidote exists; treatment is symptomatic.
- ★ **Test:** Scott test (blue precipitate).
- ★ **Diagnosis Clue:** Black tongue and dilated pupils.

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is "Free-basing"?**
    - _Ans:_ Taking cocaine through the smoking route in a purified/concentrated form.
2. **How is a "Body Packer" different from a "Body Stuffer"?**
    - _Ans:_ Body packers swallow well-wrapped packets for smuggling; stuffers swallow loose drugs hurriedly to hide evidence from police.
3. **Why is Naloxone sometimes given in the "Coma Cocktail"?**
    - _Ans:_ Because many cocaine addicts use multiple drugs (speed-balling), and Naloxone reverses the opioid component.
4. **What is the "Double-condom" sign?**
    - _Ans:_ A radiological rim of air seen in X-rays of body packers between the layers of drug packaging.

# ❄️👁️☠️ **COCAINE POISONING (THE SYMPATHOMIMETIC TOXIDROME)** ☠️👁️❄️

---

### **Basic Concepts & Taxonomy** 📚

#### **Definition**

**Cocaine poisoning** is an acute, life-threatening sympathomimetic crisis caused by the ingestion, inhalation (snorting), smoking (freebasing/crack), or injection of benzoylmethylecgonine, a highly addictive alkaloid deliriant. It induces a profound hyperadrenergic state and severe central nervous system (CNS) excitation followed by cardiovascular and respiratory collapse.

#### **Introduction**

- **The Divine Shrub:** Cocaine is a naturally occurring alkaloid obtained from the dried leaves of _**Erythroxylum coca**_, a plant indigenous to South America (Peru, Bolivia) and Mexico.
- **Common Street Slangs:** Crack, coke, snow, white lady, pasta, bazooka, and snuff.
- **Forensic Classification:** It is classified as a **Cerebral Deliriant Neurotic Poison** and acts as a potent central nervous system stimulant and local vasoconstrictive anesthetic.
- **Illicit Preparations:**
    - _Cocaine Hydrochloride:_ The water-soluble salt form, typically snorted or injected.
    - _Freebase / Crack:_ The purified, lipid-soluble basic cocaine molecule produced by ether/alkaline extraction. It vaporises at low temperatures and is smoked, producing a characteristic cracking sound.

---

### **The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

1. **What?** A metabolic and cardiac emergency characterized by an acute, toxic catecholamine surge (sympathomimetic storm).
2. **Why?** Abused recreationally for its rapid, intense euphoric effects ("rush"); occasionally ingested in massive smuggling quantities leading to accidental leakage.
3. **How?** By blocking the presynaptic reuptake of monoamine neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin).
4. **When?** Symptoms manifest within **7 seconds** of smoking/inhalation, **15 seconds** of intravenous injection, and **10 minutes** of oral ingestion.
5. **Where?** Systemic target zones include the **cerebral cortex** (early excitement, late necrosis), the **myocardium**(infarction, vasospasm), and **skeletal muscles** (rhabdomyolysis).
6. **Who?** Classically encountered in recreational drug abusers ("cocainomaniacs") and international drug couriers (**"Body Packers"** or **"Mules"**).

---

### **Relevant Law (Latest 2023 Forensic Codes Update)** ⚖️

★ **Important Exam Point:** Replicating the updated **BNS, BNSS, and BSA (2023)** acts in place of old IPC, CrPC, and IEA codes is mandatory to secure maximum marks in university examinations [Image 15, Image 16].

#### **I. Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985**

Cocaine is strictly regulated under the NDPS Act [213, 218, Image 16]. Possession and trafficking carry severe penalties based on the seized quantity [Image 16]:

- ★ **Small Quantity (< 2 grams):** Punishable by rigorous imprisonment (RI) up to **6 months, a fine up to ₹10,000, or both** [Image 16].
- ★ **Commercial Quantity (> 100 grams):** Punishable by RI for **10 to 20 years and a fine of ₹1 to ₹2 Lakhs**[Image 16].

#### **II. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- ★ **Section 123 BNS (formerly Sec 328 IPC):** Punishes voluntarily causing **hurt by means of poison** or any stupefying drug with the intent to commit a crime [218, Image 15].
    - _Punishment:_ Up to 10 years imprisonment and a fine [Image 15].
- ★ **Section 286 BNS (formerly Sec 284 IPC):** Punishes **negligent conduct with respect to poisonous substances**[Image 15].

#### **III. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- ★ **Section 33 BNSS (formerly Sec 39 CrPC):** Mandates that every Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) **must immediately notify the police** of any suspected poisoning death or case [Image 15].
- ★ **Section 53(1) BNSS (formerly Sec 53 CrPC):** Empowers the police to request a **mandatory medical examination of the accused** (including body cavity searches for drug packets), utilizing reasonable force if necessary [Image 16].

#### **IV. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- ★ **Section 39 BSA (formerly Sec 45 IEA):** Admits the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL) quantitative toxicological report as **admissible Expert Opinion** in court [Image 15].

### **Clinical Features & Toxidromes** 🩺

Cocaine toxicity presents as a classic **biphasic cycle**: the **"Rush"** (excitation) followed by the **"Crush"** (depression).

#### **I. Stage of Excitement ("The Rush")**

- **CNS:** Extreme euphoria, talkativeness, hyperactivity, visual/auditory hallucinations, and acute delirium. Widespread muscle twitching and **tonic-clonic seizures** occur.
- **Pupils:** ★ **Profound Mydriasis (dilated pupils)** due to pupillary dilator muscle sympathetic stimulation.
- **Cardiovascular:** Severe tachycardia, acute hypertension, and life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Systemic:** **Profound Hyperthermia** (pyrexia) due to constant muscle contraction and hypothalamic resetting.
- **GIT:** Dryness of the mouth, vomiting, and hyperactive bowel sounds.

#### **II. Stage of Depression ("The Crush")**

- Occurs roughly **1 hour** post-exposure as synaptic catecholamines are exhausted.
- The patient becomes dull, lethargic, and enters a **profound coma with areflexia**.
- **Respiration:** Becomes slow, shallow (Cheyne-Stokes type), culminating in **apnea and respiratory failure**.
- **Cardiovascular:** Severe hypotension, bradycardia, and terminal cardiac arrest.

1. ★ **Magnan's Syndrome (Cocaine Bugs / Delusional Parasitosis):**
    - _Pathophysiology:_ A severe tactile hallucination (formication).
    - _Presentation:_ The addict experiences a vivid, terrifying sensation that **small insects ("bugs") or grains of sand are crawling under or on their skin**.
    - _Result:_ Patients create **severe self-inflicted fingernail excoriations, cuts, and scratches** in a desperate attempt to gouge out the imaginary parasites.
2. ★ **Cocaine Washed-Out Syndrome:**
    - _Pathophysiology:_ Follows prolonged binge use of cocaine, caused by the **complete exhaustion of central dopamine stores**.
    - _Presentation:_ A deeply lethargic, comatose state where the patient cannot speak or follow commands.
    - _Differentiating Feature:_ Unlike intracranial hemorrhages, **all reflexes, pupillary sizes, and cranial nerves remain completely normal**, and the patient can be easily aroused to full orientation temporarily.
3. ★ **Crack Lung:**
    - _Pathophysiology:_ An acute, localized hypersensitivity pneumonitis triggered within 1–48 hours of smoking crack cocaine.
    - _Presentation:_ Chest pain, dyspnea, hemoptysis, diffuse alveolar infiltrates, and pronounced **systemic eosinophilia**.

There is **no specific chemical antidote** for cocaine poisoning; management is entirely supportive, directed at controlling the hyperadrenergic state.

#### **II. Specific Therapeutic Guidelines**

1. **Gastric Decontamination:** Perform gastric lavage with **KMnO4 (1:5000)** if swallowed. Administer **Activated Charcoal (1 g/kg)** to physically adsorb remaining drug molecules [257, 739, Image 20].
2. ★ **Control of Convulsions & Agitation:** **IV Diazepam (0.1–0.5 mg/kg)** is the absolute drug of choice.
    - _Warning:_ **Physical restraints must be strictly avoided** because struggling against restraints in a hyperadrenergic state dramatically worsens rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and lactic acidosis.
3. **Hyperthermia Management:** Treat aggressively with ice water baths, cooling blankets, and fans.
4. ★ **Cardiovascular Warning (The Unopposed Alpha Trap):**
    - In treating cocaine-induced hypertension and tachycardia, **never administer a pure beta-blocker (like Propranolol) alone**.
    - _Rationale:_ Blocking only beta-2 receptors leaves alpha-1 receptors completely unopposed, causing **catastrophic coronary artery vasoconstriction and fatal myocardial infarction**.
    - _Solution:_ Use **Esmolol** (ultra-short-acting) or combined alpha/beta blockers (such as Labetalol).
5. ★ **Acidic Diuresis:** Cocaine is a basic drug. Its renal clearance is historically promoted via **Acidic Diuresis** using Ammonium Chloride or Vitamin C [404, Image 23]. (Note: This is clinically obsolete today due to risks of metabolic acidosis but remains highly testable in traditional theory exams [Image 23]).
6. **Dialysis:** Hemodialysis is **useful** and indicated in severe, life-threatening cocaine poisoning.

### **Medico-Legal Autopsy & Specimen Collection** 🔍🩺

#### **I. External Findings**

- Widespread signs of asphyxia (congested face, cyanosed fingernails, and dark purple postmortem staining).
- ★ **Magnan's Excoriations:** Multiple self-inflicted scratch marks and fingernail cuts across the arms and torso.
- ★ **"Mainlining" and "Skin Popping" Markers:** Linear needle scars over the antecubital fossa (IV drug abuse) or depressed circular scars (subcutaneous popping).
- Thermal burns on the fingers, thumbs, or face from hot crack pipes.

#### **II. Internal Findings**

- **Brain:** Pronounced cerebral edema, meningeal congestion, or massive intracranial hemorrhage.
- **Heart:** Subendocardial hemorrhages in the left ventricle, coronary artery thrombosis, or contraction-band necrosis.
- **GIT:** In cases of oral smuggling, multiple **intact or ruptured drug packages** may be found in the stomach or intestines.
    - _Dissection Rule:_ The stomach must be isolated using the **Double Ligature Method** and opened strictly along its **greater curvature** to preserve mucosal tracking [Page 2, Image 2].

- **Routine Viscera (Bottle 1 & 2):** Preserve the stomach with contents, liver, and kidneys in a **Saturated solution of common salt** [43, 49, Image 17].
- ★ **Blood (Bottle 3):** Collect 10 mL of blood strictly from the **femoral vein** [43, 49, Image 17].

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: Why are pure beta-blockers like Propranolol strictly contraindicated in acute cocaine toxicity?**  
**Answer:** Cocaine stimulates both alpha and beta-adrenergic receptors. If a pure beta-blocker is administered alone, it selectively blocks vasodilatory beta-2 receptors, leaving alpha-1 vasoconstrictive receptors completely unopposed. This results in severe, unchecked coronary artery vasospasm, precipitating fatal myocardial infarction or acute hypertensive crisis.

**Q2: What is Magnan's Syndrome, and how does the patient physically respond to it?**  
**Answer:** Magnan's syndrome is a pathognomonic tactile hallucination (formication) seen in chronic cocaine abusers. The patient experiences a terrifying sensation that small insects ("cocaine bugs") are crawling under their skin. In a desperate attempt to scratch out these imagined parasites, they inflict severe, deep cuts and scratches on their own skin.

**Q3: Why must postmortem blood samples for cocaine analysis be preserved in Sodium Fluoride?**  
**Answer:** Cocaine is highly unstable in blood and is rapidly broken down by plasma pseudocholinesterase enzymes postmortem. **Sodium fluoride (10 mg/mL)** is a powerful enzyme inhibitor that arrests this postmortem enzymatic degradation, preserving the parent drug and its active metabolites for accurate FSL analysis.

---

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

- **Preserving Cocaine Blood in Standard Saline/Salt:** Blood must be preserved in **Sodium Fluoride (10 mg/mL)**with potassium oxalate [46, Image 17]. Saturated common salt is strictly for solid viscera [49, Image 17].
- **Recommending Physical Restraints for Seizures:** This is a dangerous mistake. Restraining a patient in a hyperadrenergic state causes isometric muscle struggle, catastrophically worsening hyperthermia, lactic acidosis, and lethal rhabdomyolysis. **IV Diazepam** must be used instead.
- **Confusing Pupil Reactions:** Writing that cocaine causes miosis (constricted pupils). Opioids, OPCs, and organophosphates cause miosis [234, 301, Image 15]; **Cocaine causes pronounced Mydriasis (dilated pupils)**.
