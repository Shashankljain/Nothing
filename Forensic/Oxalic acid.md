# 🧪💀 **OXALIC ACID POISONING: UNIVERSITY EXAMINATION-ORIENTED GUIDE** 💀🧪

---

### **Basic Concepts** 📚

#### **Definition**

**Oxalic Acid (commercially known as "Acid of Sugar")** is a highly toxic, colorless, organic corrosive acid. It acts locally as a corrosive on skin and mucous membranes, and systemically (remotely) after being absorbed into the bloodstream.

#### **Introduction**

- **Physical Properties:** It occurs as **colorless, transparent, prismatic crystals** that physically resemble Magnesium Sulfate (\(MgSO_4\)) and Zinc Sulfate (\(ZnCl_2\)).
- **Natural Sources:** It is a naturally occurring component of plants and is found in high levels in **dark-green leafy vegetables** (e.g., spinach, rhubarb, parsley, beet leaves, and purslane).
- **Commercial Uses:** It forms soluble chelates with iron, making it highly useful for:
    - Removing bloodstains and rust stains.
    - Cleaning metals and flushing car radiators.
    - Dyeing and bleaching processes.
    - Removing signatures or writings from documents in cases of **forgery**.

#### **The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

1. **What?** An organic corrosive poison that causes tissue destruction locally and hypocalcemia and renal failure systemically.
2. **Why?** Ingested most commonly by accident due to its close physical resemblance to common salts like Epsom salt (\(MgSO_4\)) or sodium bicarbonate.
3. **How?** It destroys tissue locally through corrosive action and systemically by **chelating serum calcium**, forming insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that block renal tubules.
4. **When?** Symptoms manifest immediately (within minutes) in large doses (fulminating form) or over days in chronic/delayed forms.
5. **Where?** Local damage occurs in the mouth, esophagus, and stomach; systemic effects target the cardiovascular, neuromuscular, and renal systems (specifically the proximal convoluted tubules of the kidneys).
6. **Who?** Suicidal and homicidal ingestions are rare due to its sour/bitter taste; it is mostly seen in accidental ingestions or used as an abortifacient to procure illegal abortions.

1. **Local Corrosive Action:** Causes moderate coagulative necrosis, protein precipitation, and mucosal bleaching of the upper gastrointestinal tract.
2. **Systemic Calcium Chelation:** Once absorbed, oxalic acid reacts with ionized calcium in the extracellular fluid to form **insoluble calcium oxalate**. This rapidly depletes serum calcium levels, causing **profound hypocalcemia** and cardiovascular collapse.
3. **Mechanical Renal Blockade:** The precipitated calcium oxalate crystals lodge in the **proximal convoluted tubules (PCT)** of the kidneys, causing mechanical blockage, acute tubular necrosis, uremia, and renal failure.

### **Clinical Features** 🩺

The clinical presentation is divided into three highly testable clinical forms:

#### **1. Fulminating Poisoning**

- Occurs with the intake of a large dose (**>15 g**).
- Causes immediate burning, sour, or bitter taste in the mouth with a sense of constriction around the throat.
- Severe burning pain radiates from the mouth down to the stomach and over the entire abdomen.
- **Vomiting:** Persistent vomiting of altered blood and mucus, presenting a characteristic **"coffee-ground" (blackish) appearance** due to acid-hematin formation.
- Presents with severe thirst, diarrhea, rapid electrolyte imbalance, shock, and death within minutes.

#### **2. Acute Poisoning**

- The clinical picture is dominated entirely by **severe hypocalcemia**:
    - Tingling and numbness of the fingers, toes, and mouth.
    - Paresthesia, muscle weakness, and **carpopedal spasms** (the hand flexes at the wrist and the thumb adducts to the palm—the _Accoucheur's hand_ or **Trousseau sign**).
    - Hyperirritability of peripheral nerves (**Chvostek/Weiss sign**).
    - **Tetany**, generalized convulsions, dilated pupils, metabolic acidosis, ventricular fibrillation, coma, and death.

#### **3. Delayed Poisoning**

- Occurs if the patient survives the initial hypocalcemic crisis.
- Characterized by acute nephritis, scanty urine (oliguria), hematuria, albuminuria, and **oxaluria**.
- Microscopic examination of urine reveals pathognomonic **envelope-shaped calcium oxalate crystals**.

#### **II. Specific Antidote Therapy**

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Specific Precipitants:**
    - **Oral Antidotes:** **Limewater, Calcium Lactate, Calcium Gluconate, or Calcium Chloride** given orally at a dose of **150 mg/kg**. These act as specific chemical antidotes by reacting with free oxalic acid in the stomach to precipitate it as completely insoluble, non-absorbable **calcium oxalate**, which is safely excreted in the feces.
    - **Systemic Antidotes (For Tetany):** **Calcium Gluconate** (10 mL of a 10% solution administered slowly IV, repeated frequently based on clinical response).
    - **Hormonal Antidote:** **Parathyroid Extract** (100 units IM) to help mobilize bone calcium stores to combat severe hypocalcemia.

#### **III. Gastrointestinal Decontamination & Supportive Care**

1. **Cautious Gastric Lavage:** Performed using a **1% to 3% Calcium Lactate** solution (or 2 teaspoons of calcium lactate per lavage) to precipitate the acid in the stomach before it can absorb.
2. **Demulcent Drinks:** Give milk, egg white, or starch water to coat the mucosal linings.
3. **Bowel Elimination:** Administer enema bowel washes and saline purgatives (e.g., \(MgSO_4\)) to flush out the precipitated crystals.
4. **Extracorporeal Elimination:** **Hemodialysis** and exchange transfusion are highly effective and life-saving, particularly when acute tubular necrosis and uremia develop.

### **Autopsy & Postmortem Findings** 🔍🩺

#### **I. External Findings**

- Usually non-specific. Occasional chemical burns or staining may be present on the face, chin, and lips.
- Rigor mortis may appear early due to systemic calcium and ATP depletion.

#### **II. Internal Findings**

1. **Upper GIT:** Mucosa of the mouth, tongue, pharynx, and esophagus appears bleached, white, and corroded.
2. **Stomach:**
    - Mucosa is intensely congested, reddened, and punctate due to erosions, giving it a characteristic **velvety-red or blackish appearance**.
    - The gastric wall is softened and thinned, but **no perforation** is typically seen.
    - Contains gelatinous, dark-brown material due to **acid-hematin** formation.
3. **Kidneys:**
    - Swollen, pale, and heavily congested.
    - ★ **Histopathology:** Shows extensive acute tubular necrosis, primarily involving the **proximal convoluted tubules (PCT)**, heavily packed with birefringent, **envelope-shaped calcium oxalate crystals**.
4. **Other Viscera:** Generalized, intense congestion of all internal organs.

#### **III. Viscera Preservation Guidelines**

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Preservative Selection:** Harvested viscera must be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit**.
- _Note:_ Saturated common salt is _not_ preferred for general organic acid toxicities because alcohol is the superior tissue stabilizer for these organic components.

### **Medico-Legal Importance** ⚖️

- **Accidental Ingestions:** Most cases are purely accidental. Because oxalic acid resembles Epsom salt (\(MgSO_4\)) or sodium bicarbonate, it is frequently swallowed by mistake.
- **Homicidal/Suicidal Rarity:** Suicides and homicides are extremely rare due to the acid's highly sour, bitter, and repulsive taste.
- **Criminal Abortifacient:** It was historically introduced into the vagina or taken orally as a uterine stimulant/irritant to procure criminal abortion.

---

### **Relevant Law (BNS & BNSS, 2023 Update)** ⚖️

- ★ **Section 123 BNS (formerly Sec 328 IPC):** Voluntarily causing hurt by means of poison with intent to commit an offense.
- **Section 286 BNS (formerly Sec 284 IPC):** Negligent conduct with respect to poisonous substances (punishes improper storage of household cleaning acids).
- ★ **Section 33 BNSS (formerly Sec 39 CrPC):** Mandatory duty of every Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) to immediately report all cases of suspected poisoning to the police.

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: Why are oral calcium salts considered specific antidotes in Oxalic Acid poisoning?**  
**Answer:** Calcium salts (lactate, gluconate, chloride, or limewater) react directly with unabsorbed oxalic acid in the gastrointestinal tract to form **calcium oxalate**. Because calcium oxalate is completely insoluble and non-absorbable, it prevents systemic absorption of the toxin and is safely excreted in the feces.

**Q2: What is "oxaluria" and what is the characteristic shape of the crystals under a microscope?**  
**Answer:** Oxaluria is the excretion of oxalic acid crystals in the urine. Under light microscopy, they appear as highly diagnostic, birefringent, **envelope-shaped (or octahedral) calcium oxalate crystals**.

**Q3: Can we perform gastric lavage in Oxalic Acid poisoning?**  
**Answer:** Yes. While gastric lavage is absolutely contraindicated in strong mineral acids (like \(H_2SO_4\) or \(HNO_3\)) due to perforation risks, a cautious gastric lavage is indicated in oxalic acid poisoning. It is performed using a **1% to 3% calcium lactate** solution to precipitate and remove the toxin.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

- **Writing "Gastric Lavage is Contraindicated":** Students often write that lavage is banned because oxalic acid is a corrosive. They must remember that **cautious lavage with calcium lactate is indicated** because the stomach wall is softened but rarely perforates.
- **Preserving Viscera in Saturated Salt:** Oxalic acid is an organic acid; therefore, the viscera should be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit**, not common salt.
- **Confusing the Spot Urine Crystals:** Describing the crystals as needle-shaped. (Needle-shaped crystals are associated with Phenol or Calcium Sulfate; Oxalic acid produces **envelope-shaped** crystals).

#### **2 Marks: Define Oxaluria and state its diagnostic significance.**

**Answer:** Oxaluria refers to the excretion of oxalic acid crystals in the urine. On microscopic examination of the urine sediment, they appear as pathognomonic, birefringent, **envelope-shaped calcium oxalate crystals**. This is highly diagnostic of the delayed phase of Oxalic Acid poisoning.

#### **5 Marks: Describe the clinical features of acute and delayed Oxalic Acid poisoning.**

**Answer:**

1. **Acute Phase (Hypocalcemic Tetany):**
    - Caused by systemic calcium chelation, leading to profound hypocalcemia.
    - Features include tingling and numbness of limbs, paresthesia, and muscle weakness.
    - **Carpopedal spasm** (Accoucheur's hand/Trousseau sign) and hyperirritability of motor nerves (Chvostek sign) are highly characteristic.
    - Can progress to convulsions, dilated pupils, metabolic acidosis, ventricular fibrillation, coma, and death.
2. **Delayed Phase (Nephritis/Uremia):**
    - Occurs 2 days to 2 weeks post-ingestion due to renal tubular crystal deposition.
    - Characterized by scanty urine (oliguria), hematuria, albuminuria, and **oxaluria** (envelope crystals under the microscope).
