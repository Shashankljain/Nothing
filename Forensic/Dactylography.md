[[Identification]]
### **Dactylography (Fingerprinting)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** **Dactylography**, also known as **Dermatoglyphics** or the **Galton system**, is the study of fingerprints as a method of identification. It involves the study of the friction ridge skin patterns on the fingers, palms, and soles.

**Introduction** A fingerprint match is widely accepted as one of the most reliable forms of identification. The dermal ridges appear for the first time between the **12th and 16th week** of intrauterine life (IUL) and their formation is completed by the **24th week** (6th month).

**Principles of Dactylography (★ Important Exam Point)**

1. **Individuality:** No two individuals, including identical (monozygotic) twins, have the same fingerprints.
2. **Permanency:** Ridges remain constant throughout life and are unaltered even after death until decomposition sets in.
3. **Persistence:** The probability of two individuals having the same fingerprint is approximately **1 in 1 billion** (Quetlet rule).

#### **Classification of Fingerprint Patterns**

![[image 70.jpg]]

|**Pattern Type**|**Frequency**|**Characteristics**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Loop (Ulnar/Radial)**|**60–70%**|Most common type; ridges enter from one side, recurve, and exit from the same side; has **one core and one delta**.|
|**Whorl**|**30–35%**|Ridges make a complete 360° circuit around the center; has **one core and two deltas**.|
|**Arch**|**5–10%**|Ridges enter from one side and exit from the other; has **no core and no delta**.|
|**Composite**|**2–3%**|Least common; contains two or more different patterns (e.g., a loop and a whorl).|

- **Core:** The ridge curving on itself at the center.
- **Delta (Triradius):** The point where ridges from three directions converge.

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Criminal Identification:** Identifying criminals whose prints are found at a crime scene.
- **Identification of Deceased:** Identifying unknown dead bodies, especially in mass disasters or when bodies are decomposed/mutilated.
- **Civil Applications:** Used for licensing (passports, firearms), detection of bank forgeries, and preventing the exchange of newborn infants in hospitals.
- **Biometric Attendance:** The National Medical Commission (NMC) has mandated the **Aadhar Enabled Biometric Attendance System (AEBAS)** for faculty and residents to monitor absenteeism.
- **Sex Determination:** Amino acid content in fingerprints can sometimes be used to determine the sex of an individual.
- Detection of drug use- The secretion of the fingerprint contain residue of chemicals and their Metabolite

#### **Recording and Examination Procedure**

**1. Recording of Fingerprints**

- **Plain (Dab) Impression:** Obtained by gently pressing the inked finger tip onto paper.
- **Rolled Impression:** Taken by rolling the inked finger from one side to the other to record the full ridge pattern.
- **Standard Practice:** For legal documents in India, it is customary to take the **left thumb** impression for males and the **right thumb** for females.

**2. Development of Latent Prints (Invisible Prints)** Latent prints are invisible to the naked eye and require processing to be seen.

- **Powder Method:** Dusting with milled aluminum, brass, or charcoal.
- **Chemical Method:** Using **Ninhydrin** (produces purple color) or **Silver Nitrate**.
- **Fuming Method:** **Iodine fuming** or **Superglue fuming** (cyanoacrylate).

**3. Comparison Protocol (ACE-V)** The analysis follows four stages: **A**nalysis, **C**omparison, **E**valuation, and **V**erification. By convention, **12 points of fine comparison** were traditionally required for proof of identity, though modern digital systems may use 4–5 points.

---

#### **Conditions Affecting Fingerprints**

- **Temporary Loss:** Swelling of fingers (e.g., bee stings) or skin diseases like dermatitis.
- **Permanent Loss:** Seen in **Leprosy**, electric injuries, radiation damage, and certain genetic disorders (e.g., Adermatoglyphia).
- **Alteration:** Ridge atrophy can occur in Celiac disease or due to certain chemotherapy drugs like Capecitabine.

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Loop:** Most common pattern (70%).
- ★ **Kolkata (1897):** Site of the world's first Fingerprint Bureau.
- ★ **Poroscopy:** The study of sweat gland pores on the ridges, introduced by **Edmond Locard** (1912).
- ★ **ACE-V:** Acronym for the examination protocol.
- ★ **Identical Twins:** Have different fingerprints despite identical DNA.

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Do identical twins have the same fingerprints?**
    - _Ans:_ No. While their DNA is the same, fingerprints are influenced by environmental factors in the womb and are unique.
2. **What is a "Latent" print?**
    - _Ans:_ It is an invisible print left by sweat/sebum that requires chemical or powder development to become visible.
3. **Which pattern has no core and no delta?**
    - _Ans:_ The Arch pattern.
4. **What is the minimum number of comparison points for a match?**
    - _Ans:_ Historically 12, but modern experts/systems accept 4–5.
5. **Can fingerprints be permanently lost?**
    - _Ans:_ Yes, in conditions like Leprosy or deep scarring that involves the dermal layer (1–2 mm deep).

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Dactylography:** Study of fingerprints for absolute identification.
- **History:** Galton (systematized), Herschel (first used), Henry (classification).
- **Patterns:** Loop (70%), Whorl (30%), Arch (5%).
- **Formation:** Completed by 24 weeks IUL.
- **Law:** BNSS allows collection; NMC mandates AEBAS.
- **Poroscopy:** Study of sweat pores on ridges (Locard).
- **Integrity:** Unique even in twins; permanent unless dermal damage occurs.
