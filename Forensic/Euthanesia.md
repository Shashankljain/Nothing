[[Medical law]]
### **1. Definitions**

- ★ **Euthanasia (Mercy Killing):** It is defined as the intentional, painless, and peaceful termination of the life of a patient suffering from a terminal, painful, and incurable disease, undertaken for their benefit to relieve them from agonizing suffering [image/slide].
- **Etymology:** Derived from two Greek words:
    - _Eu_ = **Good**
    - _Thanatos_ = **Death** (Hence, literally translates to "a good death") [81, 82].
- ★ **Right to Life vs. Right to Die:**
    - **Article 21 of the Indian Constitution** guarantees the fundamental **"Right to Life and Personal Liberty"** [image/slide].
    - The Supreme Court of India has repeatedly ruled that Article 21 **does not include the right to die** (except for passive euthanasia under strict judicial and medical supervision). Active euthanasia violates Article 21 [image/slide].

---

### **2. The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

1. **What?** The act of ending a terminal patient's life painlessly to stop intractable physical suffering [image/slide].
2. **Why?** Undertaken to relieve patients from terminal agony when medical science can no longer offer a cure.
3. **How?** Executed either **actively** (via lethal injections like phenobarbitone, thiopental, or potassium) or **passively** (withholding or withdrawing ventilators, feeding tubes, or ICU support) [image/slide, 223, 224].
4. **When?** Considered only in cases of documented, irreversible vegetative states (PVS), brainstem death, or incurable terminal malignancies [87, 89, image/slide].
5. **Where?** Heavily regulated; passive euthanasia is permitted in hospital environments under strict medical boards, whereas active euthanasia is legally prohibited in India [image/slide].
6. **Who?** Involves the **Patient** (possessing a Living Will), the **Treating Doctor**, **Autonomous Medical Boards**, and the **Judicial Magistrate** [image/slide].

## **PART II: CLASSICAL CLASSIFICATION OF EUTHANASIA** 📊

Euthanasia is classified based on the **mechanism of action** and the **will of the patient**:

```
                              [CLASSIFICATION OF EUTHANASIA]
                                            │
         ┌──────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                     ▼
 [Based on Action]                                                     [Based on Patient's Will]
   • **Active (Aggressive)**: Lethal drugs.                             • **Voluntary**: Patient's explicit consent.
   • **Passive (Negative)**: Withdraw life-support.                    • **Non-Voluntary**: Patient unconscious (Coma).
                                                                       • **Involuntary**: Against patient's will.
```

### **1. Based on the Mechanism of Action** 🧪

#### **A. Active Euthanasia (Positive / Aggressive Euthanasia)**

- **Mechanism:** Actively and deliberately administering a lethal substance or performing an intervention to cause immediate, painless death [image/slide].
- ★ **Commonly Used Agents:**
    - **Phenobarbitone (Most Common / M/C)** [image/slide]
    - **Thiopental Sodium (IV)** (Dutch/Belgian protocol: Thiopental to induce coma, followed by pancuronium bromide to paralyze muscles) [224]
    - **Morphine / Opium** [215, 216]
    - **Potassium Chloride (KCl)** (induces cardiac arrest in diastole) [image/slide]
    - **Insulin** (causes fatal hypoglycemia) [image/slide]
- **Legal Status in India:** **Strictly illegal** and prosecuted as criminal homicide [image/slide].

#### **B. Passive Euthanasia (Negative Euthanasia)**

- **Mechanism:** Painlessly letting a patient die by **withholding (not starting) or withdrawing (stopping)** life-sustaining medical interventions (e.g., turning off a mechanical ventilator, disconnecting an ICU monitor, or removing a feeding tube) [image/slide].
- _Historical Landmark:_ The **Netherlands** was the first country in the world to legalize passive euthanasia [image/slide].
- **Legal Status in India:** **Legally permitted** under strict medical and judicial guidelines [image/slide].

---

### **2. Based on the Will of the Patient** 🗣️

- ★ **Voluntary Euthanasia:** Carried out at the explicit, competent, and persistent request of the patient themselves (using a _Living Will_ or verbal request) [image/slide].
- ★ **Non-Voluntary Euthanasia:** Done when the patient is physically or mentally **incapable of giving valid consent** or expressing their will (e.g., patients in a deep coma, Persistent Vegetative State [PVS], or anencephalic infants) [87, image/slide].
- ★ **Involuntary Euthanasia:** Done **without the consent of the patient** or against their explicitly expressed will [image/slide]. Medically and legally, this is equated directly to **murder**in all jurisdictions.

## **PART III: LIVING WILL (ADVANCED MEDICAL DIRECTIVE)** 📜✍️

### **1. Definition & Core Rules**

- ★ **Living Will:** A legal, document written by a competent individual specifying their treatment options, preferences, or refusal of life-sustaining treatment for a future time when they might be terminally ill and unable to express their consent [image/slide].
- ★ **Eligibility Criteria:**
    1. The individual must be **at least 18 years of age** [image/slide].
    2. Must be of **sound mind (compos-mentis)** at the time of executing the document [image/slide, 160].
- ★ **Formalities & Safeguards:**
    - Must be signed in the presence of **two independent witnesses** [image/slide].
    - Must designate a **Nominated Representative** (either a relative or treating doctor) who is authorized to make decisions on their behalf [image/slide].
    - Must be **attested by a 1st Class Judicial Magistrate** [image/slide].
    - ★ **The Recency Rule:** If a person executes multiple living wills over their lifetime, **only the last-executed living will is legally valid** [image/slide].

---

### **2. Step-by-Step Clinical Implementation of a Living Will** 🔄

When a terminally ill patient with a Living Will is admitted, the hospital must follow a strict statutory protocol before withdrawing life support [image/slide]:

```
                     [STEPS FOR EXECUTING A PASSIVE EUTHANASIA]
                                        │
                                        ▼
                                [Treating Doctor]
                      (Certifies terminal/incurable state)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                             [Primary Medical Board]
                        (3 Doctors: 1 Specialist + 2 RMPs)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                            [Review Medical Board]
                        (3 Independent Doctors appointed)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                        [Attesting Judicial Magistrate]
                       (Verifies and authorizes withdrawal)
```

1. **Step 1 – Treating Doctor Evaluation:** The treating doctor certifies that the patient is suffering from a terminal or incurable disease with no realistic chance of recovery [image/slide].
2. **Step 2 – Primary Medical Board:** The hospital constitutes a Medical Board comprising **three doctors** (including a subject specialist and two senior RMPs) to verify the clinical status of the patient [image/slide].
3. **Step 3 – Secondary/Review Medical Board:** An independent, secondary medical board (constituted by the hospital/district health authority, also containing three doctors) reviews the findings of the primary board to prevent any collusion or error.
4. **Step 4 – Judicial Attestation:** The final decision is communicated to the **1st Class Judicial Magistrate**, who verifies the validity of the Living Will and legally authorizes the withdrawal of life support [image/slide].

## **PART IV: RELEVANT LAW & LEGAL ASPECTS** ⚖️

### **1. Relevant Sections under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

Any doctor performing **Active Euthanasia** or assisting in **Physician-Assisted Suicide** is criminally liable under the updated 2023 penal codes:

- ★ **Section 101 BNS (formerly Sec 302 IPC) – Punishment for Murder:**
    - _Application:_ Performing Active Euthanasia (e.g., injecting potassium chloride to end a patient's suffering) without a judicial mandate is prosecuted as **murder**.
    - _Punishment:_ Death penalty or life imprisonment, along with a fine.
- ★ **Section 105 BNS (formerly Sec 304 IPC) – Culpable Homicide Not Amounting to Murder:**
    - _Application:_ Framed if the court finds the doctor acted strictly out of mercy, but still caused death through an active, unauthorized procedure.
    - _Punishment:_ Imprisonment for 10 years to life.
- ★ **Section 108 BNS (formerly Sec 306 IPC) – Abetment of Suicide:**
    - _Application:_ Framed in cases of **Physician-Assisted Suicide (PAS)** where a doctor prescribes a lethal dose of a sedative (like barbiturates) and instructs the patient on how to self-administer it [223].
    - _Punishment:_ Imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine [52].

---

### **2. Decriminalization of Suicide Attempt: Mental Healthcare Act (MHCA), 2017** 🧠

- ★ **Section 115 MHCA, 2017:**
    - _The Law:_ This section states that notwithstanding anything contained in the penal code, any person who attempts to commit suicide shall be **presumed to have severe stress** and **shall not be tried or punished under the law** [201].
    - _Clinical Impact:_ Effectively abolished the prosecution of suicide attempts (formerly Section 309 IPC / Section 226 BNS) in India, aligning legal codes with modern psychiatric compassion.

---

### **3. Medico-Legal Importance (MLI) & Ethics** 🩺

- ★ **Professional Misconduct:** The National Medical Commission (NMC) issues a warning notice to all doctors. Participating in or executing **unauthorized active euthanasia is classified as serious professional misconduct (Infamous Conduct)**, leading to **Penal Erasure**[image/slide].
- ★ **International Declarations (Declaration of Venice):**
    - The World Medical Association (WMA) **Declaration of Venice on Terminal Illness** outlines that a physician's primary duty is to relieve suffering and maintain the dignity of terminal patients, but strictly prohibits active euthanasia [image/slide].

## **PART V: HIGH-YIELD EXAM CORNER** ★

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: What was the landmark case that legalized Passive Euthanasia in India?**  
**Answer:** The landmark case was **Aruna Shanbaug v. Union of India (2011)**, which legalized passive euthanasia under strict High Court guidelines. This was later streamlined and expanded in the **Common Cause v. Union of India (2018)** judgment, which recognized the legal validity of the "Living Will" (Advanced Medical Directive).

**Q2: Why is Potassium Chloride (KCl) an effective active euthanasia agent, and how does it act?**  
**Answer:** Potassium chloride increases extracellular potassium concentrations, obliterating the resting membrane potential of cardiac myocytes. This instantly stops electrical conduction, causing **cardiac arrest in diastole**.

**Q3: Can a doctor withdraw life support based on a verbal request from a patient's relatives?**  
**Answer:** No. A doctor cannot withdraw life support based on a verbal request. It requires either a pre-executed **written Living Will** validated by a 1st Class Judicial Magistrate, or a formal petition to the High Court, followed by authorization from two independent Medical Boards [image/slide].

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

- **Writing that Active Euthanasia is legal in India:** Students often confuse passive and active euthanasia. Always make it clear that **Active Euthanasia remains strictly ILLEGAL and is prosecuted as murder (Section 101 BNS)**.
- **Confusing the old IPC codes with 2023 BNS codes:** Under the newly implemented codes, make sure to write **Section 101 BNS** instead of 302 IPC, and **Section 108 BNS** instead of 306 IPC.
- **Not mentioning the attesting authority for a Living Will:** Always write that a Living Will must be attested by a **1st Class Judicial Magistrate** to carry legal value [image/slide].
