### **Procedure of Recording Evidence in Court**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**
**Definition of Evidence**  
**Evidence** means any statement, document, or object presented in the court by a witness in relation to a case under trial.

**Types of Evidence**
*   **Oral Evidence:** Given orally in the witness box.
*   **Documentary Evidence:** Includes medical certificates (fitness, death) and medico-legal reports (autopsy, rape).
*   **Direct Evidence:** Directly proves a fact (e.g., an eyewitness who saw the crime).
*   **Circumstantial (Indirect) Evidence:** Derived from circumstances that connect the accused to the victim (e.g., fingerprints at a crime scene).

---

#### **2. Legal Procedure: Step-by-Step** - ==143BSA==

1. Oath
2. Examination in chief
3. Cross examination 
4. Re examination 
5. Court question

The procedure for recording evidence in Indian courts follows a logical sequence to ensure a fair trial under the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**.

**Step 1: Attendance (Summons/Subpoena)**
*   A **Summons** is a document compelling a witness to attend court under penalty.
*   **Classification:**
    1.  *Subpoena ad testificandum:* To give oral evidence (for common witnesses).
    2.  *Subpoena duces tecum:* To bring and produce documents/records (for expert witnesses/doctors).

**Step 2: Oath or Affirmation**
*   Evidence is recorded under oath in the **witness box**.
*   The witness swears in the name of God to state "the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth".
*   ★ **Important Exam Point:** A **child below 12 years** of age is not required to take an oath.

**Step 3: The Three Stages of Examination**
Witnesses are questioned in the following order:

| **Stage** | **Who Questioned?** | **Objectives & Rules** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Examination-in-chief** | Lawyer of the party who called the witness. | To elicit all relevant facts and conclusions. **Leading questions are not allowed**. |
| **2. Cross-examination** | Lawyer of the opposite party. | To discredit the witness and test the accuracy of statements. ★ **Leading questions are allowed**. There is **no time limit**. |
| **3. Re-examination** | Lawyer of the party who called the witness. | To correct mistakes or clarify details made during cross-examination. **Leading questions are not allowed**. No new matter can be introduced without the judge's permission. |

---

#### **3. Relevant Legal Concepts**
*   **Leading Question:** A question that suggests the answer within itself (e.g., "Was the injury caused by a firearm?").
*   **Hostile Witness:** A witness who is not desirous of telling the truth or gives evidence against the party that called them.
*   **Perjury:** Giving false evidence under oath. Punishable under **Section 229 BNS** with up to 7 years imprisonment and a fine.
*   **Conduct Money:** Money paid to a witness in **civil cases** for travel and food expenses. In criminal cases, it is generally not paid, though doctors can request it.

### **Dying Declaration (DD)**

---

#### **1. Basic Concepts**
**Definition**  
A **Dying Declaration** is a statement made by a person who is dying, relating to the circumstances or causes which lead to their condition.

**Relevant Law**
*   **Section 26(1) BSA, 2023** (Formerly Section 32 of the Indian Evidence Act).
*   It is an **exception** to the rule that documentary evidence must be authenticated by oral evidence in court.

---

#### **2. Principles of Recording**
To be valid in court, the following principles must be followed:
*   **Compos Mentis:** The doctor must certify that the patient is of "sound disposing mind" (conscious and coherent) before recording.
*   **Language:** The statement must be recorded **verbally** in the victim's own language.
*   **Leading Questions:** No leading questions or suggestions should be given.
*   **Witnesses:** Once complete, it should be signed by the person recording it and **two witnesses**. The investigating police officer should **not** be present during the recording.
*   **Oath:** No oath is administered because it is believed that "a person will not meet his Maker with a lie in his mouth".

---

#### **3. Medico-Legal Importance**
*   **Admissibility:** It is admissible as substantive evidence if the person dies.
*   **Survival:** If the person survives, the statement loses its status as a dying declaration but can be used as **corroborative evidence** to support their testimony (under Section 157 IEA/BSA).
*   **Recorded By:** It is best if recorded by a **Magistrate**. However, if time is short, a doctor, police officer, or even a common person can record it.

---

#### **4. Comparison: Dying Declaration vs. Dying Deposition**
★ **Important Exam Point: Only Dying Declaration is practiced in India.**

| **Feature** | **Dying Declaration** | **Dying Deposition** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oath** | Not administered. | Administered. |
| **Recorded By** | Doctor/Police/Magistrate/Village head. | **Only Magistrate** records. |
| **Accused presence** | Accused/Lawyer is **not** present. | Accused/Lawyer **is** present. |
| **Cross-examination** | Not allowed. | Allowed. |
| **Prevalence** | Practiced in India. | **Not practiced in India** (USA/UK). |

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**
*   ★ **Summons:** Sections 61 to 69 CrPC/BNSS.
*   ★ **Leading Questions:** Allowed **only** in cross-examination.
*   ★ **Dying Declaration:** Admissible under **Section 26(1) BSA**.
*   ★ **Compos Mentis:** The most critical medical prerequisite for a valid DD.
*   ★ **Perjury:** Punishable under **Section 229 BNS**.

### **Quick Revision Summary**
1.  **Court Procedure:** Summons $\rightarrow$ Oath $\rightarrow$ Chief Exam (no leading questions) $\rightarrow$ Cross Exam (leading questions allowed) $\rightarrow$ Re-Exam.
2.  **DD Definition:** Statement by a dying person about the cause of their death.
3.  **DD Law:** Section 26(1) BSA.
4.  **DD Requirements:** *Compos mentis* certification, verbatim recording, two witnesses, no oath.
