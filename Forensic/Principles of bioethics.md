**Introduction & Definition** 📚

- ★ **Bioethics:** It is the branch of ethics that addresses the moral, social, and legal implications of healthcare, medical research, and biological sciences.
- In modern clinical practice, medical decisions are guided by **four primary pillars of bioethics** (originally popularized by Beauchamp and Childress) [46, Slide page 65].

---

**2. The Four Primary Principles** 📊

```
                          [THE 4 PILLARS OF BIOETHICS]
                                       │
         ┌─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                             ▼                             ▼
    [AUTONOMY]                   [BENEFICENCE]               [NON-MALEFICENCE]
 • Patient's right to choose. • Act in patient's best interest.• **Primum non nocere** (Do no harm).
                                       │
                                       ▼
                                   [JUSTICE]
                        • Fair distribution of care/resources.
```

1. ★ **Autonomy (Self-Rule):**
    - **Concept:** Refers to the absolute right of a mentally competent patient to retain control over their own body, make informed decisions, and choose or refuse treatment [46, Slide page 65].
    - _Practical Application:_ Forms the legal and ethical foundation for **Informed Consent** and the **Doctrine of Informed Refusal** [46, Slide page 74].
2. ★ **Beneficence (Do Good):**
    - **Concept:** The moral obligation of the healthcare provider to act in the **best interest of the patient** to promote wellness and alleviate suffering [46, Slide page 65].
    - _Practical Application:_ Developing treatment plans that offer maximal clinical benefits while actively helping the patient [46, Slide page 65].
3. ★ **Non-Maleficence (Do No Harm):**
    - **Concept:** The duty of the physician to **not intentionally inflict harm, injury, or pain** on the patient [46, Slide page 65].
    - _Key Maxim:_ Relies on the historic Latin rule: **"Primum non nocere"** (First, do no harm) [69, 46, Slide page 65].
4. ★ **Justice (Fairness):**
    - **Concept:** Treating all patients equally and fairly, ensuring a **just distribution of scarce healthcare resources**, benefits, and burdens regardless of age, gender, race, or social status [46, 47, Slide page 65].
