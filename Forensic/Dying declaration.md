[[legal procedure]]
### **Dying Declaration (DD)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
A **Dying Declaration** is a statement made by a person (the declarant) who is dying, relating to the cause of their death or the circumstances of the transaction which resulted in their death.

**Introduction**  
It is based on the legal maxim _“Nemo moriturus praesumitur mentiri,”_ which means "a person will not meet his Maker with a lie in his mouth" [Previous History]. It is an oral or written statement that becomes relevant when the person who made it is dead.

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Substantive Evidence:** It is admissible in court as ==direct evidence== even without the person being present for cross-examination [Previous History].
- **Proof of Crime:** It helps establish the identity of the ==assailant== and the ==manner== of the crime [Previous History].
- **`Exception` to `Hearsay`:** While generally "hearsay" evidence is not valid, a dying declaration is a major legal exception [Previous History].
- **Survivorship:** If the person survives, the statement is no longer a "Dying Declaration" but can be used as **==corroborative evidence==** in court.

★ **Important Exam Point:** The doctor’s primary role is to certify the **mental fitness** of the patient to make the statement.

#### **Procedure of Recording (How it is done)**

**1. Who can record?**  
It is best if recorded by a **Magistrate**. However, if time is short and the patient is sinking, it can be recorded by a **Doctor**, a **Police Officer**, or even a **common person**.

**2. The Doctor’s Duty: Compos Mentis**  
Before recording begins, the doctor must examine the patient and certify that they are **"Compos Mentis"**—meaning they are conscious, coherent, and have a "sound disposing mind".

**3. The Recording Process**

- **Language:** Should be recorded in the victim’s **own language** and **verbatim** (exact words).
- **Format:** No oath is administered.
- **Leading Questions:** The person recording must **not** ask leading questions (questions suggesting a "Yes" or "No" answer).
- **Signatures:** Once finished, it should be read over to the victim. It must be signed or thumb-printed by the **victim**and signed by the **person recording** it and **two witnesses**.
- **Police Presence:** The investigating officer should ==not== be present during the recording to avoid the suspicion of coaching the victim [Previous History].

#### **Comparison: Dying Declaration vs. Dying Deposition**

★ **Important University Question**

|**Feature**|**Dying Declaration**|**Dying Deposition**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Prevalence**|Practiced in **India**.|Practiced in **Developed Countries** (Not in India).|
|**Law**|Section **26 (1) BSA**.|Follows regular court rules of testimony.|
|**Recorded By**|Magistrate, Doctor, Police, or Public.|**Only** by a Magistrate/Judge.|
|**Oath**|**Not** administered.|Administered.|
|**Cross-examination**|**Not** done.|**Cross-examination** by defense lawyer is done.|
|**Presence of Accused**|Not required [Previous History].|Accused or their lawyer **must** be present.|
|**Legal Weight**|High, but inferior to Deposition.|**Superior** weight due to cross-examination.|

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Section 26 (1) BSA:** The specific section for DD in the new law.
- ★ **Compos Mentis:** The most vital medical requirement for a valid DD.
- ★ **Verbatim:** The statement must be recorded in the patient's exact words.
- ★ **Survival:** If the declarant survives, it is NOT a dying declaration.

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **Why is an oath not required for a Dying Declaration?**
    - _Ans:_ Because of the belief that a person who is about to die will not tell a lie [Previous History].
2. **What happens if a victim starts the statement but dies before completing it?**
    - _Ans:_ It is still valid if the recorded part is coherent and identifies the cause of death.
3. **Can a "dumb" witness give a dying declaration?**
    - _Ans:_ Yes, they can provide it through **writing or signs/gestures**.
4. **What is the "AEIOU" rule?** (Note: From sources, regarding death simulation/apparent death).
    - _Ans:_ A mnemonic for causes of **Suspended Animation** (Alcohol, Epilepsy, Injury, Opium, Uremia).

#### **Quick Revision Summary (One-Page)**

1. **Definition:** Statement by a dying person regarding their cause of death.
2. **Law:** Section 26(1) BSA, 2023.
3. **Medical Role:** Certify "Compos Mentis" before and after recording.
4. **How to Record:** Verbatim, own language, two witnesses, no oath, no leading questions.
5. **Validity:** Only valid if the person **dies**. If they survive, it is corroborative evidence.
6. **Superiority:** Dying Deposition is legally stronger but not practiced in India.
