[[Thermal deaths]]
**Scalds** are wet thermal injuries produced by the application of **moist heat**—such as hot liquids (water, oil, tea, tar), steam, or hot gases—to the outer or inner surfaces of the body12.

**Introduction**

- Unlike dry burns caused by flames, scalds do ==not== involve actual combustion or charring of tissue [Image 5].
- The severity of a scald is primarily determined by the 
1. **==temperature of the liquid==**, 
2. the **==duration of contact==**, and 
3. **==surface area==** involved.
- A minimum temperature of $44^\circ\text{C}$ **for 5–6 hours** is required to cause tissue damage, while hot liquids at $60^\circ\text{C}$ cause immediate tissue injury, and $70^\circ\text{C}$ causes a full-thickness scald [Image 5].

---

**Topic-Specific Explanation & Pathophysiology**

**Etiology (Causes)**

1. **Hot Water:** Most common cause in domestic settings (cooking accidents, bathing).
2. **Hot Oils/Fats:** Produce deeper and more severe injuries than water because oil has a ==higher== boiling point and ==adheres== tenaciously to the skin.
3. **Steam:** Causes severe, deep scalds, often involving the respiratory tract if inhaled.
4. **Molten Tar/Asphalt:** Adheres ==tightly== to the skin, causing ==prolonged heat transfer== and ==deep tissue destruction==.

**Mechanism of Injury**

- **Heat Transfer:** Moist heat transfers thermal energy to the skin, causing **coagulative necrosis** of the epidermal and dermal cells34.
- **Gravity-Dependent Flow (Dripping):** Liquids naturally flow downward under the influence of gravity [Image 5]. Hence, scald injuries show a **"trickle mark" or "dripping pattern"** pointing downwards, indicating the direction of liquid flow56.
- **Role of Clothing:** Scalding can occur through clothes [Image 5]. Clothes actually **worsen the damage** because they absorb and retain the boiling liquid against the skin, prolonging thermal contact and preventing rapid cooling [Image 5].

**Clinical Features & Local Examination Findings**

When examining a scald victim, the medical officer will observe the following characteristic findings [Image 5]:

- **Skin State:** The skin appears **sodden, bleached, or macerated** [Image 5].
- **Vesicles (Blisters):** Blisters are **present directly over the burnt area** [Image 5].
- **Line of Redness:** An erythematous inflammatory **line of redness** is present at the margins of the vesicles (indicative of a vital antemortem reaction) [Image 5].
- **Absence of Charring:** There is **no blackening, charring, or carbonisation** of tissue (since there is no dry flame) [Image 5].
- **Absence of Singeing:** Body hairs in the affected area are **not singed, shriveled, or clubbed** [Image 5]. Singeing requires the ==high temperatures of dry heat==7.
- **Absence of Ulceration:** True ulceration and deep necrotic sloughing are generally absent in acute scalds compared to chemical/corrosive burns [Image 5].
- **Scarring:** Scalds heal to form a **thin, smooth, and less contracted scar** [Image 5], unlike the thick, disfiguring, and highly contracted scars produced by dry burns or corrosive acids [72, Image 5].


**Medico-Legal Importance**

**1. Differentiating Manner of Death**

- **Accidental (Most Common):** Splashing of boiling water in kitchens, industrial steam bursts, or children pulling down hot vessels.
- **Suicidal (Rare):** Extremely painful and uncommon, though psychiatric patients may pour boiling water over themselves.
- **Homicidal:** Throwing boiling water, oil, or liquid asphalt onto a sleeping victim.

**2. Child Abuse (Battered Baby Syndrome)**

★ **Important Exam Point:**

- **The Glove and Stocking Pattern:** If a child's hands or feet are forcibly held down in a tub of hot water as punishment, it produces a highly characteristic **"glove and stocking" distribution of scald burns** with a sharp, straight line of demarcation [Image 11].
- Unlike accidental spills (which show irregular splash and trickle marks), forced immersion lacks splash marks on the rest of the body [Image 5, Image 11].

**Relevant Law (BNS, 2023)**

**1. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 115 BNS (formerly Sec 323 IPC):** Voluntarily causing **Hurt**. If a scald causes mild, temporary tissue injury without permanent damage, it is classified as simple hurt.
- **Section 117 BNS (formerly Sec 325/326 IPC):** Voluntarily causing **Grievous Hurt**. If the scald causes permanent disfigurement of the face or head, destroys vision, or disables the limb, it is prosecuted under Section 117 BNS with severe imprisonment.
- **Section 80 BNS (formerly Sec 304B IPC):** **Dowry Death**. If a married woman dies of suspicious scalds/burns within **7 years of marriage**, it is legally presumed to be a dowry death.

**2. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- **Section 184 BNSS:** Mandates the medical examination of a female victim to be conducted by a **female Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP)**.
- **Section 194 BNSS:** Relates to the **Police Inquest** for unnatural/suspicious deaths. In cases of suspicious scald deaths of a female within 7 years of marriage, a mandatory autopsy must be performed by a panel of **two government medical officers**.

**3. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 39 BSA:** Governs the admissibility of **Expert Opinion** (medical evidence and autopsy reports) in court proceedings.

**High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **First cavity opened in suspected thermal/asphyxial death:** The **Cranium (Skull)** to decompress cerebral blood vessels first [Image 2, 111].
- ★ **Surest Sign of Antemortem Burn/Scald:** **Line of redness (erythema)** and inflammatory cell infiltration at the margin [Image 7].
- ★ **Microscopic Hair Examination:** Scalds show **intact, normal hair follicles** with no singeing, vacuum vesicles, or brittleness, differentiating them from dry burns.
- ★ **Glove and Stocking Pattern:** Pathognomonic of forced immersion in child abuse [Image 11].

**Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: Why are body hairs not singed in a scald case?** **Answer:** Singeing of hair requires the high dry heat of a flame (>100°C) which curls and carbonises keratin. Moist heat (hot liquid) usually does not reach temperatures high enough to singe keratin before flowing off, leaving hair microscopic structure intact [90, Image 5].

**Q2: What is the clinical significance of clothing in scald injuries?** **Answer:** While clothing acts as a barrier to flame burns, in scalds it **worsens the injury**. Clothes absorb the boiling liquid and hold it close to the skin, prolonging the duration of heat transfer and worsening deep tissue damage [Image 5].

**Q3: How do you differentiate a scald scar from an acid burn scar?** **Answer:** A scald scar is **thin, smooth, and less contracted** [Image 5]. An acid burn scar is **thick, irregular, and firmly contracted** to deeper tissues [72, Image 5].

**Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **IPC vs. BNS Confusion:** Writing old IPC sections (323, 325, 304B) instead of the new **BNS, 2023** sections (**115, 117, 80**).
- **Confusing Hair Findings:** Stating that hair is singed in scalds. Hairs are **never singed in scalds**; this only occurs in dry burns and close-range firearm discharges [90, Image 5].
- **Misunderstanding Clothes:** Assuming clothes always protect against scalds. In reality, they trap the hot liquid and **worsen** the severity of the scald [Image 5].
