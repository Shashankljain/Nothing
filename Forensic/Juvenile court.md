[[legal procedure]]
### **Juvenile Justice Board (Juvenile Court)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** A **Juvenile Justice Board (JJB)** is a specialized judicial body constituted under the **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act** to deal with children in conflict with the law (juveniles).

**Introduction** In the Indian legal system, a **Juvenile** is defined as any person who has not completed **18 years of age**. The primary philosophy of the Juvenile Court is **reformation and rehabilitation** rather than punishment or retribution.

**Composition of the Board** The Board consists of three members:

1. A **First Class Judicial Magistrate** (who acts as the presiding officer).
2. Two **Social Workers** (at least one of whom must be a **woman**).

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Age Estimation (★ Important Exam Point):** Since the jurisdiction of the court depends entirely on the age of the accused, medical practitioners are frequently called to determine age using:
    - **Birth Certificates** (Primary evidence).
    - **Dental Examination** (Dentition status).
    - **Radiological Examination** (Ossification centers of long bones).
- **Psychiatric Assessment:** For juveniles aged **16–18 years** accused of **heinous crimes**, a medical board provides a psychiatric assessment to determine if the child had the mental capacity to understand the consequences of the offense.
- **Competency of Evidence:** Under **Section 118 BSA (formerly IEA)**, a child of any age can give evidence if the court is satisfied they understand the questions and can give rational answers, though no oath is required for those **under 12 years**.

---

#### **Relevant Law (BNS & BNSS, 2023)**

The new criminal codes define the limits of criminal responsibility for children:

|**Act**|**Section**|**Provision**|**Medico-Legal Significance**|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**BNS**|**20**|**Infancy (<7 years)**|A child under 7 is **not held criminally responsible** (_Doli incapax_).|
|**BNS**|**21**|**Child 7–12 years**|Responsibility depends on **attained maturity**; the court/doctor must assess if the child understood the nature of the act.|
|**BNS**|**103**|**Murder**|If committed by a juvenile, they are tried by the JJB unless they are 16–18 and the crime is heinous.|
|**BNSS**|**Inquest**|**Magistrate Inquest**|While not specific to JJB, Magistrate Inquests (Sec 196) are mandatory for deaths in "Mental Asylums" or custody, which may involve juveniles.|

---

#### **Classification of Offenses and Punishments**

The Juvenile Justice Board categorizes offenses to determine the reformative path:

|**Category**|**Definition**|**Punishment/Action**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Normal Offense**|Minor crimes.|Sent to a **Juvenile Home** for up to **3 years**.|
|**Serious Offense**|Crimes with 3–7 years prison for adults.|3 to 7 years in a reformatory.|
|**Heinous Crime**|Crimes with >7 years prison (e.g., Rape/Murder).|If the child is **16–18 years**, they may be **tried as an adult** after psychiatric assessment.|

★ **Important Exam Point:** No juvenile can be awarded the **death penalty** or **life imprisonment** without the possibility of release.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Juvenile Age:** Under 18 years.
- ★ **Criminal Immunity:** Absolute under **7 years** (Section 20 BNS).
- ★ **Board Composition:** 1 Magistrate + 2 Social Workers (1 female).
- ★ **The 16-18 Rule:** Heinous offenders in this bracket can be tried as adults.
- ★ **Reformatory:** Juveniles are sent to "Special Homes" or "Juvenile Homes," never regular adult jails.

---

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is the minimum age of criminal responsibility in India?**
    - _Ans:_ 7 years (Under Section 20 BNS).
2. **Can a 17-year-old be sentenced to death for murder?**
    - _Ans:_ No. Juveniles are exempt from the death penalty, though 16-18 year olds can face long-term detention in adult facilities if tried as adults for heinous crimes.
3. **How does a doctor help the Juvenile Court?**
    - _Ans:_ By estimating age (ossification/dentition) and conducting mental health assessments.
4. **Is a 10-year-old required to take an oath in court?**
    - _Ans:_ No. Children **below 12 years** are exempt from taking an oath.
