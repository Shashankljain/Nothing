[[Identification]]
### **Footprints (Podography)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Podography** is the study of footprints for the purpose of identification. A **Podogram** is the technical term for the recorded impression of a footprint.

**Introduction**  
The skin patterns found on the toes and heels are as **distinct** and **permanent** as those of the fingers (fingerprints). These dermal ridge patterns remain constant throughout an individual's life.

**Principles**  
Like fingerprints, footprint patterns are unique to every individual. Even in identical twins, while the genetic makeup is the same, the friction ridge patterns on the feet are distinguishably different due to environmental factors in the womb.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Why are Footprints Significant?**

1. **Prevention of Newborn Exchange:** Footprints of newborn infants are routinely taken in **maternity hospitals**immediately after birth to prevent the accidental or intentional exchange or substitution of infants.
2. **Criminal Identification:** Footprints found at a crime scene (primary or secondary) can be used to link a suspect to the location.
3. **Aviation Records:** Footprints are often recorded and kept for **air force flying personnel** to aid in identification in case of fatal crashes where bodies may be mutilated.
4. **Forensic Podiatry:** This specialized field uses clinical podiatric knowledge for person identification using:
    - Podiatry medical records.
    - Analysis of **gait forms** captured on CCTV cameras.
    - Comparison of foot impressions found **inside shoes** (one of the most valuable techniques).
5. **Identification of Deceased:** Footprints are considered "Primary Characteristics" of identification and remain unaltered even after death until decomposition destroys the skin.

#### **Examination and Procedure**

**1. Recording of Prints**

- In the living, the foot is cleaned and an impression is taken using printer's ink on unglazed paper.
- In the dead, if the skin is peeling due to putrefaction or drowning, it may come off like a "glove" or "stocking," which can be preserved in **10% formalin** to develop the prints later.

**2. Latent Footprints**

- Invisible prints left by sweat/sebum at a crime scene can be "visualized" using **UV light**, powders, or chemical reagents similar to those used for fingerprints.

**3. Comparison**

- Experts look for specific ridge characteristics (minutiae), scars, or deformities to establish a match.

---

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Permanency:** Footprint patterns are formed by the 24th week of intrauterine life and do not change until death.
- ★ **Maternity Hospitals:** The most common real-world application is the identification of neonates.
- ★ **Inside Shoes:** Foot impressions inside a suspect's shoes are highly individualistic due to the unique way each person's foot interacts with the footwear.
- ★ **Ridgeology:** The study of friction ridge identification on any volar area (palms or soles).

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is a Podogram?**
    - _Ans:_ It is a recorded impression of a footprint used for identification.
2. **Why are infants' footprints taken in hospitals instead of fingerprints?**
    - _Ans:_ Infants' fingers are very small and ridges are fine, making them difficult to record clearly. The foot provides a larger surface area for a more distinct print.
3. **Are footprints as reliable as fingerprints?**
    - _Ans:_ Yes, they have the same biological basis of uniqueness and permanency.
4. **What is 'Forensic Podiatry'?**
    - _Ans:_ It is a specialty that uses foot-related evidence (footprints, gait, shoes) to identify individuals in legal cases.

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Podography:** Study of footprints.
- **Key Use:** Newborn identification in hospitals and identifying air force personnel.
- **Reliability:** Unique even in identical twins; permanent for life.
- **Forensic Podiatry:** Includes gait analysis and shoe impression matching.
- **Law:** BNSS (2023) supports collection of these biometric measurements.
