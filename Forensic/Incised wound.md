[[Mechanical injuries]]
An **incised wound** (also known as a cut, slash, or slice) is one of the most common sharp force injuries encountered in medico-legal practice. For an MBBS student, understanding its characteristics is vital for determining the nature of the weapon, the direction of force, and the manner of death (suicide, homicide, or accident).

---

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** An incised wound is a clean-cut wound through the tissues (usually the skin and subcutaneous layers), where the **length is the greatest dimension** (longer than it is deep), caused by a sharp-edged instrument.

**Mechanism** It is produced by the application of **pressure and friction** against the tissue by an object with a sharp cutting edge or point.

**Causative Weapons**

- Knives (single-edged or double-edged).
- Razors, scalpels, or box cutters.
- Glass fragments (thin glass usually causes incised wounds; thick glass may cause a mix of incised and lacerated wounds).

**Classification of Sharp Force Injuries**

|**Type**|**Primary Characteristic**|
|:--|:--|
|**Incised Wound**|Length > Depth.|
|**Stab Wound**|Depth > Length or Width.|
|**Chop Wound**|Heavy weapon; combination of blunt and sharp force.|
|**Therapeutic Wound**|Produced by medical personnel (e.g., surgical incisions).|

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Weapon Identification:** The clean-cut margins indicate a **sharp-edged** weapon was used.
- **Direction of Force:** Determined by the phenomenon of **tailing** (see below).
- **Age of Injury:** Estimated by the appearance of the edges and the presence of a scab or scar.
- **Manner of Death:**
    - **Suicidal:** Often multiple, superficial, and in accessible parts (e.g., wrists, neck).
    - **Homicidal:** Often deep, deliberate, and found on non-accessible parts (e.g., back, face, genitals).
    - **Accidental:** Commonly seen on the hands.
- **Site of Impact:** Unlike bruises, the incised wound always indicates the exact point of contact.

★ **Important Exam Point:** Incised wounds are rarely life-threatening unless they occur over major superficial arteries, such as the wrist or the neck.

---

### **Relevant Law (2023)**

**Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 2(14):** Defines **Injury** as any illegal harm to body, mind, reputation, or property.
- **Section 114:** Relates to voluntarily causing **Hurt** (incised wounds are generally simple hurt).
- **Section 116:** Defines **Grievous Hurt** (if the wound causes permanent disfiguration of the face, head, or loss of an organ).

**Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- **Section 194:** Police Inquest—The medical officer reports the findings of the wound to the Investigating Officer.

**Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 39:** Expert Opinion—The doctor's findings regarding the weapon and manner are admissible in court.

### **Characteristics of an Incised Wound**

A medical officer must note these features to differentiate an incision from a laceration:

1. **Margins:** They are **clean-cut**, well-defined, and usually **everted**.
2. **Dimensions:** The **length** is the maximum dimension; it is always greater than the width or depth.
3. **Gaping:** The wound gapes more if it is perpendicular to **Langer’s lines** (cleavage lines of the skin).
4. **Hemorrhage:** Usually **profuse** because blood vessels are cut cleanly and cannot retract as easily as in blunt trauma.
5. **Tissue Bridging:** **Absent.** This is a critical diagnostic point—nerves and vessels are cut cleanly.
6. **Hair Bulbs:** Found to be **cleanly cut**.
7. **Bones:** A sharp weapon may leave a linear cut or a nick on the underlying bone.

---

### **Special Phenomena**

#### **Tailing**

- **Definition:** The wound is deeper at the point of commencement ("Head") and becomes progressively shallower toward the termination.
- **Significance:** It indicates the **direction** in which the weapon was moved.

#### **Beveling**

- **Definition:** An undermined or slanting edge produced when the weapon strikes the skin **obliquely**.
- **Significance:** It helps determine the **relative positions** of the assailant and the victim.

### **Dating/Age Estimation of an Incised Wound**

|**Duration**|**Features**|
|:--|:--|
|**Fresh**|Red with clotted blood.|
|**12 Hours**|Edges are red, swollen, and adherent with lymph.|
|**24 Hours**|Endothelial cells cover margins; a scab of dried lymph forms.|
|**36 Hours**|Capillary network is completed.|
|**2–3 Days**|Reddish-brown color; edges are weakly adherent.|
|**3–5 Days**|Fibrils and collagen fibers appear.|
|**7 Days**|Scab falls off; scar formation begins.|

### **Medico-Legal Differences: Suicidal vs. Homicidal Cut-Throat**

|**Feature**|**Suicidal Cut-Throat**|**Homicidal Cut-Throat**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Situation**|Usually front of neck; **left side** in right-handed persons.|Usually on the **sides** of the neck.|
|**Level**|**High**, above the thyroid cartilage.|**Low**, on or below the thyroid cartilage.|
|**Direction**|Oblique, above downwards.|Transverse or below upwards.|
|**Hesitation Cuts**|**Present** (multiple, shallow, parallel cuts).|**Absent**.|
|**Defense Wounds**|**Absent**.|**Present** (on palms or forearms).|
|**Severity**|Less severe; deep tissues often spared.|More severe; may cut through vertebrae.|
|**Vessels**|**Carotid artery often escapes** (head thrown back).|**Likely to be cut**.|
|**Weapon at Site**|Usually **present**.|Usually **absent** (taken by assailant).|

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Hesitation Cuts:** Also called tentative or trial cuts; multiple shallow incisions at the start of a suicidal wound.
- ★ **Fabricated Injuries:** Self-inflicted non-fatal incised wounds used to support a false charge (e.g., rape or assault).
- ★ **Laceration vs. Incision:** The presence of **tissue bridging** and **crushed hair bulbs** always proves a laceration.
- ★ **Signature Fracture:** A depressed skull fracture reflecting the shape of the weapon (often seen in chop wounds).

### **Viva Questions**

**Q1: Why is there more bleeding in an incised wound than a laceration?** **Answer:** In an incision, blood vessels are cut cleanly, which prevents the vessel walls from retracting and sealing off effectively. In a laceration, vessels are crushed and torn, which stimulates faster clotting.

**Q2: What are "Incised-looking Lacerated Wounds"?** **Answer:** When a blunt force strikes skin over a bony prominence (like the scalp or shin), the skin splits linearly, mimicking an incision. They are differentiated by looking for tissue bridges and crushed hair bulbs under a magnifying lens.

**Q3: What is "Seppuku" or "Harakiri"?** **Answer:** A ritualistic form of suicide involving a single large abdominal stab followed by a transverse pull, creating an L-shaped cut.

### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Definition:** Length > Depth; margins clean-cut; no tissue bridging.
- **Weapon:** Sharp-edged (knife, razor).
- **Tailing:** Indicates direction.
- **Hesitation marks:** Diagnostic of suicide.
- **Defense wounds:** Diagnostic of homicide.
- **Law:** BNS Sec 2(14), 114, 116.
