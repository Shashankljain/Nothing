[[Mechanical injuries]]
For an MBBS student, understanding **stab wounds** (also known as **punctured wounds**) is critical, as they are frequently encountered in criminal cases and are a favorite topic in university examinations.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Definition** A **stab wound** is a mechanical injury produced by the penetration of a long, narrow, pointed instrument into the depths of the body.

**Key Characteristic (★ Important Exam Point)** The most defining feature of a stab wound is that its **==depth is the greatest dimension==**, being significantly greater than both its length and width on the skin surface.

**Causative Weapons**

- **Most common:** Knives (single-edged or double-edged).
- **Others:** Scissors, ice picks, screwdrivers, daggers, or even broken glass fragments.

---

### **2. Classification of Stab Wounds**

Clinically and medico-legally, these wounds are divided into two types based on the extent of penetration:

| **Type**              | **Description**                                                                                                               |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Penetrating Wound** | The weapon enters a body cavity (e.g., chest or abdomen) but does not come out. There is **only an ==entry== wound**.         |
| **Perforating Wound** | The weapon passes "through and through" the body or an organ. There is both an **==entry== wound** and an **==exit== wound**. |

---

### **3. Medico-Legal Examination: Features to Note**

When examining a stab wound during an autopsy or in the casualty department, a medical officer must meticulously document the following features to assist in the investigation:

#### **A. Margins and Edges**

- **Appearance:** Usually **clean-cut**, regular, and well-defined.
- **Abrasions/Bruising:** Generally absent, but in cases of full penetration, a **==hilt-guard mark==** (patterned abrasion) may be seen where the handle struck the skin.

#### **B. Length, Width, and Depth**

- **Length:** Usually slightly **less** than the width of the weapon's blade because the skin stretches during penetration.
- **Width (Breadth):** Usually **more** than the thickness of the blade due to the ==gaping== of the wound edges.
- **Depth:** This is the most vital dimension. **★ Important Exam Point:** It is not safe to probe the depth of a wound in a living patient as it may ==disturb clots== and cause ==fatal hemorrhage==; it should only be determined during surgery or autopsy.

#### **C. Shape (Indicates the Weapon)**

The shape of the wound on the skin often reflects the cross-section of the weapon:

- **Single-edged knife:** Produces a **wedge-shaped** or triangular wound with one ==sharp== angle and one ==blunt==/rounded angle.
- **Double-edged knife:** Produces an **==elliptical or spindle-shaped==** wound with both angles being sharp.
- **Fish-tailing:** If a single-edged knife is used, the blunt back-edge may split the skin during withdrawal, producing a small split called "fish-tailing".
- **Round objects (e.g., spear/ice pick):** Produce circular or small slit-like wounds.

#### **D. Direction and Track**

- The doctor must describe the ==track of the wound== through internal organs in three planes (e.g., "downward, backward, and to the left").
- **Beveling:** If the weapon enters at an angle, one margin will be "undermined" (beveled), indicating the direction from which the assailant struck.

---

### **4. Medico-Legal Importance**

Stab wounds provide crucial evidence in court for the following reasons:

1. **Identification of Weapon:** The shape and dimensions can help determine if the weapon was single-edged, double-edged, or pointed.
2. **Estimation of Force:** The depth and whether bone was penetrated indicate the **degree of force** used by the assailant.
3. **Position of Assailant:** The direction of the wound track helps reconstruct the relative positions of the victim and the attacker.
4. **Manner of Death:**
    - **Homicidal:** Usually multiple wounds, often in "non-accessible" areas, and may be accompanied by **defense wounds** on the hands.
    - **Suicidal:** Usually a single wound (or a cluster) in an "accessible" area like the precordium (heart) or abdomen. May be accompanied by **tentative (hesitation) wounds**.
5. **Volitional Activity:** Determining if the victim could have performed any physical activity (e.g., running or shouting) after being stabbed.

---

### **5. Relevant Law**

- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:**
    - **Section 2(14):** Defines **Injury** as any harm illegally caused to a person.
    - **Section 116:** Defines **Grievous Hurt** (a stab wound that endangers life or causes permanent organ damage falls here).
    - **Section 103:** Punishment for **Murder** (often the charge in homicidal stabbings).
- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023:**
    - **Section 194:** Police Inquest—The IO requisitions the medical officer for autopsy in a stabbing death.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:**
    - **Section 39:** Relates to **Expert Opinion**—The medical report of the stab wound is admissible as expert evidence.

### **Quick Revision Notes**

- **Definition:** Depth > Length or Width.
- **Pathognomonic sign:** Inward compression/hilt mark.
- **Harakiri:** A specific form of suicidal disembowelment (L-shaped cut).
- **Caution:** Never probe a stab wound in a living patient; determine depth through imaging or surgery.
