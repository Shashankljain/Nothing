### **Poisons Acting on the Central Nervous System (Neurotics)**
[[Toxicology]]
#### **1. Basic Concepts & Classification**

**Definition**  
Neurotic poisons, also known as **Systemic Poisons**, are substances that act primarily on the Central Nervous System (CNS) after absorption. They can act as stimulants or depressants of various brain and spinal cord functions.

**Classification of CNS Poisons**  
According to their specific site and mode of action, neurotic poisons are classified as follows:

| **Category**        | **Sub-type**                     | **Examples**                                      |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| **I. Cerebral**     | **Somniferous** (Sleep-inducing) | Opioids (Morphine), Barbiturates                  |
|                     | **Inebriants** (Intoxicating)    | Ethanol (Alcohol), Ether, Anesthetics             |
|                     | **Deliriants** (Cause delirium)  | **Dhatura**, Cannabis, Cocaine                    |
| **II. Spinal**      | Act on the spinal cord           | _Strychnos nux-vomica_ (Strychnine),==Gelsemium== |
| **III. Peripheral** | Act on peripheral nerves         | Curare, Conium                                    |

### **Dhatura Poisoning (Deliriant Poisoning)**

#### **1. Basic Concepts**

**Introduction**  
Dhatura is a member of the _Solanaceae_ family. In India, common species include _**Datura alba**_ (white flowers) and _**Datura fastuosa**_ (purple flowers).

**What? (Common Names):** Known as Thorn Apple,Roadside poison, Jimson Weed, Hell’s Bell, Devil’s Trumpet, and Rail-road poison.

**Active Principles:** The plant contains **Tropane alkaloids**:

1. **Hyoscine** (Scopolamine)
2. **Hyoscyamine**
3. **Atropine**.

#### **2. Clinical Features (Signs and Symptoms)**

Symptoms typically appear within 30–60 minutes of ingestion and can last for 24–48 hours because the alkaloids delay gastric emptying.

**The Anticholinergic Mnemonic:** To remember the overall appearance of the patient:

- **"Red as a beet"** (Flushed face).
- **"Dry as a bone"** (Dry mouth and skin).
- **"Blind as a bat"** (Dilated pupils/loss of accommodation).
- **"Mad as a hatter"** (Delirium).
- **"Hot as a hare"** (High fever).

**The 11 "Ds" of Dhatura Poisoning (★ High-Yield University Question):**

1. **Dryness of mouth:** Difficulty in swallowing and intense thirst.
2. **Dysphagia:** Difficulty in swallowing.
3. **Dysarthria:** Difficulty in talking; mumbling or slurred speech.
4. **Dilatation of cutaneous vessels:** Flushed face and congested conjunctiva.
5. **Diplopia:** Double vision and widely **dilated pupils (Mydriasis)**.
6. **Dry hot skin:** Fever due to inhibition of sweat; axillae are dry.
7. **Distention of bladder:** Urinary retention (Full as a flask).
8. **Drunken gait:** Staggering and giddiness.
9. **Delirium:** The patient is "mad as a wet hen." Includes **carphologia** (picking at imaginary threads) and **pill-rolling**movements of fingers.
10. **Diminished bowel sounds:** Paralytic ileus.
11. **Drowsiness:** May progress to stupor, coma, and death from respiratory paralysis.

#### **3. Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Stupefying Poison:** The most common use in India. It is used to make victims unconscious prior to **robbery, kidnapping, or rape**.
- **Rail-road Poison:** Often used on travelers; robbers disguised as saints offer "Prasad" mixed with Dhatura seeds to rob passengers.
- **Accidental:** Children may chew the fruit or seeds mistaking them for chilli seeds.
- **Adulterant:** Sometimes added to country liquor to increase its "kick" or intoxicating effect.
- **Homicide/Suicide:** Very rare.
- **Stupefying Dose:** 40–50 seeds; **Fatal Dose:** 75–125 seeds (about 60 mg of alkaloids).

#### **5. Management (Treatment)**

1. **Gastric Lavage:** Performed with **Potassium Permanganate (1:5000)** or Tannic Acid to oxidize/precipitate the alkaloids.
2. **Physiological Antidote:** **Physostigmine salicylate** (0.5–1 mg slow IV). It is a reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that antagonizes CNS manifestations.
3. **Agitation Control:** Agitated patients are treated with intravenous **Benzodiazepines** (Diazepam).
4. **Temperature Control:** Tepid sponge baths for hyperthermia.
5. **Urinary Retention:** Catheterization is often required.
6. **Environment:** Keep the patient in a low-stimulus, dark room.

---

#### **6. Post-Mortem Findings**

- **External:** General signs of asphyxia (cyanosis, congestion).
- **Internal:**
    - ★ **Presence of Seeds:** Dhatura seeds are often found in the stomach or small intestine. They **resist putrefaction** and can be found even in decomposed bodies.
    - **Stomach:** Mucosa may show signs of inflammation.
    - **Organs:** Congestion of the brain, lungs, and liver.
