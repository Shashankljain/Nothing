[[Identification]]
### **Anthropometry (Bertillon System)**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Anthropometry**, also known as the **Bertillon System** or **Bertillonage**, was the first scientific method used for the identification of ==criminals==. It is based on the physical measurements of various parts of the human body.
Not used now.

**Principle**  
The system is based on the principle that the measurements of the human skeletal structure ==**do not**== change after the age of **21 years** (adult age). Bertillon believed that the probability of two individuals having exactly the same measurements for several body parts was extremely low.

---

#### **Components of the Bertillon System**

★ **Important Exam Point:** The system consists of four distinct parts:

1. **Anthropometry (Body Measurements):** A set of **11 specific measurements**:
    - Height.
    - Anteroposterior (AP) diameter of the head.
    - Anteroposterior (AP) diameter of the trunk.
    - Span of outstretched arms.
    - Length of the middle finger.
    - Length of the left little finger.
    - Length of the left forearm.
    - Length of the left foot.
    - Length of the right ear.
    - Breadth of the right ear.
    - Color of the left iris.
2. **Portrait Parlé (Descriptive Data):** A "spoken picture" including the color of hair, eyes, complexion, and the specific shapes of the nose, ears, and chin.
3. **Body Marks:** Meticulous documentation of moles, scars, and tattoo marks.
4. **Photographs:** Front-view and right-profile photographs of the head.

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Criminal Identification:** Historically used to maintain records of repeat offenders to ensure they could not hide their identity by using aliases.
- **Missing Persons:** Helped in the systematic search for and identification of missing individuals.
- **Establishment of Identity:** Contributed to the "Body of Essence" or **Corpus Delicti**, proving that a specific individual was involved in a crime.

---

#### **Relevant Law**

In the updated Indian legal framework (**2023**), the authority to collect measurements is governed by:

|**Act**|**Section/Provision**|**Medico-Legal Significance**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**BNSS, 2023**|**Identification**|Allows the police and authorized authorities to collect measurements (biometric data, fingerprints, or physical traits) from an arrested person for investigation.|
|**BSA, 2023**|**Expert Evidence**|Anthropometric data, when recorded by a trained expert, is admissible as forensic evidence in court.|

---

#### **Why was it replaced by Fingerprinting?**

★ **Important University Question**  
Although it was the first scientific system, it was replaced by **Dactylography** because:

1. **Complexity:** Anthropometry required specialized, expensive instruments and highly trained personnel to take exact measurements.
2. **Accuracy:** Different individuals were found to occasionally have the same measurements (unlike fingerprints, which are unique even in twins).
3. **Growth:** Measurements could only be taken after age 21, whereas fingerprints are permanent from the 24th week of intrauterine life.

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is the 'Portrait Parlé'?**
    - _Ans:_ It is a "spoken picture," a systematic descriptive profile of a person's features (nose, eyes, hair) used in the Bertillon system.
2. **Why can't we use Anthropometry for identifying a 15-year-old?**
    - _Ans:_ Because the skeleton is still growing; the system relies on the principle that measurements stabilize only after age 21.
3. **Which measurement was considered most unique in the 11-point set?**
    - _Ans:_ While all were used in combination, measurements like the color of the left iris and the length of the left middle finger were highly prioritized.
4. **Is Anthropometry still used today?**
    - _Ans:_ It has been replaced by more reliable methods like Dactylography and DNA Fingerprinting for criminal identification, though basic anthropometric principles are still used in determining "General Development and Stature" during autopsies.

- **Inventor:** Alphonse Bertillon (First Forensic Expert).
- **Basis:** 11 skeletal measurements + Portrait Parlé + Body marks.

- **Legacy:** Known as the "first scientific method," it paved the way for modern biometrics.
