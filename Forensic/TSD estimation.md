[[PM Changes]]
The accurate estimation of the **Postmortem Interval (PMI)**, or **Time Since Death (TSD)**, is one of the most critical responsibilities of a forensic expert. It involves analyzing a variety of physical, chemical, and environmental factors to determine the interval between death and the examination of the body.

---

### **1. Enumeration of Postmortem Signs for TSD Estimation**

Evidence for estimating the time since death is generally categorised into corporal (physical), environmental/associated, and anamnestic (habits/daily activities) sources.

#### **A. Early Physical Signs (Corporal Evidence)**

- **Immediate Changes:** Cessation of brain, circulation, and respiratory functions.
- **Algor Mortis:** The cooling of the dead body.
- **Livor Mortis (Postmortem Staining):** The gravitational settling of blood.
- **Rigor Mortis:** Postmortem stiffening of the muscles.
- **Changes in the Eye:** Includes retinal segmentation, fall in intraocular pressure, and corneal opacity.

#### **B. Delayed Physical Signs**

- **Putrefaction/Decomposition:** Discoloration, marbling, gas formation, and liquefaction.
- **Adipocere Formation:** Fat turning into a soap-like waxy substance.
- **Mummification:** Desiccation/drying of the body.
- **Skeletonization:** Complete loss of soft tissue.

#### **C. Ancillary Investigations**

- **Vitreous Humor Analysis:** Measuring potassium (K⁺) and hypoxanthine levels.
- **Entomology:** Studying the life cycle of maggots and flies found on the body.
- **Stomach and Intestinal Contents:** Analyzing the degree of digestion of the last meal.
- **Biochemical Changes:** Variations in Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) and blood components.

★ **Important Exam Point:** No single sign is definitive; a range of time must always be provided based on the correlation of all findings.

---

### **2. Use of Early Signs of Death to Estimate TSD**

The "Classical Triad" of **Algor Mortis, Livor Mortis, and Rigor Mortis** is most frequently used to estimate the postmortem interval during the first 24 hours.

#### **I. Algor Mortis (Cooling of the Body)**

Algor mortis is considered the ==most useful== indicator of TSD during the first 24 hours in temperate climates.

- **Mechanism:** The body temperature falls after death until it equilibrates with the environment.
- **The Curve:** It follows a **sigmoid** or inverted 'S' shaped pattern:
    1. **Isothermic Phase:** No heat loss for the first 1–2 hours due to skin insulation.
    2. **Intermediate Phase:** A regular, linear, and constant rate of fall.
    3. **Terminal Phase:** Slow cooling as the ambient temperature is approached.
- **Estimation Formula:** \[\text{TSD in hours} = \frac{\text{Normal rectal temp} - \text{Measured rectal temp}}{\text{Rate of fall per hour}}\].
- **Rule of Thumb:** The temperature typically falls at about **1.5°F/hour**.

#### **II. Livor Mortis (Postmortem Staining)**

Lividity gives a rough idea of TSD and indicates whether the body was moved after death.

- **Commencement:** Begins 30 minutes to 1 hour after death as mottled patches.
- **Development:** Patches coalesce into large uniform areas within 4 hours.
- **Fixation (★ Important Exam Point):** Lividity becomes "fixed" (no longer blanches upon thumb pressure) in **8–12 hours**.
- **Significance:** If the body is moved before 8–12 hours, the staining will shift to the new dependent parts.

#### **III. Rigor Mortis (Postmortem Stiffening)**

Rigor mortis indicates the molecular death of the muscles and is highly dependent on environmental temperature.

- **Sequence (Nysten’s Law):** It usually follows a descending pattern: Eyelids → Jaw/Face → Neck/Thorax → Upper Limbs → Abdomen/Lower Limbs → Small fingers/toes.
- **The Rule of 12 (TSD Estimation in India):**
    - **Onset:** 1–2 hours after death.
    - **Development (Head to Toe):** Takes about 9–12 hours.
    - **Establishment (Stay):** Persists for about 12 hours.
    - **Resolution (Disappearance):** Takes another 12 hours to pass off in the same sequence.

#### **IV. Early Eye Changes**

- **Kevorkian Sign (Cattle Trucking):** Segmentation of blood in retinal vessels occurs **immediately** after death and persists for about 1 hour.
- **Tache Noire:** If the eyelids remain open for **3–4 hours**, brownish/black triangular opacities appear on the sclera.
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Falls to 0 mm Hg within **4–8 hours**.
- **Corneal Opacity:** The cornea becomes hazy within **6–8 hours**.

---

### **3. Medico-Legal Importance of TSD Estimation**

- **Criminal Cases:** Helps the police establish the timeline of a crime, confirm or disprove an alibi, and exclude suspects.
- **Civil Cases:** Determines inheritance rights and whether insurance policies were active at the moment of death.
- **Legal Duty:** Under **Section 194 BNSS, 2023** (Police Inquest), medical officers are obligated to provide their expert opinion on the time since death to the investigating officer.
