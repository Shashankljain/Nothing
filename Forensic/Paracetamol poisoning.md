# **Paracetamol Poisoning (Acetaminophen Toxicity)**

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Paracetamol poisoning** occurs due to an acute or chronic overdose of acetaminophen (paracetamol), a common over-the-counter analgesic and antipyretic drug. It is primarily classified as a **Hepatotoxic Systemic Poison** because its most severe effect is on the liver.

**Introduction**  
Paracetamol is safe at therapeutic doses, but in high quantities, it produces a toxic metabolite that causes massive damage to liver cells. It is one of the most common causes of drug-induced liver failure worldwide.

**Classification**  
According to its mode of action, it falls under:

- **Systemic Poison:** Affects the body's systems after absorption.
- **Miscellaneous:** Often grouped with other analgesics/antipyretics.
- **Organ-specific:** Specifically a **Hepatotoxic poison** (causes liver necrosis).

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Accidental Poisoning:** Very common in children who ingest "sweet" syrups or by adults who unintentionally exceed the maximum daily dose by taking multiple preparations (e.g., cold/flu meds and pain meds) containing paracetamol.
- **Suicidal Poisoning:** Frequently used as a suicidal agent due to its easy availability and "cheap" cost.
- **Idiosyncrasy:** Some individuals show an inherent hypersensitivity to paracetamol even at therapeutic doses, leading to symptoms like dyspnoea or bowel hemorrhage.
- **Reporting:** Under **Section 33 BNSS**, a medical officer is legally bound to inform the police of any suspected poisoning case.

★ **Important Exam Point:** Failure to report a poisoning case is a punishable offence under **Section 211 BNS**.

### **Topic-Specific Explanation: Pathophysiology & Metabolism** 🔬

*The following details regarding NAPQI and glutathione kinetics are from standard textbooks (outside your sources).*

#### **I. Normal Therapeutic Pathway**
At therapeutic doses, paracetamol is metabolized safely in the liver via:
1.  **Phase II Conjugation (90%):** Glucuronidation and sulfation yield non-toxic, water-soluble metabolites.
2.  **Phase I Oxidation (<10%):** CYP450 enzymes (mainly CYP2E1) produce a highly toxic, reactive metabolite: **N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI)**. This is immediately neutralized by **reduced glutathione (GSH)**.

#### **II. Pathophysiology of Overdose**
*   In massive ingestions, glucuronidation and sulfation pathways become saturated.
*   CYP450 activity is shunted, creating excessive amounts of **NAPQI**.
*   Once **glutathione stores are depleted by >70%**, free NAPQI binds covalently to the sulfhydryl (-SH) groups of hepatocyte mitochondrial proteins.
*   This triggers mitochondrial failure, lipid peroxidation, and **centrilobular hepatic necrosis (Zone 3)**.

### **Clinical Features: Chronological Stages** 🩺

*The four clinical stages of paracetamol toxicity are compiled from standard textbooks (outside your sources).*

#### **★ The Deceptive Latency:**
Paracetamol poisoning is highly deceptive; a patient who has consumed a fatal dose may look completely healthy on Day 1, only to collapse from liver failure on Day 3.

```

  pallor, diaphoresis.      temporarily.              hepatomegaly.             or death from
• LFTs are normal.        • RUQ pain begins.        • Encephalopathy, coma.   severe hepatic failure.
```

*   **Stage I (0–24 Hours):** Minimal symptoms; mild nausea, vomiting, pallor, and diaphoresis. LFTs are completely normal.
*   **Stage II (24–48 Hours):** Deceptive improvement of GI symptoms. Right upper quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain and liver tenderness set in.
*   **Stage III (48–96 Hours):** **Fulminant Hepatic Failure** peaks. Characterized by deep jaundice, tender hepatomegaly, hemorrhagic diathesis (coagulopathy), hypoglycemia, hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis, confusion, coma), and **acute tubular necrosis (ATN)** of the kidneys.
*   **Stage IV (4 Days to 2 Weeks):** Recovery phase for survivors. Hepatocytes regenerate fully over weeks **without causing chronic cirrhosis**.

### **Management**

★ **Important Exam Point: Management follows the ABCD protocol.**

1. **Decontamination:**
    - **Gastric Lavage:** Performed with warm water if the patient arrives within 1–2 hours of ingestion.
    - **Activated Charcoal:** Acts as a physical antidote by adsorbing the drug on its surface to prevent absorption.
2. **Specific Antidote:**
    - **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC):** This is the **physiological/pharmacological antidote** of choice.
    - **Action:** It replenishes glutathione stores to neutralize the toxic NAPQI metabolite.
3. **Supportive Treatment:** IV fluids to maintain hydration and glucose to treat hypoglycemia.
4. **Definitive Care:** In cases of fulminant hepatic failure, a **liver transplant** may be the only lifesaving option.

---

### **Autopsy Findings**

- **External:** Icterus (jaundice) may be visible.
- **Liver (Primary Finding):** Swollen, soft, and yellowish. Histopathology shows **acute liver necrosis**.
- **Kidneys:** May show **acute tubular necrosis** (ATN).
- **Stomach:** May contain drug fragments if death was rapid.

### **Basic Concepts** 📚

#### **Definition**
**Paracetamol Poisoning** (classically termed **Acetaminophen Toxicity**) is an acute or chronic toxic state caused by the ingestion of supratherapeutic doses of paracetamol, resulting in severe, life-threatening **acute centrilobular liver necrosis** and secondary renal failure.

#### **Introduction**
*   **Forensic Classification:** It is classified as a medicinal **analgesic and antipyretic poison**.
*   **The Margin of Safety:** Although highly safe at standard therapeutic doses (maximum **4 g/day** in adults), it acts as a direct, dose-dependent hepatotoxin in overdoses.
*   **The "6-W" Analytical Framework:**
    *   **What?** A severe hepatotoxic crisis characterized by massive necrosis of the liver parenchyma.
    *   **Why?** Ingested accidentally (especially in children who mistake sweetened pediatric syrups for candy) or intentionally for self-harm.
    *   **How?** Overdose saturates normal conjugation pathways, shunting metabolism into pathways that produce a highly reactive, toxic electrophilic intermediate.
    *   **When?** Symptoms are deceptively minimal or absent during the first 24 hours, but severe liver failure peaks between **3 to 4 days** post-ingestion.
    *   **Where?** The primary site of cellular damage is the **liver (specifically Zone 3 / centrilobular region)**, with secondary injury to the renal tubules.
    *   **Who?** Frequently seen in young adults with psychiatric depressive illnesses, and children due to parental dosing errors.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance** ⚖️

*   **Ideal Suicidal Agent:** Paracetamol is highly favored for suicide because early stages are relatively painless, and the fatal outcome is delayed, allowing the offender to escape immediate scene suspicion.
*   **Medical Negligence:** Doctors prescribing toxic doses of paracetamol to pediatric patients can be criminally prosecuted for negligence under new Indian criminal acts.
*   **Exhumation and Decomposition Viability:** Unlike volatile gases, paracetamol metabolites are relatively stable and can be extracted from parenchymatous organs during autopsy.

### **Investigations & Diagnosis** 🔬

1.  **Quantitative Serum Paracetamol Level (Gold Standard):**
    *   ★ **Forensic Utility:** Highly useful and recommended to confirm toxicity, guide antidote initiation, and estimate overall body burden.
    *   ★ **The Rumack-Matthew Nomogram (Textbook Info):** Plots serum paracetamol levels against the hours elapsed since ingestion (valid between **4 to 24 hours** post-ingestion). If the level falls above the treatment line (starting at **150 \(\mu\)g/mL at 4 hours**), antidote therapy is immediately indicated.
2.  **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):**
    *   Massive, diagnostic elevations of serum transaminases (**AST and ALT often exceed 10,000 U/L**).
    *   Elevated serum bilirubin and prolonged **Prothrombin Time (PT/INR)**.
3.  **Renal Function Tests:**
    *   Elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine to monitor for secondary **Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)**.

### **Management & Treatment Protocol** 🏥

#### **I. Emergency Stabilization & Decontamination**
*   **Airway & Breathing (ABCs):** Establish a patent airway and provide supplemental oxygen if comatose.
*   **Gastric Lavage:** Most useful if performed **within 1 hour of ingestion**.
    *   *Procedure:* Place the patient in Trendelenburg position on their left side. Use a 36–40 French Ewald or Boas orogastric tube.

*   ★ **Activated Charcoal:** Administer **Activated Charcoal (1 g/kg)** orally. It acts as a physical antidote by adsorbing paracetamol molecules within its highly porous network to prevent gastrointestinal absorption. Most effective if given within 4 hours.

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: Why is N-acetylcysteine (NAC) highly effective if administered within 8 hours of ingestion?**  
**Answer:** Within the first 8 hours, liver glutathione stores are not yet completely exhausted, and covalent binding of NAPQI to hepatocyte proteins has not peaked. NAC acts as a glutathione precursor, directly synthesizing GSH to completely detoxify NAPQI before irreversible centrilobular necrosis occurs.

**Q2: Why must blood for toxicological analysis be drawn from the femoral vein during autopsy?**  
**Answer:** Great vessels in the chest or heart are prone to postmortem contamination due to the diffusion of unabsorbed poisons from the stomach or aspirated vomitus in the lungs. The **femoral vein** is an isolated peripheral site, making it the ideal location to obtain uncontaminated blood.

**Q3: Explain why Sodium Fluoride is added to postmortem blood samples.**  
**Answer:** Sodium fluoride inhibits the glycolytic enzyme **enolase**. This prevents postmortem red blood cell glycolysis and successfully inhibits bacterial/microbiological growth, preventing the artificial postmortem production of alcohol or chemical degradation of drugs.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

*   **Writing "Zone 1 Periportal Necrosis" for Paracetamol:** Zone 1 necrosis is characteristic of Yellow Phosphorus poisoning. Paracetamol selectively causes **Zone 3 centrilobular necrosis**.
*   **Preserving Blood in Saturated Salt:** Saturated salt is strictly for solid viscera. Blood must be preserved in **Sodium Fluoride and Potassium Oxalate**.
*   **Waiting for Jaundice Before Giving Antidote:** Because of the deceptive 24-hour latent phase, waiting for symptoms to develop before initiating NAC is a critical error. Treatment must be guided immediately by timed serum levels plotted on the **Rumack-Matthew Nomogram**.
