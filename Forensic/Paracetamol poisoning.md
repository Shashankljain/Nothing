# **Paracetamol Poisoning (Acetaminophen Toxicity)**

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition**  
**Paracetamol poisoning** occurs due to an acute or chronic overdose of acetaminophen (paracetamol), a common over-the-counter analgesic and antipyretic drug. It is primarily classified as a **Hepatotoxic Systemic Poison** because its most severe effect is on the liver.

**Introduction**  
Paracetamol is safe at therapeutic doses, but in high quantities, it produces a toxic metabolite that causes massive damage to liver cells. It is one of the most common causes of drug-induced liver failure worldwide.

**Classification**  
According to its mode of action, it falls under:

- **Systemic Poison:** Affects the body's systems after absorption.
- **Miscellaneous:** Often grouped with other analgesics/antipyretics.
- **Organ-specific:** Specifically a **Hepatotoxic poison** (causes liver necrosis).

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Accidental Poisoning:** Very common in children who ingest "sweet" syrups or by adults who unintentionally exceed the maximum daily dose by taking multiple preparations (e.g., cold/flu meds and pain meds) containing paracetamol.
- **Suicidal Poisoning:** Frequently used as a suicidal agent due to its easy availability and "cheap" cost.
- **Idiosyncrasy:** Some individuals show an inherent hypersensitivity to paracetamol even at therapeutic doses, leading to symptoms like dyspnoea or bowel hemorrhage.
- **Reporting:** Under **Section 33 BNSS**, a medical officer is legally bound to inform the police of any suspected poisoning case.

★ **Important Exam Point:** Failure to report a poisoning case is a punishable offence under **Section 211 BNS**.

### **Topic-Specific Explanation: Pathophysiology & Metabolism** 🔬

*The following details regarding NAPQI and glutathione kinetics are from standard textbooks (outside your sources).*

#### **I. Normal Therapeutic Pathway**
At therapeutic doses, paracetamol is metabolized safely in the liver via:
1.  **Phase II Conjugation (90%):** Glucuronidation and sulfation yield non-toxic, water-soluble metabolites.
2.  **Phase I Oxidation (<10%):** CYP450 enzymes (mainly CYP2E1) produce a highly toxic, reactive metabolite: **N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI)**. This is immediately neutralized by **reduced glutathione (GSH)**.

#### **II. Pathophysiology of Overdose**
*   In massive ingestions, glucuronidation and sulfation pathways become saturated.
*   CYP450 activity is shunted, creating excessive amounts of **NAPQI**.
*   Once **glutathione stores are depleted by >70%**, free NAPQI binds covalently to the sulfhydryl (-SH) groups of hepatocyte mitochondrial proteins.
*   This triggers mitochondrial failure, lipid peroxidation, and **centrilobular hepatic necrosis (Zone 3)**.

### **Clinical Features: Chronological Stages** 🩺

*The four clinical stages of paracetamol toxicity are compiled from standard textbooks (outside your sources).*

#### **★ The Deceptive Latency:**
Paracetamol poisoning is highly deceptive; a patient who has consumed a fatal dose may look completely healthy on Day 1, only to collapse from liver failure on Day 3.

```
 [Stage 1: Day 1]          [Stage 2: Day 2]          [Stage 3: Day 3-4]        [Stage 4: Day 5+]
• Nausea, vomiting,       • Symptoms improve        • Deep jaundice, tender   • Complete recovery
  pallor, diaphoresis.      temporarily.              hepatomegaly.             or death from
• LFTs are normal.        • RUQ pain begins.        • Encephalopathy, coma.   severe hepatic failure.
```

*   **Stage I (0–24 Hours):** Minimal symptoms; mild nausea, vomiting, pallor, and diaphoresis. LFTs are completely normal.
*   **Stage II (24–48 Hours):** Deceptive improvement of GI symptoms. Right upper quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain and liver tenderness set in.
*   **Stage III (48–96 Hours):** **Fulminant Hepatic Failure** peaks. Characterized by deep jaundice, tender hepatomegaly, hemorrhagic diathesis (coagulopathy), hypoglycemia, hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis, confusion, coma), and **acute tubular necrosis (ATN)** of the kidneys.
*   **Stage IV (4 Days to 2 Weeks):** Recovery phase for survivors. Hepatocytes regenerate fully over weeks **without causing chronic cirrhosis**.

### **Management & Treatment Protocol** 🏥

#### **I. Emergency Stabilization & Decontamination**
*   **Airway & Breathing (ABCs):** Establish a patent airway and provide supplemental oxygen if comatose.
*   **Gastric Lavage:** Most useful if performed **within 1 hour of ingestion**.
    *   *Procedure:* Place the patient in Trendelenburg position on their left side. Use a 36–40 French Ewald or Boas orogastric tube.
    *   *Contraindications:* Absolute contraindications include corrosives, convulsants, and comatose patients (unless airway is protected by a cuffed endotracheal tube).
*   ★ **Activated Charcoal:** Administer **Activated Charcoal (1 g/kg)** orally. It acts as a physical antidote by adsorbing paracetamol molecules within its highly porous network to prevent gastrointestinal absorption. Most effective if given within 4 hours.

### **Medico-Legal Autopsy & Postmortem Findings** 🔍🩺

#### **I. External Findings**
*   Deep jaundice (yellow discoloration of the skin, sclera, and mucous membranes).
*   Petechial skin hemorrhages due to severe coagulopathy.

#### **II. Internal Findings**
*   ★ **The Stomach:** Must be isolated using the **Double Ligature Method** to prevent leakage. Incise strictly along the **greater curvature** to document any remaining tablet residues or mucosal erosions.
*   ★ **The Liver (Primary Target):** 
    *   The liver is shrunken, yellow, soft, greasy, and **easily ruptured on touch**.
    *   ★ **Histopathology (Zone 3 Necrosis):** Shows widespread **acute centrilobular necrosis**, severe ballooning degeneration, and steatosis (fatty change) of hepatocytes.
*   **The Kidneys:** Swollen and congested, with histopathology revealing **acute tubular necrosis (ATN)** of the proximal convoluted tubules (PCT).

*   ★ **Blood (Bottle 3):** Collect 10 mL of blood strictly from the **femoral vein** (the ideal autopsy site).
    *   *Preservative:* **Sodium or potassium fluoride (10 mg/mL of blood)** and anticoagulant **potassium oxalate (30 mg/10 mL of blood)**. Fluoride inhibits the enzyme enolase to prevent glycolysis and bacterial degradation.
