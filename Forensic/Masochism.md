[[Sexual offence]]
**Definition** **Masochism**, also known as **passive algolagnia**, is a sexual perversion in which an individual derives sexual gratification or satisfaction from **receiving pain**, ill-treatment, or humiliation from a partner.

**Introduction**

- The term is derived from **Leopold von Sacher-Masoch**, an Austrian novelist who described this behavior in his writings.
- It is the opposite of **Sadism** (active algolagnia), where pleasure is derived from _inflicting_ pain.
- When both sadism and masochism are present in a relationship, it is referred to as **Sado-masochism** or **Bondage**.

**Classification** Masochism is classified under **Sexual Perversions / Paraphilias**, which are abnormal acts performed to obtain sexual gratification without traditional sexual intercourse.

---

**Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Accidental Deaths:** Masochistic practices are frequently associated with **Autoerotic Asphyxia**. Victims may engage in "bondage" (tying themselves up) to enhance the experience, which can lead to accidental hanging or suffocation.
- **Divorce:** In civil law, habitual masochistic behavior by one spouse that causes mental or physical agony to the other can be used as a ground for **divorce** or legal separation.
- **Identification of Assailant:** In cases of non-consensual injury, the pattern of "pleasure-pain" injuries can help profilers understand the psychology of the parties involved.
- **★ Important Exam Point:** Masochism is a **"silent" background factor** in many forensic cases involving "Bondage," where the victim is found tied with ropes or chains in a secluded spot (like a bathroom).

---

**Relevant Law (2023)**

Under the new **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, the legal view of sexual perversions has shifted significantly:

- **Consensual Acts (General Rule):** Consensual sexual acts between adults (>18 years) that do not involve "natural" intercourse are generally **not punishable**.
- **Age of Consent:** If a masochistic act involves a person **under 18 years**, it is prosecuted under the **POCSO Act, 2012**, and **Section 65 BNS** as a serious sexual offence.
- **Grievous Hurt:** If masochistic "play" exceeds the limit of safety and causes permanent disfigurement or life-threatening injury, the perpetrator can be charged under **Section 117 BNS** (Grievous Hurt), regardless of the victim's initial consent.

---

**Clinical Correlation: Bondage**

**Bondage** is the combination of **Sadism + Masochism**.

- **Mechanism:** One partner (the masochist) is bound, restrained, or subjected to pain by the other (the sadist).
- **Forensic Finding:** In the event of a death during bondage, the medical officer must look for **"Ligature marks"** that are often padded (to prevent permanent scarring) and located on the wrists, ankles, or neck.

---

**Differentiating Sadism and Masochism**

|**Feature**|**Sadism (Active Algolagnia)**|**Masochism (Passive Algolagnia)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Core Source of Pleasure**|**Giving** pain/humiliation.|**Receiving** pain/humiliation.|
|**Role in Bondage**|The "Dominant" partner.|The "Submissive" partner.|
|**Associated Forensic Risk**|Homicidal violence/Overkilling.|Accidental death (Autoerotic Asphyxia).|
|**Synonym**|Algolagnia.|Passive algolagnia.|
