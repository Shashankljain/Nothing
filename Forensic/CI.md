[[Identification]]
### **Cephalic Index**

---

#### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** The **Cephalic Index (CI)**, also known as the **Index of Breadth** or **Cranial Index**, is the percentage of the breadth of the skull to its length.

**Introduction** Identified by the Swedish anatomist **Anders Retzius**, it is a fundamental tool used in forensic anthropology to determine racial differences and individual identity based on skull shape.

**Formula (★ Important Exam Point)** The measurements (length and breadth) are taken using **calipers**. The index is calculated as follows: $$CI = \frac{\text{Maximum transverse breadth of skull}}{\text{Maximum anteroposterior length of skull}} \times 100$$.

---

#### **Classification of Skull Types**

Forensic medicine classifies human skulls into three primary categories based on the Cephalic Index:

|**Type of Skull**|**Cephalic Index Range**|**Associated Racial Groups**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Dolichocephalic** (Long-headed)|**70–74.9**|Negroid (Aryans, Aborigines, Negroes).|
|**Mesaticephalic** (Medium-headed)|**75–79.9**|**Caucasoids (Indians, Europeans, Chinese)**.|
|**Brachycephalic** (Short-headed)|**80–84.9**|Mongoloids (Mongolians, Native Americans).|

- **Hyperbrachycephalic:** A variant with a very round or broad head (CI 85–89.9), seen among the Kyushu of Japan or in individuals with **Apert syndrome**.
- **Indian Context:** Since the Indian skull is Caucasoid with few Negroid characters, it is classified as **Mesaticephalic**(75–79.9).

---

#### **Medico-Legal Importance**

★ **Important Exam Point: Why is CI Significant?**

1. **Race Determination:** It is a primary tool to differentiate between Caucasian, Mongolian, and Negroid remains in mass disasters (e.g., plane crashes).
2. **Age Estimation:** The index is useful in estimating the age of **fetuses** for legal and obstetrical reasons.
3. **Corpus Delicti:** It contributes to establishing the "Body of Crime" by identifying skeletal remains.
4. **Unaltered Marker:** Along with age and stature, ancestry (race) is a primary characteristic of identification that remains unaltered even after death.

#### **Other Related Indices for Identification**

While the Cephalic Index focuses on the skull, other indices of long bones are used to support racial and sex identification:

- **Brachial Index:** $(\text{Length of Radius} / \text{Length of Humerus}) \times 100$.
- **Crural Index:** $(\text{Length of Tibia} / \text{Length of Femur}) \times 100$.
- **Humerofemoral Index:** $(\text{Length of Humerus} / \text{Length of Femur}) \times 100$.
- **Intermembral Index:** $(\frac{\text{Humerus} + \text{Radius}}{\text{Femur} + \text{Tibia}}) \times 100$.

#### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Anders Retzius:** The anatomist who identified the Cephalic Index.
- ★ **Indian Skull:** Classified as **Mesaticephalic**.
- ★ **Dolichocephalic:** The characteristic head shape for the **Negroid** race.
- ★ **Instruments:** Calipers are used to measure the breadth and length of the skull.

#### **Viva Questions**

1. **What is the Cephalic Index of a typical Indian?**
    - _Ans:_ Between 75 and 79.9 (Mesaticephalic).
2. **How do you measure the length of the skull for CI?**
    - _Ans:_ It is the maximum anteroposterior length, measured using calipers from the glabella to the farthest point on the back of the head.
3. **In which racial group would you expect a 'Short-headed' (Brachycephalic) skull?**
    - _Ans:_ Mongoloids.
4. **Can the Cephalic Index help in fetal age estimation?**
    - _Ans:_ Yes, it is used for both legal and obstetrical age estimation in fetuses.

#### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Definition:** (Breadth / Length) × 100.
- **Dolicho- (Long):** <75 (Negroid).
- **Mesa- (Medium):** 75–80 (Caucasoid/Indian).
- **Brachy- (Short):** >80 (Mongoloid).
- **Use:** Racial identification and fetal age estimation.
