[[Thermal deaths]]
**External Postmortem Findings**

**1. General Appearance (Pugilistic Attitude)**

- ★ **Important Exam Point:** This is also known as the **Boxer’s, Fencing, or Defense Attitude**.
- **Appearance:** The limbs are flexed, the hands are clenched, and the head is tilted back.
- **Mechanism:** It is caused by the **coagulation of muscle proteins** and dehydration when the body is exposed to temperatures **>65°C**.
- **Common Mistake:** Students often mistake this for a "sign of struggle" before death; however, it is a purely physical postmortem change.

**2. Skin Changes**

- **Soot and Blackening:** In dry heat burns, the skin is blackened and hair is **==singed==** (swollen, curled, and brittle).
- **Heat Ruptures:** The skin may ==split due to dehydration and coagulation==, mimicking incised or lacerated wounds.
    - **Differentiation:** Unlike true wounds, heat ruptures have **intact** blood vessels, nerves, and lymphatics spanning the gap, and there is an **absence of bleeding** or vital reaction.
- **Postmortem Staining:** If the victim inhaled smoke, the staining may appear **==cherry red==** due to the formation of carboxyhemoglobin (CO-Hb).

**3. Blisters (Vesicles)**

- **Antemortem Blisters:** Contain albuminous fluid, chlorides, and show a **red line of demarcation** (vital reaction) at the base.
- **Postmortem Blisters:** Contain mostly air or thin fluid and lack the red inflammatory line.

**Internal Postmortem Findings**

**1. Respiratory System (The "==Surest== Sign")**

- ★ **Important Exam Point:** The presence of **soot/carbon particles in the trachea** is the most characteristic sign of an antemortem burn.
- The mucosa of the larynx, trachea, and bronchi may be ==congested==, ==edematous==, and covered in ==carbonaceous== soot.

**2. Gastrointestinal System**

- **Stomach:** May show ==blackish== charring and a "==peppery== ==feel==" if corrosives were involved, or mucosal hyperemia in asphyxial burn deaths.
- **Curling’s Ulcer:** These are acute stress ulcers found in the **first part of the duodenum** in victims who survive for a few days.

**3. Head and Brain (Heat Artifacts)**

- **Heat Hematoma:** A collection of ==blood== above the dura mater (==extradural==).
    - **Appearance:** It is chocolate brown, has a **==honeycomb==/==friable==** appearance, and results from the rupture of dural venous sinuses due to heat.
    - **Differentiation:** It must be distinguished from a traumatic extradural hemorrhage caused by a blow to the head.
- **Heat/Thermal Fracture:** The skull bones may dry out and fracture. These fractures **==`can cross suture lines`==**, unlike traumatic fractures.

**4. Other Organs**

- **Puppet Organs:** Internal organs (liver, spleen, kidneys) may appear shrunken, firm, and "cooked".
- **Heart:** May show ==subendocardial hemorrhages== in the left ventricle.
