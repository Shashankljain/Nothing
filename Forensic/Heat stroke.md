[[Thermal deaths]]
### **Basic Concepts**

#### **Definition**

**Heat Stroke** (also known as sunstroke, thermic fever, or heat hyperpyrexia) is a life-threatening medical emergency caused by the ==failure== of the body's ==thermoregulatory== ==system== [Image 24]. 

It is clinically defined by a characteristic triad: **CNS dysfunction**, a **core body temperature exceeding \(40.5^\circ\text{C}\)**, and a **history of heat exposure** [Image 24].

#### **Introduction**

- **Systemic Effect of Heat:** Unlike burns or scalds, which are localized thermal injuries, heat stroke is a **general systemic effect** resulting from exposure to high environmental temperatures [Passage 214, Image 23].
- **Anhidrosis:** It is the **only heat injury** where sweating is characteristically ==absent== (anhidrosis) [Image 24].
- **Classification:** It is classified as an unnatural environmental hazard [Passage 48] and falls under the category of **general heat-related syndromes** [Image 23, 24].

### **Classification of Heat-Related Syndromes**

★ **Important Exam Point:** Differentiating between the three main heat-related syndromes is a highly repeated university exam question [Image 24].

|**Feature**|**Heat Cramps**[Image 24]|**Heat Syncope (Exhaustion)** [Image 24]|**Heat Stroke** [Image 24]|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Synonyms**|Miner's or Fireman's cramps|Heat prostration or heat collapse|Sunstroke, Thermic fever, Heat hyperpyrexia|
|**Pathophysiology**|Heavy sweating leading to **loss of Sodium (\(Na^+\))**|Profuse sweating + loss of water \(\rightarrow\) blood volume \(\downarrow\) \(\rightarrow\) BP \(\downarrow\) \(\rightarrow\) cerebral perfusion \(\downarrow\)|Failure of the thermoregulatory center under extreme heat|
|**Thermoregulation**|**Normal**|**Normal**|**Impaired / Stopped**|
|**Core Temperature**|**Normal**|**Normal**|**Severely Elevated (\(>40.5^\circ\text{C}\))**|
|**Clinical Signs**|Painful muscle spasms|Fainting, dizziness, lightheadedness, **dilated pupils**, cold/moist skin, active sweating|**Disorientation, altered sensorium**, constricted pupils, **dry, hot skin, anhidrosis** (no sweating)|
|**Management**|Oral/IV fluids and electrolytes|Intravenous (IV) fluids|**Medical Emergency:**Remove clothes, apply ice to the body, IV fluids, AC/Fan|

### **Relevant Legal Concepts**

#### **Suspended Animation (Apparent Death)**

- **Definition:** A state in which the vital signs of life (heartbeat and respiration) are reduced to a minimum and cannot be detected by routine clinical methods [Passage 67].
- **Medico-Legal Significance:** Heat stroke is a classic cause of involuntary suspended animation [Passage 68, 70, 125]. A doctor must never issue a death certificate in such cases without confirming somatic death via an **ECG or EEG** [Passage 68], as individuals in suspended animation have occasionally been wrongly declared dead.

---

### **Relevant Law (2023 Framework)**

- **Section 106 Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:** Relates to **death by negligence**. This applies to cases where individuals (such as children, workers, or detainees) are locked in unventilated, hot spaces (e.g., closed vehicles) leading to fatal heat stroke.
- **Section 115 & 117 BNS, 2023:** Voluntarily causing hurt or **grievous hurt**. Applies if an individual is deliberately subjected to extreme heat environments as a form of physical assault or torture (such as "El Planton" or prolonged standing in hot sun) [Passage 221, Image 53].
- **Section 194 Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023:** Governs the **Police Inquest** in unnatural or suspicious deaths. The autopsy surgeon must meticulously document systemic heat-related findings to establish the cause of death.
- **Section 39 Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:** Regulates the admissibility of **Expert Opinion** (medical examiner's report on the depletion of cerebellar Purkinje cells) in court.

### **Pathophysiology / Mechanism**

The progression of heat stroke is centered around thermoregulatory collapse:

```
                [Extreme Environmental Heat Exposure]
                                 │
                                 ▼
           [Dehydration & Progressive Failure of Heat Loss]
                                 │
                                 ▼
           [Thermoregulatory Center (Hypothalamus) Shuts Down]
                                 │
                                 ▼
          [Anhidrosis (Sweating Stops) & Core Temp Rises >40.5°C]
                                 │
                                 ▼
     [Systemic Cytotoxicity, Cerebral Edema & Cerebellar Damage]
                                 │
                                 ▼
         [Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS) -> Death]
```

### **Clinical Features**

- **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Disorientation, confusion, altered sensorium, stupor, or coma [Image 24].
- **Skin:** Dry, flushed, and **extremely hot** to the touch [Image 24].
- **Anhidrosis:** Complete absence of sweating (except in rare cases of exertional heat stroke) [Image 24].
- **Pupils:** Characteristically **constricted** [Image 24].

---

### **Medico-Legal Examination & Autopsy Findings**

#### **External Findings**

- ★ **Postmortem Caloricity:** Instead of cooling down (algor mortis), the dead body's temperature **remains high or even rises** for the first 1 to 2 hours after death [Passage 90, 127, Image 24, 86]. This is due to the pre-mortem shutdown of the hypothalamic heat-regulating center combined with ongoing postmortem glycogenolysis [Passage 90].
- **Rigor Mortis:** Onset of muscular rigidity is **unusually early** and passes off rapidly [Passage 96].

#### **Internal Findings**

- **The Cerebellum (Most Characteristic Finding):** The most diagnostic histopathological finding in heat stroke is **brain edema** accompanied by a marked **decrease/depletion of Purkinje fibers in the cerebellum** [Image 24].
- **Brain:** Severe cerebral congestion and edema [Passage 53, Image 24].
- **Lungs & Viscera:** Generalized congestion of all internal organs, occasionally showing petechial hemorrhages [Passage 55].

---

### **Management**

Heat stroke is a **critical medical emergency** [Image 24]. Treatment must be immediate:

1. **Rapid Cooling:** Move the patient to a shaded/AC room, remove clothing, and apply **ice packs or ice-water baths**directly to the body [Image 24, Passage 231]. Use fans to promote evaporative cooling [Passage 231].
2. **Fluid Resuscitation:** Administer intravenous (IV) crystalloids to correct hypovolemia and support circulation [Image 24].
3. **Supportive Care:** Monitor core temperature continuously and manage seizures with IV benzodiazepines if they arise [Passage 231].

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **The Anhidrosis Rule:** Heat stroke is the _only_ heat-related syndrome where sweating is absent [Image 24].
- ★ **Purkinje Fiber Depletion:** The gold-standard histopathological diagnostic marker for heat stroke at autopsy [Image 24].
- ★ **Postmortem Caloricity:** The phenomenon where the cadaver remains hot or warms up postmortem [Passage 90, 127, Image 24, 86].
- ★ **Suspended Animation:** Heat stroke is a key differential diagnosis for apparent death [Passage 70, 125].

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: Why does postmortem caloricity occur in heat stroke?**  
**Answer:** Because the heat-regulating center in the brain is severely damaged and ceases to function before death, preventing heat loss, while metabolic postmortem glycogenolysis continues to generate heat after somatic death [Passage 90].

**Q2: What is the primary differentiator between heat syncope and heat stroke?**  
**Answer:** In heat syncope, thermoregulation is normal, core body temperature is normal, skin is wet/cold, and pupils are dilated [Image 24]. In heat stroke, thermoregulation is failed, core temperature is \(>40.5^\circ\text{C}\), skin is dry/hot (anhidrosis), and pupils are constricted [Image 24].

**Q3: Where do you observe the most diagnostic microscopic change in a fatal heat stroke case?**  
**Answer:** In the cerebellum, which shows a significant loss or depletion of Purkinje cells [Image 24].

### **Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **Confusing Pupil Signs:** Writing that pupils are dilated in heat stroke. In reality, pupils are **constricted** in heat stroke, whereas they are **dilated** in heat syncope [Image 24].
- **Sweating Misconception:** Believing that a heat stroke patient must be sweating heavily. Sweating is **absent**(anhidrosis) [Image 24].
- **Warming up Autopsy Timings:** Forgetting that postmortem caloricity can skew the estimation of the Time Since Death (TSD) if rectal temperature is measured without accounting for the initial temperature plateau [Passage 83, 127].

### **Quick Revision Summary (Last-minute Points)**

- **Definition:** Core temp \(>40.5^\circ\text{C}\) + CNS dysfunction + heat exposure history [Image 24].
- **Pathognomonic sign:** Dry, hot skin with **absent sweating** (Anhidrosis) [Image 24].
- **Autopsy signs:** Postmortem caloricity [Passage 127, Image 24], early rigor mortis [Passage 96], and **loss of Purkinje cells in the cerebellum** [Image 24].
- **Clinical Trap:** Can cause **Suspended Animation** [Passage 125].
- **Legal:** Negligence prosecuted under **Section 106 BNS**; inquest handled under **Section 194 BNSS**.
