[[Asphyxia]]
The following comprehensive guide is designed for MBBS students preparing for university examinations, covering the essential aspects of **Asphyxial Deaths** and **Drowning** according to the latest standards and legal nomenclature.

---

### **1. Basic Concepts of Asphyxial Death**

#### **Definition**

- **Etymology:** Derived from the Greek word _asphyxia_, meaning 'pulselessness' or 'absence of pulse'.
- **Scientific Definition:** It is a condition caused by interference with the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the body, literally meaning 'defective aeration of blood'.

#### **Pathophysiology (The Vicious Cycle)**

Asphyxia leads to a chain reaction: **Deficient oxygenation** \(\rightarrow\) **Decreased oxygenation in lungs** \(\rightarrow\) **Capillary dilatation** \(\rightarrow\) **Engorgement** \(\rightarrow\) **Stasis of blood** \(\rightarrow\) **Decreased venous return** \(\rightarrow\) **Decreased pulmonary blood flow**.

### **2. Classification of Asphyxial Deaths**

Asphyxial deaths are classified based on the etiology and the level of obstruction:

|**Category**|**Specific Type**|**Example/Mechanism**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Mechanical/Violent**|**Obstruction of Orifices**|Smothering (nose/mouth).|
||**Obstruction of Air Passages**|Hanging, Strangulation, Throttling (external pressure).|
||**Internal Obstruction**|Choking, Gagging (foreign bodies).|
||**Occlusion by Fluid**|**Drowning**.|
||**Pressure on Chest**|Traumatic Asphyxia (stampede, building collapse).|
|**Pathological**|Diseases|Laryngeal edema, tumors, or spasm.|
|**Toxic/Chemical**|Poisoning|CO, Cyanide (inhibition of oxidative processes).|
|**Environmental**|Vitiated Atmosphere|High altitude, sewer gas.|

### **3. Drowning: Definition and Detailed Types**

#### **Definition**

Drowning is the process of respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion in a liquid medium. A liquid-air interface at the entrance to the airway prevents the individual from breathing oxygen.

#### **Classification of Drowning**

**A. Typical Drowning (Wet Drowning)** Water or fluid is inhaled into the lungs (seen in 80–90% of cases).

1. **Freshwater Drowning:** Hypotonic water enters the blood, causing **hemodilution**, hemolysis, and hyperkalemia, leading to **==ventricular== ==fibrillation==**. Fatal period: ~5 minutes.
2. **Saltwater Drowning:** Hypertonic water draws fluid out of the blood, causing **hemoconcentration** and fulminant pulmonary edema, leading to **cardiac ==arrest==/asystole**. Fatal period: 8–12 minutes.

**B. Atypical Drowning**

1. **Dry Drowning:** Water does not enter the lungs due to **laryngeal spasm** induced by a small amount of water entering the larynx. Seen in 1–2% of cases.
2. **Immersion Syndrome (Hydrocution):** Sudden death upon contact with very cold water (\(\geq\) 5°C lower than body temp). Death is due to **vagal inhibition** causing cardiac arrest.
3. **Secondary Drowning (Near Drowning):** The victim survives the initial episode but dies later (>24 hours) from complications like pneumonia, pulmonary edema, or ARDS.
4. **Shallow Water Drowning:** Submersion of the unconscious (e.g., epileptics, intoxicated) in shallow water like a drain or pit.

### **4. Postmortem Findings in Drowning**

#### **External Findings**

- **Froth:** Presence of fine, copious, white, **'shaving lather-like' froth** at the mouth and nostrils.
    - ★ **Important Exam Point:** This is a **vital phenomenon**, meaning the person was alive when they entered the water.
- **Cadaveric Spasm:** Clenching of weeds, mud, or sand in the hands is **vital proof** of antemortem drowning.
- **Washerwoman's Hand:** Skin of the palms/soles becomes ==wrinkled==, ==sodden==, and ==bleached==.
- **Cutis Anserina (Goose Skin):** Puckered skin due to contraction of erector pilorum muscles (non-specific).
- **Position:** A submerged body typically floats **face down** with extremities hanging.
- **Tête de Nègre:** In putrefaction, the skin of the head and neck becomes dark/bronze.
- Body and cloths are wet

#### **Internal Findings**

- **Lungs (Emphysema Aquosum):** Lungs are voluminous, heavy, and **ballooned**, bulging out when the sternum is removed.
- **Paltauf's Hemorrhages:** Subpleural petechiae found in the lower lobes (characteristic of antemortem drowning).
- **Stomach & Intestines:** Presence of the drowning medium (e.g., muddy water) and micro-ruptures of the gastric mucosa (**==Sehrt’s sign==**).
- **Middle Ear:** Presence of water and hemorrhage (**Ueno's sign**).
- **Wydler's Sign:** Gastric contents in a beaker separate into three layers (foam, liquid, sediment).

### **5. Laboratory Investigations**

1. **Diatom Test:**
    - **Gold Standard** for diagnosing drowning in decomposed bodies.
    - Diatoms (microscopic algae) enter the circulation only if the person was alive and the heart was beating.
    - **Best Site:** Bone marrow of the **femur**.
2. **Gettler's Test:** Differentiates fresh from salt water by comparing **chloride content** between the left and right heart chambers (25% difference is significant).

---

### **6. Relevant Law (2023)**

- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023:**
    - **Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest**. The medical officer is duty-bound to examine drowning cases and collect evidence (froth, diatoms) to assist the Investigating Officer.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:**
    - **Section 39:** Findings like 'Emphysema Aquosum' and the 'Diatom Test' are admissible as **Expert Opinion** in court.

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Quintet of Asphyxia (Mnemonic: FRCPC):** **F**luid blood, **R**ight heart engorgement, **C**yanosis, **P**etechiae, **C**ongestion.
- ★ **Broken Neck Sign:** Paralyzed neck-flexing muscles in neurotoxic snakebite (often confused with asphyxia).
- ★ **Casper's Dictum:** Putrefaction rate in **Soil : Water : Air** is **1 : 2 : 8**.

### **Quick Revision Notes**

- **Asphyxia:** Failure of the "Tripod of Life" (Lungs).
- **Typical Drowning:** Inhalation of fluid into the lungs.
- **Dry Drowning:** Laryngeal spasm; no water in lungs.
- **Surest Antemortem Sign:** Fine, lathery froth and cadaveric spasm.
- **Ballooned Lungs:** Emphysema Aquosum.
- **Diatom Test:** Used for highly putrefied bodies.
