[[Thermal deaths]]
For an MBBS student, **Filigree burns** are a classic topic in Forensic Medicine, specifically within the study of **Lightning Injuries**. They are a frequent "short note" or "viva" question because they are diagnostic of lightning strikes but are technically not true burns.

---

### **Basic Concepts**

**Definition** **Filigree burns** (also known as **Arborescent markings**, **Keraunographic markings**, or **Lichtenberg figures**) are transient, branching, tree-like or fern-like patterns found on the skin of victims struck by ==lightning==.

**Introduction**

- **Not a "True" Burn:** Despite the name, they are ==not caused by heat== (thermal energy). There is no charring, singeing of hair, or blistering involved in this specific marking.
- **Diagnostic Value:** Their presence is considered pathognomonic (specifically diagnostic) of a lightning strike.

# **Mechanism (Pathophysiology)**

- **Haemoglobin Staining:** When a lightning bolt strikes, the high-voltage electrical discharge passes over the skin surface. This causes the **denaturation of Red Blood Cells (RBCs)**.
- The released haemoglobin then stains the surrounding tissues along the path of the current, creating the branching pattern.

---

### **Clinical Features**

![[image 27.jpg]]
- **Appearance:** They appear as **pink or erythematous** (reddish), superficial, tortuous markings.
- **Pattern:** They resemble the branches of a tree or a fern (Arborescent).
- **★ Important Exam Point (Duration):** They are highly **evanescent** (temporary). They usually appear shortly after the strike and fade away or disappear completely within **24 hours** in survivors.
- **Location:** Most common on the shoulders, flanks, or chest.

---

### **Medico-Legal Importance**

- **Establishing Cause of Death:** In cases where a body is found in an open field, the presence of filigree burns confirms that the person was struck by lightning, helping to rule out homicide or other causes of death.
- **Accidental Manner:** Lightning strikes are almost always classified as **accidental** (acts of God).
- **Differential Diagnosis:** It is crucial for the doctor to differentiate these from "marbling" (a sign of decomposition).

---

### **Differentiating Filigree Burns from Marbling**

|**Feature**|**Filigree Burns (Lightning)**|**Marbling (Decomposition)**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Nature**|Vital reaction (antemortem/immediate)|Sign of Putrefaction (postmortem)|
|**Cause**|RBC denaturation by electricity|Formation of Sulph-haemoglobin by bacteria|
|**Vascular Pattern**|**Does NOT** follow blood vessel paths|**FOLLOWS** the path of superficial veins|
|**Color**|Pink/Erythematous|Greenish-black or reddish-brown|
|**Duration**|Fades within 24 hours|Increases as decomposition progresses|

---

### **Relevant Law**

- **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 194:** Relates to the **Police Inquest** in cases of unnatural death. Since lightning is an unnatural cause, the Medical Officer must conduct a thorough autopsy and document the filigree marks to assist the Investigating Officer (IO) in confirming the cause of death.
- **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023:** The doctor’s observation of these markings is presented as **Expert Opinion** to the court.

---

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **Keraunographic markings:** The technical synonym for Filigree burns.
- ★ **Not Thermal:** No heat is involved in their formation.
- ★ **Vascular Independence:** A common examiner favorite is to ask if they follow blood vessels—the answer is **No**.
- ★ **24-Hour Rule:** They usually disappear within a day.

---

### **Viva Questions**

**Q1: Why is it called "Arborescent"?** **Answer:** Because the word _arbor_ means tree in Latin, and the markings look like the branches of a tree.

**Q2: Will you find filigree burns in all lightning deaths?** **Answer:** No. They are present in about 1/3rd of cases. Their absence does not rule out lightning, but their presence confirms it.

**Q3: Is there any treatment for filigree burns?** **Answer:** No treatment is required for the markings themselves as they are superficial and disappear on their own. The medical focus is on treating cardiac or neurological complications of the strike.
