**Q1: Describe Grievous Hurt (Sec. 116 BNS, 2023 / formerly Sec. 320 IPC)**

- ★ **Definition:** Grievous hurt is a legally designated, highly severe form of bodily injury that carries significantly enhanced criminal penalties compared to simple hurt [116 BNS].

**The Eight Statutory Clauses of Grievous Hurt** [116 BNS]:

1. **Emasculation:** Depriving a male of his virility, masculine power, or ability to have intercourse (e.g., severe testicular trauma or spinal cord injury) [116 BNS].
2. **Permanent privation of sight of either eye:** Complete and irreversible loss of vision in one or both eyes [116 BNS].
3. **Permanent privation of hearing of either ear:** Permanent, irreversible deafness in one or both ears [116 BNS].
4. **Privation of any member or joint:** Physical amputation or loss of a limb, digit, or joint [116 BNS]. _(Note: Loss of a fingernail does not qualify)_.
5. **Destruction or permanent impairing of the powers of any member or joint:** Complete functional loss of a joint or limb without physical amputation (e.g., ankylosis or nerve transection) [116 BNS].
6. **Permanent disfiguration of head or face:** Permanent cosmetic scars, most classically caused by acid throwing (**Vitriolage**) [116 BNS].
7. **Fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth:** Even a simple fracture, crack, or dislocation of a bone or tooth constitutes grievous hurt [116 BNS].
8. **★ The Updated 15-Day Incapacity Rule:** Any hurt which endangers life, or which causes the sufferer to be in severe bodily pain, or unable to follow his ordinary pursuits for a period of **15 days**.
    - _★ High-Yield Exam Point:_ Under the old IPC, this was a 20-day rule. The **BNS, 2023 has reduced it to 15 days**, which is a highly tested exam favorite! [116 BNS].

**Medico-Legal Importance & Punishment:**

- Differentiates simple hurt (Sec. 115 BNS) from grievous hurt, shifting the case into a cognizable, non-bailable offense.
- **Section 117 BNS, 2023 (formerly Sec. 325 IPC):** Voluntarily causing grievous hurt is punishable by up to **7 years of imprisonment** and a fine.
- **Section 118 BNS, 2023 (formerly Sec. 326 IPC):** Voluntarily causing grievous hurt by **dangerous weapons** (like knives or firearms) is punishable by **3 to 10 years of imprisonment** and a fine [118 BNS].

**Q2: Define Grievous Injury. Enumerate in detail, three injuries caused by sharp, blunt, and pointed weapons.**

- ★ **Definition:** A **Grievous Injury** is any mechanical, chemical, thermal, or physical trauma that fulfills at least one of the eight statutory clauses defined under **Section 116 BNS, 2023** [116 BNS].

```
                           [MECHANICAL WEAPON SPECTRUM]
                                         │
         ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                               ▼                               ▼
  [Blunt Weapons]                 [Sharp Weapons]                [Pointed Weapons]
 • Abrasions, Contusions,        • Incised, Chop, and           • Penetrating, Perforating,
   and Lacerations [1].          Hesitation wounds [2, 3].  and Concealed Punctures [4].
```

**1. Injuries Caused by Blunt Weapons (Stones, Lathis, Hammers)**1:

- **Abrasions:** Removal of the superficial epithelial layer of the skin (epidermis) by friction against a rough surface2. _(e.g., Graze abrasions in road traffic accidents)_3.
- **Contusions (Bruises):** Extravasation of blood into subcutaneous tissues due to the rupture of blood vessels (capillaries) caused by blunt pressure, while the overlying skin remains intact4.
- **Lacerations:** Tears, splits, or gashes in skin and subcutaneous tissues, typically seen where skin is tightly stretched over bone (e.g., scalp, forehead, shins)56.

**2. Injuries Caused by Sharp Weapons (Knives, Scalpels, Razors)**7:

- **Incised Wounds (Clean Cuts):** Linear, clean-cut wounds where the length is greater than the depth, characterized by clean-cut hair bulbs and profuse hemorrhage87.
- **Chop Wounds:** Deep, gaping wounds caused by heavy sharp-edged weapons (axes, choppers) applied with massive force, often resulting in underlying bone fractures69.
- **Hesitation Cuts (Tentative Cuts):** Multiple, parallel, shallow incised wounds made by a suicide victim at the beginning of a self-inflicted cut-throat attempt8.

**3. Injuries Caused by Pointed Weapons (Ice Picks, Daggers, Needles)**10:

- **Penetrating Stab Wounds:** A deep wound where the depth is the maximum dimension, entering a body cavity (e.g., chest, abdomen) but lacking an exit wound811.
- **Perforating Stab Wounds:** A through-and-through wound presenting both an entry wound and an exit wound8.
- **Concealed Punctures:** Needle punctures or pinpricks hidden in anatomical crevices (axilla, vagina, perineum, or scalp hair) to inject lethal toxins without leaving obvious marks10.

**Q3: Dowry Death—Define, describe the inquest procedure.**

- ★ **Definition (Section 80 BNS, 2023 / formerly Sec. 304B IPC):**
    1. The death of a woman is caused by burns, bodily injury, or occurs otherwise than under normal circumstances [80 BNS].
    2. The death occurs within **7 years of her marriage** [80 BNS].
    3. It is shown that **soon before her death**, she was subjected to cruelty or harassment by her husband or in-laws in connection with any demand for dowry [80 BNS].
- **Punishment:** Imprisonment for a term of 7 years to life imprisonment [80 BNS].

**★ The Mandatory Inquest Procedure (Section 196 BNSS, 2023 / formerly Sec. 176 CrPC):**

```
                       [DOWRY DEATH INQUEST PROTOCOL]
                                     │
                 [Mandatory Magistrate Notification (<7 Years)]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                  [On-Scene Crime Investigation & Report]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                [Autopsy by a Board of Two Government Doctors]
                                     │
                                     ▼
               [Mandatory Video-Recording & Viscera Preservation]
```

1. **Immediate Magistrate Notification:** When a woman dies within 7 years of marriage, the police are legally bound to inform the **Executive or Judicial Magistrate** immediately [196 BNSS].
2. **Magistrate Inquest:** The Magistrate (not police) must conduct the primary inquiry on-scene to prepare the crime scene report (_Panchanama_) to identify any foul play or torture signs.
3. **Mandatory Board Autopsy:** Under Indian regulations, the autopsy **must be conducted by a board/panel of at least two government medical officers** to prevent external influence or bias12.
4. **Autopsy Video Filming:** NHRC guidelines mandate that the entire postmortem examination must be video-recorded with the doctor's voice narrating their observations in real-time13.
5. **Preservation of Viscera:** Viscera (stomach, small intestine, half of each kidney, part of the liver) must be preserved and sent to the Forensic Science Laboratory (FSL) to rule out poisoning14.

**Q4: Discuss the role of a doctor in identifying different types of torture.**

- ★ **Definition:** Torture is the deliberate, systematic, or wanton infliction of physical or mental pain on a person to extort a confession, gather information, or punish them15. It is governed globally by the **Declaration of Tokyo**and the **Istanbul Protocol**1516.

**Core Role of a Medical Practitioner in Identifying Torture:**

```
                  [THE ISTANBUL PROTOCOL PHYSICAL MARKERS]
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                           ▼                           ▼
    [Falanga]                    [Telefono]                  [Picana]
 • Sole of feet beating.      • Simultaneous ear slapping.• Electrical shock scars.
 • Plantar tears & hematoma.  • Tympanic membrane rupture.• Pinpoint depigmented centers.
```

1. **Physical Trauma Mapping:** Doctors must carefully inspect the body to identify specific, recognized physical torture patterns:
    - **Falanga (Bastinado):** Beating the soles of the feet $\rightarrow$ look for deep plantar fascial tears, hematomas, and compartment syndrome17.
    - **Telefono:** Simultaneous slapping of both ears with cupped open palms $\rightarrow$ look for bilateral tympanic membrane rupture and middle ear bleeding1718.
    - **Picana:** Focused electric shocks using high-voltage batons $\rightarrow$ leaves pinpoint reddish-brown lesions with depigmented centers and hyperpigmented margins1719.
    - **Dry Submarine:** Near-suffocation using a plastic bag over the head $\rightarrow$ look for subconjunctival hemorrhages and facial petechiae (Tardieu's spots)1718.
    - **Belana (Parrot Perch):** Rolling a heavy wooden or metal rod over the muscles of the thighs/legs $\rightarrow$ look for muscle crushing, compartment syndrome, and rhabdomyolysis1718.
2. **Deep Tissue Incisions (Postmortem):** In alleged custodial deaths, doctors should perform Patowary's cosmetic incisions along the back, gluteal region, and soles of the feet to expose hidden, deep muscle bruises that are invisible externally1720.
3. **Psychological Assessment:** Map symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), severe anxiety, cognitive disorientation, or clinical depression1621.
4. **Evidence Preservation & Custody:** Meticulously photograph all lesions with a metric scale and maintain a bulletproof **chain of custody** for all physical exhibits22.

**PART II: SHORT ANSWERS (3 MARKS EACH)** ⏱️

**Q1: Section 116 BNS, 2023 (Grievous Hurt) Quick-Reference Card**

- **The Code:** BNS Section 116 replaces the historic Section 320 IPC [116 BNS].
- **Core Meaning:** It defines "Grievous Hurt" under eight strict clauses: Emasculation, permanent blindness, permanent deafness, loss of limb/digit, permanent joint immobility, permanent facial/head disfiguration, bone or tooth fracture/dislocation, and **endangerment to life or 15 days of incapacity** [116 BNS].
- **The Crucial Change:** The incapacity period has been **reduced from 20 days (IPC) to 15 days (BNS)** [116 BNS].

---

**Q2: Justifiable Homicide**

- ★ **Definition:** Lawful homicide that is fully authorized, ordered, or permitted by the laws of the State, thereby carrying **zero criminal liability**.
- **Examples:**
    1. **Execution of Death Penalty:** A judicial executioner carrying out a death sentence ordered by a competent court of law.
    2. **Law Enforcement:** A police officer or military personnel shooting a fleeing, dangerous felon under lawful, authorized orders.
    3. **Private Defense:** Acts committed in the exercise of the **Right of Private Defense** of one's own body or property under the General Exceptions of the BNS.

---

**Q3: Dowry Death (Section 80 BNS, 2023)**

- ★ **The Law:** Replaces Section 304B IPC [80 BNS].
- **Three Essential Ingredients:**
    1. Unnatural death (burns, bodily injury, or abnormal circumstances) of a woman [80 BNS].
    2. Occurs within **7 years of marriage** [80 BNS].
    3. Evidence proves that "soon before death" she was subjected to cruelty/harassment by her husband or in-laws in connection with a dowry demand [80 BNS].
- **Legal Presumption:** The court presumes the husband/in-laws caused the death; the burden of proving innocence shifts to them.

---

**Q4: Dangerous Weapons (Section 118 BNS, 2023)**

- ★ **The Law:** Replaces Section 324 and 326 of the IPC [118 BNS].
- **Definition:** Any instrument used for shooting, stabbing, cutting, or any tool that is highly likely to cause death when used offensively [118 BNS].
- **Scope:** Also includes fire, heated substances, corrosive substances (acids), explosives, or any poison/gaseous agent deleterious to the human body [118 BNS].
- **Punishment:** Causing grievous hurt under this section carries a prison term of **3 to 10 years** [118 BNS].

---

**Q5: Torture (BNS Sec. 120/121)**

- **Definition:** Deliberate physical or mental suffering inflicted by public officials to force a confession or gather information15.
- ★ **The BNS Code:** Under Section 120 and 121 BNS, voluntarily causing hurt or grievous hurt to **extort a confession** or force restoration of property is highly illegal23.
- **Punishment:** Up to **7 years of imprisonment** for simple hurt, and up to **10 years of imprisonment** for grievous hurt plus heavy fines23.

---

**Q6: Crush Syndrome**

```
 [Muscle Trauma] ➔ [Rhabdomyolysis] ➔ [Myoglobin Release] ➔ [Acidic Urine Precipitation] ➔ [Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)]
```

- **Definition:** A systemic complication arising from massive, prolonged compression of muscle groups (e.g., in building collapses or earthquakes).
- **Pathophysiology:** Ischemic muscle damage triggers **Rhabdomyolysis**, releasing large amounts of **Myoglobin** and potassium into the bloodstream. Myoglobin precipitates in the renal tubules, leading to **Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)** and Acute Renal Failure (ARF).
- **Clinical Presentation:** Severe muscle pain, dark reddish-brown urine (myoglobinuria), hypovolemic shock, hyperkalemia (causing cardiac arrhythmias), and oliguria.

**Q7: Differentiate Between Antemortem and Postmortem Injuries**24

★ **Important Exam Point:** Replicating this table in university exams is highly rewarded by examiners:

|**Diagnostic Feature**|**Antemortem Injuries (Inflicted Before Death)** 🔴|**Postmortem Injuries (Inflicted After Death)** ⚪|
|---|---|---|
|**1. Vital Reaction & Congestion**|**Present.** Redness, swelling, and localized inflammatory reactions25.|**Absent.** Tissues are pale, dry, and cold25.|
|**2. Hemorrhage / Bleeding**|**Spurting & profuse.** Blood is under arterial pressure, showing active infiltration into surrounding tissues2627.|**Passive trickling only.** No arterial pressure; blood does not infiltrate deep tissues.|
|**3. Extravasated Blood (Washing Test)**|**Clotted firmly.** Blood cannot be washed away with water because it has infiltrated the tissues28.|**Unclotted & loose.** Blood washes away easily with water28.|
|**4. Wound Margins**|**Gaping and retracted** due to vital muscle elasticity and tension11.|**Not gaping.** Margins remain flaccid and closely approximated.|
|**5. Histopathology**|Fibrin network, polymorphonuclear cell infiltration, and healing signs present.|No cellular vital reaction or inflammatory infiltration.|
