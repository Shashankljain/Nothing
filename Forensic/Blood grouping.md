[[Trace evidence]]
- **Blood Group (Phenotype):** A classification of blood based on the presence or absence of inherited antigenic substances (proteins, carbohydrates, glycoproteins, or glycolipids) on the surface of red blood cells (RBCs).
- **Agglutinogen (Antigen):** The protein or carbohydrate molecule situated on the RBC membrane (e.g., Antigen A, Antigen B, or Rh Antigen D).
- **Agglutinin (Antibody):** The corresponding immunological protein present in the blood plasma (e.g., Anti-A or Anti-B).

**3. Classification of Blood Group Systems** 📊

There are over 30 recognized human blood group systems, but the ones of primary medico-legal significance include:

```
                            [BLOOD GROUP SYSTEMS]
                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                            ▼                            ▼
   [ABO System]                 [Rhesus System]              [Other Systems]
 • Discovered by Landsteiner  • Rh positive or negative    • MNS, Kell, Duffy, Kidd,
 • Groups: A, B, AB, O.       • Based on Antigen D.        • Lutheran, and HLA.
```

1. **The ABO System:** Comprises four primary blood groups (A, B, AB, and O) based on the presence of Antigens A, B, or H36.
2. **The Rhesus (Rh) System:** Based on the presence or absence of the **D antigen** on the RBC membrane (Rh-positive or Rh-negative)78.
3. **Other Red Cell Antigen Systems:** Includes **MNS, Kell, Duffy, Kidd, and Lutheran** systems, used as secondary markers in parentage testing29.
4. **The Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) System:** Highly polymorphic tissue-typing antigens present on nucleated white blood cells (lymphocytes and granulocytes)9.
5. **Serum Protein and Enzyme Markers:**
    - **Serum Proteins:** Gm and Km systems2.
    - **Red Cell Enzymes:** **Phosphoglucomutase (PGM)** and **Peptidase A (Pep A)**1011.

**PART IV: MEDICO-LEGAL IMPORTANCE (MLI) OF BLOOD GROUPS** ⚖️

The forensic and clinical applications of blood grouping span civil, criminal, and medical jurisdictions:

**1. Civil Cases (Disputed Parentage)

- ★ **Disputed Paternity & Legitimacy:** If a married man suspects his wife of adultery and claims her child is illegitimate, blood groups can be used to resolve the dispute32.
- **Disputed Maternity:**
    - ★ **Hospital Baby Swaps:** Accidental or intentional interchange of newborns in maternity wards33.
    - **Suppositious Child:** When a woman fabricates a pregnancy and delivery, presenting a kidnapped or bought child as her own to claim inheritance33.
    - **Kidnapped Children:** Resolving custody claims by matching genetic markers to the claiming mother33.

**2. The Genetics of Paternity Exclusion** 🧬

Blood groups **can never definitively prove paternity** (they can only include a man as a potential father); however, **they can 100% exclude a wrongly accused man**1534.

**First-Order Exclusion:**

- Occurs when the child possesses a blood group gene (antigen) that is **absent in both the biological mother and the alleged father**35.
- _Example:_ If the mother is Group O ($OO$) and the alleged father is Group O ($OO$), but the child is Group A ($AO$), the "A" gene must have come from the true biological father34. The alleged father is excluded.

**Second-Order Exclusion:**

- Occurs when the alleged father is **homozygous** for a gene, but that gene is **completely absent in the child**35.
- _Example:_ If the alleged father is homozygous Group AB ($AB$) and the child is Group O ($OO$), the father had to pass on either an A or a B gene1434. Because the child has neither, the alleged father is excluded34.

**Exclusion Probability Rates:**3235

- **ABO System alone:** Excludes **17.6% (1/6th)** of wrongly accused men35.
- **ABO + MNS System:** Excludes **44%**35.
- **ABO + MNS + Rh Subgroups:** Excludes **60%**35.
- **HLA (Human Leukocyte Antigen) System:** Excludes **90%** of cases35.
- **HLA + Red Cell Antigens + Serum Proteins:** Achieves an exclusion rate of **98%**32.
- ★ **DNA Fingerprinting (STR Profiling):** Provides **100% absolute certainty** in both inclusion and exclusion of paternity1532.

**3. Criminal Cases**36

- **Murder & Assault:** Bloodstains found on a suspect's clothes, weapons, or at the crime scene can be compared to the victim's blood group36.
- **Scratching & Struggle:** In cases of manual strangulation (throttling), the victim may scratch the assailant, trapping the assailant's bloodstains under the **victim's fingernails**36.
- **Vehicular Hit-and-Run:** Bloodstains on the bumper or chassis of a suspected vehicle can be matched to the run-over victim16.
- **Crime Scene Reconstruction:** Analyzing the shape, size, and distribution of blood drops (Bloodstain Pattern Analysis) helps determine the angle of impact, distance, and sequence of blows1637.

---

**4. Medical & Professional Negligence**38

- **Mismatched Transfusions:** Negligent administration of incompatible blood (e.g., giving Group A blood to a Group O patient) leading to acute intravascular hemolysis, renal failure, and death38.
- **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections:** Failing to screen donor blood for lethal pathogens such as **HIV**, **Hepatitis B**, or **Malaria** before transfusion38.
- ★ **Blood Doping:** The illegal practice of artificially increasing an athlete's red blood cell count (via autologous blood transfusion or recombinant erythropoietin/EPO) to boost athletic performance3839. Banned strictly by the **World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA)**39.

---

**5. Identification of Malingering & Rare Groups**40

- Certain individuals possess extremely rare blood groups (e.g., the _Bombay Blood Group_ or _Rh-Null_). In such cases, the blood group is as highly individualistic as a fingerprint, allowing absolute identification of a suspect1540.
