### 🧼🧪 **CARBOLURIA**

---

#### **1. Definition (What? & When?)**

**Carboluria** is the characteristic **dark smoky-green discoloration of urine** observed in patients suffering from acute or chronic **Carbolic acid (Phenol) poisoning** (also known as _carbolism_).

---

#### **2. Pathophysiology & Mechanism (How? & Why?)**

```
               [Ingested Carbolic Acid / Phenol]
                               │
                               ▼ (Metabolised in Liver)
         [Hydroquinone]   &   [Pyrocatechol]
                               │
                               ▼ (Excreted in Urine)
             [Exposed to Atmospheric Oxygen (Air)]
                               │
                               ▼ (Further Oxidation)
               [Dark Smoky-Green Discoloration]
```

- **Hepatic Metabolism:** Once absorbed, Phenol is conjugated and metabolised in the liver into **hydroquinone** and **pyrocatechol**.
- **Renal Excretion:** These metabolites are excreted in the urine, partly free and partly bound in an unstable combination with **glucuronic acid**.
- **Atmospheric Oxidation:** When the urine sample is passed and exposed to atmospheric air, these metabolites undergo further **oxidation**, turning the urine a classic **dark smoky-green color**.

---

#### **3. Medico-Legal & Clinical Importance (MLI)** ⚖️

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Diagnostic Bedside Test:** Carboluria is diagnosed by performing the **Ferric Chloride Test** on a urine sample. Adding a few drops of ferric chloride to the urine produces a **violet/blue color** if phenol is present.
- **Associated Sign (Ochronosis):** Prolonged excretion of these metabolites can lead to **ochronosis**—a bluish-black discoloration of tissues, such as the ear cartilage, joint cartilages, and ocular sclera.
- **Putrefaction Delay:** Bodies of phenol poisoning victims show retarded/delayed decomposition because phenol acts as a powerful antiseptic.
- **Relevant Law (Latest 2023 Forensic Framework):** Under **Section 33 BNSS, 2023** (formerly Sec 39 CrPC), any medical practitioner diagnosing suspected homicidal or suspicious carbolism must immediately notify the police.
