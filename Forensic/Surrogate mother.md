### **Basic Concepts** 👶🤰

#### **Definition**

**Surrogate Motherhood (Surrogacy)** is an Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) arrangement where a woman (the surrogate mother) agrees to carry a pregnancy to term and deliver a child for another couple or individual (the intending parents), with the legal agreement to hand over the child after birth [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136].

#### **Introduction**

- **The Parties involved:**
    1. **Intending Couple:** The couple who cannot naturally conceive and desire to have a child through surrogacy [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
    2. **Surrogate Mother:** The woman who carries the child in her womb [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136].
- **Objectives:** To provide a legally and medically structured option for couples suffering from intractable infertility to have their own biological child [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137].

### **Medico-Legal Importance** ⚖️

- **Establishing Legal Paternity/Maternity:** Under Indian law, a child born through a registered, lawful surrogacy arrangement is legally considered the **legitimate, biological child** of the intending couple [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Preventing Child Exploitation:** Strict laws prevent the commercial selling, trafficking, or abandonment of surrogate children [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Litigation in Clinical Negligence:** If a doctor violates the statutory guidelines (e.g., performing commercial surrogacy or failing to verify eligibility), they face severe criminal charges and cancellation of their medical registration [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Matrimonial Disputes:** If surrogacy is performed without the husband’s consent, or if disputes arise during divorce proceedings, the legal status of the child is protected under statutory provisions [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137].

★ **Important Exam Point:** **Commercial surrogacy is completely illegal** in India. Any doctor or individual facilitating it is subject to severe imprisonment and heavy fines [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137].

### **Relevant Law (Must Include)** 📜

#### **I. Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021 & 2022**

This is the principal legislation governing surrogacy in India. It lays down strict eligibility and penalty frameworks [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137]:

##### **1. Eligibility Criteria for the Intending Couple**

- **Marital Status & Duration:** Must be an **Indian married couple** with at least **5 years of marriage** and proven infertility [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Age Limits:**
    - **Female:** 23 to 50 years [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
    - **Male:** 26 to 55 years [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Exclusions:** Live-in couples and single/unmarried individuals (except widows or divorcees under specific criteria) are **not eligible** [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **No Child Selection:** The couple must not have any surviving biological, adopted, or surrogate child (except if the surviving child is mentally or physically challenged, as certified by a medical board) [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].

##### **2. Eligibility Criteria for the Surrogate Mother**

- **Relationship:** Must be a **close relative** of the intending couple [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Marital & Parental Status:** Must be **married** and must have at least **one healthy child** of her own [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137].
- **Age Limit:** Must be between **25 to 35 years** of age [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136].
- **Lifetime Limit:** A woman can act as a surrogate mother **only once in her lifetime** [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Fitness Certification:** Must possess a valid certificate of **mental and physical fitness** from an authorized medical board [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136].

#### **II. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 93 BNS (Exposure and Abandonment of Child):** If the intending parents **abandon or refuse to accept**the surrogate child after birth for any reason (including congenital defects, gender, or twins), they are prosecuted under Section 93 BNS for child abandonment (punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years, a fine, or both) [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Section 116 BNS (Grievous Hurt):** Performing uncertified, dangerous, or forced obstetric procedures on a surrogate mother that results in permanent injury is prosecuted under Section 117 BNS.

- **Section 184 BNSS (Medical Examination of Rape/Victim):** If disputes arise involving sexual assault or illegal medical interventions under the guise of surrogacy, medical protocols must be carried out by a registered female medical practitioner.

- ★ **Section 116 BSA (Presumption of Legitimacy):** Protects the child’s legal status. Although Section 116 BSA traditionally presumes a child born during a valid marriage to be the legitimate child of the husband, the Surrogacy Act specifically extends full legal biological status to the intending parents, overriding any competing claims by the surrogate mother [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
- **Section 39 BSA (Expert Opinion):** DNA fingerprinting is the gold-standard scientific evidence admissible in court to resolve any parentage or identity disputes [Marrow Ed 8, Page 494].

3. **Mode 3:** **Donor's sperm + Surrogate mother's ovum** are used [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: What is the main difference between altruistic and commercial surrogacy?**  
**Answer:** Altruistic surrogacy involves no monetary payment to the surrogate mother except for direct medical/hospital expenses and 3 years of health insurance [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136]. Commercial surrogacy involves paying the surrogate mother a fee or buying/selling the child, which is strictly banned in India [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136].

**Q2: What is the age limit for a surrogate mother in India?**  
**Answer:** The surrogate mother must be married, a close relative of the couple, have at least one healthy child of her own, and be between **25 to 35 years** of age [Marrow Ed 8, Page 136, 137].

**Q3: Can an NRI (Non-Resident Indian) or a live-in couple opt for surrogacy in India?**  
**Answer:** No. Under the Surrogacy Regulation Act, live-in couples, NRIs, and foreign nationals are strictly **not eligible**for surrogacy arrangements in India [Marrow Ed 8, Page 137].
