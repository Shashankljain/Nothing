[[Autopsy]]
### **Basic Concepts**

- **Definition:** A medicolegal autopsy is the systematic scientific examination of a dead body performed under the authority of the State to protect the rights of its citizens.
- **Synonyms:** Necropsy, Thanatopsy, Postmortem Examination.
- **Principle:** "The only thing worse than no autopsy is a **partial autopsy**." In every forensic case, the examination **must be complete**, meaning all primary body cavities (Cranial, Thoracic, and Abdominal) must be opened and every organ examined.

---

### **Objectives of Medicolegal Autopsy**

The primary goal is to answer the "Who, When, Where, Why, What, and How" of a death.

1. **Identification:** To establish the identity of the deceased, especially in decomposed, mutilated, or unidentified bodies.
2. **Cause of Death (COD):** To determine the precise injury or disease that resulted in death (e.g., a gunshot wound or arsenic poisoning).
3. **Manner of Death (MOD):** To determine if the death was **Natural, Suicidal, Homicidal, Accidental**, or **Undetermined**.
4. **Time Since Death (TSD):** To estimate the approximate time interval between death and the examination.
5. **Weapon Identification:** To determine the type of weapon or poison responsible for death.
6. **Vital Reactions:** To determine if injuries found on the body were sustained **antemortem** (before death) or **postmortem** (after death).
7. **In Newborns:** To determine if the child was born alive and if it was viable (capable of surviving outside the womb).
8. **Volitional Activity:** To assess if the victim was capable of any physical activity after receiving the fatal trauma.

---

### **Formalities and Legal Requirements**

In India, the procedure is strictly governed by the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023** (formerly CrPC).

#### **1. Authorization (The Inquest)**

An autopsy cannot be performed without a formal legal order called a **Requisition**.

- **Section 194 BNSS (Police Inquest):** Conducted by a Police Officer (Investigating Officer) in cases of unnatural or suspicious death.
- **Section 196 BNSS (Magistrate Inquest):** Mandatory in cases of custodial deaths (deaths in police or jail custody), dowry deaths (within 7 years of marriage), or exhumations.

#### **2. Consent**

★ **Important Exam Point:** Relative consent is **NOT required** for a medicolegal autopsy. It is a legal obligation, and even if relatives refuse, the postmortem must proceed if ordered by a Magistrate.

#### **3. Who Conducts it?**

It must be performed by a **Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP)**, preferably a Forensic Medicine expert.

- **Custodial deaths:** Autopsy should be conducted by a **Medical Board** (panel of doctors) and video-recorded.

### **Procedural Formalities (Step-by-Step)**

|**Step**|**Requirement**|**Medico-Legal Significance**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Inquest Papers**|Requisition letter, Inquest report (Panchanama), and Dead-body challan.|Provides the doctor with the circumstances and history of the case.|
|**Identification**|Body identified by a Police Officer and two relatives in front of the doctor.|Ensures the autopsy is performed on the correct individual.|
|**Place**|Conducted in an **authorized mortuary/center**.|Maintains the integrity of the evidence and chain of custody.|
|**Timing**|Should be conducted in **daylight**.|Artificial light can mask color changes like jaundice, bruising, or postmortem staining.|
|**No Embalming**|Body must **never be embalmed** before the autopsy.|Embalming chemicals (formaldehyde) make toxicological and histopathological analysis impossible.|
|**Clothing**|Examined for tears, stains, bullet holes, or trace evidence.|Helps reconstruct the event; clothes are later handed to police in sealed bags.|
|**Chain of Custody**|Meticulous documentation of every person who handles the evidence.|Prevents tampering; ensures evidence is admissible in a court of law.|
|**Handing Over**|After autopsy, the body is handed back to the **Investigating Officer (IO)**.|The state has legal custody until the formalities are finished.|

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- **Negative Autopsy:** An autopsy where no cause of death is found despite gross, microscopic, and toxicological analysis (2–5% of cases).
- **Obscure Autopsy:** An autopsy where gross findings are minimal/inconclusive, and the cause is only found after laboratory tests.
- **Diener:** The person responsible for handling and cleaning the body in the mortuary.
- **Virtopsy:** A "virtual autopsy" using CT or MRI scans; it is a non-invasive alternative but does not replace physical autopsy in India.

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: Can a doctor perform a medicolegal autopsy at night?** **Model Answer:** Generally, it is done in daylight to observe color changes. However, under new guidelines, it can be done at night if there is a serious threat to law and order or under specific instructions from a Magistrate, provided it is video-recorded and infrastructure is adequate.

**Q2: What is the "Body of Offence"?** **Model Answer:** Known as **Corpus Delicti**, it refers to the principle that it must be proven a crime actually occurred (e.g., identifying a victim's body in a homicide) before a person can be convicted.

---

### **Quick Revision Summary**

- **Authority:** Police (Sec 194 BNSS) or Magistrate (Sec 196 BNSS).
- **Consent:** Never required from relatives.
- **Rule:** Always a **complete** examination (Cranium, Thorax, Abdomen).
- **Handover:** Return body to the **Investigating Officer**, not the family.
- **Goal:** Determine **COD, MOD, TSD**, and **Identity**.
