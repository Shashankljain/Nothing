[[Gen toxicology]]
For an MBBS student, understanding the medical and legal responsibilities of a doctor in cases of poisoning is one of the most critical and highly tested topics in **General Toxicology** and **Medical Jurisprudence**.

---

### **Basic Concepts** 🏥

#### **Definitions**

- **Toxicology:** The branch of science that deals with the properties, actions, toxicity, fatal dose, detection, estimation, treatment, and autopsy findings of poisonous substances.
- **Clinical Toxicology:** The branch dealing with human diseases caused by or associated with abnormal exposure to chemical substances.
- **Forensic Toxicology:** The branch dealing with the medical and legal aspects of the harmful effects of chemicals on human beings, focusing on identification, quantification, and legal interpretation.
- **Poison:** Any substance (solid, liquid, or gaseous) which, if introduced into the living body or brought into contact with any part of it, produces ill-health or death by its local or constitutional effects.

---

### **The "What, Why, How, When, Where, Who" of Poisoning Duties** ❓

- **What?** A dual responsibility: **Medical** (resuscitating and treating the patient) and **Legal** (assisting law enforcement, preserving evidence, and fulfilling statutory reporting mandates).
- **Why?** To save human life, prevent therapeutic or legal misadventure, and assist the criminal justice system in detecting and prosecuting crimes involving poison.
- **How?** By initiating immediate life support (ABCD), preserving diagnostic body fluids/viscera, documenting cases meticulously, and notifying the appropriate authorities.
- **When?** Immediately upon the arrival of a patient with suspected acute or chronic poisoning at any hospital or clinical setup.
- **Where?** In emergency casualty departments, intensive care units (ICUs), clinical wards, and autopsy theaters.
- **Who?** All Registered Medical Practitioners (RMPs)—whether in government services or private practice.

### **Medico-Legal Importance (MLI)** ⚖️

- **Establishing the Manner of Death:** Meticulous documentation helps distinguish between accidental, suicidal, and homicidal poisoning.
- **Detection of Foul Play:** Many homicidal poisons (like arsenic) mimic natural diseases such as cholera. The doctor's alert eye is the first line of defense against undetected murders.
- **Standard of Care:** Proper execution of duties protects the doctor from allegations of **professional negligence** or complicity in concealing a crime.
- ★ **Important Exam Point (The Reporting Rule):** Private practitioners are legally bound to report **homicidal poisoning**, but they are not strictly bound to report suicidal or accidental cases unless they suspect foul play or abetment. However, government medical officers must report **all cases** (accidental, suicidal, and homicidal) admitted to the hospital.

### **Relevant Law (Must Include) 📜**

#### **1. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023**

- ★ **Section 33 BNSS (formerly Sec 39 CrPC):** Lays down the **mandatory legal duty of every medical practitioner** to report cases of suspected homicidal or suspicious poisoning to the nearest police officer or magistrate [Image 13, Image 15, Passage 227].

#### **2. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**

- **Section 123 BNS (formerly Sec 328 IPC):** Covers voluntarily causing **hurt by means of poison** or stupefying drugs with intent to commit an offence (e.g., drugging travelers for robbery) [Image 15, Passage 206]. Punishment: up to 10 years imprisonment and a fine.
- **Section 286 BNS (formerly Sec 284 IPC):** Relates to **negligent conduct** with respect to poisonous substances [Image 15, Passage 205]. Punishment: up to 6 months imprisonment, a fine, or both.
- **Section 211 BNS (formerly Sec 176 IPC):** Punishes **non-compliance** or failure of a doctor to inform the police about a legally reportable offence [Image 15, Passage 227].
- **Section 212 BNS (formerly Sec 177 IPC):** Punishes a doctor for **giving false information** regarding a poisoning case [Image 15, Passage 227].
- **Section 238 BNS (formerly Sec 201 IPC):** Punishes the **destruction or disappearance of evidence** [Image 5, Passage 226]. If a doctor intentionally fails to preserve vomitus or stomach wash samples to protect the accused, they face up to 7 years imprisonment.
- **Section 229 BNS (formerly Sec 193 IPC):** Punishes the **fabrication of false evidence** or perjury during judicial proceedings [Image 5, Passage 230].

#### **3. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023**

- **Section 26 BSA (formerly Sec 32 IPC):** Governs **Dying Declarations** [Image 15, Passage 230]. If a poisoned patient is in a critical condition, the doctor must arrange for a magistrate to record their dying declaration [Image 15, Passage 230]. If time is short, the doctor can record it in the presence of two witnesses [Image 15, Passage 230].

### **Detailed Medico-Legal Responsibilities & Staging** 🔍

#### **A. Clinical & Medical Responsibilities**

2. **Patient Stabilization (The ABCDs):**
    - **A**irway: Ensure a patent airway.
    - **B**reathing: Assist ventilation if needed.
    - **C**irculation: Monitor blood pressure and perfusion.
    - **D**rugs: Administer the **"Coma Cocktail"** (Dextrose + Thiamine + Naloxone + Flumazenil) in unconscious patients of unknown poisoning.
2. **Removal of Unabsorbed Poison (Decontamination):**
    - Perform **Gastric Lavage** using an Ewald or Boas tube (for adults) ideally **within 1 hour** of ingestion.
    - _Contraindications (starts with 'C'):_ Corrosives (except carbolic acid), Convulsants, Comatose patients, and Hydrocarbons.
3. **Preservation of Samples:**
    - Preserve the **first gastric aspirate/washout** (500 mL) and **urine** (100 mL) for toxicological analysis.
    - ==Never== add chemical ==preservatives== to clinical samples; simply seal and label them.

#### **B. Legal & Administrative Responsibilities**

1. **Documentation & Preliminary Data:** Meticulously record the name, age, sex, brought by whom, date, time, and history.
2. **Police Intimation:** Notify the police in homicidal cases (private clinics) or all suspected cases (government setups).
3. **Handling Custody and Records:**
    - Maintain inpatient records for **3 years**; refusing to share them with the patient or legal authorities within 72 hours constitutes professional misconduct.
    - **Never hand over original MLC records** to the police; only provide attested photocopies and obtain a written receipt.
4. **In Case of Death:**
    - **Do not issue a death certificate** in suspected or unnatural poisoning cases.
    - Send the body for a **forensic autopsy** to establish the cause of death.

### **High-Yield Exam Points**

- ★ **First cavity opened in suspected poisoning autopsy:** The **Cranium (Skull)** to detect and document unusual toxic odors (like bitter almonds in cyanide or garlic in phosphorus) before they are masked by other visceral smells.
- ★ **Rectified Spirit Contraindications (Mnemonic: PAPPA):** Rectified spirit (95% ethanol) is the ideal preservative for viscera, but is **contraindicated** in poisoning by: **P**henol, **A**lcohol, **P**ara-aldehyde, **P**hosphorus, and **A**cetic acid [Image 22, Passage 100].
- ★ **Coma Cocktail:** Dextrose (50%) + Thiamine (100 mg) + Naloxone (0.4–2 mg) + Flumazenil (0.2–0.5 mg).
- ★ **The Dictum:** "If it is not in the record—it did not occur!".

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers**

**Q1: What are the absolute contraindications for gastric lavage?**  
**Answer:** Lavage is absolutely contraindicated in corrosive acid or alkali poisoning (due to the risk of esophageal/gastric perforation). The only exception is **carbolic acid (phenol)** poisoning because it hardens the stomach mucosa, making it less prone to perforation [250, 333, Image 22].

**Q2: Can a doctor hand over original medical records to the police?**  
**Answer:** No. Original hospital records of a medico-legal case (MLC) are hospital property and must never be handed over to the police. Only attested photocopies should be supplied, and a receipt must be obtained.

**Q3: Why is sodium fluoride added to preserved blood samples?**  
**Answer:** Sodium fluoride is added to prevent glycolysis (glucose breakdown) and to inhibit the growth of micro-organisms that can metabolize and produce alcohol in postmortem samples.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **Confusing Section 33 BNSS with BNS:** Writing that notifying the police is under the BNS. Police reporting is an administrative/procedure law, hence under **Section 33 BNSS, 2023** (formerly Sec 39 CrPC) [Image 15].
- **Formalin as Preservative:** Writing that tissues for toxicological analysis are preserved in formalin. This is incorrect; formalin is only used for histopathology, as it makes chemical extraction of non-volatile organic compounds difficult. Saturated salt is used for toxicology.
- **Failing to cite new 2023 laws:** Writing old IPC/CrPC sections instead of BNS, BNSS, and BSA equivalents.

### **Quick Revision Notes (Last-minute Points)**

1. **Primary duty:** Medical treatment to save life.
2. **Homicide Reporting:** Mandatory under **Sec 33 BNSS, 2023** [Image 15, Passage 227].
3. **Suicide/Accident Reporting:** Private practitioners not strictly bound, but advised to report to rule out abetment.
4. **Record keeping:** Indoor MLC cases must be kept for **3 years**.
5. **Evidence:** Collect 500 mL vomit/lavage and 100 mL urine.
6. **Preservative:** Saturated common salt is the gold standard.
