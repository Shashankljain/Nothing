# Hyperkalemia — Causes, Clinical Features & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hyperkalemia** is defined as a serum potassium concentration exceeding **>5.5 mEq/L**.
- It represents one of the most immediate and life-threatening electrolyte emergencies in pediatrics due to its potential to induce severe **cardiac arrhythmias** and **cardiac arrest**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Hyperkalemia occurs through four principal mechanism-based categories:

1. **Decreased Renal Excretion**:
    - **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** or **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**.
    - **Renal Tubular Disorders**: Pseudohypoaldosteronism, urinary tract obstruction.
    - **Adrenal Insufficiency**: Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (**CAH**), Addison disease.
    - **Medications**: **ACE inhibitors**, **ARBs**, **potassium-sparing diuretics** (spironolactone, amiloride), **NSAIDs**, **heparin**.
2. **Extracellular Potassium Shift**:
    - **Metabolic Acidosis**: Hydrogen ions enter cells while potassium shifts into extracellular fluid.
    - **Low Insulin States**: Diabetic ketoacidosis (**DKA**).
    - **Medications / Toxins**: **\(\beta\)-blockers**, **digitalis toxicity**, **succinylcholine**, **fluoride**.
    - **Specific Conditions**: Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis, **malignant hyperthermia**.
3. **Cellular Breakdown / Tissue Destruction**:
    - **Tumor Lysis Syndrome**.
    - **Rhabdomyolysis** and **crush injuries**.
    - **Massive Intravascular Hemolysis**.
4. **Increased Potassium Intake**:
    - Inappropriate intravenous or oral potassium supplementation.
    - Transfusion of aged **packed red blood cells (PRBCs)**.

---

### 3. Classification / Diagnostic Artifacts

- **Factitious or Pseudohyperkalemia**:
    - Caused by excessive squeezing of extremities during phlebotomy.
    - Hemolysis of blood sample during or after collection.
    - Severe **thrombocytosis** or **leukocytosis**.

- High extracellular potassium reduces the transmembrane potassium gradient, leading to cell membrane depolarization.
- Depolarization inactivates sodium channels, causing impaired membrane excitability, cardiac conduction delay, and altered repolarization.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Neuromuscular**: Muscle fatigue, non-specific muscle weakness (skeletal and respiratory), paresthesias.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, and **ileus**.

#### B. Signs & Cardiotoxicity [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cardiovascular**: Irregular pulse, bradycardia, hypotension, cardiac arrest.
- **ECG Manifestations (Correlated with Serum Potassium Levels)**:
    - **Serum K+ 5.5–6.5 mEq/L**: **Tall, peaked T waves** (**>5 mm** in limb leads, **>10 mm** in chest leads).
    - **Serum K+ 6.5–8.0 mEq/L**: **Prolonged PR interval**, **flattened or absent P waves**, and **widened QRS complex**.
    - **Serum K+ >8.0 mEq/L**: **Bundle branch blocks**, progression to a **sine wave pattern**, followed by **ventricular fibrillation** or **asystole**.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations

- **Serum Electrolytes**: Confirms serum potassium **>5.5 mEq/L**.
- **Continuous ECG Monitoring**: Mandatory in all patients with severe hyperkalemia (**>7.0 mEq/L**) or symptomatic presentation.
- **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Assesses pH for co-existing **metabolic acidosis**.
- **Renal Function Tests (RFT)**: Evaluates blood urea nitrogen (**BUN**) and **serum creatinine** to assess for AKI/CKD.
- **Urinary Electrolytes**: Transtubular potassium gradient (**TTKG**); **TTKG >10** indicates intact renal response, while **<7** suggests aldosterone deficiency or resistance.

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

|Feature|**True Hyperkalemia**|**Pseudohyperkalemia**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Pathophysiology**|In vivo elevation of extracellular serum potassium|In vitro release of intracellular potassium during/after phlebotomy|
|**Common Causes**|AKI, CKD, acidosis, tissue lysis, medications|Squeezing extremity, hemolysis, severe thrombocytosis/leukocytosis|
|**ECG Abnormalities**|Present (**tall peaked T waves**, **wide QRS**, **sine wave**)|Strictly absent (Normal ECG)|
|**Management**|Urgent emergency intervention required|Re-sample blood carefully without tourniquet/squeezing|

---

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Non-Pharmacological Measures

- Immediate cessation of all oral and intravenous potassium intake.
- Discontinuation of nephrotoxic or potassium-sparing medications.
- Continuous cardiac monitoring and supportive airway/circulatory management.

#### B. Pharmacological Treatment [⭐ High-Yield]

|Drug|Dose|Route|Frequency|Duration|Key Caution|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**10% Calcium Gluconate**|**0.5–1 mL/kg** (or **0.5–1 g/kg**)|IV|Over **5–10 minutes**under cardiac monitoring|Single emergency dose; repeat if ECG changes persist|**Stabilizes myocardial membrane**; does not alter serum K+; slow infusion required to prevent cardiac conduction block.|
|**Regular Insulin with Dextrose**|**0.3 U regular insulin/g glucose** over 2 hours OR **0.1–0.2 U/kg** insulin with **0.3–1 g/kg** dextrose|IV|Infusion over **1–2 hours**|As indicated|**Shifts K+ into cells**; monitor blood glucose to avoid hypoglycemia.|
|**Sodium Bicarbonate (7.5%)**|**1–2 mEq/kg** (or **1–2 mL/kg**)|IV|Over **20–30 minutes**|Single dose|**Shifts K+ into cells**; monitor for fluid overload and hypernatremia.|
|**Salbutamol (Nebulized)**|**5–10 mg**|Inhalation (Nebulizer)|Single dose|Over **10–15 minutes**|**Shifts K+ into cells**; may cause tachycardia.|
|**Sodium Polystyrene Sulfonate (Kayexalate)**|**1 g/kg** (max **15 g/dose**)|Oral or Rectal (enema)|Every **4–8 hours** as needed|As indicated|**Removes K+ from body** via GI exchange; slow onset of action.|
|**Furosemide**|Not specified in source — verify with standard guideline|IV|As needed|As indicated|Loop diuretic; **removes K+ from body** if renal function is preserved.|

#### C. Renal Replacement Therapy / Surgical Management

- **Peritoneal Dialysis or Hemodialysis**: Indicated for severe, refractory hyperkalemia (**K+ >7.0 mEq/L**), particularly in patients with acute kidney injury, chronic renal failure, or tumor lysis syndrome.

---

### 9. Complications

- Lethal cardiac arrhythmias (**ventricular tachycardia**, **ventricular fibrillation**).
- **Asystole** and sudden cardiac death.
- Respiratory muscle paralysis causing acute respiratory failure.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- Excellent if hyperkalemia is recognized early on ECG and promptly reversed using membrane-stabilizing and intracellular-shifting agents.
- Poor if ECG changes (**sine wave**) are unrecognized, leading to fatal arrhythmias.

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: C-B-I-G-K

- **C** - **Calcium Gluconate**: Protects the heart (membrane stabilization)
- **B** - **Bicarbonate**: Shifts potassium into cells
- **I** - **Insulin (with Dextrose)**: Drives potassium intracellularly
- **G** - **Glucose**: Prevents hypoglycemia during insulin therapy
- **K** - **Kayexalate / Kidneys (Dialysis)**: Removes potassium from the body

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Calcium gluconate does NOT lower serum potassium.** Its sole function in hyperkalemia is to stabilize the cardiac resting membrane potential and prevent lethal arrhythmias while other agents (insulin, bicarbonate, salbutamol) work to shift potassium into cells.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the therapeutic goal of **Calcium Gluconate** with that of **Insulin/Glucose**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Calcium Gluconate** acts within **1–3 minutes** to protect the myocardium without changing serum K+ levels. **Insulin and Bicarbonate** lower serum K+ levels by driving potassium into the intracellular space.

### Diagram 1: Progressive ECG Changes in Hyperkalemia

- **Type**: Sequential wave diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Horizontal ECG baseline grid with three sequential cardiac cycles.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Early (K+ 5.5–6.5 mEq/L)**: **Tall, narrow, peaked T waves** with short QT interval.
    2. **Moderate (K+ 6.5–8.0 mEq/L)**: **PR interval prolongation**, **P wave flattening**, and **QRS widening**.
    3. **Severe (K+ >8.0 mEq/L)**: **Loss of P wave**, merging of QRS and T wave to form a **Sine Wave pattern**.
- **Arrows/connections**: Peaked T Wave → QRS Widening → Sine Wave → Ventricular Fibrillation / Asystole.
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: **Sine wave** pattern represents imminent cardiac arrest and requires immediate **IV Calcium Gluconate**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Serum K+ **>5.5 mEq/L**.
- **Primary Causes**: AKI/CKD, metabolic acidosis, cell breakdown (tumor lysis), and drugs (**ACEi**, **spironolactone**).
- **Classic ECG Sequence**: **Peaked T waves** → **PR prolongation / QRS widening** → **Sine wave**.
- **Cardioprotection**: **10% Calcium Gluconate** (**0.5–1 mL/kg IV**) stabilizes myocardium immediately.
- **Intracellular Shift**: **Insulin + Dextrose**, **Sodium Bicarbonate**, and **Nebulized Salbutamol**.
- **Elimination**: **Kayexalate** (1 g/kg) and **Dialysis** for refractory cases.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Hyperkalemia is a life-threatening pediatric electrolyte emergency defined as serum potassium >5.5 mEq/L, requiring rapid identification of peaked T waves on ECG and immediate myocardial stabilization with intravenous calcium gluconate.**
