[[Immunisation]]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vaccine**: A biological preparation containing killed, live-attenuated, or fractionated antigenic material derived from a pathogen that, when administered, stimulates active immune memory and protective antibody/cellular responses without causing clinical disease.
- **MMR Vaccine**: A trivalent live-attenuated viral vaccine providing active immunization against **Measles, Mumps, and Rubella**.

---

### 3. Classification / Types of Vaccines [⭐ High-Yield]

Vaccines are classified based on the nature of the immunizing antigen into five major categories:

1. **Live Attenuated Vaccines**:
    - Composed of wild pathogens rendered non-pathogenic by repeated tissue culture passages or genetic modification.
    - **Characteristics**: Induce robust, long-lasting humoral (IgG, IgA) and cell-mediated immunity with fewer doses.
    - **Examples**:
        - **Viral**: **MMR** (Measles, Mumps, Rubella), **OPV** (Oral Polio Vaccine), **Rotavirus**, **Varicella**, **Yellow Fever**, **Japanese Encephalitis** (SA 14-14-2 strain).
        - **Bacterial**: **BCG** (Bacille Calmette-Guérin), **Oral Typhoid** (Ty21a).
2. **Killed or Inactivated Vaccines**:
    - Organisms killed by heat or chemicals (formalin).
    - **Characteristics**: Require multiple primary doses and periodic boosters; produce mainly humoral immunity.
    - **Examples**: **IPV** (Inactivated Polio Vaccine), **Whole cell Pertussis**, **Inactivated Hepatitis A**, **Rabies**, **Inactivated Influenza**.
3. **Toxoids**:
    - Inactivated bacterial exotoxins that retain immunogenicity while losing toxicity.
    - **Examples**: **Diphtheria toxoid**, **Tetanus toxoid** (TT/Td).
4. **Subunit / Fractionated Vaccines**:
    - Contain specific purified antigenic components of the pathogen.
    - **Capsular Polysaccharide**: Typhoid (Vi), Pneumococcal (PPV-23), Meningococcal.
    - **Conjugated Vaccines**: Capsular polysaccharide linked to a protein carrier (Diphtheria CRM197 or Tetanus Toxoid) to recruit T-cell help in children <2 years — **Hib**, **PCV-10/13/14**, **MCV**.
    - **Recombinant Protein**: **Hepatitis B** (HBsAg synthesized in yeast), **HPV** (Human Papillomavirus), **Acellular Pertussis**.
5. **Novel Platforms (Viral Vector & Nucleic Acid)**:
    - **Viral Vector-borne**: SARS-CoV-2 (Covishield).
    - **mRNA Vaccines**: SARS-CoV-2 (Pfizer/Moderna).

---

### Differential Diagnosis / Comparison Table: Live Attenuated vs. Inactivated Vaccines

|Feature|**Live Attenuated Vaccines (e.g., MMR, OPV)**|**Inactivated / Killed Vaccines (e.g., IPV, HepB)**|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Pathogen State**|Viable, attenuated organism|Dead organism / Subunit antigen|
|**Immune Response**|Humoral (IgG, IgA) + Strong Cell-Mediated Immunity|Predominantly Humoral (IgG) antibody response|
|**Doses Required**|Usually 1–2 doses produce long-lasting immunity|Requires multiple primary doses + periodic boosters|
|**Cold Chain Sensitivity**|Highly heat-sensitive; strict cold chain required (+2°C to +8°C)|Relatively stable; sensitive to freezing|
|**Pregnancy Safety**|**Strictly Contraindicated** (theoretical fetal infection)|Safe to administer during pregnancy|
|**Reversion to Virulence**|Rare risk (e.g., Vaccine-Derived Poliovirus from OPV)|Absolute zero risk of live infection|

### 8. Treatment / Management / Immunization Schedule & Dosage Rule [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. MMR Vaccine Composition & Strains [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Measles Component**: **Edmonston-Zagreb strain** or Schwarz strain (propagated in chick embryo fibroblast cells).
- **Mumps Component**: **Jeryl Lynn strain** or Urabe AM9 strain (propagated in chick embryo cell culture).
- **Rubella Component**: **RA 27/3 strain** (propagated in human diploid cells WI-38 or MRC-5).

#### B. Dosage Rule

|Parameter|Clinical Guideline Detail|
|:--|:--|
|**Name**|**MMR Vaccine** (Measles, Mumps, Rubella live attenuated trivalent vaccine)|
|**Mechanism of Action**|Subcutaneous replication of attenuated viruses induces active humoral IgG/IgA and cell-mediated immunity.|
|**Dose**|**0.5 mL**|
|**Dosage / Route**|**Subcutaneous (SC)** injection|
|**Frequency**|2-dose or 3-dose regimen depending on national/IAP guidelines|
|**Features & Reconstitution**|Freeze-dried (lyophilized) pale yellow cake. Reconstituted ONLY with manufacturer-supplied sterile water diluent. **Must be stored at +2°C to +8°C** and protected from direct light. **4-Hour Rule**: Reconstituted vaccine MUST be discarded within **4 hours**.|
|**Side Effects**|Fever (5–12 days post-vaccination), transient maculopapular rash, parotid swelling, **postauricular lymphadenopathy**, transient **arthralgia / arthritis** (rubella component), febrile seizures, rare ITP.|
|**When to Start / Stop**|Start at **9 months** (IAP) or **9–12 months** (UIP MR dose). **Contraindicated in pregnancy** and severe immunodeficiency.|

#### C. Immunization Schedules

1. **Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) Recommended Schedule**:
    
    - **1st Dose (MMR-1)**: **9 months** of age (or MR vaccine).
    - **2nd Dose (MMR-2)**: **15 months** of age.
    - **3rd Dose (MMR-3 / Booster)**: **4–6 years** of age.
2. **Universal Immunization Program (UIP - India National Schedule)**:
    
    - **MR 1st Dose**: **9–12 completed months** (along with Vitamin A 1 Lakh IU).
    - **MR 2nd Dose**: **16–24 months** (along with DPT 1st booster, OPV booster, Vitamin A 2 Lakh IU).

### 5. Clinical Features / Side Effects & Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Common Expected Side Effects

- **Local**: Mild pain, erythema, and induration at the subcutaneous injection site.
- **Systemic**:
    - **Fever (38°C–39°C)**: Occurs in 5–15% of children, typically **5–12 days** after vaccination (measles component).
    - **Vaccine Rash**: Non-contagious transient maculopapular rash appearing **7–10 days** post-vaccination.
    - **Parotid Swelling**: Mild, unilateral or bilateral parotitis **10–14 days** post-vaccination (mumps component).
    - **Joint Symptoms**: Transient **arthralgia or arthritis** appearing **1–3 weeks** post-vaccination (due to rubella RA 27/3 component, most common in post-pubertal females).

#### B. Rare Complications / Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI)

- **Febrile Seizures**: Occur in 1 in 3,000 children, usually **6–11 days** post-vaccination during fever spikes.
- **Idiopathic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)**: Rare, self-limiting transient drop in platelet count (1 in 30,000 doses) occurring within 6 weeks.
- **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS)**: Severe bacterial (Staphylococcal) contamination occurring when reconstituted vials are kept beyond **4 hours**.
- **Anaphylaxis**: Extremely rare (1 in 1 million doses).

### 10. Contraindications & Precautions [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Absolute Contraindications**:
    - **Pregnancy**: Absolute contraindication due to theoretical risk of viral transmission to fetus. Women must avoid pregnancy for **1 month (28 days)** after vaccination.
    - **Severe Immunodeficiency**: Congenital T-cell immunodeficiency, advanced HIV/AIDS with severe immunosuppression, leukemia, lymphoma, or systemic chemotherapy/high-dose steroids (>2 mg/kg/day prednisolone for >14 days).
    - **Severe Anaphylactic Reaction**: History of severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or vaccine components (**neomycin** or **gelatin**).
2. **Precautions & Delayed Administration**:
    - **Recent Blood / Immunoglobulin Transfusion**: Postpone MMR administration for **3–11 months** (depending on blood product dose) to prevent neutralization of vaccine virus by passive donor antibodies.
    - **Tuberculin Skin Test (Mantoux)**: MMR can temporarily suppress tuberculin reactivity. Administer Mantoux test on the same day as MMR or delay it by **4–6 weeks**.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: M-M-R V-A-C-C-I-N-E

- **M** - **Measles (Edmonston-Zagreb strain)**: Protects against measles & SSPE
- **M** - **Mumps (Jeryl Lynn strain)**: Prevents parotitis, orchitis, and aseptic meningitis
- **R** - **Rubella (RA 27/3 strain)**: Eradicates Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)
- **V** - **Viral live attenuated**: Replication induces both T-cell and antibody immunity
- **A** - **Avoid in pregnancy**: Strictly contraindicated; avoid conception for 1 month
- **C** - **Cold chain (+2°C to +8°C)**: Must be kept chilled in ILR
- **C** - **Cutaneous Subcutaneous route**: **0.5 mL SC** in upper deltoid
- **I** - **IAP Schedule**: Given at **9 months**, **15 months**, and **4–6 years**
- **N** - **Neomycin allergy**: Absolute contraindication
- **E** - **Exogenous discard at 4 hours**: Reconstituted vial discarded within 4 hours to prevent TSS

### Clinical Pearl

> **Always observe the 4-Hour Discard Rule for reconstituted MMR vaccine.** Reconstituted live lyophilized vaccines do not contain preservatives. Keeping open vials beyond **4 hours** at ambient temperature can lead to rapid proliferation of _Staphylococcus aureus_, causing fatal **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS)** in vaccinated infants.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the administration route of **MMR** with **HepB / DPT** vaccines.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **MMR** is a live viral vaccine given **Subcutaneously (SC)** at a **45° angle** in the upper deltoid. **HepB, DPT, and Pentavalent** are adsorbed killed/subunit vaccines given **Intramuscularly (IM)** at a **90° angle** in the anterolateral thigh.

### Quick Revision

- **Vaccine Types**: Live attenuated (MMR, OPV, BCG), Inactivated (IPV, Rabies), Toxoid (TT, Diphtheria), Subunit/Conjugate (HepB, Hib, PCV).
- **MMR Composition**: **Edmonston-Zagreb** (Measles), **Jeryl Lynn** (Mumps), **RA 27/3** (Rubella).
- **Dose & Route**: **0.5 mL Subcutaneous (SC)**.
- **IAP Schedule**: **9 months**, **15 months**, and **4–6 years**.
- **Golden Discard Rule**: Must discard reconstituted vaccine strictly within **4 hours**.
- **Key Contraindications**: Pregnancy (avoid conception for 1 month) and severe immunodeficiency.
