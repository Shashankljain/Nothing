# Complications of Sickle Cell Anemia [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sickle Cell Anemia** is an inherited autosomal recessive hemoglobinopathy caused by a point mutation (glutamic acid to valine substitution at position 6 of the β-globin chain) producing abnormal hemoglobin S (HbS), which polymerizes under deoxygenated conditions, causing red blood cells to deform into rigid sickle shapes, leading to microvascular occlusion and chronic hemolysis.

---

### 2. Major Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vaso-Occlusive Crises (VOC) & Acute Chest Syndrome**: Microvascular occlusion causes painful **dactylitis**("hand-foot syndrome" in infants), bone pain crises, priapism, and **Acute Chest Syndrome** (fever, chest pain, tachypnea, and new pulmonary infiltrates).
- **Neurological Complications**: Elevated risk of **acute ischemic stroke**, transient ischemic attacks (TIAs), and **cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST)** secondary to cerebral vasculopathy and thrombosis.
- **Splenic & Hematological Crises**: **Acute Splenic Sequestration Crisis** (rapid intrasplenic blood pooling causing sudden splenomegaly and hypovolemic shock), **Aplastic Crisis** (transient erythroblast arrest triggered by Parvovirus B19), and hyperhemolytic crisis.
- **Infectious Complications**: Early functional asplenia (secondary to recurrent micro-infarctions and progressive autosplenectomy) predisposes children to severe, life-threatening invasive infections by encapsulated organisms (_**Streptococcus pneumoniae**_, _**Haemophilus influenzae type b**_) and _**Salmonella**_ osteomyelitis.
- **Chronic End-Organ Damage**: Chronic hyposthenuria and **Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus**, avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head, proliferative retinopathy, gallstones (pigment stones from chronic hemolysis), and leg ulcers.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Vaso-Occlusive Phenomena**: Pain crises, dactylitis, and life-threatening Acute Chest Syndrome.
- **CNS Hazards**: High incidence of pediatric ischemic stroke and cerebral venous sinus thrombosis.
- **Splenic Crises**: Acute splenic sequestration (hypovolemic shock) and early autosplenectomy.
- **Infection Predisposition**: Severe sepsis from encapsulated bacteria (_S. pneumoniae_) due to functional asplenia.
- **Renal Impact**: Chronic hyposthenuria and Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Sickle cell anemia is a hereditary hemoglobinopathy whose major morbidity stems from microvascular vaso-occlusion and chronic hemolysis, manifesting as painful crises, acute chest syndrome, stroke, splenic sequestration, and severe infectious susceptibility due to functional asplenia.**
