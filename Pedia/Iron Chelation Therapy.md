# Iron Chelation Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Iron Chelation Therapy** is the targeted pharmacological administration of iron-binding agents to remove excess unbound, toxic iron (non-transferrin-bound iron) from systemic tissues and organs, preventing life-threatening hemosiderosis (cardiac, hepatic, and endocrine failure) in patients with transfusion-dependent chronic anemias, such as **β-Thalassemia Major**.

---

### 2. Indications & Core Clinical Points [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Initiation Criteria**: Started after a patient receives **10 to 20 packed RBC transfusions** or when serum ferritin levels exceed **>1000 μg/L** (or liver iron concentration >3 mg/g dry weight).
- **First-Line Oral Agent — Deferasirox**:
    - **Dose & Route**: **20 to 40 mg/kg/day** as a single daily oral dose (dispersible or film-coated tablet).
    - **Features**: Highly convenient oral once-daily dosing; primary first-line chelator in pediatric populations.
    - **Monitoring**: Check monthly serum creatinine and proteinuria (risk of mild renal impairment) and liver enzymes.
- **Cardiac Specific Oral Agent — Deferiprone**:
    - **Dose & Route**: **75 to 100 mg/kg/day** orally divided into 3 daily doses.
    - **Features**: Superior ability to cross cell membranes and clear **myocardial iron deposits** (treats/prevents heart failure).
    - **Toxicity & Monitoring**: Mandatory **weekly or biweekly absolute neutrophil count (ANC) monitoring** due to the risk of life-threatening **agranulocytosis** (1–2%) and severe neutropenia.
- **Parenteral Agent — Deferoxamine (Desferrioxamine)**:
    - **Dose & Route**: **20 to 40 mg/kg/day** administered as a slow subcutaneous or IV infusion over **8 to 12 hours**nightly (5 to 6 nights/week) using a portable infusion pump.
    - **Toxicity & Monitoring**: Requires annual **audiometry** (risk of sensorineural hearing loss), **ophthalmic examination** (cataracts/retinal toxicity), and growth monitoring.

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Deferiprone is the chelation drug of choice for clearing cardiac iron overload.** In thalassemia patients with severe cardiac iron deposition (cardiac MRI T2* <10 ms) or iron-induced heart failure, combination therapy using oral Deferiprone with subcutaneous Deferoxamine yields rapid synergetic clearance of cardiac and hepatic iron.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the key fatal side effect of **Deferiprone** with **Deferoxamine**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Deferiprone** causes severe **agranulocytosis** (requires strict routine ANC monitoring; stop immediately if fever or infection occurs). **Deferoxamine** causes **sensorineural ototoxicity and ocular toxicity**(requires annual audiometry and eye check-ups).

---

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: C-H-E-L-A-T-E

- **C** - **Cardiac iron clearance**: **Deferiprone** is most effective for heart iron
- **H** - **High Serum Ferritin (>1000 μg/L)**: Standard threshold to initiate chelation
- **E** - **Excretion of iron**: Excreted via stool (feces) and urine
- **L** - **Leukopenia / Agranulocytosis**: Major adverse effect of Deferiprone
- **A** - **Administration oral**: **Deferasirox (20–40 mg/kg/day)** provides once-daily oral convenience
- **T** - **Toxicity surveillance**: Renal/creatinine for Deferasirox; Audiometry/Vision for Deferoxamine
- **E** - **Eight to twelve hours**: Nightly subcutaneous infusion duration for Deferoxamine

---

### Quick Revision

- **Timing**: Start chelation after **10–20 transfusions** or when serum ferritin **>1000 μg/L**.
- **Deferasirox (Oral)**: **20–40 mg/kg/day** once daily (monitor serum creatinine).
- **Deferiprone (Oral)**: **75–100 mg/kg/day** in 3 divided doses (best for heart iron; monitor ANC for agranulocytosis).
- **Deferoxamine (SC/IV)**: **20–40 mg/kg/day** over 8–12 hours nightly (monitor hearing & vision).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Iron chelation therapy is an essential life-preserving intervention in transfusion-dependent thalassemic children, initiated when serum ferritin exceeds 1000 μg/L to prevent fatal cardiac and hepatic hemosiderosis using agents like oral Deferasirox, oral Deferiprone, or parenteral Deferoxamine.**
