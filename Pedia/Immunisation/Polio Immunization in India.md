[[Immunisation]]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Polio Immunization in India** refers to the comprehensive national public health strategy combining routine immunization under the Universal Immunization Program (**UIP**), **Pulse Polio Immunization (PPI)** campaigns, and **Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP) Surveillance** to eliminate and sustain the eradication of poliovirus.
- **Eradication Milestone**: India was officially certified **Polio-Free** by the WHO on **27th March 2014**, following three consecutive years with zero wild poliovirus cases (last case reported on **13th January 2011** in Howrah, West Bengal).

---

### 2. Etiology & Poliovirus Serotypes

- **Causative Agent**: Poliovirus, an enterovirus belonging to the _Picornaviridae_ family.
- **Serotypes**:
    - **Type 1 (Brunhilde)**: Most paralytogenic and historically responsible for most epidemics.
    - **Type 2 (Lansing)**: Globally eradicated in wild form (1999); primary cause of vaccine-derived poliovirus (**cVDPV**) outbreaks.
    - **Type 3 (Leon)**: Globally eradicated in wild form (2019).
- **Transmission**: Primary **fecal-oral route** through contaminated water and food.

---

### 3. Classification / Types of Polio Vaccines [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV / Sabin Vaccine)

- **Type**: Live attenuated viral vaccine containing attenuated strains.
- **Current Formulation**: **Bivalent OPV (bOPV)** containing serotypes **1 and 3** (tOPV containing Type 2 was globally withdrawn in **April 2016** to prevent Type 2 cVDPV).
- **Mechanism**: Replicates in gut intestinal epithelial cells, inducing robust local mucosal **secretory IgA** (preventing viral shedding and transmission) and systemic **IgG**.

#### B. Inactivated Polio Vaccine (IPV / Salk Vaccine) / Fractional IPV (fIPV)

- **Type**: Inactivated / Killed whole-virus vaccine containing killed serotypes **1, 2, and 3**.
- **Mechanism**: Induces high titers of circulating systemic **IgG antibodies**, preventing viremia and central nervous system invasion (paralysis).
- **Fractional IPV (fIPV)**: Intradermal administration of **0.1 mL** (**one-fifth** of full dose) introduced into India's UIP to stretch supply and provide Type 2 immunity.

### 5. Clinical Features

#### A. Signs

- **Asymmetric Flaccid Paralysis**: Sudden onset, lower limbs more commonly affected than upper limbs.
- **Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Signs**: Hypotonia, flaccidity, diminished or absent deep tendon reflexes (**areflexia**), and muscle atrophy.
- **Bulbar Palsy**: Difficulty swallowing, nasal regurgitation, and facial weakness if cranial nerve nuclei are involved.

#### B. Symptoms

- **Biphasic Illness**: Initial prodrome of fever, headache, sore throat, and gastrointestinal upset, followed 1–3 days later by severe myalgias and sudden motor weakness.
- **Intact Sensation**: Sensory perception remains completely normal despite severe motor loss.

---

### 6. Diagnosis & AFP Surveillance [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP) Surveillance**: The cornerstone strategy for maintaining polio-free status in India.
- **AFP Definition**: Any child **<15 years of age** presenting with sudden onset of flaccid paralysis.
- **Stool Specimen Collection Protocol**:
    - Collect **two stool specimens** (at least **8 g** each, size of an adult thumb).
    - Collected **24–48 hours apart**, within **14 days** of paralysis onset.
    - Transported under strict **reverse cold chain** (**+2°C to +8°C**) to WHO-accredited laboratories.
- **Laboratory Isolation**: Viral culture on **L20B** and **RD** cell lines followed by intratypic differentiation via PCR.
- **Surveillance Quality Benchmark**: Non-polio AFP rate must be maintained at **≥1 to ≥2 cases per 100,000 children <15 years**.

#### B. Pulse Polio Immunization (PPI) Program

- Launched in **1995** as a mass supplementary immunization strategy.
- **Target Population**: All children **under 5 years of age**.
- **Operational Strategy**: Conducted as National Immunization Days (**NIDs**) and Sub-National Immunization Days (**SNIDs**), administering **2 drops of bOPV** to every child regardless of previous routine vaccination status ("booth day" followed by house-to-house search and cover).

#### C. Vaccine Dosage & Administration Rules

1. **bivalent Oral Polio Vaccine (bOPV)**:
    
    - **Name**: bOPV (Types 1 and 3 live attenuated).
    - **Mechanism**: Mucosal IgA and systemic IgG immunity.
    - **Dose & Route**: **2 drops orally**.
    - **Frequency**: At birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks, 16–24 months, and all PPI rounds.
    - **Side Effects**: Extremely rare **Vaccine-Associated Paralytic Poliomyelitis (VAPP)**.
    - **When to Start/Stop**: Start at birth; routine doses up to 2 years, PPI rounds up to 5 years.
2. **Fractional Inactivated Polio Vaccine (fIPV)**:
    
    - **Name**: fIPV (Types 1, 2, 3 killed).
    - **Mechanism**: Systemic humoral IgG immunity protecting against paralysis.
    - **Dose & Route**: **0.1 mL Intradermal (ID)**.
    - **Frequency**: Minimum **2 doses** at **6 and 14 weeks**.
    - **Side Effects**: Mild local site induration or erythema.
    - **When to Start/Stop**: Initiated at 6 weeks of age.

---

### 9. Complications

- **Vaccine-Associated Paralytic Poliomyelitis (VAPP)**: Occurs in approximately 1 in 2.5 million doses of OPV due to spontaneous neurovirulence reversion of attenuated virus.
- **Circulating Vaccine-Derived Poliovirus (cVDPV)**: Reversion of OPV strain in under-immunized communities leading to person-to-person transmission.
- **Permanent Residual Paralysis**: Fixed joint deformities, limb shortening, and respiratory insufficiency requiring long-term rehabilitation.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Sustained herd immunity in India with zero wild poliovirus transmission since 2011.
- **Poor**: If paralytic polio occurs, anterior horn cell loss is permanent, causing lifelong paralysis.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-O-L-I-O

- **P** - **Pulse Polio (1995)**: Mass bOPV rounds for all children **<5 years**
- **O** - **OPV Zero dose**: Administered at birth alongside BCG and HepB
- **L** - **L20B & RD Cell Lines**: Laboratory culture for stool viral isolation
- **I** - **Intradermal fIPV (0.1 mL)**: Given at **6 and 14 weeks**
- **O** - **One in 100,000 Target**: Non-polio AFP surveillance baseline rate

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Always combine bOPV and fIPV.** While bOPV provides essential gut mucosal IgA immunity to stop community fecal-oral transmission, fIPV provides reliable systemic IgG immunity against Type 2 poliovirus, protecting the individual child from paralysis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the dosage and route of full-dose **IPV** vs. **fractional IPV (fIPV)**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Full-dose **IPV** is **0.5 mL Intramuscularly (IM)** or **Subcutaneously (SC)**. **Fractional IPV (fIPV)** used in India's UIP is **0.1 mL Intradermal (ID)** at a 15° angle over the right upper arm.
