# Nephrotic Syndrome [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Nephrotic Syndrome** is a clinical state characterized by heavy glomerular proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, generalized edema (anasarca), and hyperlipidemia resulting from an abnormal increase in glomerular basement membrane permeability to plasma proteins.
- **Diagnostic Criteria (Tetrad)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    1. **Heavy (Nephrotic-Range) Proteinuria**: Excretion **>40 mg/m²/hr** (or **>1 g/m²/day**); spot urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (**Up/Uc**) **>2.0 mg/mg**; spot urine dipstick **3+ to 4+**.
    2. **Hypoalbuminemia**: Serum albumin concentration **<3.0 g/dL** (frequently **<1.5–2.0 g/dL**).
    3. **Generalized Edema (Anasarca)**: Soft, pitting edema starting periorbitally in the morning and progressing to legs, presacral region, scrotum/labia, ascites, and pleural effusions.
    4. **Hyperlipidemia**: Serum total cholesterol **>200–400 mg/dL** and elevated serum triglycerides secondary to hepatic upregulation of lipoprotein synthesis.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Primary / Idiopathic Nephrotic Syndrome (INS) (>90% of Childhood Cases)

- **Minimal Change Disease (MCD / MCNS)**: Accounts for **80%** of childhood nephrotic syndrome (peak age **2–6 years**; higher incidence in boys).
- **Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)**: Accounts for **10–15%** of cases.
- **Mesangial Proliferative Glomerulonephritis (MesPGN)**: Accounts for **3–5%** of cases.
- **Membranous Nephropathy & C3 Glomerulopathy**: Rare in young children.

#### B. Secondary Nephrotic Syndrome

- **Infections**: Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV, Malaria (_Plasmodium ==malariae==_), Syphilis.
- **Systemic / Autoimmune**: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (**SLE**), Henoch-Schönlein Purpura (IgA Vasculitis), Amyloidosis.
- **Drugs & Toxins**: Heavy metals (gold, lead, cadmium), NSAIDs, penicillamine.

#### C. Congenital & Infantile Nephrotic Syndrome

- Manifests within the first **3 months** of life (Congenital NS) or **3–12 months** (Infantile NS).
- **Finnish Type (_NPHS1_ gene mutation)**: Codes for **nephrin** (autosomal recessive); characterized by microcystic dilatation of proximal tubules.
- **Steroid-Resistant Podocin Defect (_NPHS2_ gene mutation)**: Codes for **podocin** (autosomal recessive).
- **Denys-Drash Syndrome (_WT1_ gene mutation)**: Autosomal dominant; associated with Wilms tumor and pseudohermaphroditism.

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Based on Histopathology

1. **Non-Proliferative Glomerulopathy**: Minimal Change Disease (MCNS), Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), Membranous Nephropathy.
2. **Proliferative Glomerulopathy**: Mesangial Proliferative GN, Membranoproliferative GN (MPGN), C3 Glomerulopathy.

#### B. Based on Response to Corticosteroids

|Category|Diagnostic Operational Definition|
|:--|:--|
|**Steroid-Sensitive (SSNS)**|Attainment of complete remission (urine albumin nil/trace or **<4 mg/m²/hr** for **3 consecutive days**) within **4–6 weeks** of daily prednisolone.|
|**Steroid-Resistant (SRNS)**|Absence of remission despite treatment with oral prednisolone at **2 mg/kg/day (60 mg/m²/day)** for **6 weeks**.|
|**Infrequent Relapser**|**<2 relapses** in the first 6 months, or **<3 relapses** in any 12-month period.|
|**Frequent Relapser (FRNS)**|**≥2 relapses** in the first 6 months, or **≥3 relapses** in any 12-month period.|
|**Steroid-Dependent (SDNS)**|**2 consecutive relapses** occurring while on alternate-day prednisolone or within **14 days** of stopping steroids.|

### 4. Histopathological Changes [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Minimal Change Disease (MCNS / MCD)

- **Light Microscopy (LM)**: Glomeruli appear **morphologically unremarkable** or normal; no significant cellular proliferation or basement membrane thickening.
- **Electron Microscopy (EM)**: **Diffuse effacement (fusion) of epithelial podocyte foot processes**, actin condensation, and microvillous transformation.
- **Immunofluorescence (IF)**: **Negative**; does not demonstrate deposition of immune reactants (except occasional minimal mesangial IgM).

#### B. Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)

- **Light Microscopy (LM)**: Sclerosis involving a segment of the glomerular tuft in a focal distribution (affecting <50% of glomeruli on biopsy).
- **Electron Microscopy (EM)**: Diffuse podocyte foot process effacement with focal detachment of podocytes from GBM.
- **Immunofluorescence (IF)**: IgM and C3 trapping in sclerotic segments.

#### C. Membranous Nephropathy

- **Light Microscopy (LM)**: Diffusely thickened glomerular basement membrane with "spike and dome" projections on silver stain.

- **Podocyte Exudation & Charge Defect**: Cell-mediated immune dysregulation leads to circulating permeability factors that neutralize negatively charged polyanionic heparan sulfate in the Glomerular Basement Membrane (**GBM**), causing **podocyte foot process effacement** and non-selective albuminuria.
- **Underfill Mechanism (Classic MCNS)**: Massive proteinuria → hypoalbuminemia → reduced plasma oncotic pressure → fluid transudation into interstitium → intravascular hypovolemia → activation of Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (**RAAS**) and antidiuretic hormone (**ADH**) → secondary sodium and water retention.

### 6. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Insidious Edema**: Swelling starts periorbitally upon waking in the morning, gradually expanding over days to involve the legs, presacral area, abdomen, and genitalia.
- **Urinary Changes**: Decreased urine volume (**oliguria**) and dark, frothy urine.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Anorexia, vague abdominal pain, and diarrhea (secondary to bowel wall mucosal edema).

#### B. Signs

- **Periorbital & Dependent Edema**: Soft, doughy, pitting edema.
- **Abdominal Distension**: Shifting dullness and fluid thrill indicating **ascites**; hydrocele or scrotal edema.
- **Cardiovascular & Vitals**:
    - Blood pressure is **normal** in >85–90% of MCNS cases.
    - Tachycardia, cool extremities, low pulse volume, and capillary refill **>3 seconds** indicate **intravascular hypovolemia**.

---

### 7. Diagnosis / Investigations (Urinary & Biochemical Changes) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Urinary Changes

- **Proteinuria**: Spot dipstick **3+ to 4+** (sulfosalicylic acid test positive).
- **Protein Quantitation**: Spot urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (**Up/Uc**) **>2.0 mg/mg** (or 24-hour urine protein **>40 mg/m²/hr** / **>1 g/m²/day**).
- **Microscopy**: Hyaline and granular casts, oval fat bodies; microscopic hematuria is **infrequent (<20% in MCNS)**; gross hematuria suggests non-minimal lesions.

#### B. Biochemical & Blood Changes

- **Serum Albumin**: Markedly reduced to **<3.0 g/dL** (frequently **<1.0–2.0 g/dL**).
- **Lipid Profile**: Markedly elevated serum cholesterol (**>200–500 mg/dL**), triglycerides, VLDL, and LDL.
- **Renal Function Tests**: Blood urea nitrogen (**BUN**) and serum creatinine are typically **normal**; mild elevations indicate prerenal hypovolemia or AKI.
- **Serum Electrolytes**: Dilutional hyponatremia (Na⁺ **<135 mEq/L**); serum calcium is low due to reduced protein-bound fraction, but ionized calcium is normal.
- **Serum Complement C3**: **Normal** in MCNS (low C3 indicates MPGN, C3 glomerulopathy, SLE, or PSGN).

#### C. Pre-Steroid Infection Screening

- **Mantoux Test (TST)** and **Chest X-ray**: Mandatory prior to starting immunosuppressive steroids to rule out latent tuberculosis.
- Hepatitis B (HBsAg), Hepatitis C, and HIV serology.

#### D. Indications for Kidney Biopsy

Kidney biopsy is **NOT required** for the typical initial episode of nephrotic syndrome in children aged **1 to 12 years**.

- **Indications for Pre-Treatment Biopsy**: Age **<1 year** or **>12 years**, persistent gross hematuria, persistent hypertension, persistent elevation of serum creatinine, or low serum C3.
- **Indications for Post-Treatment Biopsy**: **Steroid-Resistant Nephrotic Syndrome (SRNS)** after 6 weeks of daily prednisolone.

1. **Phase 1 (Daily Steroids)**:
    - **Oral Prednisolone**: **2 mg/kg/day** (or **60 mg/m²/day**) as a single morning dose (maximum **60 mg/day**) for **6 weeks**.
2. **Phase 2 (Alternate-Day Steroids)**:
    - **Oral Prednisolone**: **1.5 mg/kg/dose** (or **40 mg/m²/dose**) as a single morning dose on alternate days (maximum **40 mg/day**) for **6 weeks**.
    - **Total Duration of Initial Episode Regimen**: **12 weeks** (6 weeks daily + 6 weeks alternate days).

#### B. Supportive Care & Symptomatic Management

- **Dietary Measures**:
    - **Salt Restriction**: Low sodium diet (**no added salt**) during active edema; resume normal salt once in remission.
    - **Protein Intake**: Normal recommended age-appropriate protein (**1.5–2.0 g/kg/day**); **high-protein diets are strictly contraindicated** as they worsen glomerular hyperfiltration and proteinuria.
- **Diuretic Therapy**:
    - **Oral Furosemide (1–3 mg/kg/day)** ± **Spironolactone (2–4 mg/kg/day)** for significant symptomatic edema.
    - _Caution_: Avoid aggressive diuresis to prevent intravascular volume depletion, hypovolemic shock, and thrombosis.
- **Management of Severe Refractory Edema / Hypovolemia**:
    - **20% Salt-Poor Human Albumin Infusion**: **0.5–1.0 g/kg IV** over **2–4 hours**, followed mid-way or immediately by IV **Furosemide (1–2 mg/kg)**.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Severe Infections (Leading Cause of Morbidity & Mortality)

- **Pathophysiology**: Urinary loss of IgG, complement Factor B, and Properdin + impaired opsonization + steroid immunosuppression.
- **Key Pathogens**: _**Streptococcus pneumoniae**_ (most common), _E. coli_, _Klebsiella_, and Varicella virus.
- **Clinical Presentations**: **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** (fever, abdominal pain, diffuse tenderness), cellulitis, pneumonia, and sepsis.

#### B. Thromboembolic Complications (Medical Emergency)

- **Pathophysiology**: Urinary loss of Antithrombin III, Protein S, and Protein C + increased hepatic synthesis of Fibrinogen (Factors V, VII, VIII, X) + hemoconcentration & aggressive diuretic use.
- **Sites**: Renal vein thrombosis, deep vein thrombosis (**DVT**), pulmonary embolism, and sagittal sinus thrombosis (stroke).

#### C. Intravascular Hypovolemia & Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

- Severe intravascular volume depletion ("underfill") secondary to acute fluid shifts, diarrhea, vomiting, or excessive unmonitored diuretic use leads to abdominal pain, tachycardia, delayed capillary refill, elevated BUN/creatinine ratio, and prerenal AKI.

#### D. Steroid Toxicity

- Cushingoid features, short stature, hypertension, posterior subcapsular cataract, osteoporosis, behavioral changes, and impaired glucose tolerance.

---

### 11. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Long-Term Outcome**: Over **85%** of children with Minimal Change Disease (MCNS) achieve permanent remission by late adolescence without long-term decline in GFR or progression to chronic kidney disease.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: N-E-P-H-R-O-T-I-C

- **N** - **Nephrotic-range proteinuria**: **>40 mg/m²/hr** or Up/Uc **>2.0 mg/mg**
- **E** - **Edema (Anasarca)**: Insidious periorbital and dependent pitting swelling
- **P** - **Prednisolone first-line**: **2 mg/kg/day for 6 wks**, then **1.5 mg/kg alternate days for 6 wks**
- **H** - **Hypoalbuminemia**: Serum albumin **<3.0 g/dL**
- **R** - **Relapses frequent**: Treated with Levamisole, Cyclophosphamide, MMF, or CNIs
- **O** - **Opsonization loss**: Predisposes to **_Streptococcus pneumoniae_ SBP**
- **T** - **Thromboembolism risk**: Loss of Antithrombin III & elevated fibrinogen
- **I** - **IgG loss & Normal C3**: Normal complement C3 rules out nephritic syndromes
- **C** - **Cholesterol elevation**: Hyperlipidemia secondary to hepatic lipid synthesis

### Clinical Pearl

> **Never perform a routine kidney biopsy in a typical 2-year-old presenting with an initial episode of nephrotic syndrome.** Over 80–85% of children aged 1 to 12 years with typical features (normotensive, normal C3, no gross hematuria) have Minimal Change Disease (MCNS) and achieve remission with oral prednisolone.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Recommending a high-protein diet to "replace" urinary protein losses in nephrotic syndrome.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: High-protein diets increase intraglomerular pressure, accelerate glomerular damage, and increase proteinuria without raising serum albumin levels. Children should receive an **age-appropriate normal protein diet (1.5–2 g/kg/day)** with low salt during the active edema phase.

### Quick Revision

- **Classic Tetrad**: Heavy proteinuria (**>40 mg/m²/hr**), Hypoalbuminemia (**<3.0 g/dL**), Edema (Anasarca), and Hyperlipidemia.
- **MCNS Frequency**: Accounts for **80%** of pediatric nephrotic syndrome (peak age 2–6 years).
- **Initial Steroid Protocol**: Prednisolone **2 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks**, followed by **1.5 mg/kg alternate days for 6 weeks** (total 12 weeks).
- **Kidney Biopsy Rule**: Not required before treatment in typical cases (age 1–12 yrs, normal BP, normal C3, no gross hematuria).
- **Major Infection Risk**: **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** caused by _Streptococcus pneumoniae_.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Pediatric Nephrotic Syndrome is a glomerular permeability disorder defined by heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, anasarca, and hyperlipidemia, with Minimal Change Disease accounting for 80% of cases and responding to a 12-week regimen of oral prednisolone.**
