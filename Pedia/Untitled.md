
# Acute Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP) [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acute Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)** is an acquired, self-limiting autoimmune hematological disorder of childhood characterized by **isolated severe thrombocytopenia** (platelet count **<100,000/μL**, frequently **<20,000/μL**) without anemia or leukopenia.
- It presents with the sudden onset of cutaneous and mucosal petechial bleeding in an otherwise healthy child.
- **Acute Duration**: Symptoms last **<3 months**; spontaneous complete recovery occurs in **>80%** of pediatric patients within 3 to 6 months.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Post-Viral Antecedent Prodrome**: Occurs **1 to 4 weeks** following a viral upper respiratory tract infection, exanthematous fever, or gastroenteritis in up to 80% of children.
- **Common Triggering Pathogens**:
    - Epstein-Barr Virus (**EBV**), Varicella-Zoster Virus (**VZV**), Cytomegalovirus (**CMV**), Parvovirus B19, Measles, Mumps, Rubella, Influenza, and SARS-CoV-2.
- **Vaccine-Associated**: Rare cases follow **MMR vaccination** (self-limiting, risk far lower than natural rubella or measles infection).
- **Peak Age & Gender**: Peak incidence between **2 and 6 years** of age, affecting males and females equally in childhood.

---

### 3. Classification

- **Newly Diagnosed (Acute) ITP**: Onset to duration **<3 months** (spontaneous resolution in **80–85%**).
- **Persistent ITP**: Thrombocytopenia lasting between **3 and 12 months** without spontaneous remission.
- **Chronic ITP**: Thrombocytopenia persisting for **>12 months** (more common in adolescents and females; requires evaluation for secondary autoimmune diseases like SLE).

- **Autoantibody Generation**: Viral antigens induce production of anti-platelet autoantibodies (predominantly **IgG**) via molecular mimicry.
- **Target Antigens**: Antibodies target platelet membrane glycoprotein complexes (**GPIIb/IIIa** or **GPIb/IX**).
- **Splenic Clearance**: Opsonized platelets bind to **Fcγ receptors** on tissue macrophages in the **spleen**, undergoing rapid destruction.
- **Bone Marrow Compensation**: Decreased circulating platelets stimulate erythropoietin-like megakaryocyte stimulation, producing **megakaryocytic hyperplasia** in the bone marrow.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Signs

- **Cutaneous Bleeding**:
    - **Petechiae**: Non-blanching pinpoint dark red/purple spots over bony prominences, face, and trunk.
    - **Purpura & Ecchymoses**: Spontaneous, prominent blue-purple bruises over lower extremities and extensor surfaces.
- **Mucosal Bleeding ("Wet Purpura")**:
    - **Epistaxis** (nosebleeds) and gingival bleeding.
    - **Hemorrhagic bullae** on buccal mucosa and lips ("wet purpura" indicates higher risk of severe internal bleeding).
    - Gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis, melena) or hematuria.
- **Physical Examination Hallmark**:
    - **Normal Spleen Size**: Absence of significant splenomegaly or lymphadenopathy.
    - _Clinical Rule_: Marked hepatosplenomegaly or significant lymphadenopathy strongly argues **against** acute ITP and points toward acute leukemia or lymphoma.

#### B. Symptoms

- **Well-Child Presentation**: The child appears **conspicuously well, active, and afebrile**, despite extensive generalized bruising.
- Sudden, abrupt onset of cutaneous bleeding in a previously healthy child.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**:
    - **Isolated Severe Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<20,000/μL** in acute severe presentations (normal reference: 150,000–450,000/μL).
    - **Hemoglobin & Red Cell Indices**: Completely normal (unless significant secondary acute blood loss anemia has occurred).
    - **Total Leukocyte Count (TLC) & Differential**: Normal.
- **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**:
    - **Decreased platelet numbers** with **large/giant platelets** (**megathrombocytes**), indicating active accelerated bone marrow release.
    - Normal RBC and WBC morphology; **total absence of blast cells** or schistocytes.
- **Bone Marrow Aspirate (BMA)**:
    - **Not routinely required** in classic acute pediatric ITP.
    - **Indications for BMA**: Atypical features (unexplained fever, bone pain, systemic symptoms, weight loss, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, unexplained anemia/leukopenia) OR prior to initiating systemic corticosteroid therapy (to rule out Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia).
    - **Findings**: **Increased / hyperplastic megakaryocytes** with normal erythropoiesis and granulopoiesis.
- **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (**PT**) and Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (**aPTT**) are strictly normal.

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Management Principles

- **Core Objective**: The goal of treatment is to prevent life-threatening intracranial hemorrhage (**ICH**, risk **<0.5–1%**), not merely to normalize platelet counts.

#### B. Non-Pharmacological / Observation Strategy

- Indicated for children with **mild cutaneous bleeding only** (petechiae and ecchymoses) regardless of platelet count, or platelet count **>20,000/μL** without active mucosal bleeding.
- **Supportive Care**: Strict avoidance of contact sports/trauma, soft toothbrushing, and total avoidance of anti-platelet/NSAID medications (**aspirin, ibuprofen**).

#### C. Pharmacological Treatment Protocol & Dosage Table [⭐ High-Yield]

#### D. Emergency Management of Life-Threatening Hemorrhage (Intracranial Hemorrhage)

- Administer **Platelet Transfusion** immediately in combination with **IVIG (1 g/kg)** and **IV High-Dose Methylprednisolone (30 mg/kg)**.
- Urgent neurosurgical consultation if intracranial bleeding is identified on emergency non-contrast CT head.

---

### 9. Complications

- **Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH)**: Rare (**<0.5–1%**) but most lethal complication.
- Severe gastrointestinal hemorrhage or heavy hematuria.
- Progression to **Chronic ITP** (15–20% of pediatric cases).

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Over **80–85%** of pediatric patients achieve complete spontaneous remission within **3 to 6 months**.
- Overall mortality is extremely low (**<0.5%**), almost exclusively attributable to intracranial hemorrhage.

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: I-T-P

- **I** - **Isolated Thrombocytopenia**: Platelets **<20,000/μL** with normal Hb and WBCs in an active, well child
- **T** - **Transient / T-cell & Fc-mediated**: Responds rapidly to **IVIG (1 g/kg)** and Steroids
- **P** - **Post-viral prodrome**: Onset 1–4 weeks following viral illness

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **A child with acute ITP looks surprisingly well despite dramatic generalized bruising.** If a child presenting with purpura displays high fever, severe pallor, bone tenderness, or organomegaly, suspect **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL)** or sepsis rather than acute ITP.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Ordering immediate emergency platelet transfusions for non-bleeding ITP children with low platelet counts (<10,000/μL).
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Transfused platelets are destroyed within minutes by circulating autoantibodies and splenic macrophages. Platelet transfusions are strictly reserved for **life-threatening hemorrhage (e.g., intracranial bleeding)** and must be co-administered with **IVIG and high-dose IV steroids**.

- **Type**: Labeled cell-interaction pathway diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Oval splenic macrophage with surface Fc receptors interacting with antibody-coated platelets.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Platelet Surface**: GP IIb/IIIa and GP Ib/IX receptors.
    2. **Anti-Platelet IgG Autoantibodies**: Y-shaped autoantibodies bound to platelet membrane.
    3. **Splenic Macrophage Fcγ Receptors**: Surface receptors mediating phagocytosis.
    4. **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: Exogenous IgG molecules flooding and competitively blocking Fcγ receptors.
- **Arrows/connections**: Autoantibody Binding → Platelet Opsonization → Fcγ Receptor Engagement → Macrophage Phagocytosis → Thrombocytopenia (**Competitive Blockade by IVIG**).
- **Extra Mark Annotation**: IVIG saturates macrophage Fcγ receptors within **24–48 hours**, halting native platelet clearance without producing marrow suppression.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Isolated severe thrombocytopenia (**<20,000/μL**) in a healthy-looking child, usually 1–4 weeks post-viral infection.
- **Key Physical Finding**: Generalized petechiae and purpura with **no hepatosplenomegaly** or lymphadenopathy.
- **Bone Marrow Rule**: BMA shows **megakaryocytic hyperplasia**; BMA is mandatory before starting corticosteroids if features are atypical to rule out ALL.
- **First-Line Medical Therapy**: **IVIG (1 g/kg)** or **Oral Prednisolone (2–4 mg/kg/day)** for active mucosal bleeding ("wet purpura").
- **Platelet Transfusions**: Strictly reserved for **life-threatening hemorrhage (e.g., ICH)** alongside IVIG and IV steroids.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Acute immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) is a self-limiting autoimmune disorder of childhood characterized by isolated severe thrombocytopenia following a viral prodrome, presenting as acute cutaneous and mucosal bleeding in an otherwise well child.**
