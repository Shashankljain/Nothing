# Beta Thalassemia Major — Laboratory Diagnosis & Treatment [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Beta Thalassemia Major (Cooley's Anemia)** is a severe, hereditary microcytic hemolytic anemia inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, characterized by the absent or severely reduced synthesis of β-globin chains (β⁰/β⁰ or β⁰/β⁺), resulting in excess unpaired α-globin chains that precipitate within erythroid precursors, causing severe ineffective erythropoiesis and peripheral hemolysis.

---

### 2. Laboratory Diagnosis & Peripheral Smear Findings [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Severe Microcytic Hypochromic Anemia**: Hemoglobin levels are profoundly reduced (frequently **<5–7 g/dL**) with low MCV (**<70 fL**) and low MCH (**<20 pg**).
- **Classic Peripheral Smear Poikilocytosis**:
    - **Target Cells (Codocytes)**: Abundant "bullseye" red cells with central hemoglobin condensation.
    - **Anisopoikilocytosis**: Severe variation in red cell size and shape, with teardrop cells (dacrocytes), fragmented RBCs (schistocytes), and hypochromic microcytes.
    - **Nucleated Red Blood Cells (Normoblasts)**: Marked presence of circulating erythroblasts reflecting extreme bone marrow erythroid hyperplasia.
    - **Basophilic Stippling**: Coarse blue punctate inclusions within erythrocytes.
- **Confirmatory HPLC / Hemoglobin Electrophoresis**: Demonstrates **marked elevation of HbF (>90–95%)**, variable or elevated **HbA2 (>3.5%)**, and complete **absence or trace amounts of adult HbA**.

---

### 3. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Blood Transfusion Regimen

- **Hypertransfusion Program**: Regular, lifelong packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusions administered every **2 to 4 weeks** to maintain pre-transfusion hemoglobin levels between **9.5 and 10.5 g/dL** (and post-transfusion Hb around 13–14 g/dL) to suppress ineffective erythropoiesis, inhibit extramedullary hematopoiesis, and promote normal growth.
- **Transfusion Type**: Leukoreduced, phenotypically matched, washed PRBCs to minimize febrile non-hemolytic reactions and alloimmunization.

#### B. Iron Chelation Therapy (To Prevent Transfusion Hemosiderosis)

- **Indication**: Initiate after receiving **10–20 packed cell transfusions** or when serum ferritin exceeds **1000 μg/L**.
- **Chelating Agents**:
    - **Deferasirox**: Oral iron chelator given once daily at **20–40 mg/kg/day** (first-line oral agent).
    - **Deferiprone**: Oral iron chelator at **75–100 mg/kg/day** in 3 divided doses (highly effective for myocardial iron clearance; monitor ANC for agranulocytosis).
    - **Deferoxamine**: Subcutaneous or IV infusion at **20–40 mg/kg/day** over 8–12 hours nightly via a portable pump.

#### C. Definitive Therapy & Supportive Care

- **Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)**: The only proven curative treatment option when an HLA-matched donor is available.
- **Supportive Care**: Daily Folic Acid supplementation (**1 mg/day**), splenectomy (if annual transfusion requirement exceeds **>200 mL/kg/year** of PRBCs), and routine screening for cardiac, hepatic, and endocrine complications.

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Never prescribe iron supplements to a child with Beta Thalassemia Major.** Despite having microcytic hypochromic red cells on peripheral smear, these children suffer from severe systemic iron overload due to chronic transfusions and hyperabsorption; giving exogenous iron exacerbates life-threatening cardiac and hepatic hemosiderosis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Differentiating **Beta Thalassemia Trait (Minor)** from **Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)** on HPLC and indices.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Mentzer Index (MCV ÷ RBC count) is **<13** in Thalassemia Trait and **>13** in IDA. Hemoglobin Electrophoresis/HPLC shows **HbA2 >3.5%** in Thalassemia Trait, whereas HbA2 is normal or decreased in IDA.

---

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: T-H-A-L

- **T** - **Target cells & Teardrop cells**: Characteristic poikilocytes on peripheral smear
- **H** - **HbF >90% on HPLC**: Confirmatory diagnostic hallmark of Thalassemia Major
- **A** - **Aim for pre-transfusion Hb 9.5–10.5 g/dL**: Goal of hypertransfusion therapy
- **L** - **Lowers iron via Chelation (Deferasirox 20–40 mg/kg/day)**: Essential once ferritin exceeds 1000 μg/L

---

### Quick Revision

- **Peripheral Smear**: Severe microcytic hypochromia, target cells, teardrop cells, nucleated RBCs, and basophilic stippling.
- **HPLC Confirmation**: Marked elevation of **HbF (>90–95%)** with low or absent HbA.
- **Transfusion Goal**: Hypertransfusion to maintain pre-transfusion Hb **9.5–10.5 g/dL**.
- **Iron Chelation**: Oral **Deferasirox (20–40 mg/kg/day)** started when serum ferritin >1000 μg/L.
- **Curative Therapy**: Allogeneic HLA-matched Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT).
