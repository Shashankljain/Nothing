# Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP) — Investigations & Treatment [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)** is an acquired autoimmune hematological disorder of childhood characterized by **isolated severe thrombocytopenia** (platelet count **<100,000/μL**, frequently **<20,000/μL**) with normal red and white cell lines, resulting from autoantibody-mediated platelet destruction following a post-viral prodrome.

---

### 2. Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Demonstrates isolated severe thrombocytopenia (frequently **<20,000/μL**) with completely normal hemoglobin concentration and total/differential leukocyte counts.
- **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Markedly reduced platelet numbers with large, young **megathrombocytes**; red and white blood cell morphology is completely normal with a total absence of blast cells or schistocytes.
- **Bone Marrow Aspirate (BMA)**: **Not routinely required** in classic acute pediatric ITP; indicated only for atypical features (fever, organomegaly, bone pain, unexplained anemia/leukopenia) or prior to starting corticosteroids to rule out Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL). Shows **megakaryocytic hyperplasia**.
- **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT) and Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT) are strictly normal.

---

### 3. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Non-Pharmacological / Observation

- **Observation Strategy**: Indicated for children with mild cutaneous bleeding only (petechiae and ecchymoses) or platelet count **>20,000/μL** without active mucosal bleeding.
- **Supportive Care**: Avoidance of contact sports, hard physical trauma, and strict avoidance of anti-platelet agents / NSAIDs (**aspirin, ibuprofen**).

#### B. Pharmacological Therapy (For Active Mucosal Bleeding / "Wet Purpura")

- **Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG)**: First-line agent for rapid platelet elevation; given at **1 g/kg IV** as a single dose (or 400 mg/kg/day for 2–5 days) to competitively block splenic macrophage Fcγ receptors.
- **Corticosteroids**: Oral **Prednisolone at 2–4 mg/kg/day** (maximum **60 mg/day**) for 4–7 days, or IV **Methylprednisolone 30 mg/kg/day** for 3 days. _(Note: BMA is recommended prior to starting corticosteroids if atypical features are present)._
- **Intravenous Anti-D**: **50–75 μg/kg IV** as a single dose (effective only in Rh-D positive, non-splenectomized children with Hb >10 g/dL).

#### C. Emergency Management (Life-Threatening / Intracranial Hemorrhage)

- Administer immediate **Platelet Transfusion** combined with **IVIG (1 g/kg)** and **IV High-Dose Methylprednisolone (30 mg/kg)**.

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Platelet transfusions are ineffective for routine non-bleeding ITP.** Transfused platelets are cleared within minutes by circulating autoantibodies. Transfusions are strictly reserved for life-threatening emergencies (e.g., intracranial hemorrhage) and must be co-administered with IVIG and high-dose steroids.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing a bone marrow aspiration on every child with suspected ITP.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: BMA is **NOT required** for typical acute ITP presenting with isolated thrombocytopenia in a well child. BMA is mandatory only if atypical features (fever, bone pain, organomegaly, blast cells) exist or before starting corticosteroids.

---

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: I-T-P

- **I** - **Isolated Thrombocytopenia**: Platelets **<20,000/μL** with normal Hb and WBCs
- **T** - **Treatment with IVIG (1 g/kg) or Steroids**: First-line for active mucosal bleeding
- **P** - **Platelet transfusion reserved**: Strictly for emergency life-threatening hemorrhage

---

### Quick Revision

- **Peripheral Smear**: Isolated thrombocytopenia with megathrombocytes and no blasts.
- **Bone Marrow Findings**: Megakaryocytic hyperplasia (indicated only for atypical features or pre-steroids).
- **First-Line Medical Therapy**: **IVIG (1 g/kg)** or **oral Prednisolone (2–4 mg/kg/day)** for "wet purpura".
- **Emergency Bleeding**: Platelet transfusion + IVIG + high-dose IV Methylprednisolone.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP) is an autoimmune disorder diagnosed by isolated severe thrombocytopenia on peripheral smear and managed with IVIG (1 g/kg) or corticosteroids for active mucosal bleeding, reserving platelet transfusions strictly for life-threatening hemorrhage.**
