# Anemia and Thalassemia Major — Definition, Classification, Management & Prevention [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition of Anemia

- **Anemia** is defined as a reduction in circulating hemoglobin (Hb) concentration, hematocrit (packed cell volume), or total red blood cell (RBC) mass below **2 standard deviations (-2 SD)** of the mean for healthy individuals of the same age, sex, and altitude.
- **Age-Specific Diagnostic Hemoglobin Cut-Offs (WHO / Pediatric Criteria)**:
    - **Birth (Term Newborn)**: Hb <14.0 g/dL
    - **1 to 2 Months**: Hb <9.5 g/dL (Physiological Nadir)
    - **6 Months to 5 Years**: Hb <11.0 g/dL
    - **5 to 11 Years**: Hb <11.5 g/dL
    - **12 to 14 Years**: Hb <12.0 g/dL

#### B. Definition of Beta Thalassemia Major

- **Beta Thalassemia Major (Cooley's Anemia)** is a severe, inherited autosomal recessive microcytic hemolytic anemia resulting from the complete absence (β⁰) or profound reduction (β⁺) of beta-globin chain synthesis [⭐ High-Yield].
- Characterized by an absolute excess of unpaired α-globin chains, leading to intramedullary destruction of red cell precursors (ineffective erythropoiesis), severe peripheral hemolysis, and lifelong **transfusion dependency**presenting in early infancy.

#### A. Morphological Classification (Based on Mean Corpuscular Volume / MCV) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Microcytic Hypochromic Anemia (MCV <80 fL, MCH <27 pg)**:
    - **Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)** (most common cause worldwide).
    - **Beta-Thalassemia Trait / Beta-Thalassemia Major**.
    - **Anemia of Chronic Disease** (late stage).
    - **Sideroblastic Anemia** and **Lead Poisoning**.
2. **Normocytic Normochromic Anemia (MCV 80–100 fL, MCH >27 pg)**:
    - **Acute Hemorrhage / Blood Loss**.
    - **Hemolytic Anemias**: Hereditary Spherocytosis, G6PD Deficiency, Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA), Sickle Cell Disease.
    - **Aplastic Anemia / Bone Marrow Infiltration** (Leukemia).
    - **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)** (reduced erythropoietin).
3. **Macrocytic Anemia (MCV >100 fL)**:
    - **Megaloblastic**: Vitamin B₁₂ deficiency, Folate deficiency.
    - **Non-Megaloblastic**: Hypothyroidism, Liver disease, Aplastic anemia, Diamond-Blackfan anemia, Reticulocytosis.

#### B. Etiological / Physiological Classification

1. **Decreased / Impaired Red Blood Cell Production**:
    - **Nutritional Deficiencies**: Iron, Vitamin B₁₂, Folate, Copper, Protein-Energy Malnutrition.
    - **Bone Marrow Failure / Suppression**: Aplastic anemia, Fanconi anemia, Pure Red Cell Aplasia, Leukemia, Infection (Parvovirus B19, HIV).
    - **Chronic Systemic Disease**: Chronic kidney disease, Chronic inflammation, Hypothyroidism.
2. **Increased Red Blood Cell Destruction (Hemolysis)**:
    - **Intrinsic (Corpuscular) Defects**:
        - _Membrane_: Hereditary Spherocytosis, Hereditary Elliptocytosis.
        - _Enzyme_: G6PD deficiency, Pyruvate Kinase deficiency.
        - _Hemoglobin_: Thalassemia major, Sickle cell anemia, HbE disease.
    - **Extrinsic (Extracorpuscular) Defects**:
        - _Immune_: Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA), Rh/ABO Isoimmunization.
        - _Non-Immune / Mechanical_: Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS), DIC, Hypersplenism, Malaria, Wilson disease.
3. **Blood Loss**:
    - **Acute**: Trauma, surgical hemorrhage, placental abruption, neonatal IVH.
    - **Chronic**: Hookworm infestation, Meckel diverticulum, milk-induced enteropathy, IBD.

---

### 3. Classification of Thalassemia Syndrome

1. **Beta Thalassemia Major (Cooley's Anemia)**: Homozygous or compound heterozygous (β⁰/β⁰, β⁰/β⁺); severe transfusion-dependent anemia presenting at 6–12 months of age; Hb <5–7 g/dL.
2. **Beta Thalassemia Intermedia**: Moderately severe anemia (Hb 7–10 g/dL); non-transfusion-dependent or requiring occasional transfusions during infections or growth spurts.
3. **Beta Thalassemia Minor / Trait**: Heterozygous carrier state (β/β⁰, β/β⁺); asymptomatic mild microcytic anemia (Hb 10–12 g/dL) with elevated HbA₂ (>3.5%).

### 4. Pathophysiology of Beta Thalassemia Major [⭐ High-Yield]

```
A[Point Mutation in Beta-Globin Gene Cluster Chromosome 11p15.5]:::primary --> B[Absent Beta-0 or Severely Reduced Beta-+ Beta Chain Synthesis]:::danger
B --> C[Unbalanced Accumulation of Free Unpaired Alpha-Globin Chains]:::danger
C --> D[Precipitation of Insoluble Alpha-Chain Inclusions in Erythroblasts]:::danger
D --> E1[Ineffective Erythropoiesis Intramedullary Apoptosis >80-90%]:::danger
D --> E2[Peripheral Hemolysis Splenic Destruction of Rigid RBCs]:::danger
E1 & E2 --> F[Profound Anemia Hb <5-7 g/dL & Severe Tissue Hypoxia]:::danger
F --> G1[Massive EPO Surge -> 20-fold Marrow Expansion & Thalassemic Facies]:::danger
F --> G2[Extramedullary Hematopoiesis -> Massive Hepatosplenomegaly]:::danger
F --> G3[Suppression of Hepcidin -> Marked Intestinal Iron Absorption + Transfusions]:::danger
G3 --> H[Systemic Iron Overload Hemosiderosis in Heart, Liver & Endocrine Glands]:::danger
```

- **Alpha-Chain Precipitation**: Unpaired free α-chains form unstable homotetramers that precipitate inside erythroblast membranes, generating reactive oxygen species (**ROS**) and causing severe cell membrane damage.
- **Ineffective Erythropoiesis**: Over 80–90% of developing erythroid precursors undergo premature intramedullary apoptosis within the bone marrow.
- **Hypoxia-Driven EPO Surge**: Tissue hypoxia triggers uncompensated **erythropoietin (EPO)** hypersecretion, expanding the erythroid marrow by 20-fold, eroding cortical bone, and creating skeletal dysmorphisms.
- **Extramedullary Hematopoiesis**: The liver and spleen reactivate fetal hematopoietic capacity, producing marked **hepatosplenomegaly**.
- **Secondary Iron Overload**: Hepcidin suppression causes hyperabsorption of dietary iron, which, combined with regular packed RBC transfusions, leads to toxic non-transferrin-bound iron (**NTBI**) deposition in cardiac myocytes, hepatocytes, and endocrine glands.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Onset**: Emerges between **6 and 12 months** of age as fetal hemoglobin (HbF) transitions to adult hemoglobin (HbA).
- **Presentation**: Progressive, severe pallor, listlessness, poor feeding, irritability, and failure to thrive.
- **Abdominal & Facial Changes**: Gradual abdominal enlargement and widening of the facial skeleton.

#### B. Signs

- **Cutaneous & Scleral Findings**: Severe pallor with a mild subicteric / muddy-bronze complexion (from combined anemia, mild hemolysis, and skin hemosiderosis).
- **Thalassemic / Hemolytic Facies ("Chipmunk Facies")** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Prominent **frontal and parietal bossing** (thickened skull cap).
    - Prominent **malar bones (maxillary hyperplasia)** with a **depressed nasal bridge**.
    - Protrusion of upper central incisors with dental malocclusion and exposed gums.
- **Abdominal Examination**:
    - **Massive Splenomegaly**: Spleen extends into the right iliac fossa; secondary hypersplenism develops over time.
    - **Hepatomegaly**: Firm, enlarged liver from extramedullary hematopoiesis and iron deposition.
- **Growth & Endocrine Failure**: Severe short stature, delayed bone age, delayed puberty, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, diabetes mellitus, and hypothyroidism.
- **Cardiovascular Signs**: Hyperdynamic circulation, systolic ejection flow murmurs, cardiomegaly, and late-stage congestive heart failure.

### 6. Diagnosis / Laboratory Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Hematological Findings

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**:
    - **Hemoglobin**: Profoundly reduced (**<5–7 g/dL**, often 2–4 g/dL at presentation).
    - **Red Cell Indices**: Marked microcytosis (**MCV <70 fL**), hypochromia (**MCH <20 pg**), and elevated **RDW (>15–20%)**.
    - **Mentzer Index** (MCV ÷ RBC count): **<13** (indicates thalassemia trait/major rather than iron deficiency).
- **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Severe microcytic hypochromic red blood cells.
    - **Target Cells (Codocytes)**: Abundant "bullseye" cells with central hemoglobin condensation.
    - **Anisopoikilocytosis**: Teardrop cells (dacrocytes), schistocytes, and microspherocytes.
    - **Nucleated Red Blood Cells (Normoblasts)**: Prominent circulating erythroblasts (10–100 per 100 WBCs).
    - Coarse **basophilic stippling** and Cabot rings.

#### B. Confirmatory Hemoglobin Analysis (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) / Hb Electrophoresis**:
    - **Hemoglobin F (HbF)**: Markedly elevated (**>90–95%** in β⁰/β⁰ major).
    - **Hemoglobin A₂ (HbA₂)**: Variable or elevated (**>3.5%**).
    - **Hemoglobin A (HbA)**: **Completely absent** in β⁰/β⁰ or trace in β⁰/β⁺.

#### C. Biochemical & Iron Parameters

- **Serum Ferritin**: Markedly elevated (**>1000–5000 μg/L**) in transfused patients.
- **Serum Iron & Transferrin Saturation**: Serum iron elevated with **transferrin saturation >70–80%**.
- **Liver Function Tests**: Elevated serum indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin and transaminases.

#### D. Imaging Studies

- **Skull Radiograph (X-Ray)**: Widening of the diploic space, thinning of the outer cortex, and pathognomonic **"Hair-on-end" appearance** (vertical trabeculations).
- __Cardiac & Liver MRI (T2_ / R2_)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Gold standard for tissue iron quantification. __Cardiac MRI T2_<20 ms_* indicates myocardial iron overload; __T2_ <10 ms_* indicates high risk of acute heart failure.

#### A. Hypertransfusion Regimen

- **Goal**: Maintain pre-transfusion hemoglobin levels between **9.5 and 10.5 g/dL** (post-transfusion Hb 13–14 g/dL).
- **Rationale**: Suppresses ineffective erythropoiesis, prevents skeletal deformities, inhibits extramedullary hepatosplenomegaly, and supports normal physical growth.
- **Administration**: Packed Red Blood Cells (**PRBCs**) administered at **10–15 mL/kg** every **2 to 4 weeks** over 3–4 hours.
- **Blood Specifications**: Use **leukoreduced**, phenotypically matched, fresh PRBCs (<7–14 days old) to reduce alloimmunization and febrile non-hemolytic transfusion reactions.

#### B. Iron Chelation Therapy Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Initiation Criteria**: Started after receiving **10 to 20 packed cell transfusions** or when serum ferritin exceeds **>1000 μg/L**.

#### D. Curative Therapies

1. **Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)**:
    - Only established curative treatment; performed using an **HLA-matched sibling donor**.
    - Optimal results when performed early in childhood before severe organ iron damage or liver fibrosis develops (Lucarelli Class I).
2. **Gene Therapy & CRISPR-Cas9**:
    - Autologous CD34+ stem cell modification via lentiviral β-globin gene insertion or CRISPR-Cas9 disruption of the _BCL11A_ enhancer to reactivate fetal hemoglobin (HbF) production.

#### E. Surgical & Supportive Care

- **Splenectomy Indications**: Annual transfusion requirement exceeds **>200 mL/kg/year** of PRBCs (indicating hypersplenism), progressive cytopenias, or massive painful splenomegaly.
- **Pre- & Post-Splenectomy Protocol**: Defer surgery until **>5 years of age**. Administer **Pneumococcal (PCV13 & PPSV23)**, **Meningococcal**, and **Hib vaccines** at least 2–4 weeks prior to surgery. Maintain daily oral **Penicillin V prophylaxis** post-operatively.

#### A. Population & Carrier Screening [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Target Populations**: Premarital young adults, pregnant women during early antenatal care, and cascade screening of extended family members of affected index cases.
- **Screening Tools**:
    1. **Red Cell Indices (CBC)**: Microcytosis (**MCV <80 fL**), hypochromia (**MCH <27 pg**), and **Mentzer Index <13**.
    2. **NESTROFT (Naked Eye Single Tube Red Cell Osmotic Fragility Test)**: Cost-effective, high-sensitivity mass screening test in field conditions.
    3. **Confirmatory HPLC / Electrophoresis**: **HbA₂ >3.5%** confirms the diagnosis of **Beta Thalassemia Trait (Heterozygous Carrier)**.

#### B. Genetic Counseling

- When both partners are identified as carriers of Beta Thalassemia Trait, counsel them regarding the **25% (1 in 4) chance** of having a child with Thalassemia Major in every pregnancy, a 50% chance of having a carrier child, and a 25% chance of having a normal child.

#### C. Antenatal / Prenatal Diagnosis

- **Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS)**: Performed at **10 to 12 weeks** of gestation under ultrasound guidance to obtain fetal trophoblastic tissue.
- **Amniocentesis**: Performed at **15 to 18 weeks** of gestation as an alternative.
- **Fetal Molecular Diagnosis**: DNA extraction from fetal cells followed by PCR-based mutation analysis to test for maternal and paternal β-globin gene mutations.
- **Selective Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)**: Offered to informed parents if the fetus is confirmed to be homozygous or compound heterozygous for Thalassemia Major within legal gestational limits.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Transfusion Hemosiderosis (End-Organ Damage)**:
    - **Cardiac (Leading Cause of Mortality)**: Arrhythmias, dilated cardiomyopathy, and congestive heart failure.
    - **Hepatic**: Progressive liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma.
    - **Endocrine**: Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (delayed puberty/infertility), Diabetes Mellitus, Hypothyroidism, and Hypoparathyroidism.
2. **Skeletal Complications**: Osteoporosis, cortical thinning, pathologic fractures, and premature epiphyseal closure.
3. **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections (TTIs)**: Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV, and Malaria.
4. **Post-Splenectomy Severe Sepsis**: Fulminant bacteremia caused by encapsulated organisms (_Streptococcus pneumoniae_, _Neisseria meningitidis_, _Haemophilus influenzae_).

---

### 11. Prognosis

- Without blood transfusions, mortality occurs in early childhood (<2–5 years) secondary to severe anemia and high-output heart failure.
- With modern hypertransfusion and compliant iron chelation, survival extends well into the 4th and 5th decades with good quality of life.

### Clinical Pearl

> **Never prescribe iron supplements to a child with Beta Thalassemia Major.** Despite having microcytic hypochromic red cells on peripheral smear, these children suffer from severe systemic iron overload due to chronic transfusions and hyperabsorption. Administering oral iron exacerbates fatal cardiac and hepatic hemosiderosis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Differentiating **Beta Thalassemia Trait (Minor)** from **Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA)** on HPLC and indices.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Mentzer Index (MCV ÷ RBC count) is **<13** in Thalassemia Trait and **>13** in IDA. Hemoglobin Electrophoresis/HPLC demonstrates **HbA₂ >3.5%** in Thalassemia Trait, whereas HbA₂ is normal or decreased in IDA.

### Quick Revision

- **Anemia Definition**: Reduction in Hb <2 SD below mean for age; microcytic (MCV <80 fL), normocytic (80–100 fL), macrocytic (>100 fL).
- **Thalassemia Etiology**: Autosomal recessive mutation on chromosome 11p15.5 causing absent (**β⁰**) or reduced (**β⁺**) β-globin chains.
- **Peripheral Smear**: Severe microcytic hypochromia, target cells, teardrops, basophilic stippling, and nucleated RBCs.
- **Confirmatory Test**: HPLC showing **HbF >90–95%** (Major) or **HbA₂ >3.5%** (Trait).
- **Hypertransfusion Goal**: PRBCs every 2–4 weeks maintaining pre-transfusion Hb **9.5–10.5 g/dL**.
- **Iron Chelation**: Oral **Deferasirox (20–40 mg/kg/day)** started when ferritin >1000 μg/L.
- **Prevention**: Carrier screening (NESTROFT / HPLC), genetic counseling, and antenatal CVS at 10–12 weeks.
