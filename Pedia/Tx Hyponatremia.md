# Treatment of Hyponatremia [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hyponatremia** is defined as a plasma sodium concentration below **<135 mEq/L**.
- **Symptomatic Hyponatremia / Hyponatremic Encephalopathy**: A life-threatening emergency usually occurring when serum sodium drops below **<120–125 mEq/L** or declines rapidly (**<24 hours**).
- **Acute Hyponatremia**: Duration of onset **<48 hours**.
- **Chronic Hyponatremia**: Duration of onset **>48 hours**.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

Hyponatremia is categorized by extracellular fluid (**ECF**) volume status:

1. **Hypovolemic Hyponatremia** (sodium loss in excess of water loss):
    - **Renal Losses**: Diuretic use, osmotic diuresis, renal salt-wasting (**CSW**), adrenal insufficiency, pseudohypoaldosteronism.
    - **Extrarenal Losses**: Diarrhea, vomiting, drains, fistula, severe burn sweating, third-spacing effusions, ascites.
2. **Normovolemic / Euvolemic Hyponatremia** (excess free water retention):
    - **Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuresis (SIAD)**.
    - Inflammatory CNS disorders (meningitis, encephalitis, tumors).
    - Pulmonary diseases (severe asthma, pneumonia).
    - Drugs (cyclophosphamide, vincristine).
    - Postoperative state and nausea.
3. **Hypervolemic Hyponatremia** (excess total body water and sodium retention):
    - Congestive heart failure (**CHF**), cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, acute kidney injury (**AKI**), chronic kidney disease (**CKD**).

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mild Hyponatremia**: Serum sodium **130–134 mEq/L**.
- **Moderate Hyponatremia**: Serum sodium **125–129 mEq/L**.
- **Severe / Acute Symptomatic Hyponatremia**: Serum sodium **<125 mEq/L** or presence of acute neurological manifestations.

- **Cytotoxic Cerebral Edema**: Hypo-osmolality causes influx of water into the intracellular space of brain cells, increasing intracranial pressure.
- **Brain Adaptation**: Brain extrudes intracellular electrolytes and organic osmolytes (**inositol, taurine, glutamine**) to adapt to chronic hyponatremia.
- **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS)**: Rapid overcorrection of chronic hyponatremia causes rapid brain cell dehydration, leading to demyelination in the pons.

---

### 5. Clinical Features

#### A. Symptoms

- **Mild to Moderate**: Headache, nausea, vomiting, lethargy, confusion, anorexia, malaise.
- **Severe / Advanced**: Seizures, coma, decorticate posturing, dilated pupils, anisocoria, papilledema, cardiac arrhythmias, respiratory arrest.

#### B. Signs

- **Hypovolemic**: Tachycardia, sunken eyes, dry mucous membranes, delayed capillary refill, hypotension.
- **Hypervolemic**: Edema, ascites, hepatomegaly, pulmonary congestion, hypertension.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations

- **Serum Electrolytes**: Confirms serum sodium **<135 mEq/L**.
- **Plasma Osmolality**: Differentiates true hypo-osmolar hyponatremia (**<280 mOsm/kg**) from pseudohyponatremia (hyperglycemia, hypertriglyceridemia).
- **Urine Spot Sodium & Urine Osmolality**:
    - **Urine Spot Na+ <20 mEq/L**: Extrarenal losses or hypervolemic states.
    - **Urine Spot Na+ >20–40 mEq/L**: Renal salt wasting, diuretics, **SIAD**, or adrenal insufficiency.
    - **Urine Osmolality >100 mOsm/kg**: Inappropriate antidiuresis (**SIAD**).

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

|Feature|**Hypovolemic Hyponatremia**|**Euvolemic Hyponatremia (SIAD)**|**Hypervolemic Hyponatremia**|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**ECF Volume**|Decreased (Dehydrated)|Normal (Euvolemic)|Increased (Edematous)|
|**Urine Spot Na+**|**<20 mEq/L** (extrarenal) / **>20 mEq/L** (renal)|**>20 mEq/L**|**<20 mEq/L**|
|**Primary Mechanism**|Sodium loss > water loss|Inappropriate ADH release & free water retention|Water retention > sodium retention|
|**Primary Management**|**0.9% Normal Saline** rehydration|**Fluid restriction** + Furosemide|**Fluid & Sodium restriction**+ Diuretics|

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Non-Pharmacological / General Principles

- **Determine Chronicity & Symptoms**: Differentiate acute symptomatic (**<48 hours**) from chronic asymptomatic (**>48 hours**) hyponatremia.
- **Fluid Restriction**: Mainstay of treatment for euvolemic (**SIAD**) and hypervolemic hyponatremia.

#### B. Emergency Management of Acute / Symptomatic Hyponatremia [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated irrespective of chronicity if acute neurological symptoms (**seizures, coma, altered mental status**) are present.
- **Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl) Protocol**:
    - Administer **3–5 mL/kg 3% NaCl** (maximum **100 mL**) IV over **1 hour**.
    - Alternatively: **2 mL/kg 3% NaCl** over **10–20 minutes** (or **12 mL/kg** over **30–90 min** in AKI setting to raise sodium by **10 mEq/L**).
    - **First Hour Goal**: Increase serum sodium by **5 mEq/L** and achieve symptom resolution (patient alert, seizure-free).
    - **If goal not achieved**: Repeat **3% NaCl** infusion until symptoms resolve, monitoring serum sodium every **2 hours**.
    - **If goal achieved**: Discontinue **3% NaCl** and transition to chronic management protocol; monitor serum sodium every **4–6 hours**.

#### C. Management of Chronic / Asymptomatic Hyponatremia [⭐ High-Yield]

- Correct slowly over **48–72 hours** or longer to prevent **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS)**.
- **Sodium Deficit Calculation Formula**: \[\text{Sodium Deficit (mEq)} = (130 - \text{Serum Na}^+) \times 0.6 \times \text{Body Weight (kg)}\]
- **Safe Rate of Correction**:
    - Target rate of rise: **0.3–0.5 mEq/L/hour**.
    - **Maximum 24-hour rise**: **8–10 mEq/L** in the first 24 hours.
    - **Maximum subsequent rise**: **8 mEq/L** every 24 hours thereafter until serum sodium reaches **130 mEq/L**.

#### D. Etiology-Specific Management

1. **Hypovolemic Hyponatremia**: Restore intravascular volume using **0.9% Normal Saline**; WHO **ORS** for oral rehydration.
2. **SIAD (Euvolemic)**: **Fluid restriction**, oral salt supplementation, and **furosemide**.
3. **Hypervolemic Hyponatremia**: **Fluid restriction**, **sodium restriction**, and loop diuretics (**furosemide**).
4. **Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW)**: Replace urinary sodium and water losses with **normal saline** and **fludrocortisone**.

### 9. Complications

- **Cerebral Edema & Brain Herniation**: Caused by rapid uncorrected acute hyponatremia (**<125 mEq/L**).
- **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS) / Central Pontine Myelinolysis**:
    - Caused by overrapid correction of chronic hyponatremia (**>10–12 mEq/L/day**).
    - Biphasic course: Initial neurological improvement followed 2–7 days later by dysarthria, dysphagia, spastic quadriparesis, lethargy, and pseudobulbar palsy.

---

### 10. Prognosis

- Excellent in acute symptomatic cases if **3% hypertonic saline** is promptly administered to raise sodium by **5 mEq/L**.
- High morbidity and risk of permanent neurological deficit if chronic hyponatremia is corrected too rapidly (**>10 mEq/L/24 hours**).

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: SODIUM

- **S** - **Seizures & Coma**: Indications for emergency **3% Hypertonic Saline**
- **O** - **Osmotic Demyelination**: Risk of rapid overcorrection (**>10 mEq/L/day**)
- **D** - **Deficit formula**: \((130 - \text{Serum Na}^+) \times 0.6 \times \text{Weight (kg)}\)
- **I** - **Increase 5 mEq/L**: Target rise in 1st hour for acute symptomatic cases
- **U** - **Under 0.5 mEq/L/hr**: Maximum safe hourly rate of correction
- **M** - **Max 8-10 mEq/L/24h**: Strictly capped daily limit to protect brain cells

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **In acute hyponatremic seizures, do NOT wait to calculate the sodium deficit.** Immediately give **3–5 mL/kg of 3% hypertonic saline** over 1 hour (or 2 mL/kg over 10–20 min) to raise serum sodium by **5 mEq/L** and stop brain herniation.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the fluid management of **SIAD** vs. **Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW)**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **SIAD** is euvolemic and requires **fluid restriction**. **CSW** is hypovolemic and requires **volume replacement with normal saline** plus **fludrocortisone**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Plasma sodium **<135 mEq/L**; severe/symptomatic at **<120–125 mEq/L**.
- **Acute Emergency Dose**: **3–5 mL/kg 3% NaCl** over 1 hour to raise \(\text{Na}^+\) by **5 mEq/L**.
- **Chronic Deficit Formula**: \(\text{Na deficit (mEq)} = (130 - \text{Serum Na}^+) \times 0.6 \times \text{Weight (kg)}\).
- **Max Correction Rate**: **0.3–0.5 mEq/L/hour**; max **8–10 mEq/L in 24 hours**.
- **Major Risk**: Rapid overcorrection causes **Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS)**.
