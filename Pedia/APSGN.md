# Acute Poststreptococcal Glomerulonephritis (APSGN) [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acute Poststreptococcal Glomerulonephritis (APSGN)** is an immune complex-mediated, non-suppurative inflammatory disorder of the renal glomeruli occurring as a delayed sequela following a Group A β-hemolytic Streptococcal (**GABS**) infection of the pharynx or skin.
- **Classic Clinical Triad / Tetrad**: Characterized by the sudden onset of **gross hematuria** (cola- or tea-colored urine), **oliguria**, **periorbital edema**, and **hypertension**.

---

### 2. Etiology, Nephritogenic Strains & Predisposing Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Causative Pathogen & Nephritogenic Strains

- **Causative Organism**: Group A β-hemolytic _Streptococcus_ (_Streptococcus pyogenes_).
- **Nephritogenic Strains**: Only specific M-protein serotypes of GABS possess nephritogenic potential:
    1. **Pharyngitis-Associated Strains**: M-types **1, 2, 4, 12** (M-type **12** is most commonly associated with throat infection).
    2. **Pyoderma / Skin Infection-Associated Strains**: M-types **49, 55, 57, 60** (M-type **49** is most commonly associated with impetigo/pyoderma).
- **Latent Period (Lag Time)**:
    - Post-Pharyngeal APSGN: **1 to 2 weeks** (7–14 days) after throat infection.
    - Post-Pyoderma APSGN: **3 to 6 weeks** (14–42 days) after skin infection.

#### B. Predisposing Factors

- **Age**: Predominantly affects school-age children between **5 and 15 years** of age (uncommon in children **<3 years**).
- **Sex**: Male predominance with a male-to-female ratio of approximately **2:1**.
- **Environmental & Socioeconomic Factors**: Overcrowded living conditions, poor personal hygiene, low socioeconomic status, and tropical/warm humid climates (favoring pyoderma).
- **Preceding Infections**: Untreated or inadequately treated streptococcal pharyngitis, tonsillitis, or impetigo/scabies pyoderma.

---

### 3. Classification

- **Post-Infectious Glomerulonephritis (PIGN)**:
    - **Post-Streptococcal GN (APSGN)**: Most common cause in children.
    - **Non-Streptococcal Post-Infectious GN**: Caused by _Staphylococcus aureus_, _Pneumococcus_, _Salmonella_, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV, and Parvovirus B19.

- **Target Antigens**: Streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxin B (**SpeB**) and Nephritis-Associated Plasmin Receptor (**NAPlr**).
- **Immune Complex Deposition**: Circulation or in situ formation of antigen-antibody complexes that deposit on the **subepithelial side** of the glomerular basement membrane (**GBM**).
- **Complement Cascade**: Activates the **alternate complement pathway**, resulting in consumption and marked depletion of serum **C3 complement** levels.
- **Glomerular Ischemia**: Proliferation of endothelial and mesangial cells with neutrophil infiltration compresses capillary lumens, leading to decreased GFR, compensatory tubular reabsorption of sodium/water, intravascular expansion, and hypertension.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Abrupt Onset**: Symptoms appear suddenly after the characteristic latent period following pharyngitis (1–2 weeks) or pyoderma (3–6 weeks).
- **Cola- or Tea-Colored Urine (Gross Hematuria)**: The hallmark presenting complaint, reported in up to 30–50% of overt cases.
- **Puffiness of Face / Edema**: Most prominent around the eyes (periorbital edema) upon waking in the morning.
- **Decreased Urine Output (Oliguria)**: Reduced urine frequency and volume.
- **Non-Specific Symptoms**: Mild fever, malaise, headache, anorexia, lethargy, and dull flank pain.

#### B. Signs

- **Periorbital & Facial Edema**: Soft, pitting edema localized predominantly to the eyelids and face; mild pedal edema may be present.
- **Systemic Hypertension**: Present in **60–80%** of hospitalized children secondary to fluid overload; may be asymptomatic or present with headache and vomiting.
- **Fluid Overload Signs**: Tachypnea, basal crepitations, gallop rhythm, and hepatomegaly indicate incipient heart failure.

---

### 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Urinalysis

- **Microscopy**:
    - **Dysmorphic Red Blood Cells**: **>20%** dysmorphic RBCs on phase-contrast microscopy confirm glomerular origin.
    - **Red Blood Cell Casts**: Pathognomonic marker of active glomerular inflammation.
    - Leukocytes and granular casts.
- **Proteinuria**: Mild to moderate (**1+ to 2+** on dipstick; <2 g/m²/day); non-nephrotic range.

#### B. Blood & Serological Investigations

- **Serum C3 Complement Level**: **Markedly reduced (<50 mg/dL)** in **>90%** of patients during the acute phase.
    - _Key Rule_: Serum C3 levels return to normal within **8 to 12 weeks**.
- **Evidence of Preceding Streptococcal Infection**:
    - **Anti-Streptolysin O (ASO) Titer**: Elevated (**>200 IU/mL**) in **>80%** of post-pharyngeal cases; less sensitive in pyoderma.
    - **Anti-DNase B Titer**: Best marker for preceding **streptococcal pyoderma/skin infection**.
    - **Streptozyme Test**: Detects antibodies against 5 streptococcal enzymes (ASO, DNase B, hyaluronidase, SK, NADase).
- **Renal Function Tests**: Elevated blood urea nitrogen (**BUN**) and serum creatinine (azotemia); dilutional hyponatremia and hyperkalemia may occur during severe oliguria.

#### C. Indications for Kidney Biopsy in Acute Nephritis [⭐ High-Yield]

Renal biopsy is **NOT routinely required** for classic APSGN. Indications include:

|Category|Specific Clinical Indication for Renal Biopsy|
|:--|:--|
|**Atypical Features at Presentation**|Absence of serological evidence of GABS; normal serum C3 during acute phase; presence of systemic signs (fever, rash, joint pain); persistent severe anuria/AKI requiring dialysis.|
|**Delayed Resolution / Atypical Course**|Persistent oliguria / azotemia beyond **7–10 days**; gross hematuria persisting beyond **3–4 weeks**; low serum C3 levels persisting beyond **12 weeks**; nephrotic-range proteinuria beyond **2 weeks**.|

#### D. Histopathology Findings (When Biopsy Performed)

- **Light Microscopy (LM)**: Diffuse **endocapillary proliferative glomerulonephritis** with prominent neutrophil and monocyte infiltration; bloodless, enlarged glomeruli.
- **Immunofluorescence (IF)**: Coarse **granular deposits of IgG and C3** along capillary walls and mesangium, producing a classic **"starry sky"** or "lumpy-bumpy" pattern.
- **Electron Microscopy (EM)**: Large, discrete **subepithelial electron-dense deposits ("humps")**.

### 8. Treatment & Management of Acute Nephritis [⭐ High-Yield]

APSGN is a self-limiting disease; management is primarily supportive, focusing on controlling fluid overload, hypertension, and renal failure.

#### A. General & Supportive Care

- **Bed Rest**: Recommended during the acute phase until gross hematuria, edema, and hypertension resolve.
- **Fluid & Salt Restriction**:
    - **Sodium Restriction**: Strictly limit salt intake (**<1–2 g/day**; no added salt) until hypertension and edema resolve.
    - **Fluid Restriction**: Total daily fluid allowance = **Insensible water loss (300–400 mL/m²/day)** + **previous 24-hour urine output**.
    - **Potassium Restriction**: Limit dietary potassium during oliguric phase to prevent life-threatening hyperkalemia.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hypertensive Encephalopathy**: Sudden severe rise in blood pressure leading to cerebral edema; presents with severe headache, confusion, somnolence, visual disturbances, and generalized tonic-clonic convulsions.
2. **Acute Heart Failure & Pulmonary Edema**: Secondary to massive expansion of extracellular fluid volume; presents with dyspnea, orthopnea, tachypnea, tachycardia, jugular venous distension, and bilateral basal crepitations.
3. **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) & Uremia**: Severe oligo-anuria leading to rapidly rising BUN and creatinine, severe metabolic acidosis, and life-threatening hyperkalemia (**K⁺ >6.5 mEq/L**).

---

### 10. Prognosis

- **Overall Prognosis**: **Excellent** in children (**>95%** recover completely without long-term renal sequelae).
- **Timeline of Recovery**:
    - Diuresis begins within **7 to 10 days**.
    - Hypertension and gross hematuria resolve within **2 to 3 weeks**.
    - Serum **C3 complement levels normalize within 8 to 12 weeks**.
    - Microscopic hematuria and mild proteinuria may persist for **6 to 12 months** (does not portend chronic renal disease).

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: S-T-R-E-P

- **S** - **Subepithelial humps & Starry-sky IF**: Classic electron microscopy and immunofluorescence findings
- **T** - **Tea- / Cola-colored urine**: Gross hematuria containing dysmorphic RBCs and RBC casts
- **R** - **Reduced C3 Complement**: Serum C3 <50 mg/dL (normalizes strictly in 8–12 weeks)
- **E** - **Edema & Elevated BP**: Periorbital fluid retention and fluid-overload hypertension
- **P** - **Penicillin 10 days**: Eradicates carriage (no long-term secondary prophylaxis needed)

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Serum C3 complement is the ultimate diagnostic clock in APSGN.** If C3 is low at presentation, it confirms an immune complex-mediated process. If C3 remains depressed beyond **12 weeks**, you must re-evaluate the diagnosis and perform a **kidney biopsy** to rule out C3 Glomerulopathy, MPGN, or Lupus Nephritis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Prescribing long-term monthly Benzathine Penicillin prophylaxis for APSGN children as done in Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF).
- **Distinguishing Fact**: APSGN requires only a **single 10-day curative course of penicillin** to eradicate nephritogenic streptococci from the patient and contacts. **Long-term continuous antibiotic prophylaxis is NOT indicated in APSGN** because recurrences are extremely rare.

- **Extra Mark Annotation**: Subepithelial humps located between the GBM and visceral epithelial cells (podocytes) are the hallmark ultrastructural feature of APSGN under Electron Microscopy.

### Quick Revision

- **Nephritogenic Strains**: M-type **12** (pharyngitis; 1–2 wk lag) and M-type **49** (pyoderma; 3–6 wk lag).
- **Classic Triad**: Gross hematuria (cola-colored), periorbital edema, and hypertension.
- **Serology**: Low serum **C3 complement** (<50 mg/dL; normalizes in 8–12 wks) + elevated ASO / Anti-DNase B.
- **Biopsy Hallmarks**: Endocapillary proliferation (LM), "starry sky" granular IgG+C3 (IF), subepithelial humps (EM).
- **Treatment**: Supportive (salt/water restriction + Furosemide + Amlodipine) + 10 days Penicillin (no long-term prophylaxis).
