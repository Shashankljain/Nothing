[[Fluid and electrolyte imbalance]]
# Hypocalcemia — Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hypocalcemia** is defined as total serum calcium **<8 mg/dL** (or ionized calcium **<4 mg/dL**) in term neonates and children, and total serum calcium **<7 mg/dL** in preterm neonates.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neuromuscular Irritability (Tetany & Spasms)**: Manifests as muscle twitching, **carpopedal spasm**, **cramps**, **laryngospasm** with **stridor**, paresthesias, and **seizures**.
- **Latent Tetany Signs**:
    - **Chvostek sign**: Facial muscle twitching (orbicularis oculi/oris) elicited by tapping over the **facial nerve**anterior to the auditory meatus.
    - **Trousseau sign**: **Carpopedal spasm** induced by inflating a blood pressure cuff above systolic pressure for **3 minutes**.
- **Neonatal Presentations**:
    - **Early onset (<72 hours)**: Manifests as **jitteriness**, **lethargy**, **poor feeding**, and **seizures**.
    - **Late onset (>7 days)**: Classic **tetany**, **seizures**, **apnea**, **cyanosis**, and **tachycardia**.
- **Cardiac & ECG Manifestations**: **Prolonged QTc interval** (**>0.45 seconds**), **dilated cardiomyopathy**, and impaired myocardial contractility.

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Chvostek and Trousseau signs** serve as classic bedside physical examination markers to detect latent tetany before overt spasms or life-threatening laryngospasm develop.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing **jitteriness** caused by hypocalcemia with **neonatal seizures**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Jitteriness** stops when the infant's limb is held gently and is stimulus-sensitive, whereas **true seizures** do not stop with physical restraint and are accompanied by autonomic signs or abnormal eye movements.

---

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: CATS

- **C** - **Convulsions / Seizures** & **Carpopedal spasm**
- **A** - **Arrhythmias** & **Prolonged QTc interval** (>0.45 s)
- **T** - **Tetany** (**Chvostek sign** & **Trousseau sign**)
- **S** - **Stridor** & **Laryngospasm**

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Total serum calcium **<8 mg/dL** (term/children) or **<7 mg/dL** (preterm).
- **Pathognomonic Signs**: **Chvostek sign** (facial nerve tap) and **Trousseau sign** (BP cuff inflation).
- **Neonatal Features**: **Jitteriness**, **poor feeding**, **apnea**, **cyanosis**, and **seizures**.
- **Cardiac Hallmark**: **Prolonged QTc interval** (**>0.45 s**) on ECG.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Hypocalcemia in pediatrics is characterized by heightened neuromuscular excitability, presenting clinically as tetany, Chvostek and Trousseau signs, seizures, and a prolonged QTc interval on ECG.**
