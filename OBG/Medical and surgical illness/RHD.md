# Management of 25-Year-Old Primigravida with 7 Months Amenorrhea (28–30 Weeks) Complicated by Rheumatic Heart Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Case Scenario & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Presentation**: A 25-year-old primigravida presenting with **7 months amenorrhea (28–30 weeks gestation)** and **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)**.
- **Clinical Significance of 28–30 Weeks Gestation**: This gestational age represents the **peak window of physiological cardiac output expansion (+40% to +50% above baseline)** and maximum intravascular volume expansion.
- **Primary Concern**: Mitral Stenosis (MS)—which accounts for 80% of RHD cases in pregnancy—is particularly poorly tolerated because physiological tachycardia shortens diastolic filling time, raising left atrial and pulmonary venous pressures, leading to **acute pulmonary edema and congestive heart failure (CHF)**.

---

### 2. Definition & Etiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)**: An acquired chronic inflammatory cardiac valvular disorder resulting from group A β-hemolytic streptococcal pharyngitis.
- **Valvular Distribution in Pregnancy**:
    - **Mitral Stenosis (MS)**: Most common lesion (**80% of all rheumatic lesions**).
    - **Mitral Regurgitation (MR)**: Second most common; generally better tolerated due to reduced Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR) in pregnancy.
    - **Aortic Stenosis (AS)** & **Aortic Regurgitation (AR)**: Less common.
- **Precipitating Factors for Heart Failure in 3rd Trimester**:
    - Physiological volume surge peaking at 28–32 weeks.
    - Superadded anemia, infections (UTI, respiratory, dental).
    - Arrhythmias (especially Atrial Fibrillation).
    - Pre-eclampsia / Gestational Hypertension.

### 5. Clinical Features

#### A. Symptoms

- **Progressive Exertional Dyspnea** (exaggerated at 28–30 weeks due to peak blood volume).
- **Orthopnea** and **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND)**.
- **Nocturnal Cough** with pink frothy hemoptysis.
- **Palpitations** (due to sinus tachycardia or Atrial Fibrillation).
- Easy fatigability and lower limb swelling.

#### B. Signs

- **General Signs**: Tachycardia (pulse rate >100 bpm), elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), cyanosis, and clubbing.
- **Auscultatory Findings (Mitral Stenosis)**:
    - Loud S1 with prominent splitting.
    - **Mid-Diastolic Rumbling Murmur** with presystolic accentuation at the apex (heard best in left lateral position with bell of stethoscope).
    - Opening Snap following S2.
    - Loud pulmonary component of second heart sound (P2) indicating Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension.
- **Chest Examination**: **Basal pulmonary crepitations / rales** (earliest sign of failure/pulmonary congestion).

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transthoracic Echocardiography with Color Flow Doppler (Investigation of Choice - IOC)**:
    - Confirms valve anatomy, quantifies Mitral Valve Area (Normal: 4–6 cm²; Mild MS: ≥1.5–4 cm²; **Severe MS: <1.5 cm² or <1.0 cm²**).
    - Measures transvalvular pressure gradient, left atrial size, presence of thrombus, LVEF, and pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
- **Electrocardiography (ECG)**:
    - Shows left atrial enlargement (P-mitrale), right axis deviation, right ventricular hypertrophy, or Atrial Fibrillation.
- **Chest X-Ray (with Abdominal Lead Shielding)**:
    - Shows cardiomegaly, prominent pulmonary conus, and pulmonary congestion (Kerley B lines).
- **Routine Antenatal Blood Work**:
    - Hemoglobin & Hematocrit (to rule out physiological vs true anemia).
    - Urine routine & culture (to rule out UTI triggering heart failure).
- **Fetal Surveillance at 28–30 Weeks**:
    - **Obstetric Ultrasound**: Assess fetal growth parameters, rule out Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), and evaluate Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).
    - **Umbilical Artery Doppler**: Monitor uteroplacental perfusion.
    - **Non-Stress Test (NST)** and **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Conducted weekly from 28–30 weeks onwards.

#### A. Immediate Management at 28–30 Weeks Presentation

1. **Mandatory Hospital Admission**:
    - **Admission Timing**: Admitted immediately at 28–30 weeks (the peak physiological hemodynamic surge) for complete cardiovascular and obstetric evaluation.
2. **Multidisciplinary Team Approach**:
    - Joint supervision involving Obstetrician, Cardiologist, Anesthetist, and Neonatologist.
3. **General Supportive Measures**:
    - Strict bed rest (minimum **10 hours at night** and **2 hours in afternoon** in left lateral recumbent position) to maximize uterine blood flow and minimize SVR fluctuations.
    - Dietary restriction of sodium and caffeine; prevent weight gain >10 kg.
    - Treat co-existing anemia aggressively (target Hb >11 g/dL).
4. **Rheumatic Fever Secondary Prophylaxis**:
    - **Inj. Benzathine Penicillin 1.2 million units IM every 4 weeks** throughout pregnancy and puerperium.

#### B. Medical Pharmacotherapy in Pregnancy

- **Rate Control (Beta-Blockers)**: **Metoprolol 25–50 mg PO BD** to reduce heart rate, prolong diastolic filling time, and decrease left atrial pressure.
- **Preload Reduction (Diuretics)**: **Inj./Tab Frusemide 20–40 mg PO/IV** if pulmonary congestion or dyspnea occurs.
- **Inotropic / Rate Control Support**: **Digoxin 0.25 mg PO OD** if Atrial Fibrillation or left ventricular failure occurs.
- **Anticoagulation**: **LMWH (Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC BD)** if Atrial Fibrillation, prior thromboembolism, or mechanical prosthetic valves are present.
- **Interventional Management (PBMV)**: **Percutaneous Balloon Mitral Valvotomy (PBMV)** is indicated if severe MS (<1.0 cm²) remains refractory to medical therapy; best performed in 2nd or early 3rd trimester.

#### C. Fetal Surveillance Protocol at 28–30 Weeks

- Weekly **Non-Stress Test (NST)** and **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**.
- Serial Ultrasound every 3 weeks for fetal biometry and growth.
- Umbilical artery Doppler velocimetry to monitor fetal hypoxia.
- Antenatal Corticosteroid Coverage: If preterm delivery is anticipated (<34 weeks), administer **Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM 2 doses 24 hours apart** for fetal lung maturity.

#### D. Intrapartum Management Protocol (During Labor)

1. **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery is PREFERRED**.
    - **Cesarean Section Indications**: Reserved ONLY for obstetric reasons OR specific cardiac lesions: Coarctation of aorta, Aortic dissection/aneurysm, Dilated aortic root >4 cm, Severe symptomatic Aortic Stenosis, Warfarin use within 2 weeks, or Refractory cardiac failure.
2. **First Stage of Labor**:
    - **Positioning**: **Semi-recumbent position with left lateral tilt** to prevent aortocaval compression.
    - **Oxygen Therapy**: Administer humidified O₂ at **5–6 L/min** via face mask.
    - **Analgesia**: **Continuous Epidural Analgesia** is the gold standard (eliminates pain-induced catecholamine surges and tachycardia).
    - **Fluid Restriction**: **Strictly restrict IV fluids to ≤75 mL/hr** (Normal Saline) to prevent acute pulmonary edema.
    - **Infective Endocarditis (IE) Prophylaxis**: Administered at labor onset / membrane rupture: **Inj. Ampicillin 2 g IV + Inj. Gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV**.
    - **Monitoring Threshold**: If pulse rate exceeds **>110 bpm** between contractions or basal rales appear, give **Inj. Digoxin 0.5 mg slow IV** and **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV**.
3. **Second Stage of Labor**:
    - **Curtail Pushing**: **Maternal bearing-down efforts are strictly avoided**.
    - **Operative Shortening**: Perform **prophylactic Low Forceps or Vacuum (Ventouse) delivery** as soon as full cervical dilatation (10 cm) is reached.
4. **Third Stage of Labor**:
    - **Active Management (AMSTL)**: Administer **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV infusion**.
    - **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**: **Methylergometrine / Ergometrine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** because it causes severe uterine and venous constriction, driving a massive autotransfusion surge of 400–500 mL into circulation, precipitating fatal pulmonary edema.
    - **Diuretic Therapy**: Administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** immediately after delivery to promote diuresis and reduce cardiac preload.

#### E. Postpartum Care

- Keep patient propped up with **legs kept at a lower level than heart** to pool blood in extremities.
- ICU/HDU monitoring of pulse, BP, SpO₂, and lung bases for **24–48 hours postpartum**.
- **Breastfeeding**: Permitted in compensated heart disease.
- **Contraception**: Barrier methods (condoms), Progestin-Only Pills (POP), or LNG-IUS are safe; combined oral contraceptives are contraindicated due to thromboembolic risk.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Acute Pulmonary Edema** (leading cause of maternal death in Mitral Stenosis).
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)** (peak risk immediately postpartum due to autotransfusion).
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias** (Atrial Fibrillation, Supraventricular Tachycardia).
- **Systemic Thromboembolism** (stroke, systemic vascular occlusion secondary to LA thrombus).
- **Infective Endocarditis**.

#### B. Fetal & Neonatal Complications

- **Preterm Labor & Birth** (spontaneous or indicated).
- **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR)** / Low Birth Weight (due to chronic maternal hypoxia and reduced uterine blood flow).
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) / Stillbirth**.
- **Birth Asphyxia & Neonatal Intensive Care Admission**.

---

### 11. Prognosis

- **Maternal Prognosis**: Excellent in NYHA Class I & II with mortality **<1%**; rises to **5–15% in NYHA Class III & IV**.
- Long-term maternal survival is unaffected by pregnancy provided the mother survives the perinatal period.
- **Fetal Prognosis**: Excellent in acyanotic RHD provided maternal heart failure is avoided.

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Auscultating Murmurs in Pregnancy**: A soft **systolic ejection murmur (Grade <3/6)** at the left sternal border is a **normal physiological finding** present in >90% of pregnant women due to hyperdynamic circulation. However, **ANY DIASTOLIC MURMUR** (such as the mid-diastolic rumbling murmur of Mitral Stenosis) is **ALWAYS PATHOLOGICAL** and mandates immediate Echocardiography.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering **Methylergometrine (Methergine)** for active management of the third stage of labor (AMSTL) or PPH management in a patient with heart disease.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Methylergometrine causes tetanic uterine contraction and severe venoconstriction, producing a sudden, massive autotransfusion surge of 400–500 mL blood into the central circulation, triggering fatal acute pulmonary edema. **Oxytocin is the DRUG OF CHOICE**.

### Quick Revision

- **Presentation Window**: 28–30 weeks coincides with the **peak hemodynamic expansion (+40% to +50% cardiac output)**.
- **Primary Pathology**: **Mitral Stenosis (80% of RHD)** causes LA hypertension and pulmonary edema due to tachycardia-induced shortened diastolic filling.
- **Antenatal Protocol**: Hospital admission at 28–30 weeks, bed rest, sodium restriction, Benzathine Penicillin q4wks, and Metoprolol.
- **Intrapartum Protocol**: Vaginal delivery, semi-recumbent left lateral position, continuous **epidural analgesia**, IV fluid restriction **≤75 mL/hr**, IE prophylaxis, and **prophylactic Low Forceps/Vacuum**.
- **Postpartum Protocol**: **Oxytocin 10 IU ONLY** (Ergometrine strictly contraindicated), IV Frusemide 20–40 mg, propped-up position with legs dependent, and ICU watch for 24–48 hours.
