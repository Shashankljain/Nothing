### 1. New York Heart Association (NYHA) Functional Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

- The **NYHA Functional Classification** categorizes cardiac disease based on the severity of maternal symptoms and physical activity limitation.
- It is the most widely utilized clinical tool in obstetrics to assess functional cardiac reserve, predict maternal prognosis, and guide labor management decisions.

|NYHA Class|Functional Capacity / Symptom Profile|Clinical Features during Pregnancy|Maternal Mortality Risk|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Grade I**|**Uncompromised**: No limitation of physical activity.|Ordinary physical activity causes no undue fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, or anginal pain.|**Minimal (<1%)**|
|**Grade II**|**Slightly Compromised**: Slight limitation of physical activity.|Comfortable at rest; ordinary physical activity results in dyspnea, fatigue, or palpitations.|**Minimal (<1%)**|
|**Grade III**|**Markedly Compromised**: Marked limitation of physical activity.|Comfortable at rest; less-than-ordinary physical activity causes dyspnea, fatigue, or palpitations.|**Moderate to High (5–15%)**|
|**Grade IV**|**Severely Compromised**: Severe limitation of activity.|Inability to carry out any physical activity without discomfort; symptoms of cardiac insufficiency or angina present even at rest.|**Major / Extreme (25–50%)**|

- **Limitations of NYHA Classification**:
    1. It relies solely on subjective maternal symptoms rather than underlying anatomical type or structural severity.
    2. A patient's class can change dynamically across pregnancy as hemodynamics peak (at 28–32 weeks and during labor).

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes of Cardiac Disease in Pregnancy

- **Acquired Valvular Heart Disease**:
    - **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)**: Accounts for the majority of cardiac lesions in developing countries (predominantly **Mitral Stenosis - 80%**).
- **Congenital Heart Disease (CHD)**:
    - **Acyanotic (Left-to-Right Shunts)**: Atrial Septal Defect (ASD - most common), Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA).
    - **Cyanotic**: Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF), Eisenmenger Syndrome.
- **Other Lesions**: Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM), Coarctation of Aorta, Marfan Syndrome, and Ischemic Heart Disease.

---

### 3. Classification Systems (NYHA vs WHO Classification) [⭐ High-Yield]

- While NYHA assesses functional capacity, the **Modified WHO Classification** integrates structural pathology with maternal risk:

|WHO Risk Class|Associated Cardiac Lesions|NYHA Equivalence|Management Recommendation|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**WHO Class I**|Small ASD/VSD, PDA, Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP), repaired simple lesions.|**NYHA Grade I**|Antenatal review 1–2 times during pregnancy.|
|**WHO Class II–III**|Unoperated ASD/VSD, Mild Mitral Stenosis, Mechanical Valves, repaired TOF.|**NYHA Grade II–III**|Cardiology review every trimester or every 1–2 months.|
|**WHO Class IV**|Severe MS, Severe AS, Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension, LVEF <30%, Marfan with aorta >45 mm.|**NYHA Grade IV**|**Pregnancy Contraindicated**; advise MTP or admit throughout pregnancy.|

### 4. Pathophysiology of Hemodynamic Strain During Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Baseline Pregnancy Strain**: Cardiac output (CO) increases by **40–50%** by 30–34 weeks due to an increase in stroke volume (+27%) and heart rate (+17%).
- **First Stage of Labor**:
    - Pain, anxiety, and uterine contractions increase sympathetic tone, raising heart rate and blood pressure.
    - Each uterine contraction squeezes out **400–500 mL of blood** from the uteroplacental bed into systemic circulation (**autotransfusion**), increasing cardiac output by an additional **+50%** over pre-labor values.
- **Second Stage of Labor**:
    - Voluntary bearing-down efforts (Valsalva maneuver) cause marked swings in intra-thoracic pressure, venous return, and cardiac workload.
- **Third Stage & Immediate Postpartum (The Critical Period)**:
    - Following delivery of the baby and placenta, sudden decompression of the inferior vena cava combined with uterine involution causes a massive autotransfusion surge.
    - Cardiac output surges up to **+70% to +80%** above pre-labor baseline within 10–30 minutes post-delivery, presenting the highest risk for sudden congestive heart failure and pulmonary edema.

### 5. Clinical Features

- **Symptoms**:
    - NYHA Grade I patients are **asymptomatic at rest and during ordinary daily activities**.
    - Early signs of decompensation during labor: **breathlessness (dyspnea)**, **nocturnal cough**, **palpitations**, **chest oppression**, and **extreme fatigue**.
- **Signs**:
    - Pulse rate **>110 beats/min** in between contractions.
    - Respiratory rate **>24 breaths/min**.
    - **Basal pulmonary rales / crepitations** (earliest sign of impending pulmonary edema).
    - Raised Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP).
    - Diastolic murmurs or grade >3/6 systolic ejection murmurs.

---

### 6. Diagnosis & Investigations

- **Clinical Assessment**: History of functional tolerance, pulse, BP, oxygen saturation (SpO₂), and chest auscultation.
- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (Color Flow Doppler)**: Gold standard to confirm valve anatomy, valve area, wall movement, ejection fraction (LVEF), and pulmonary pressures.
- **Electrocardiography (ECG)**: Rules out atrial fibrillation, hypertrophy, or ischemic changes.
- **Invasive / Intrapartum Monitoring**: Continuous pulse oximetry, continuous ECG, and partographic labor tracking.

#### A. Admission & Pre-Labor Planning at 36 Weeks Gestation

- **Elective Hospitalization**: In an uncomplicated NYHA Grade I patient, elective admission to a tertiary facility is undertaken at **36 weeks gestation**.
- **Maternal-Fetal Re-evaluation**:
    - Re-assess cardiac status with repeat Echocardiography and ECG.
    - Evaluate fetal growth and well-being via Non-Stress Test (NST) and Biophysical Profile (BPP).
    - Continue Rheumatic Fever Prophylaxis: **Injection Benzathine Penicillin 1.2 million units IM every 3–4 weeks**.
- **Timing & Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery is PREFERRED**.
    - Induction of labor is reserved for obstetric indications.
    - **Cesarean Section** is indicated ONLY for obstetric reasons OR specific high-risk cardiac lesions (e.g., Coarctation of Aorta, Aortic Dissection, Aortic Root >4 cm, Severe Symptomatic Aortic Stenosis, or Warfarin intake within 2 weeks).

---

#### B. First Stage of Labor Management Protocol

1. **Positioning**: Keep patient in **semi-recumbent position with a left lateral tilt** to prevent aortocaval compression and reduce sudden fluctuations in cardiac return.
2. **Oxygen Therapy**: Administer humidified oxygen at **5–6 L/min** via face mask.
3. **Pain Relief (Analgesia)**: **Continuous Epidural Analgesia** is the gold standard. It abolishes labor pain, lowers maternal catecholamines, prevents tachycardia, and stabilizes systemic vascular resistance.
4. **Fluid Restriction**: **Strictly restrict IV fluids to ≤75 mL/hr** (Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate) to avoid triggering acute fluid overload and pulmonary edema.
5. **Infective Endocarditis (IE) Prophylaxis**:
    - Administered when labor begins or membranes rupture:
        - **Inj. Ampicillin 2 g IV + Inj. Gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV**.
        - If Penicillin-allergic: **Inj. Vancomycin 1 g IV or Inj. Clindamycin 600 mg IV**.
6. **Maternal Surveillance**:
    - Record pulse rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and SpO₂ every 30 minutes.
    - Perform hourly auscultation of lung bases for crepitations.
    - **Action Threshold**: If pulse rate exceeds **>110 beats/min** between contractions or basal rales appear, start **rapid digitalization with IV Digoxin 0.5 mg** and **IV Frusemide 20–40 mg**.

---

#### C. Second Stage of Labor Management Protocol

1. **Curtail Maternal Pushing**: **Maternal bearing-down efforts (Valsalva) are strictly avoided** to eliminate cardiac strain and hemodynamic swings.
2. **Operative Shortening of Second Stage**: Perform **prophylactic Low Forceps or Vacuum (Ventouse) delivery** as soon as full cervical dilatation (10 cm) is reached.
3. Perform episiotomy under adequate local/epidural anesthesia to relieve perineal resistance.

---

#### D. Third Stage of Labor Management Protocol

1. **Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMSTL)**:
    - Administer **Injection Oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV infusion** (in 500 mL Normal Saline) immediately after delivery of the anterior shoulder/baby.
2. **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION**:
    - **Methylergometrine / Ergometrine is ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED**.
    - _Reason_: It induces tetanic uterine contraction and severe peripheral venoconstriction, pushing 400–500 mL of blood instantly into the circulation and precipitating fatal pulmonary edema.
3. **Diuretic Administration**: Administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** right after delivery to promote prompt diuresis and unload central venous pressure.
4. **Post-Delivery Position**: Keep patient propped up with legs kept dependent (lower than heart level) to pool blood in lower limbs and dampen the autotransfusion surge.

---

#### E. Postpartum Care (Puerperium)

- **High-Risk Window**: Monitor continuously in an ICU/HDU setting for **24–48 hours postpartum** (the peak autotransfusion phase).
- **Antibiotic Coverage**: Continue prophylactic broad-spectrum antibiotics to prevent puerperal sepsis.
- **Lactation**: Breastfeeding is encouraged in NYHA Grade I compensated patients.
- **Contraception Counsel**: **Barrier methods (condoms)**, **Progestin-Only Pills (POP)**, or **Levonorgestrel IUD** are safe. Combined oral contraceptives are contraindicated due to thromboembolic risks. Sterilization (Minilaparotomy under local anesthesia) can be done at the end of 1 week postpartum once stabilized.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal Complications**:
    - Acute Pulmonary Edema (leading cause of death).
    - Congestive Heart Failure (CHF).
    - Cardiac Arrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation, PSVT).
    - Systemic Thromboembolism.
    - Infective Endocarditis.
- **Fetal Complications**:
    - Preterm Labor & Birth.
    - Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR) / Low Birth Weight.
    - Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
    - Birth Asphyxia.

---

### 11. Prognosis

- NYHA Grade I patients have an **excellent maternal prognosis with a mortality rate <1%** when managed with strict intrapartum protocols.
- Long-term survival of women with RHD is unaffected by pregnancy provided they survive the perinatal period.

---

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Diastolic Murmurs in Pregnancy**: While a soft systolic ejection murmur (Grade <3/6) is normal due to hyperdynamic flow, **ANY DIASTOLIC MURMUR IS ALWAYS PATHOLOGICAL** and demands immediate Echocardiography.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Using **Methylergometrine (Methergine)** for third-stage management or PPH prevention in a cardiac patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Methylergometrine causes intense uterine contraction and severe venoconstriction, producing a sudden, massive autotransfusion surge of 400–500 mL blood into the central circulation, throwing the patient into fatal acute pulmonary edema. **Oxytocin is the DRUG OF CHOICE**.

### Quick Revision

- **NYHA Grade I Definition**: Patient is uncompromised with no limitation of physical activity; ordinary physical activity causes no symptoms; mortality <1%.
- **Admission Timing**: Elective hospitalization at **36 weeks gestation** for pre-labor evaluation.
- **First Stage Protocol**: Semi-recumbent left lateral position, oxygen (5–6 L/min), **continuous epidural analgesia**, IV fluid restriction **≤75 mL/hr**, and IE antibiotic prophylaxis.
- **Second & Third Stage Protocol**: Prophylactic **Low Forceps/Vacuum delivery** (no pushing); AMSTL with **Oxytocin 10 IU ONLY**; **Methylergometrine is ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED**.
- **Critical Postpartum Phase**: Administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** immediately after delivery to manage autotransfusion fluid shifts; monitor in ICU for 24–48 hours.
