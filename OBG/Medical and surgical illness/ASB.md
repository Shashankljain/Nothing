# Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB) in Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB)** is defined as the presence of actively multiplying bacteria with a quantitative colony count of **≥10⁵ colony-forming units (CFU)/mL** of a single species in a clean-catch midstream urine sample, in a pregnant woman **without any clinical symptoms of a urinary tract infection (UTI)**.
- **Incidence**: Occurs in **5–10%** of all pregnant women.
- **Recurrence Rate**: High recurrence rate of approximately **30%** during the same pregnancy.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Causative Microorganisms**:
    - ***Escherichia coli (E. coli)***: Most common pathogen, accounting for **>90%** of cases.
    - Other organisms: _Klebsiella pneumoniae_, _Proteus mirabilis_, _Enterobacter_, _Group B Streptococcus (GBS)_, and _Staphylococcus aureus_.
- **Pregnancy-Related Predisposing Factors**:
    - **High Progesterone Levels**: Causes smooth muscle relaxation, leading to ureteral peristalsis reduction and ureteral dilatation (hydroureter).
    - **Mechanical Compression**: Dextrorotated gravid uterus compresses the right ureter at the pelvic brim, causing urinary stasis (70–80% right-sided predilection).
    - **Anatomical Susceptibility**: Short female urethra (4 cm) in close proximity to the heavily colonized vulval and perineal region.
    - **Altered Urine Composition**: Increased GFR and physiological glycosuria/aminoaciduria provide a favorable culture medium for bacterial multiplication.
- **Maternal Risk Factors**:
    - Pre-existing **Diabetes Mellitus**.
    - **Sickle Cell Anemia** or Sickle Cell Trait.
    - Low socioeconomic status, multiparity, and prior history of UTI.

### 4. Clinical Features

- **Symptoms**: **ABSENT** by definition (no dysuria, urgency, frequency, fever, or flank pain).
- **Signs**: No suprapubic or costovertebral angle tenderness on clinical examination.

---

### 5. Diagnosis / Investigations (Screening & Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Screening Protocol & Timing

- **Universal Screening**: Recommended for **all pregnant women at their first antenatal visit** (ideally between 12–16 weeks).
- **Collection Technique**: Clean-catch midstream urine sample collected in a sterile container after cleansing the vulva.

#### Laboratory Tests

- **Urine Culture & Sensitivity (Gold Standard / IOC)**:
    - Diagnostic criterion: **≥10⁵ CFU/mL** of a single organism in a clean-catch midstream sample.
- **Rapid Screening Dipstick Test**:
    - Detects **nitrites** (produced by Gram-negative nitrate-reducing bacteria) and **leukocyte esterase** (enzyme from WBCs).
    - If either nitrite or leukocyte esterase is positive, formal urine culture is mandatory.

---

### 7. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Initial Antimicrobial Course (7–14 Days)

- Treatment must be started promptly based on culture and sensitivity results to prevent progression to pyelonephritis.
- **First-Line Drug of Choice (DOC)**: **Nitrofurantoin** 100 mg PO BD for 7–10 days.
- **Alternative Regimens**:
    - **Cephalexin**: 500 mg PO TID for 7–10 days.
    - **Amoxicillin**: 500 mg PO TID for 7–10 days (or single dose 3 g).

#### B. Post-Treatment Follow-Up & Suppressive Prophylaxis

- **Repeat Urine Culture**: Performed **2 weeks after completing antibiotic therapy** and repeated in **each trimester**.
- **Suppressive Prophylaxis**: Indicated in cases of **recurrent or persistent ASB** until delivery:
    - **Tab Nitrofurantoin**: 50–100 mg PO at bedtime until delivery.
    - **Tab Amoxicillin**: 250 mg PO at bedtime.
- **Postpartum Evaluation**: Women with recurrent ASB/UTI should undergo upper urinary tract imaging (renal USG) **3 months postpartum** to rule out underlying structural renal anomalies.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Acute Pyelonephritis**: Occurs in **25%** of untreated ASB cases, usually in the third trimester.
- **Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections** (30% recurrence risk).
- **Anemia** and **Gestational Hypertension**.
- Long-term risk of **chronic pyelonephritis** and **chronic renal disease** in later life.

#### B. Fetal and Neonatal Complications

- **Preterm Labor and Preterm Birth**.
- **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR)** / Low Birth Weight (LBW) babies.
- Increased **perinatal mortality** secondary to prematurity.

---

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Physiological Glycosuria vs ASB**: Glycosuria occurs in up to 50% of normal pregnant women due to an increased GFR and lowered renal glucose threshold (<180 mg/dL). However, **fasting glycosuria** or **glycosuria on ≥2 occasions after 20 weeks** alters urinary pH and promotes rapid _E. coli_ proliferation, multiplying the risk of ASB and acute pyelonephritis.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing the management of **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in non-pregnant women** with **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria in pregnancy**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: ASB in non-pregnant women is generally not treated (unless undergoing urological procedures). In contrast, **ASB in pregnancy MUST ALWAYS BE TREATED with a full course of antibiotics**, because untreated ASB carries a **25% risk of progressing to life-threatening acute pyelonephritis** and severe fetal morbidity (preterm labor, FGR).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Cut-off**: Actively multiplying bacteria with **≥10⁵ CFU/mL** of a single organism in a clean-catch midstream urine sample without UTI symptoms.
- **Offending Agent**: **_E. coli_ (>90% cases)**.
- **Key Risk if Untreated**: **25% risk of acute pyelonephritis** in 3rd trimester + increased risk of **preterm labor and FGR**.
- **First-Line Drug**: **Nitrofurantoin 100 mg PO BD for 7–10 days** (avoid near term in G-6-PD deficiency).
- **Suppressive Therapy**: **Nitrofurantoin 50–100 mg PO at bedtime** until delivery for recurrent cases.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ASB) in pregnancy is defined as a urine culture demonstrating ≥10⁵ CFU/mL of a single uropathogen (predominantly E. coli) in an asymptomatic woman, requiring mandatory universal screening at the first antenatal visit and prompt antibiotic therapy to prevent a 25% progression rate to acute pyelonephritis and associated preterm labor.**
