# Cardiovascular Changes in Pregnancy & Intrapartum Management of Heart Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Physiological Cardiovascular Adaptations**: A series of progressive hyperdynamic changes in maternal vascular structure, blood volume, and cardiac hemodynamics designed to meet the metabolic demands of the fetus, uteroplacental unit, and mother.
- **Heart Disease in Pregnancy**: Any structural, functional, or ischemic cardiac disorder complicating pregnancy, labor, or puerperium, representing the primary cause of indirect maternal mortality.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Acquired Heart Disease**:
    - **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)**: Accounts for 80% of acquired valvular lesions in developing countries, with **Mitral Stenosis (MS)** being the most common lesion.
- **Congenital Heart Disease (CHD)**:
    - **Acyanotic (Left-to-Right Shunts)**: Atrial Septal Defect (ASD - most common), Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA).
    - **Cyanotic**: Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF), Eisenmenger Syndrome.
- **Other Causes**: Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM), Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP), Coarctation of Aorta, Marfan Syndrome, and Coronary Artery Disease / Myocardial Infarction.
- **Precipitating Factors for Intrapartum Failure**: Infection, severe anemia, hypertension, fluid overload, and cardiac arrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation).

### 4. Pathophysiology & Hemodynamic Changes During Pregnancy & Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Antepartum Hemodynamic Changes (Systemic Adaptations)

1. **Blood Volume & Hemodilution**:
    - Expands by **+30% to +50% (+1500 mL)**, starting at 6 weeks and peaking at **30–34 weeks**.
    - **Plasma Volume**: Expands by **+40% to +50% (+1250 mL)**.
    - **RBC Mass**: Expands by **+20% to +30% (+350 mL)** under erythropoietin regulation.
    - **Physiological Anemia**: Disproportionate plasma expansion over RBC mass causes hemodilution, lowering blood viscosity to optimize fetoplacental gas exchange and protect against blood loss at delivery.
2. **Cardiac Output (CO)**:
    - **CO = Stroke Volume (SV) × Heart Rate (HR)**.
    - Starts increasing by 5 weeks, peaking at **+40% to +50% (6.25 L/min)** at **28–32 weeks**.
    - **Stroke Volume (SV)** increases by **+27%** (due to increased venous return).
    - **Heart Rate (HR)** increases by **+15 to +20 bpm (+17%)**.
    - Position effect: CO is lowest in sitting/supine positions and highest in the left lateral position.
3. **Vascular Resistance & Blood Pressure**:
    - **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)**: Decreases by **21%** due to progesterone, nitric oxide, prostacyclin (PGI₂), and relaxin.
    - **Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR)**: Decreases by **34%**.
    - **Blood Pressure**: Diastolic BP and Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) drop by **5–10 mmHg in mid-pregnancy**(2nd trimester) due to reduced SVR, returning to baseline near term.
4. **Venous Pressures & Supine Hypotension**:
    - **Antecubital Venous Pressure**: Unchanged.
    - **Femoral Venous Pressure**: Rises from 8–10 cm H₂O to **20–25 cm H₂O in supine position** (up to 80–100 cm H₂O standing) due to IVC compression by the dextrorotated gravid uterus. This accounts for physiological pedal edema, varicose veins, and hemorrhoids.
    - **Supine Hypotension Syndrome**: Occurs in 10% of women due to severe IVC compression reducing preload; corrected immediately by left lateral tilting.
5. **Anatomical & Auscultatory Findings**:
    - Diaphragm elevated by 4 cm; heart pushed **upward and outward with rotation to the left**. Apex beat shifts to 4th ICS, 2.5 cm lateral to mid-clavicular line.
    - **Auscultation**: Loud S1 with splitting, loud S3, soft Grade <3/6 systolic ejection murmur at left sternal border, and continuous mammary souffle. **Diastolic murmurs are NEVER physiological**.
    - **ECG**: Left axis deviation, Q wave in leads II, III, aVF, and flat/inverted T waves in lead III and V1–V3.

#### B. Intrapartum & Postpartum Hemodynamic Surges

- **First Stage of Labor**: Uterine contractions squeeze **400–500 mL of blood** into systemic circulation (**autotransfusion**), increasing cardiac output by **+50%** over pre-labor levels.
- **Second Stage of Labor**: Voluntary bearing-down efforts (Valsalva) cause marked fluctuations in intra-thoracic pressure and venous return.
- **Third Stage & Immediate Postpartum (Peak Risk Window)**:
    - Following placental delivery, sudden IVC decompression and uterine involution drive a massive autotransfusion surge.
    - Cardiac output surges up to **+70% to +80% above baseline within 10–30 minutes post-delivery**, presenting the single highest risk for sudden acute pulmonary edema and heart failure.
    - _Hierarchy of heart failure risk_: **Immediately after delivery > 2nd stage of labor > late 1st stage of labor > 28–32 weeks of pregnancy**.

### 5. Clinical Features

#### A. Symptoms

- **Normal Pregnancy**: Mild exertional dyspnea, easy fatigability, reduced exercise tolerance, and dependent pedal edema.
- **Pathological Cardiac Disease**:
    - Severe or progressive dyspnea at rest, **orthopnea**, and **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND)**.
    - **Nocturnal cough** with pink frothy hemoptysis.
    - Palpitations, angina/chest pain, and syncope.

#### B. Signs

- **General Signs**: Tachycardia (pulse >100 bpm), elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), cyanosis, clubbing, and non-dependent edema (anasarca).
- **Cardiac Auscultation**:
    - **Diastolic Murmur** (e.g., mid-diastolic rumbling murmur of Mitral Stenosis with presystolic accentuation).
    - Loud Grade >3/6 Systolic Ejection Murmur associated with a thrill.
    - Loud P2 with prominent splitting and S4 gallop.
- **Chest Auscultation**: **Basal pulmonary rales / crepitations** (earliest clinical sign of impending pulmonary congestion).

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transthoracic Echocardiography with Color Flow Doppler (Investigation of Choice - IOC)**:
    - Confirms valve anatomy, quantifies valve area (e.g., MS severe if <1.5 cm²), transvalvular gradient, left atrial size, LVEF, and pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
- **Electrocardiography (ECG)**:
    - Identifies bi-atrial enlargement, ventricular hypertrophy, ST-T segment depression, and dysrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation).
- **Chest X-Ray (with Lead Shielding)**:
    - Evaluates cardiomegaly, prominent pulmonary conus, and pulmonary congestion (Kerley B lines).
- **Routine Blood Work**:
    - Hemoglobin & Hematocrit (to rule out co-existing anemia).
    - Urine routine & microscopy (to exclude asymptomatic bacteriuria/UTI triggering failure).

|**Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)**|Normal|**Elevated**|
|**Diastolic Murmur**|**NEVER Physiological (0%)**|**ALWAYS Pathological (Mid-diastolic rumbling murmur present)**|

#### A. Decision on Mode of Delivery

- **Spontaneous Vaginal Delivery is PREFERRED**: Spontaneous labor onset is ideal; gentle Foley catheter induction may be used if obstetrically indicated.
- **Indications for Cesarean Section (CS)**:
    - Obstetric indications (e.g., malpresentation, CPD, non-reassuring fetal status).
    - **Cardiac Indications for CS**:
        1. Coarctation of Aorta
        2. Aortic Dissection or Aortic Aneurysm
        3. Dilated Aortic Root >4 cm / Marfan Syndrome
        4. Patient on oral Warfarin therapy within 2 weeks of labor (risk of fetal intracranial hemorrhage)
        5. Severe Symptomatic Aortic Stenosis
        6. Refractory Heart Failure

#### B. First Stage of Labor Protocol

1. **Positioning**: Keep patient in **semi-recumbent position with a left lateral tilt** to prevent IVC compression.
2. **Oxygen Therapy**: Administer humidified oxygen at **5–6 L/min** via face mask.
3. **Pain Relief (Analgesia)**: **Continuous Epidural Analgesia** is the gold standard (abolishes pain-induced sympathetic drive, reduces heart rate, and stabilizes SVR).
4. **Fluid Restriction**: **Strictly restrict IV fluids to ≤75 mL/hr** (Normal Saline) to avoid acute fluid overload and pulmonary edema.
5. **Infective Endocarditis (IE) Prophylaxis**:
    - Administered when labor begins or membranes rupture:
        - **Inj. Ampicillin 2 g IV + Inj. Gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV**.
        - If Penicillin-allergic: **Inj. Vancomycin 1 g IV or Inj. Clindamycin 600 mg IV**.
6. **Maternal & Fetal Monitoring**:
    - Continuous pulse oximetry, ECG, and blood pressure monitoring.
    - Hourly auscultation of lung bases for crepitations.
    - **Action Threshold**: If pulse rate exceeds **>110 beats/min** between contractions or basal rales appear, start **rapid digitalization with IV Digoxin 0.5 mg** and **IV Frusemide 20–40 mg**.

#### C. Second Stage of Labor Protocol

1. **Curtail Pushing**: **Maternal bearing-down efforts (Valsalva) are strictly avoided** to eliminate cardiac strain.
2. **Operative Shortening**: Perform **prophylactic Low Forceps or Vacuum (Ventouse) delivery** as soon as full cervical dilatation (10 cm) is reached.

#### D. Third Stage of Labor Protocol

1. **Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMSTL)**:
    - Administer **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV infusion** (in 500 mL Normal Saline).
2. **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**:
    - **==Methylergometrine / Ergometrine== is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**.
    - _Reason_: Ergometrine induces tetanic uterine contraction and severe peripheral venoconstriction, pushing a sudden 400–500 mL autotransfusion surge into the central circulation, precipitating fatal acute pulmonary edema.
3. **Diuretic Therapy**: Administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** immediately after delivery to promote diuresis and unload central venous pressure.
4. **Post-Delivery Position**: Keep patient propped up with legs kept dependent (lower than heart level) to pool blood in extremities and dampen the autotransfusion surge.

#### E. Postpartum Care (Puerperium)

- **Critical Window**: ICU/HDU monitoring of vital signs, fluid balance, and SpO₂ for **24–48 hours postpartum** (the peak autotransfusion phase).
- **Lactation**: Breastfeeding is permitted in compensated cardiac cases.
- **Contraception**: **Barrier methods (condoms)**, **Progestin-Only Pills (POP)**, or **Levonorgestrel IUD** are safe. Combined oral contraceptives are contraindicated due to thromboembolic risks. Sterilization (Minilaparotomy under local anesthesia) can be performed at the end of 1 week postpartum once stabilized.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Acute Pulmonary Edema** (leading cause of maternal mortality in Mitral Stenosis).
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)** (peak risk immediately postpartum due to autotransfusion surge).
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias** (Atrial Fibrillation, Supraventricular Tachycardia).
- **Systemic Thromboembolism** (stroke, arterial occlusion secondary to LA thrombus).
- **Infective Endocarditis**.

#### B. Fetal & Neonatal Complications

- **Preterm Labor & Birth**.
- **Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR) / Low Birth Weight** (due to chronic maternal hypoxia and reduced uterine blood flow).
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) / Stillbirth**.
- **Birth Asphyxia**.

---

### 11. Prognosis

- Maternal outcome is directly governed by **NYHA Functional Class** at presentation.
- In NYHA Class I and II, maternal mortality is **<1%**.
- In NYHA Class III and IV, maternal mortality rises significantly to **5–15%** (and up to 50% in Eisenmenger Syndrome).
- Pregnancy does not alter the long-term survival of a woman with RHD provided she survives the pregnancy itself.

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Auscultating Murmurs in Pregnancy**: A soft **systolic ejection murmur (Grade <3/6)** at the left sternal border is a **normal physiological finding** present in >90% of pregnant women due to hyperdynamic flow. However, **ANY DIASTOLIC MURMUR** (such as the mid-diastolic rumbling murmur of Mitral Stenosis) is **ALWAYS PATHOLOGICAL** and demands immediate Echocardiography.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering **Methylergometrine (Methergine)** for active management of the third stage of labor (AMSTL) or PPH management in a cardiac patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Methylergometrine causes tetanic uterine contraction and severe venoconstriction, producing a sudden, massive autotransfusion surge of 400–500 mL blood into the central circulation, triggering fatal acute pulmonary edema. **Oxytocin is the DRUG OF CHOICE**.

### Quick Revision

- **Peak Hemodynamic Strain**: Cardiac output & blood volume peak at **28–32 weeks (+40–50%)**, surge further during **labor (+50%)**, and reach maximum spike **immediately postpartum (+70–80%)** due to autotransfusion.
- **Diagnostic Golden Rule**: **Diastolic murmurs are ALWAYS pathological** in pregnancy and indicate organic heart disease.
- **First Stage Management**: Semi-recumbent left lateral position, continuous O₂ (5–6 L/min), continuous **epidural analgesia**, IV fluid restriction **≤75 mL/hr**, and IE antibiotic prophylaxis.
- **Second & Third Stage Management**: Cut short 2nd stage with **prophylactic Forceps/Vacuum** (no pushing). AMSTL with **Oxytocin 10 IU ONLY**; **Ergometrine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**.
- **Postpartum Care**: Monitor closely in ICU for 24–48 hours; administer IV Frusemide to manage autotransfusion fluid shifts.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Pregnancy induces a profound hyperdynamic circulatory state characterized by a 40–50% surge in blood volume and cardiac output peaking at 28–32 weeks, necessitating an intrapartum management protocol in cardiac disease centered on continuous epidural analgesia, fluid restriction (≤75 mL/hr), operative shortening of the second stage, and oxytocin-only third stage management to prevent fatal acute pulmonary edema.**
