# Heart Disease in Pregnancy (NYHA Grade II) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Heart Disease in Pregnancy** refers to any structural, functional, or ischemic cardiac disorder complicating gestation, labor, or puerperium.
- **NYHA Grade II (Slightly Compromised)**: Patients are comfortable at rest, but ordinary physical activity (such as climbing stairs, walking briskly, or doing household chores) results in undue fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, or anginal pain.
- It carries a low overall maternal mortality (<1%), but requires careful surveillance to prevent sudden progression to heart failure as physiological cardiac workload surges during pregnancy and labor.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Acquired Valvular Lesions**:
    - **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)**: Most common cause overall (accounting for up to 80% of valvular heart lesions), with **Mitral Stenosis (MS)** being the predominant single lesion.
- **Congenital Heart Diseases (CHD)**:
    - **Acyanotic (Left-to-Right Shunts)**: Atrial Septal Defect (ASD - most common congenital lesion), Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA).
    - **Cyanotic**: Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF), Eisenmenger Syndrome.
- **Other Causes**: Peripartum Cardiomyopathy (PPCM), Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP), Coarctation of Aorta, Marfan Syndrome, and Coronary Artery Disease.
- **Precipitating Risk Factors for Cardiac Failure**:
    - Maternal infections (urinary, respiratory, or dental).
    - Severe anemia, pre-eclampsia / gestational hypertension.
    - Obesity, multiple pregnancy, excessive salt/fluid intake, and arrhythmia.

### 4. Pathophysiology (Hemodynamic Strain & Cardiac Reserve) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Physiological Workload Expansion**:
    - **Cardiac Output (CO)** begins to rise by 5 weeks, peaking at **+40% to +50% (6.25 L/min)** at **28–32 weeks**due to an increase in stroke volume (+27%) and heart rate (+17%).
    - **Blood Volume** increases by **+30% to +50% (+1500 mL)**, leading to physiological hemodilution.
- **Hemodynamic Strain During Labor & Delivery**:
    - Labor pain, anxiety, and contractions increase sympathetic discharge.
    - Each uterine contraction squeezes **400–500 mL of blood** from the uteroplacental bed into systemic circulation (**autotransfusion**), increasing cardiac output by **+50% in the first stage**.
    - Voluntary bearing-down (Valsalva) in the second stage alters intra-thoracic pressure and venous return.
- **Peak Failure Window**:
    - **Highest risk of heart failure occurs immediately after delivery** (within 10–30 minutes) due to sudden uterine autotransfusion surge and IVC decompression.
    - _Hierarchy of heart failure risk_: **Immediately after delivery > 2nd stage of labor > late 1st stage of labor > 28–32 weeks of pregnancy**.

### 5. Clinical Features

#### A. Symptoms

- **Grade II Specific**: **Exertional dyspnea** during ordinary daily routine (e.g., house chores, walking short distances).
- **General Cardiac Symptoms**:
    - Easy fatigability and decreased exercise tolerance.
    - **Orthopnea** and **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND)**.
    - Nocturnal cough with or without pink frothy hemoptysis.
    - Palpitations and chest discomfort/pain.

#### B. Signs

- **General Examination**:
    - **Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)**.
    - Cyanosis, clubbing, and non-dependent edema / anasarca.
    - Persistent tachycardia (heart rate >100 bpm) and pulse irregularity / arrhythmia.
- **Cardiac Auscultation**:
    - **Diastolic Murmur** (e.g., mid-diastolic rumbling murmur of Mitral Stenosis with presystolic accentuation) — _always pathological_.
    - **Loud Grade >3/6 Systolic Ejection Murmur** associated with a thrill.
    - Loud S2 with prominent splitting / elevated P2.
    - Presence of S4 gallop rhythm.
- **Chest Examination**:
    - **Basal pulmonary rales / crepitations** (earliest sign of impending pulmonary congestion).

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transthoracic Echocardiography with Color Flow Doppler (Gold Standard / IOC)**:
    - Confirms valve anatomy, quantifies stenosis/regurgitation severity, valve area (e.g., MS severe if <1.5 cm²), ejection fraction (LVEF), and pulmonary artery systolic pressure.
- **Electrocardiography (ECG)**:
    - Detects bi-atrial enlargement, ventricular hypertrophy, T-wave inversion, and dysrhythmias (e.g., Atrial Fibrillation).
- **Chest X-Ray (with Lead Shielding)**:
    - Evaluates cardiomegaly, prominent pulmonary conus, and pulmonary venous congestion / Kerley B lines.
- **Routine Blood Work**:
    - Complete blood count and hemoglobin (to rule out anemia aggravating heart strain).
    - Urine routine & microscopy (to exclude asymptomatic bacteriuria and UTI).

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Antenatal Management (During Pregnancy)

- **Review Schedule**: Cardiology and obstetric review **every trimester**.
- **Lifestyle & Dietary Measures**:
    - Restrict physical activity and take compulsory 10 hours bed rest at night plus 2 hours afternoon rest.
    - Restrict sodium intake to avoid fluid retention.
    - Avoid weight gain beyond 10 kg.
- **Elective Hospitalization**:
    - Hospital admission recommended at **28–32 weeks of gestation** (the window of peak hemodynamic expansion) or earlier if any sign of decompensation appears.
- **Rheumatic Fever Secondary Prophylaxis**:
    - **Inj. Benzathine Penicillin 1.2 million units IM every 4 weeks** throughout pregnancy and puerperium.
- **Medical Pharmacotherapy**:
    - **Beta-blockers (Metoprolol / Atenolol)**: Used to decrease heart rate and prolong diastolic filling time (especially in Mitral Stenosis).
    - **Diuretics (Inj./Tab Frusemide)**: Indicated if signs of pulmonary venous congestion or heart failure appear.
    - **Anticoagulants (LMWH / Heparin)**: Indicated if atrial fibrillation or mechanical prosthetic valves are present.
- **Surgical Intervention (Percutaneous Balloon Mitral Valvotomy - PBMV)**:
    - Performed only if patient is refractory to medical management.
    - Ideal timing: **Second trimester (14–18 weeks)**.

1. **Mode of Delivery**:
    
    - **Vaginal delivery is PREFERRED** (spontaneous onset or gentle Foley catheter / PGE2 induction if obstetrically indicated).
    - **Indications for Cesarean Section**: Obstetric indications OR specific cardiac conditions (Coarctation of aorta, Severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, Aortic aneurysm/dissection, Marfan syndrome with aortic root >4 cm, Warfarin intake within 2 weeks of labor, or Refractory cardiac failure).
2. **First Stage of Labor**:
    
    - **Position**: **Semi-recumbent position with left lateral tilt** to prevent aortocaval compression.
    - **Oxygenation**: Humidified oxygen at **5–6 L/min** via face mask.
    - **Analgesia**: **Continuous Epidural Analgesia** is ideal (reduces pain-induced sympathetic drive and tachycardia).
    - **Fluid Restriction**: **Restrict IV fluids strictly to ≤75 mL/hr** (using Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate) to avoid acute pulmonary edema.
    - **Infective Endocarditis (IE) Prophylaxis**:
        - Administer when labor begins or membranes rupture: **Inj. Ampicillin 2 g IV + Inj. Gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV**.
    - **Cardiovascular Monitoring**:
        - Monitor pulse, blood pressure, SpO₂, and perform hourly auscultation of lung bases for crepitations.
        - _Action Threshold_: If pulse rate exceeds **>110 beats/min** between contractions or pulmonary rales appear, start **rapid digitalization with IV Digoxin 0.5 mg** and **IV Frusemide 20–40 mg**.
3. **Second Stage of Labor**:
    
    - **Curtail Maternal Effort**: **Maternal bearing-down efforts are strictly avoided** to eliminate Valsalva-induced cardiac strain.
    - **Operative Shortening**: Perform **prophylactic Low Forceps or Vacuum (Ventouse) delivery** as soon as full cervical dilatation is reached.
4. **Third Stage of Labor**:
    
    - **Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMSTL)**:
        - Administer **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV infusion**.
    - **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**:
        - **Inj. Methylergometrine / Ergometrine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**.
        - _Reason_: Ergometrine induces tetanic uterine contraction and severe peripheral venoconstriction, pushing a sudden 400–500 mL autotransfusion surge into circulation and precipitating fatal pulmonary edema.
5. **Postpartum Care (Puerperium)**:
    
    - Keep patient **propped up with legs at a lower level than the heart** to pool blood in lower limbs and decrease sudden venous return.
    - Administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** immediately post-delivery to promote diuresis and unload central venous pressure.
    - ICU/HDU monitoring for **24–48 hours postpartum** (the window of maximum failure risk).
    - **Breastfeeding**: Permitted in compensated Grade II cases.
    - **Contraception**: Barrier methods (condoms) or Progestin-Only Pills (POP) / LNG-IUD are safe; combined oral contraceptives are contraindicated due to thromboembolism. Minilaparotomy sterilization under local anesthesia at the end of 1 week postpartum once stabilized.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Acute Pulmonary Edema** (leading cause of maternal death, especially in Mitral Stenosis).
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)** (peak risk immediately postpartum).
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias** (Atrial Fibrillation, Supraventricular Tachycardia).
- **Systemic Thromboembolism** (stroke, systemic arterial occlusion secondary to LA thrombus).
- **Infective Endocarditis**.

#### B. Fetal & Neonatal Complications

- **Preterm Labor & Birth**.
- **Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR) / Low Birth Weight**.
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) / Stillbirth**.
- **Birth Asphyxia**.

---

### 11. Prognosis

- NYHA Grade II cardiac patients have an **excellent maternal prognosis with a mortality rate <1%** when managed with strict multidisciplinary care.
- Long-term maternal survival is unaffected by pregnancy provided the mother survives the perinatal period.

---

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Diastolic Murmurs in Pregnancy**: A soft systolic ejection murmur (Grade <3/6) at the left sternal border is a normal physiological finding due to hyperdynamic circulation. However, **ANY DIASTOLIC MURMUR IS ALWAYS PATHOLOGICAL** and mandates immediate Echocardiography.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering **Methylergometrine (Methergine)** for third-stage management or PPH prevention in a cardiac patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Methylergometrine causes tetanic uterine contraction and severe venoconstriction, pushing a massive autotransfusion surge of 400–500 mL blood into the central circulation, precipitating fatal acute pulmonary edema. **Oxytocin is the DRUG OF CHOICE**.

### Quick Revision

- **NYHA Grade II Definition**: Comfortable at rest, but ordinary physical activity causes dyspnea, fatigue, or palpitations; maternal mortality <1%.
- **Admission Timing**: Elective hospitalization at **28–32 weeks gestation** (window of peak cardiac output expansion).
- **First Stage Protocol**: Semi-recumbent left lateral position, continuous oxygen (5–6 L/min), **continuous epidural analgesia**, IV fluid restriction **≤75 mL/hr**, and IE antibiotic prophylaxis.
- **Second & Third Stage Protocol**: Prophylactic **Low Forceps/Vacuum delivery** (no pushing); AMSTL with **Oxytocin 10 IU ONLY**; **Methylergometrine is ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED**.
- **Critical Postpartum Window**: Highest risk of heart failure occurs **immediately after delivery (10–30 mins)** due to autotransfusion; administer **Inj. Frusemide 20–40 mg IV** and keep propped up with legs dependent.
