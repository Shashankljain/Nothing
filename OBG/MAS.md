# Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS)**: A life-threatening neonatal respiratory emergency occurring predominantly in term, post-term, or growth-restricted (FGR/IUGR) infants, caused by the intrapartum aspiration of meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) into the lower tracheobronchial tree in-utero or during the first breath of life.
- **Incidence**: Meconium-Stained Amniotic Fluid (MSAF) occurs in 10–15% of all live births, of which 5–10% develop clinical MAS.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Primary Trigger

- **Placental Insufficiency & Intrauterine Hypoxia**: Hypoxia stimulates hyperperistalsis of the fetal bowel, relaxation of the anal sphincter, and gasping deep fetal respiratory movements in-utero, causing meconium release and pulmonary inhalation.

#### B. Major Risk Factors

- **Post-term Pregnancy (>42 weeks)**: Reduced liquor volume (oligohydramnios) and mature vagal innervation.
- **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR)**: Chronic uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **Maternal Conditions**: Pre-eclampsia / Gestational Hypertension, Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy (ICP).
- **Intrapartum Factors**: Maternal fever / Chorioamnionitis, prolonged labor, cord compression, and abnormal non-reassuring cardiotocography (CTG) traces.

#### Core Mechanisms of Lung Injury [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Mechanical Airway Obstruction**:
    - Complete obstruction causes segmental atelectasis.
    - Partial obstruction creates a **ball-valve phenomenon** (air enters during inspiration but is trapped during expiration), producing regional hyperinflation and pulmonary air-leak syndrome (pneumothorax/pneumomediastinum).
2. **Chemical Pneumonitis**: Bile salts, bilirubin, and enzymes in meconium irritate the airway mucosa, triggering mucosal injury and inflammation within 4–6 hours.
3. **Surfactant Inactivation**: Bile acids and free fatty acids directly displace and strip endogenous surfactant, increasing surface tension and driving diffuse micro-atelectasis.
4. **Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN)**: Hypoxia and acidosis trigger intense pulmonary vasoconstriction, elevating pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) and producing right-to-left shunting through the ductus arteriosus and foramen ovale.

---

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms & Physical Signs

- **Immediate Postnatal Respiratory Distress**: Tachypnea (respiratory rate >60 breaths/minute), intercostal/subcostal retractions, grunting, nasal flaring, and central cyanosis appearing immediately at birth.
- **Physical Features of Postmaturity / Dysmaturity**:
    - Yellowish or greenish discoloration of skin, umbilical cord, and nail beds.
    - Peeling, dry skin with reduced subcutaneous fat (wrinkled appearance).
- **Chest Examination**: Barrel-shaped overexpanded chest (due to air trapping); coarse crackles and rhonchi heard on auscultation.

---

### 5. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Direct Tracheal Visualization**: Demonstration of thick meconium below the vocal cords upon endotracheal suctioning.
2. **Chest Radiography (CXR - Diagnostic Gold Standard)**:
    - Coarse, irregular, patchy bilateral infiltrates / opacities interspersed with hyperaerated areas.
    - Flattening of the diaphragmatic domes (hyperinflation).
    - Evidence of pulmonary air leaks (pneumothorax or pneumomediastinum).
3. **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) Analysis**: Reveals severe hypoxemia (PaO₂ <50 mmHg), hypercapnia (PaCO₂ >50 mmHg), and mixed metabolic/respiratory acidosis.
4. **Echocardiography**: Assesses for right-to-left shunting across the ductus arteriosus or foramen ovale and calculates right ventricular pressures to confirm co-existing PPHN.

#### A. Delivery Room Resuscitation (Current NRP Guidelines)

- **Routinely Deprecated Practice**: Intrapartum suctioning on the perineum prior to shoulder delivery is **no longer recommended**.
- **Vigorous Neonate (Good tone, HR >100 bpm, strong crying)**: Keep with mother; routine clearing of mouth and nose with bulb syringe.
- **Non-Vigorous Neonate (Depressed, hypotonic, HR <100 bpm)**: Initiate standard resuscitation under a radiant warmer. Routine endotracheal intubation solely for tracheal suctioning is **no longer mandatory** unless the airway is obstructed.

#### B. NICU Supportive & Respiratory Care

1. **Oxygenation & Thermal Care**: Maintain neutral thermal environment; deliver humidified supplemental O₂ via hood or Nasal CPAP to achieve SpO₂ 90–95%.
2. **Mechanical Ventilation**: Indicated when PaO₂ <50 mmHg, PaCO₂ >50 mmHg, or severe respiratory distress persists.
3. **Exogenous Surfactant Therapy**: Inactivation of surfactant by meconium is overcome by exogenous surfactant boluses, improving oxygenation and reducing ECMO requirements.
4. **Targeted PPHN Management**:
    - **Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO)**: Selective pulmonary vasodilator.
    - **Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO)**: Reserved for severe refractory hypoxemic respiratory failure.
5. **Empirical Antibiotic Coverage**: Ampicillin + Gentamicin or Ceftriaxone to cover secondary bacterial superinfections.
6. **Fluid & Metabolic Support**: Correct hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia, and metabolic acidosis; maintain systemic blood pressure with vasopressors (Dopamine/Dobutamine).

---

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pulmonary Air-Leak Syndromes**: Pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, and pulmonary interstitial emphysema (due to air trapping).
- **Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN)**.
- **Neurological Sequelae**: Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE), neonatal seizures, cerebral palsy, and long-term neurodevelopmental delay.
- **Chronic Lung Disease**: Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia (BPD) following prolonged mechanical ventilation.

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The "Ball-Valve" Mechanism**: Meconium doesn't just block air from entering; small particulate plugs allow air to enter during inspiration (when airways expand) but prevent air from exiting during expiration (when airways narrow). This causes **regional hyperinflation, air trapping, and a high risk of tension pneumothorax**.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Routine Intrapartum Suctioning on the Perineum.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Intrapartum suctioning of the mouth and pharynx on the perineum once the head is born is NO LONGER RECOMMENDED!** Randomized controlled trials demonstrated that perineal suctioning does NOT reduce the incidence or severity of MAS, and it can induce deep vagal bradycardia.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: M-E-C-O-N-I-U-M (Core Features of MAS)

- **M**: **Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF)** in term/post-term neonates
- **E**: **Exogenous surfactant inactivation**
- **C**: **Chemical pneumonitis & Cytokine-mediated airway edema**

- **I**: **Infiltrates (patchy coarse opacities)** on chest X-ray
- **U**: **Underlying hypoxia** causing fetal gasping in-utero
- **M**: **Mechanical ventilation** needed for severe acidemic respiratory failure

### Quick Revision

- **Target Population**: **Term, post-term, and FGR infants**.
- **Triad of Pathology**: Airway obstruction (ball-valve effect), Chemical pneumonitis, and Surfactant inactivation.
- **CXR Hallmark**: **Patchy coarse bilateral infiltrates with hyperinflated lung fields**.
- **Major Complication**: **Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN)** and tension pneumothorax.
- **Modern NRP Rule**: **No routine perineal suctioning**; manage non-vigorous infants with standard resuscitation protocols.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS) is a severe neonatal respiratory disorder in term or post-term infants resulting from intrapartum inhalation of meconium-stained amniotic fluid, producing mechanical ball-valve airway obstruction, chemical pneumonitis, surfactant dysfunction, and persistent pulmonary hypertension (PPHN).**
