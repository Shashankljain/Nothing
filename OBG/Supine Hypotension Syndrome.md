# Supine Hypotension Syndrome (Aortocaval Compression Syndrome) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Supine Hypotension Syndrome** (also known as **Aortocaval Compression Syndrome**) is an acute hemodynamic disorder occurring in late pregnancy (usually >28 weeks) when a pregnant woman lies in the supine position.
- It is characterized by maternal arterial hypotension, compensatory tachycardia, dizziness, and potential fetal distress resulting from mechanical compression of the **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC)** and lower abdominal aorta by the enlarged dextrorotated gravid uterus.
- **Incidence**: Occurs in approximately **10%** of women in late pregnancy.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors

- **Mechanical Factor**: Large, heavy gravid uterus compressing the IVC against the lumbar spine in the supine posture.
- **Inadequate Collateral Circulation**: Failure or insufficiency of collateral venous pathways (paravertebral and azygos venous systems) to return distal venous blood to the heart.
- **Risk Factors**:
    - Late third trimester of pregnancy (>28–32 weeks).
    - Polyhydramnios and multiple gestation (excessive uterine volume).
    - Fetal macrosomia (birth weight ≥4.0 kg).
    - Neuraxial (spinal/epidural) anesthesia (causes sympathetic block and peripheral vasodilation, compounding hypotension).
    - Prolonged supine positioning on examination tables or operating tables.

### 4. Clinical Features

#### A. Maternal Features

- **Symptoms**:
    - Dizziness, lightheadedness, and vertigo upon lying flat.
    - Nausea, epigastric discomfort, and retching.
    - Restlessness, anxiety, and air hunger / dyspnea.
    - Visual blurring, cold sweating, and feeling of impending syncope.
- **Signs**:
    - **First Clinical Sign**: **Compensatory maternal tachycardia** (pulse rate ↑).
    - Significant drop in Systolic Blood Pressure (fall of >15–30 mmHg).
    - Facial pallor, cold clammy skin, and profuse diaphoresis.

#### B. Fetal Features

- Reduced uteroplacental perfusion leads to **fetal hypoxia and acidemia**.
- **Cardiotocography (CTG) Findings**:
    - Baseline fetal bradycardia (<110 bpm).
    - **Prolonged or late decelerations** of fetal heart rate.
    - Loss of baseline fetal heart rate variability.

---

### 5. Diagnosis & Investigations

- **Clinical Supine Roll-Over Test**:
    - Patient is turned from lateral to supine position; a rise in diastolic BP of ≥20 mmHg or appearance of hypotensive symptoms confirms compression sensitivity.
- **Electronic Fetal Monitoring (CTG)**:
    - Demonstrates late decelerations or prolonged decelerations when the mother is kept supine, which resolve promptly upon turning to the left lateral position.

---

#### A. Immediate Therapeutic Protocol

1. **Immediate Postural Correction (Gold Standard)**:
    - Turn the patient immediately to the **Left Lateral Recumbent Position** (or apply a 15° left lateral tilt using a wedge under the right hip).
    - This shifts the gravid uterus off the IVC and abdominal aorta, instantly restoring venous return and cardiac output.
2. **Manual Uterine Displacement**:
    - Manually push the gravid uterus toward the left side if postural change is restricted.
3. **Oxygenation**:
    - Administer 100% humidified oxygen at **5–6 L/min** via face mask.
4. **Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation**:
    - Rapid IV bolus infusion of **1 L crystalloid (Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate)** to expand intravascular volume if hypotension persists.
5. **Pharmacotherapy (If Refractory under Anesthesia)**:
    - **Inj Ephedrine**: 5–10 mg IV bolus (vasopressor of choice in pregnancy as it preserves uteroplacental blood flow).
    - **Inj Mephentermine**: 6 mg slow IV.

#### B. Preventive Measures

- Advise all pregnant women past 28 weeks to **avoid sleeping or resting flat on their back (supine)** and adopt a left lateral sleeping posture.
- **Operating Room Care**: Always maintain a **15° left lateral tilt** during Cesarean section or pelvic examinations under neuraxial anesthesia.

---

### 8. Complications

#### A. Maternal Complications

- Unconsciousness and syncope leading to traumatic falls.
- Increased risk of aspiration pneumonitis (Mendelson's syndrome) if emesis occurs during syncope.
- Profound hypotension during spinal/epidural anesthesia for Cesarean delivery.

#### B. Fetal Complications

- Acute intrauterine hypoxia and metabolic acidemia.
- Non-reassuring fetal status requiring emergency Cesarean section.
- Intrapartum birth asphyxia if uncorrected during labor.

---

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **First Clinical Sign**: Compensatory **maternal tachycardia** occurs _before_ the measurable drop in blood pressure. An unexplained pulse rise in a supine pregnant patient should immediately alert the clinician to turn her to the left lateral position.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Prescribing antihypertensives or assuming pre-eclampsia when evaluating blood pressure in a supine patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Supine positioning compresses the IVC and lowers BP, but uterine contraction during labor temporarily squeezes 400–500 mL of blood into circulation (**autotransfusion**), masking supine hypotension during active labor. Always measure BP in the **sitting or left lateral position** for accuracy.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: S-U-P-I-N-E

- **S**: **Syncope & dizziness** upon lying flat in late pregnancy (>28 weeks)
- **U**: **Uterine compression** of IVC and abdominal aorta against the spine
- **P**: **Pulse rate rise (tachycardia)** is the earliest clinical sign
- **I**: **IVC occlusion** reduces right atrial preload and cardiac output
- **N**: **No flat posture** — maintain 15° left lateral tilt on operating tables
- **E**: **Emergency treatment** = Turn to **Left Lateral Position** + O₂ inhalation

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: Gravid uterus (>28 weeks) compresses the IVC in the supine position, reducing venous return and cardiac output.
- **Incidence**: ~10% of late-trimester pregnant women.
- **Earliest Sign**: **Compensatory maternal tachycardia**, followed by SBP drop >15–30 mmHg, dizziness, and fetal decelerations.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Turn patient immediately to the **Left Lateral position** (or apply 15° left lateral tilt).
- **Anesthetic Caution**: Always apply a 15° left tilt during spinal/epidural anesthesia for Cesarean delivery.
