
# Management of Second Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Second Stage of Labor**: The period of labor that begins from full dilatation of the cervix (10 cm) and ends with the complete expulsion of the fetus from the birth canal.
- **Onset Criteria**: Confirmed clinically by vaginal examination showing a fully dilated cervix, maternal urge to push (Ferguson reflex), and bulging of the perineum.

---

### 2. Clinical Phases & Duration [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Two Clinical Phases

1. **Propulsive (Passive) Phase**: Starts from full cervical dilatation (10 cm) until the descending fetal presenting part reaches the pelvic floor.
2. **Expulsive (Active) Phase**: Characterized by voluntary maternal bearing-down efforts (straining) combined with involuntary uterine contractions, ending with the birth of the baby.

#### B. Normal Duration Standards

- **Primigravidae**: Average **2 hours** (WHO allows up to 3 hours).
- **Multiparae**: Average **30 minutes** (WHO allows up to 2 hours).

---

### 3. Principles of Management [⭐ High-Yield]

1. To assist in the natural, slow, and controlled expulsion of the fetus.
2. To prevent maternal perineal injuries and pelvic floor trauma.
3. To maintain continuous vigilance of fetal and maternal condition.

---

### 4. Step-by-Step Management Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Admission & Maternal Positioning

- **Position**: Dorsal position with a **15° left lateral tilt** is preferred as it prevents aortocaval compression, optimizes uteroplacental perfusion, and facilitates maternal expulsive efforts.
- **Upright / Lateral Options**: Squatting or lateral positions may be adopted based on patient preference, but prolonged sitting should be avoided to prevent common peroneal nerve compression.

#### B. Continuous Fetal & Maternal Monitoring

- **Fetal Heart Rate (FHR)**: Monitored every **5 minutes** (or immediately following every uterine contraction) for a full 60 seconds.
- **Normal FHR Range**: **110–160 bpm**. Transient physiological decelerations during contractions should recover promptly to baseline once the contraction passes.
- **Maternal Vitals**: Pulse, blood pressure, and uterine contraction frequency/intensity recorded continuously.

#### C. Method of Pushing / Bearing-Down

- **Ferguson Reflex**: Stretching of the upper vagina by the descending head triggers an involuntary neural reflex urge to push.
- **Technique**: The woman is encouraged to take a deep breath and push downward (straining as in defecation) **only during uterine contractions**. Sustained or premature pushing outside of contractions is strictly discouraged.

#### A. Ritgen's Maneuver (Controlled Delivery of the Head)

- **Timing**: Performed when the suboccipitofrontal diameter emerges at the introitus.
- **Technique**:
    - The operator places sterile towel-covered fingers of the **right hand** over the maternal anococcygeal region to push the fetal chin forward/upward.
    - Simultaneously, the **left hand** exerts gentle downward pressure over the occiput to keep it flexed under the symphysis pubis fulcrum.
    - This allows **slow, controlled delivery of the head by extension** in between contractions, preventing rapid uncoiling and perineal tears.

#### B. Selective Mediolateral Episiotomy

- **Indication**: Done **selectively** (not routinely) when the thinned-out perineum is maximally stretched at crowning and threatens to tear.
- **Anesthesia**: Local infiltration of **10 mL of 1% Lignocaine** into the perineum prior to incision.

#### C. Delivery of Shoulders & Body

- Once the head is born, the mouth and pharynx are gently wiped/suctioned.
- Check around the fetal neck for a **nuchal cord loop** (slip over head if loose; double clamp and cut if tight).
- After restitution and external rotation occur, gently depress the fetal head downward to deliver the **anterior shoulder** under the symphysis pubis, then elevate upward to deliver the **posterior shoulder** over the perineum.

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

#### Comparison: Normal Second Stage vs Second Stage Arrest / Dystocia

|Parameter|Normal Second Stage|Second Stage Arrest / Dystocia|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Duration (Nullipara)**|**<2–3 hours**.|**>3 hours** (or >4 hours with epidural).|
|**Duration (Multipara)**|**<30 mins – 2 hours**.|**>2 hours** (or >3 hours with epidural).|
|**Progress of Descent**|Continuous descent of presenting part.|Cessation of descent/rotation for ≥1 hour.|
|**Primary Cause**|Normal uterine polarity & adequate pelvis.|CPD, malposition (OP), or uterine inertia.|
|**Management**|Spontaneous conduction + Ritgen's maneuver.|Operative vaginal delivery (forceps/ventouse) or C-section.|

---

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal**: Perineal lacerations (3rd/4th-degree tears), cervical/vaginal sulcus tears, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), exhaustion, and post-procedural urinary retention.
- **Fetal**: Intrapartum asphyxia / fetal distress (decelerations on CTG), caput succedaneum, cephalhematoma, intracranial hemorrhage, and brachial plexus trauma during shoulder delivery.

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Timing Criterion for Episiotomy**: The ideal time to perform an episiotomy is when the perineum is thinned out, bulged, and stretched by the crowning head, rather than relying solely on scalp visibility (4–5 cm). Infiltrate **10 mL of 1% Lignocaine** 2–3 minutes before giving the mediolateral incision.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Applying manual **fundal pressure (Kristeller maneuver)** to speed up delivery during the second stage.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Manual fundal pressure during the second stage is strictly NOT RECOMMENDED by WHO** as it increases the risks of uterine rupture, severe perineal tears, and liver hematomas. Use controlled bearing-down or operative vaginal delivery (forceps/ventouse) instead.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: S-E-C-O-N-D (Steps in Second Stage Management)

- **S**: **Support & Left Lateral Position** (15° tilt to prevent caval compression)
- **E**: **Evaluate FHR every 5 minutes** (immediately after contractions for 60s)
- **C**: **Contraction-synchronized pushing** (bear down only during pains)
- **O**: **Outward delivery via Ritgen's maneuver** (controls head extension)
- **N**: **No routine episiotomy** (selective use with 1% Lignocaine)
- **D**: **Delayed cord clamping** (1–3 minutes post-delivery)

---

### Quick Revision

- **Second Stage Duration**: **2 hours** in primigravidae; **30 minutes** in multiparae.
- **FHR Monitoring**: Every **5 minutes** immediately following a contraction for 60 seconds.
- **Position**: Dorsal with **15° left lateral tilt**.
- **Ritgen's Maneuver**: Right hand pushes chin forward via anococcygeal region; left hand controls occiput.
- **Episiotomy Rule**: Selective, mediolateral, after **10 mL 1% Lignocaine** local infiltration at crowning.
