# Baby Friendly Hospital Initiative (BFHI) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Baby Friendly Hospital Initiative (BFHI)**: A global program launched jointly by **WHO and UNICEF in 1992** to protect, promote, and support breastfeeding in maternity facilities worldwide by implementing the **Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding**.

---

### Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding (Core Pillars) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Institutional Policy & Staff Training**: Maintain a written breastfeeding policy routinely communicated to all healthcare staff and train staff in necessary clinical skills.
- **Antenatal Education & Early Initiation**: Inform all pregnant women about the benefits and management of breastfeeding, and help mothers initiate breastfeeding within **<1 hour** of birth.
- **Exclusive Breastfeeding & Lactation Support**: Show mothers proper positioning and latch-on techniques, and give newborns no food or drink other than breast milk unless medically indicated (avoiding prelacteal feeds).
- **Rooming-in & Demand Feeding**: Practice 24-hour rooming-in (allowing mothers and infants to remain together continuously) and encourage unrestricted demand breastfeeding.
- **No Artificial Teats & Community Referral**: Avoid giving artificial teats, pacifiers, or dummies to breastfeeding infants, and foster breastfeeding support groups for post-discharge referral.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Global Sponsoring Bodies**: Jointly established by **WHO and UNICEF (1992)**.
- **Early Initiation**: Put baby to breast within **<1 hour** of birth.
- **Exclusive Nursing**: Strict avoidance of prelacteal feeds (water, honey, or artificial formula).
- **24-Hour Rooming-in**: Mother and infant stay together continuously in the same room.
- **Prevention of Nipple Confusion**: Strictly avoid artificial teats, pacifiers, and bottle feeding.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **The Baby Friendly Hospital Initiative (BFHI) is a global WHO/UNICEF framework enforcing the "Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding" in healthcare facilities, mandating early initiation within 1 hour, exclusive breastfeeding, 24-hour rooming-in, and prohibition of prelacteal feeds or pacifiers.**
