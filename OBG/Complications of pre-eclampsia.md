### 1. Definition & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pre-eclampsia (PE)

- **Pre-eclampsia**: Defined as the new onset of hypertension accompanied by **proteinuria** OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**, arising after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 209; Bedside Clinics, p. 58].
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    1. **Hypertension**: Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on two occasions at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 210].
    2. **Proteinuria**: Excretion of **≥0.3 g protein in a 24-hour urine collection**, or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio **>0.3 mg/mg**, or dipstick reading of **≥1+** (30 mg/dL) in clean-catch random samples [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 210].
    3. **End-Organ Dysfunction Criteria (ACOG 2013)**: Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL), serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, serum transaminases (AST/ALT) >70 U/L (≥2× upper limit of normal), pulmonary edema, or new-onset persistent cerebral/visual symptoms [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 210; Marrow Vol 2, p. 392].

#### B. Severe Pre-eclampsia (Severe PIH) [⭐ High-Yield]

Pre-eclampsia is categorized as **severe** when presenting with **any one or more** of the following criteria [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213; Bedside Clinics, p. 62]:

- SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg** on 2 occasions at least 4 hours apart [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- Platelet count **<100,000/μL** (Thrombocytopenia) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- Impaired liver function (AST/ALT >70 U/L, severe persistent right upper quadrant or epigastric pain) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- Progressive renal insufficiency (serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** or oliguria <400 mL/24 hours) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- Pulmonary edema [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- Persistent cerebral disturbances (severe headache) or visual loss (PRES) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- HELLP syndrome [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 220].

### 3. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Maternal complications in severe pre-eclampsia are widespread, multi-organ, and categorized into immediate (antepartum, intrapartum, puerperal) and remote health outcomes [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Bedside Clinics, p. 62]:

#### A. Immediate Maternal Complications

1. **Central Nervous System (CNS) & Eyes** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **Eclampsia**: Occurrence of grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions; seen in **~2%** of pre-eclamptic women [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
    - **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**: Vasogenic edema predominantly in parieto-occipital subcortical white matter, causing severe headache, scotomata, and cortical blindness [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215, 221].
    - **Cerebral Hemorrhage & Infarction**: Major cause of maternal mortality resulting from severe uncontrolled hypertensive crisis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215, 223; Bedside Clinics, p. 63].
    - **Ocular Damage**: Retinal arteriolar vasospasm, flame-shaped retinal hemorrhages, cotton-wool exudates, and serous retinal detachment [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213, 215].
2. **Hepatic & Hematological System** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **HELLP Syndrome**: Occurs in **10–15%** of severe cases; defined by microangiopathic **H**emolysis (schistocytes on smear, LDH >600 U/L), **E**levated **L**iver enzymes (AST/ALT >70 U/L), and **L**ow **P**latelets (<100,000/μL) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 220; Bedside Clinics, p. 63].
    - **Hepatic Subcapsular Hematoma & Rupture**: Parenchymal necrosis can cause subcapsular bleeding; capsular rupture results in acute hemoperitoneum and fatal hypovolemic shock [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 220].
    - **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**: Consumptive coagulopathy occurring in **~15%** of severe cases due to microvascular endothelial activation and fibrin deposition [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
3. **Placental & Uterine Complications** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**: Premature separation of normally situated placenta due to spiral artery thrombosis, acute atherosis, and rupture of decidual vessels [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Ch 19, p. 288].
4. **Renal System**:
    
    - **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**: Secondary to glomerular capillary endotheliosis, intense renal cortical vasospasm, and tubular necrosis; manifests as oliguria (<400 mL/24 hr) and anuria [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Ch 39, p. 583].
5. **Cardiopulmonary System**:
    
    - **Pulmonary Edema**: Occurs in **~8%** of severe cases due to leaky capillary membranes, low plasma oncotic pressure (hypoalbuminemia), and high pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
    - **Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)** & Acute Left Ventricular Failure (LVF) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Bedside Clinics, p. 62].
6. **Labor & Puerperium Complications**:
    
    - Intrapartum eclampsia, Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) secondary to myometrial fatigue and DIC, puerperal sepsis, and vasomotor shock [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].

---

#### B. Remote / Long-Term Maternal Health Outcomes [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Recurrent Pre-eclampsia**: **~25% risk** of recurrence in subsequent pregnancies [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Bedside Clinics, p. 64].
- **Cardiovascular Sequelae**: 2 to 8-fold increased long-term risk of developing chronic essential hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, and ischemic stroke [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
- **Renal Sequelae**: Increased incidence of microalbuminuria, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, and Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
- **Metabolic Syndrome**: Increased future risk of Type-2 Diabetes Mellitus, dyslipidemia, and obesity [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].

### 4. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Fetal complications stem primarily from **chronic uteroplacental ischemia, placental infarction, and acute hypoxia**[DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Bedside Clinics, p. 62]:

1. **Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR / FGR)**:
    - Caused by chronic placental insufficiency and acute atherosis of spiral arteries, reducing nutrient and oxygen transfer [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
2. **Oligohydramnios**:
    - Scanty amniotic fluid (largest vertical pocket <2 cm, AFI <5 cm) secondary to reduced fetal renal blood flow and diminished fetal urine output [DC Dutta 1, Ch 17, p. 203; Ch 18, p. 215].
3. **Prematurity**:
    - High incidence due to spontaneous preterm labor or medically indicated preterm delivery required to save maternal life [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
4. **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth**:
    - Caused by severe acute/chronic placental hypoxia, massive placental abruption, or umbilical cord accidents [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
5. **Intrapartum Birth Asphyxia & Hypoxia**:
    - Low Apgar scores, meconium aspiration syndrome, and acidosis during labor [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
6. **Neonatal Morbidity & Mortality**:
    - Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS) from pulmonary immaturity, Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH), Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC), neonatal thrombocytopenia, and high NICU admission rates [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].

### 6. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Proteinuria is NO LONGER Mandatory for Severity**: According to current ACOG guidelines, pre-eclampsia is classified as severe even in the total absence of proteinuria if new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) is accompanied by any single end-organ feature (platelets <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT >70 U/L, pulmonary edema, or PRES) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 209; Marrow Vol 2, p. 392].

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing HELLP syndrome with Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP).
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **HELLP syndrome** presents with severe microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (schistocytes), elevated AST/ALT, and marked **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)** in a pre-eclamptic patient [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 220; Bedside Clinics, p. 63]. **AFLP** presents in the 3rd trimester with profound **hypoglycemia**, severe hyperbilirubinemia, and prolonged coagulation profile (PT/APTT) with minimal or secondary thrombocytopenia [DC Dutta 2, Ch 20, p. 288].

### Quick Revision

- **Severe Pre-eclampsia Criteria**: BP **≥160/110 mmHg**, platelets **<100,000/μL**, AST/ALT >70 U/L, creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**, pulmonary edema, or PRES [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 213].
- **Major Maternal Complications**: Eclampsia (2%), Placental Abruption, HELLP syndrome (15%), DIC (15%), Pulmonary Edema (8%), AKI, and PRES [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
- **Major Fetal Complications**: IUGR, Oligohydramnios, Prematurity, IUFD, Birth asphyxia, and NICU admissions [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215].
- **Remote Maternal Risks**: 25% recurrence in next pregnancy, future chronic hypertension, CAD, and CKD [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 215; Bedside Clinics, p. 64].
- **Definitive Treatment**: Delivery is the only definitive cure for pre-eclampsia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 18, p. 217].

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Severe pre-eclampsia is a multisystem gestational disorder characterized by systemic endothelial vasospasm, causing severe maternal end-organ complications (eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, placental abruption, AKI, pulmonary edema) and profound fetal compromise (IUGR, oligohydramnios, prematurity, IUFD) secondary to chronic uteroplacental ischemia.**
