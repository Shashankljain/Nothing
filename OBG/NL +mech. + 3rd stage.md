
# Normal Labor: Definition, Mechanism, and Active Management of Third Stage [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Labor**: A series of physiological events taking place in the genital organs in an effort to expel the viable products of conception (fetus, placenta, and membranes) out of the womb through the vagina into the outer world.
- **Normal Labor (Eutocia)**: Labor is classified as normal if it fulfills the following five strict criteria:
    1. **Spontaneous in onset and at term** (between 37°/7 and 41°/7 completed weeks of gestation).
    2. **With a vertex presentation**.
    3. **Without undue prolongation**.
    4. **Natural termination with minimal aids**.
    5. **Without having any complications** affecting the health of the mother and/or the baby.
- **Abnormal Labor (Dystocia)**: Any deviation from the defined criteria of normal labor.

1. **First Stage (Cervical Stage)**: Begins from the onset of true labor pains and ends with full dilatation of the cervix (10 cm).
    - Average duration: **12 hours** in primigravidae and **6 hours** in multiparae.
    - Divided into a **latent phase** (onset of labor to 5 cm dilatation) and an **active phase** (5 cm to 10 cm dilatation).
2. **Second Stage (Stage of Fetal Expulsion)**: Begins from full dilatation of the cervix and ends with the complete expulsion of the fetus from the birth canal.
    - Average duration: **2 hours** in primigravidae and **30 minutes** in multiparae.
3. **Third Stage (Placental Stage)**: Begins immediately after the expulsion of the fetus and ends with the separation, descent, and expulsion of the placenta and membranes.
    - Average duration: **15 minutes** (in both primigravidae and multiparae).
4. **Fourth Stage**: Stage of close observation for at least **1 hour** post-delivery to monitor maternal vitals, uterine retraction, and vaginal bleeding.

### 3. Mechanism of Normal Labor (Series of Events / Cardinal Movements) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition**: The series of passive adaptive movements undergone by the fetal presenting part during its journey through the maternal pelvic birth canal is termed the **mechanism of labor**.
- **Primary Engaging Position**: The fetal head enters the pelvic brim most commonly through the **available transverse diameter (70%)** or one of the oblique diameters. The most frequent positions are **Left Occipitolateral (LOT)** or **Left Occipitoanterior (LOA)**.
- **Engaging Diameters**:
    - **Anteroposterior Diameter**: Suboccipitobregmatic (**9.5 cm**) or Suboccipitofrontal (**10 cm**).
    - **Transverse Diameter**: Biparietal diameter (**9.5 cm**).

#### Cardinal Movements in Chronological Sequence [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**:
    
    - The passage of the widest transverse diameter of the fetal head (biparietal diameter: **9.5 cm**) through the plane of the pelvic inlet.
    - **Asynclitism (Lateral Deflection)**: Engagement usually occurs with mild lateral deflection of the sagittal suture. **Anterior Asynclitism (Naegele's Obliquity)** allows the anterior parietal bone to present, bringing the smaller **super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm)** across the brim instead of the larger biparietal diameter (**9.5 cm**).
    - In primigravidae, engagement occurs weeks before labor onset; in multiparae, it occurs during late first stage.
2. **Descent**:
    
    - A continuous process occurring throughout the first and second stages of labor.
    - **Driving Forces**: (a) Uterine contractions and retractions, (b) voluntary bearing-down efforts of abdominal muscles, and (c) straightening of the fetal ovoid following amniotic fluid release.
3. **Flexion**:
    
    - As the descending head meets resistance from the unyielding cervix, pelvic side walls, and pelvic floor muscles, flexion increases progressively.
    - **Mechanical Effect**: Replaces the larger occipitofrontal diameter (**11.5 cm**) with the smaller suboccipitobregmatic diameter (**9.5 cm**) or suboccipitofrontal diameter (**10 cm**), presenting favorable dimensions to the pelvis.
4. **Internal Rotation of the Head**:
    
    - The movement of internal rotation turns the well-flexed occiput anteriorly through **2/8ths of a circle (45°)** to lie directly behind the symphysis pubis.
    - **Mechanism (Hart's Rule)**: The sloping V-shaped gutter formed by the levator ani muscle pair directs the leading part (flexed occiput) forward and inward toward the midline during each contraction.
    - **Prerequisites**: Well-flexed head, efficient uterine contractions, favorable midpelvic shape, and good levator ani tone.
5. **Crowning**:
    
    - The progressive descent brings the maximum biparietal diameter (**9.5 cm**) to stretch the vulval introitus without any recession occurring after the uterine contraction passes off.
6. **Delivery of the Head by Extension**:
    
    - The subocciput lodges under the lower margin of the symphysis pubis, acting as a fulcrum.
    - As the head escapes through extension driven by uterine expulsive forces, the **chin, mouth, nose, forehead, and occiput** sweep sequentially over the distended perineum.
7. **Restitution**:
    
    - A visible, passive movement of the born head outside the vulva caused by the **spontaneous 45° untwisting of the fetal neck** that was sustained during internal rotation.
    - The occiput rotates through **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the direction opposite to internal rotation, realigning the head at right angles with the shoulders.
8. **External Rotation**:
    
    - Visible movement of the head through another **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the same direction as restitution.
    - Reflects the **internal rotation of the fetal shoulders** inside the pelvis as the bisacromial shoulder diameter turns from an oblique diameter into the anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic outlet.
9. **Delivery of Shoulders & Trunk by Lateral Flexion**:
    
    - Further descent brings the anterior shoulder under the symphysis pubis first.
    - By a movement of **lateral flexion of the fetal spine**, the posterior shoulder sweeps over the perineum, followed rapidly by the rest of the trunk.

### 5. Management of the Third Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

The third stage is the most crucial stage of labor, where an uneventful labor can suddenly convert into a life-threatening postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) within minutes.

#### A. Physiology of Placental Separation & Hemostasis

- **Placental Separation**: Following the birth of the fetus, the surface area of the upper uterine segment decreases rapidly due to myometrial retraction. Because the placenta is inelastic, it shears off its attachment through the deep spongy layer (_zona spongiosa_) of the decidua basalis.
- **Modes of Separation**:
    - **Schultze Mechanism (Central - 80%)**: Separation starts at the center; placenta descends like an inverted umbrella with the fetal surface presenting at the vulva with minimal retroplacental blood loss.
    - **Mathews-Duncan Mechanism (Marginal - 20%)**: Separation starts at the lower margin; blood escapes externally and the maternal surface presents first.
- **Physiological Hemostasis ("Living Ligatures")**: Hemostasis is achieved primarily by myometrial contraction and retraction compressing the interlacing "Figure-of-8" middle muscle fibers around open blood vessels, supplemented by thrombosis of open sinuses.

---

#### B. Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL - Preferred Method) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Principle**: To excite powerful uterine contractions within **1 minute** of fetal delivery by administering a parenteral oxytocic agent.
- **Key Advantages of AMTSL**:
    - **Reduces Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) by 60%**.
    - Minimizes third-stage blood loss to approximately **one-fifth**.
    - Shortens the duration of the third stage to half.
    - Reduces maternal anemia and the need for blood transfusions.

#### Step-by-Step AMTSL Protocol (WHO Guidelines) [⭐ High-Yield]

```

                               │             AMTSL Protocol             │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │

         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│   1. Uterotonic Agent   │               │2. Controlled Cord Tract.│               │   3. Uterine Massage    │
│  Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM │               │  Brandt-Andrews Method  │               │ Fundal massage immediately│
│    within 1 minute      │               │ Counter-traction on LUS │               │ post-placental delivery │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Step 1: Uterotonic Administration**:
    
    - Administer **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM** (preferred) within **1 minute** of the delivery of the baby (after ruling out the presence of an undiagnosed second twin).
    - _Alternatives if Oxytocin is unavailable_: Tab Misoprostol **600 μg** orally/rectally or Inj. Methergine **0.2 mg IM**.
2. **Step 2: Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews Technique)**:
    
    - Perform delayed cord clamping (1–3 minutes post-delivery) and divide the cord.
    - Place the clamp close to the vulval introitus.
    - During a uterine contraction, place the palm of the abdominal hand over the lower uterine segment above the symphysis pubis, exerting **upward and backward counter-traction** toward the umbilicus.
    - Simultaneously, apply steady, gentle downward and backward traction on the clamped umbilical cord with the vaginal hand until the placenta emerges.
    - As the placenta clears the introitus, cup it in both hands and twist it slowly to peel off the membranes intact without leaving retained fragments.
3. **Step 3: Uterine Massage & Postpartum Vigilance**:
    
    - **Immediate Fundal Massage**: Massage the uterine fundus immediately after placental expulsion until it becomes hard and well-contracted.
    - **Placental Examination**: Meticulously inspect the maternal surface (cotyledons) and fetal surface (membranes and vessels) to confirm complete removal.
    - **Genital Tract Inspection**: Inspect the vulva, perineum, vagina, and cervix under good light to exclude and repair traumatic lacerations.
    - **Postpartum Vigilance**: Palpate the uterine fundus every **15 minutes for 2 hours** post-delivery to ensure the uterus remains hard and well-retracted, and estimate total blood loss.

### 6. Complications of Third Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**:
    - Blood loss **≥500 mL** following vaginal delivery or **≥1000 mL** following C-section.
    - **Causes (The 4 T's)**: **Tone** (Atonic uterus - 80%), **Tissue** (Retained placenta/cotyledon), **Trauma**(Cervicovaginal lacerations), **Thrombin** (Coagulopathy/DIC).
2. **Retained Placenta**:
    - Non-expulsion of placenta within **30 minutes** of fetal delivery. Requires **Manual Removal of Placenta (MROP)** under general anesthesia.
3. **Acute Uterine Inversion**:
    - Rare, life-threatening complication where the uterus turns inside out.
    - **Causes**: Pulling the cord when the uterus is atonic, or applying fundal pressure without counter-traction on an uncontracted uterus.
4. **Obstetric Shock**: Hemorrhagic or neurogenic shock.

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The "Living Ligatures" of the Uterus**: The middle layer of the myometrium in the upper uterine segment contains interlacing, criss-crossing muscle fibers arranged in a **"Figure-of-8" pattern**. As these fibers retract after placental separation, they physically clamp and constrict open blood vessels like mechanical ligatures, providing physiological hemostasis.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing Controlled Cord Traction (CCT) without placing the abdominal hand over the lower uterine segment.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **NEVER apply traction on the umbilical cord without simultaneous upward counter-traction on the lower uterine segment!** Traction applied to a relaxed, atonic uterus without suprapubic counter-elevation can pull the uterine fundus down through the cervix, causing catastrophic **Acute Uterine Inversion**.

### Quick Revision

- **Normal Labor Criteria**: Spontaneous onset at term, vertex presentation, minimal assistance, no complications, normal duration.
- **Cardinal Movements Sequence**: Engagement → Descent → Flexion → Internal Rotation (45° anterior) → Crowning → Extension → Restitution → External Rotation → Expulsion.
- **AMTSL Triad**: (1) Inj. **Oxytocin 10 IU IM** within 1 min, (2) **Controlled Cord Traction (Brandt-Andrews)**, and (3) Immediate **Uterine Massage**.
- **Primary AMTSL Benefit**: Reduces Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) incidence by **60%**.
- **Hemostasis Mechanism**: Retraction of criss-crossing myometrial fibers (**"Living Ligatures"**) compressing open uteroplacental vessels.
