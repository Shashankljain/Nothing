# False Labor Pains (Spurious Labor) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks**

---

### Definition

- **False Labor Pains (Spurious Labor)**: Dull, irregular, and non-progressive painful uterine contractions occurring 1–2 weeks prior to the onset of true labor in primigravidae (and a few days prior in multiparae), caused by stretching of the cervix and lower uterine segment with consequent irritation of neighboring pelvic nerve ganglia.

---

### Key Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Location & Nature**: Pain is dull and spasmodic, confined strictly to the lower abdomen and groin without radiating from the lower back to the abdomen or thighs.
- **Contraction Pattern**: Irregular, infrequent, and non-progressive in frequency, duration, or intensity (does not become progressively stronger or closer together over time).
- **Cervical & Fetal Effects**: **No progressive effacement or dilatation of the cervix**, no descent of the fetal presenting part, and no formation of a tense bag of forewaters.
- **Absence of 'Show'**: Unassociated with the expulsion of a blood-stained cervical mucus plug ('show').
- **Response to Analgesics & Rest**: Contractions dissipate and pain is characteristically relieved by rest, warm baths, or mild analgesics/sedatives.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Onset & Cause**: Appears 1–2 weeks prior to true labor due to stretching of the cervix and lower uterine segment.
- **Pain Location**: Confined strictly to the lower abdomen and groin (non-radiating).
- **Cervix**: **No progressive effacement or dilatation** (key distinguishing feature from true labor).
- **Show**: **Absent** (no blood-tinged mucoid discharge).
- **Relief**: Easily relieved by rest, warm baths, or analgesics.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **False labor pains (spurious labor) are dull, irregular, non-radiating contractions confined to the lower abdomen that produce no progressive cervical effacement or dilatation and are characteristically relieved by rest or analgesics.**
