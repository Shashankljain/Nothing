# Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension & Imminent Eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)

- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)**: Defined as hypertension that develops as a direct consequence of pregnancy after **20 weeks of gestation** (or earlier in hydatidiform mole or multiple pregnancy) in a previously normotensive woman, and resolves within **12 weeks postpartum**.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    - Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on **two occasions at least 4 hours apart** after 20 weeks of gestation.
    - **Clinical Spectrum of PIH**: Includes (1) Gestational Hypertension, (2) Pre-eclampsia, and (3) Eclampsia.

#### B. Pre-eclampsia

- Defined as the new onset of hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) accompanied by **proteinuria** (≥0.3 g/24 hours or urine dipstick ≥1+) OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction** (platelets <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, pulmonary edema, or visual/cerebral symptoms) after 20 weeks of gestation.

#### C. Imminent Eclampsia (Impending Eclampsia / Acute Fulminant Pre-eclampsia)

- **Imminent Eclampsia**: A state of acute, fulminant pre-eclampsia characterized by severe hypertension accompanied by **ominous premonitory symptoms** (severe persistent headache, visual disturbances, epigastric/RUQ pain, oliguria), signaling an **imminent eclamptic convulsion**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primigravida**: First-time exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens).
- **Maternal Age**: Extremes of reproductive age (<18 years or ≥40 years).
- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive chorionic villi exposure (twin/multiple pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, gestational diabetes).
- **Pre-existing Medical Diseases**: Chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes, chronic kidney disease, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²), antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APSA), and inherited thrombophilias.
- **Personal / Family History**: Prior pre-eclampsia or positive family history in mother/sister.

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

According to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) classification, hypertensive disorders in pregnancy are divided into:

1. **Gestational Hypertension**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks without proteinuria or end-organ damage.
2. **Pre-eclampsia**:
    - **Non-severe (Mild)**: BP 140–159/90–109 mmHg with proteinuria.
    - **Severe (Pre-eclampsia with severe features)**: BP ≥160/110 mmHg OR presence of end-organ dysfunction.
3. **Eclampsia**: Pre-eclampsia complicated by grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma.
4. **Chronic Hypertension**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg present before pregnancy or before 20 weeks, persisting >12 weeks postpartum.
5. **Superimposed Pre-eclampsia / Eclampsia**: New-onset proteinuria (>0.5 g/24 hr) or end-organ damage in a chronic hypertensive woman.

#### Core Mechanisms:

1. **Failure of Trophoblastic Invasion**: Endovascular cytotrophoblasts fail to invade the myometrial segment of spiral arterioles (16–18 weeks). Vessels remain narrow and hyper-responsive to vasoconstrictors, causing chronic **uteroplacental ischemia**.
2. **Systemic Endothelial Syndrome**: Hypoxic placenta releases anti-angiogenic factors (**sFlt-1**, **sEng**) that bind VEGF and PlGF, causing widespread endothelial cell injury, loss of Nitric Oxide (NO) and Prostacyclin (PGI₂), and intense systemic vasospasm.
3. **Cerebral Autoregulatory Failure**: Severe cerebral vasospasm and capillary hyperperfusion produce **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** in parieto-occipital subcortical white matter, precipitating eclamptic fits.

### 5. Clinical Features of Imminent Eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Ominous Premonitory Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

Patients with imminent eclampsia exhibit alarming warning symptoms signaling impending eclamptic fits:

1. **Severe Persistent Headache (70% cases)**: Located over frontal or occipital regions, resistant to ordinary analgesics.
2. **Visual Disturbances**: Blurring of vision, scotoma (flashing spots), diplopia, or cortical blindness (amaurosis fugax) due to occipital subcortical edema (PRES) or retinal vasospasm.
3. **Epigastric or Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) Pain**: Acute severe pain caused by stretching of Glisson's capsule from hepatic subcapsular edema, focal necrosis, or hematoma.
4. **Marked Oliguria**: Sudden fall in 24-hour urinary output to **<400 mL in 24 hours**.
5. **Nausea and Vomiting** (at times coffee-colored d/t hemorrhagic gastritis).

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Severe Hypertension**: SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg**.
- **Rapid Generalized Pathological Edema**: Sudden puffiness over face, hands, and abdominal wall.
- **Central Nervous System Excitability**: Hyperreflexia with brisk patellar reflexes (knee jerks) and ankle clonus.

#### A. Essential Laboratory Tests

1. **Complete Blood Count & Platelet Count**: Hemoconcentration (elevated hematocrit) and **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)**.
2. **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Checks for **schistocytes / burr cells** (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia in HELLP syndrome).
3. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL** (indicates distal tubular reabsorption changes and disease severity).
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL**.
4. **Urinalysis for Protein**: 24-hour urine collection (≥0.3 g/24 hr) or urine dipstick (≥2+).
5. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: Serum transaminases (**AST/SGOT and ALT/SGPT >70 U/L [≥2× normal]**) and serum **LDH >600 U/L**.
6. **Coagulation Profile**: PT, APTT, Fibrinogen, and D-dimers (to rule out DIC).

#### B. Imaging & Eye Evaluations

1. **Fundoscopic Examination**: Detects retinal arteriolar vasospasm (vein:arteriole ratio altered from 3:2 to 3:1), retinal edema, or flame hemorrhages.
2. **Brain MRI (FLAIR / T2)**: Confirms **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** in parieto-occipital subcortical white matter.

#### STEP 1: Emergency Hospitalization & General Care

- **Compulsory HDU Admission**: Transfer patient immediately to a High Dependency Unit (HDU) / eclampsia room in a tertiary care center.
- **Bed Rest**: Maintain **left-lateral position** in bed to prevent aortocaval compression, increase renal blood flow, and enhance uteroplacental perfusion.
- **Airway & Safety**: Keep padded mouth gag ready at bedside; insert Foley catheter connected to a urometer for hourly urine output monitoring.

#### STEP 2: Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis — Drug of Choice: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)** is the **gold-standard drug of choice** for seizure prophylaxis in imminent eclampsia (Magpie Trial 2002).

##### A. Pritchard Intramuscular (IM) Regimen (Standard in India) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose (Total 14 g MgSO₄)**:
    - **IV Loading Dose**: **4 g MgSO₄** (20% concentration: 4 ampoules of 50% MgSO₄ [8 mL] diluted with 12 mL Normal Saline) given **IV slowly over 5–10 minutes** (at 1 g/minute).
    - **IM Loading Dose**: **10 g MgSO₄** (5 g of 50% solution [10 mL] deep IM into upper outer quadrant of **each buttock**).
- **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g MgSO₄ (50% solution) deep IM every 4 hours** in alternate buttocks.

##### B. Zuspan Intravenous (IV) Regimen [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose**: **4 to 6 g MgSO₄ IV** in 100 mL Normal Saline over 15–20 minutes.
- **Maintenance Dose**: **1 to 2 g/hour** continuous IV infusion.

##### C. 3 Mandatory Pre-Requisite Clinical Checks Before Each Maintenance Dose [⭐ High-Yield]

Before administering each 4-hourly maintenance dose, verify:

1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute** (or ≥16/min).
3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL over 4 hours).

- **Therapeutic Plasma Level**: **4 to 7 mEq/L** (4.8–8.4 mg/dL).
- **Antidote for MgSO₄ Toxicity**: **Injection Calcium Gluconate 10% — 10 mL IV given slowly over 10 minutes**.

#### STEP 3: Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when SBP is **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP is **≥110 mmHg**.
- **Target Blood Pressure**: SBP **130–140 mmHg** and DBP **80–90 mmHg**.
- **First-Line Regimens**:
    - **Inj. Labetalol**: 20 mg IV bolus over 2 minutes; repeat 40–80 mg IV every 10–15 minutes up to max cumulative **300 mg**.
    - **Inj. Hydralazine**: 5 to 10 mg IV diluted in 10 mL NS over 20 minutes; repeat every 20–30 minutes up to max **30 mg**.
    - **Tab. Nifedipine**: 10 mg PO (swallowed whole, **NEVER sublingual**); repeat 10–20 mg PO after 30 minutes if needed.

#### STEP 4: Antenatal Corticosteroid Therapy (≤34 Weeks Gestation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Administer **Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM**, 2 doses given 24 hours apart (or Dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly for 4 doses) to accelerate fetal lung maturity.

#### STEP 5: Definitive Obstetric Management (Immediate Delivery) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden Rule**: **Delivery is the only definitive cure for pre-eclampsia.**.
- **Timing of Delivery in Imminent Eclampsia**: Once maternal BP and clinical status are stabilized with MgSO₄ and antihypertensives, **immediate termination of pregnancy (delivery) is indicated, IRRESPECTIVE of gestational age**.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**: Preferred if cervix is favorable (Bishop score ≥6). Performed via **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Low Rupturing) + Oxytocin infusion**. Unripe cervix is primed with **PGE₂ gel 0.5 mg** intracervically. Shorten second stage using **forceps or ventouse extraction**.
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Indicated for obstetrical reasons, unfavorable cervix in an unstable mother, or non-reassuring fetal status. **Neuraxial (epidural/spinal) anesthesia** is preferred over general anesthesia.
- **Postpartum Care**: Continue prophylactic **MgSO₄ for at least 24 hours postpartum**.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Progression to **Eclampsia (convulsions)** (2% risk in untreated cases).
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** and cerebral hemorrhage.
- **HELLP Syndrome** (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets).
- **Placental Abruptio (Abruptio Placentae)**.
- Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Oliguria.
- Pulmonary edema and Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS).
- Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
- Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH).

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR).
- Oligohydramnios.
- Iatrogenic preterm birth and prematurity complications.
- **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth** and birth asphyxia.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Ominous Triad of Imminent Eclampsia**: The triad of **severe persistent frontal/occipital headache**, **visual scotomata/blurring**, and **epigastric/RUQ pain** in a pre-eclamptic patient represents an acute emergency indicating impending convulsions and mandates immediate administration of prophylactic **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** and expedited delivery.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering sublingual Nifedipine for severe hypertension or delaying the loading dose of MgSO₄ to check kidney function.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Nifedipine must ALWAYS be swallowed whole with water**; sublingual administration causes a rapid, uncontrolled precipitate drop in BP, resulting in cerebral ischemia and acute fetal distress.
    - **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IRRESPECTIVE of renal status!** Renal function (urine output ≥30 mL/hr) is evaluated only before administering the 4-hourly _maintenance_ doses.

### Quick Revision

- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)**: New BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks gestation, resolving within 12 weeks postpartum.
- **Imminent Eclampsia**: Acute severe pre-eclampsia with alarming premonitory symptoms (severe persistent headache, visual scotomata/blurring, epigastric/RUQ pain, oliguria <400 mL/24 hr).
- **Drug of Choice for Seizure Prophylaxis**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hourly).
- **MgSO₄ Toxicity Checks**: Knee jerk present, RR ≥12/min, Urine output ≥30 mL/hr. Antidote: 10 mL 10% Calcium Gluconate IV.
- **Definitive Treatment**: **Immediate termination of pregnancy (delivery) irrespective of gestational age** once maternal BP and status are stabilized.
