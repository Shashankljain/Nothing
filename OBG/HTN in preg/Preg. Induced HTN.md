### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)

- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)**: Defined as hypertension that develops as a direct consequence of pregnancy after **20 weeks of gestation** (or earlier in hydatidiform mole or multiple pregnancy) in a previously normotensive woman, and resolves within **12 weeks postpartum**.
- **Diagnostic Criteria for Hypertension**:
    - Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on **two occasions at least 4 hours apart** after 20 weeks of gestation.
    - Severe Hypertension: SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg**, confirmed on repeat measurement within 15–30 minutes.

#### B. Pre-eclampsia (PE)

- **Pre-eclampsia**: Defined as the new onset of hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) accompanied by **proteinuria** OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**, arising after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    1. **Hypertension**: BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks on 2 occasions at least 4 hours apart.
    2. **Proteinuria**: Urinary excretion of **≥0.3 g protein in a 24-hour urine collection**, or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio **>0.3 mg/mg**, or dipstick reading of **≥1+** (persistent 30 mg/dL) in clean-catch random samples.
    3. **End-Organ Dysfunction (In the absence of proteinuria)**:
        - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL**.
        - **Renal Insufficiency**: Serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** or doubling of baseline serum creatinine.
        - **Impaired Liver Function**: Serum transaminases (AST/SGOT, ALT/SGPT) elevated to **≥2 times the upper limit of normal**.
        - **Pulmonary Edema**.
        - **New-onset persistent cerebral or visual symptoms**.

#### C. Gestational Hypertension (GH)

- **Gestational Hypertension**: Sustained rise of BP to **≥140/90 mmHg** appearing for the first time after 20 weeks of gestation **without proteinuria** and without signs of systemic end-organ damage, where BP returns to normal within **12 weeks postpartum** (retrospective diagnosis).

#### D. Eclampsia

- **Eclampsia**: Occurrence of **new-onset generalized tonic-clonic convulsions (grand mal seizures) and/or coma** in a woman with pre-eclampsia, in the absence of any other neurological condition.

### 2. Classification of Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Gestational Hypertension (GH)**: New-onset BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks without proteinuria or end-organ damage.
2. **Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia**:
    - **Pre-eclampsia without severe features (Mild PE)**: BP 140–159/90–109 mmHg with proteinuria.
    - **Pre-eclampsia with severe features (Severe PE)**: BP ≥160/110 mmHg OR presence of end-organ dysfunction.
    - **Eclampsia**: Pre-eclampsia associated with tonic-clonic convulsions or coma.
3. **Chronic Hypertension**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg antedating pregnancy or identified prior to 20 weeks of gestation, or persisting beyond 12 weeks postpartum.
4. **Chronic Hypertension with Superimposed Pre-eclampsia / Eclampsia**: Development of new-onset proteinuria (>0.5 g/24 hours) or sudden worsening of hypertension or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks in a woman with pre-existing chronic hypertension.

#### Classification Based on Timing of Onset:

- **Early-Onset Pre-eclampsia**: Develops **<34 weeks of gestation** (associated with severe placental insufficiency and higher fetal/maternal morbidity).
- **Late-Onset Pre-eclampsia**: Develops **≥34 weeks of gestation** (more common, usually milder with favorable outcome).

### 3. Etiopathogenesis & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Risk Factors

- **Primigravida**: First-time maternal exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens).
- **Maternal Age**: Extremes of reproductive age (young <20 years or elderly >35 years).
- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive trophoblastic mass (twin/multiple pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, diabetes mellitus).
- **Family / Personal History**: Prior history of pre-eclampsia or family history in mother/sister.
- **Interpregnancy Interval**: Long interval ≥7 to 10 years.
- **Pre-existing Medical Diseases**: Chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²), chronic kidney disease, antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APSA), and inherited thrombophilias.

#### B. Etiopathogenesis (Two-Stage Disease Process)

```
                       STAGE 1: ABNORMAL PLACENTATION (10–18 Weeks)
       Defective Trophoblastic Invasion → Failure of Remodeling of Uterine Spiral Arterioles
                                           │
                                           ▼
                            Placental Ischemia & Hypoxia
                                           │
                                           ▼
                       STAGE 2: MATERNAL SYSTEMIC ENDOTHELIAL SYNDROME
             Release of Anti-angiogenic Factors (sFlt-1, sEng) & Inflammatory Cytokines
                                           │
                                           ▼
                        Systemic Endothelial Dysfunction & Vasospasm
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
   Hypertension                   Increased Permeability             Organ Ischemia
(↑ Endothelin-1, TXA₂,         (Proteinuria, Pathological Edema,     (Eclampsia / PRES,
 ↓ PGI₂, Nitric Oxide)            Pulmonary & Cerebral Edema)         HELLP, Oliguria)
```

1. **Stage 1 — Failure of Endovascular Trophoblastic Invasion**:
    - Normally, endovascular cytotrophoblasts invade uterine spiral arterioles up to the myometrial segment (16–18 weeks), converting narrow, high-resistance vessels into wide, low-resistance channels.
    - In pre-eclampsia, trophoblastic invasion **fails beyond the decidua-myometrial junction**. Spiral arterioles remain narrow, muscular, and hyper-responsive to vasoconstrictors, leading to chronic **uteroplacental ischemia and hypoxia**.
2. **Stage 2 — Systemic Endothelial Dysfunction & Angiogenic Imbalance**:
    - Hypoxic placenta releases anti-angiogenic factors into maternal circulation:
        - **sFlt-1 (Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1)**: Binds and neutralizes Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF).
        - **sEng (Soluble Endoglin)**: Inhibits TGF-β1 signaling.
    - **Vasoactive Mediator Imbalance**: Increased Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂ - vasoconstrictor/platelet aggregator) and Endothelin-1 with decreased Prostacyclin (PGI₂ - vasodilator), PGE₂, and Nitric Oxide (NO).
    - **Maternal Syndrome**: Intense systemic vasospasm, capillary endothelial leak, microvascular thrombosis, and organ hypoperfusion.

- **Kidney**: **Glomerular capillary endotheliosis** (swollen endothelial cells encroaching on capillary lumens), causing reduced GFR, proteinuria, elevated serum uric acid (**>4.5–5 mg/dL** due to altered tubular secretion), and elevated serum creatinine (**>1.1 mg/dL**).
- **Liver**: Periportal hemorrhagic necrosis, focal ischemic infarcts, and subcapsular hepatic hematoma formation, presenting as elevated transaminases (AST/ALT ≥2× normal) and severe right upper quadrant/epigastric pain.
- **Brain**: Severe cerebral vasospasm, breakdown of autoregulation, and capillary hyperperfusion result in **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**, causing visual disturbances, scotoma, cortical blindness, and eclamptic seizures.
- **Hematology**: Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (schistocytes on peripheral smear), intravascular platelet aggregation/consumption resulting in **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)**, and DIC.
- **Uteroplacental Bed**: Acute atherosis of spiral arteries and reduced uteroplacental blood flow, causing Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), oligohydramnios, and **Placental Abruption**.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Signs

- **Elevated Blood Pressure**: SBP ≥140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg (Severe: SBP ≥160 mmHg and/or DBP ≥110 mmHg).
- **Pathological Edema**: Pitting edema over ankles/tibia after 12 hours bed rest, rapid weight gain >4 lb/week in late pregnancy, and facial/vulval puffiness.
- **Hyperreflexia**: Brisk patellar reflexes (knee jerks) and ankle clonus.

#### B. Symptoms of Imminent Eclampsia (Impending Eclampsia)

These premonitory symptoms indicate severe neuro-vascular involvement and signal an imminent eclamptic convulsion:

1. **Severe, Persistent Headache**: Frontal or occipital, present in ~70% of impending cases, unresponsive to ordinary analgesics.
2. **Visual Disturbances**: Blurring of vision, scotoma (flashing spots), diplopia, or cortical blindness (amaurosis fugax).
3. **Epigastric or Right Upper Quadrant Pain**: Severe continuous pain due to stretching of Glisson's capsule from hepatic subcapsular edema/hematoma.
4. **Marked Oliguria**: Urine output falling to **<400 mL in 24 hours**.
5. **Nausea and Vomiting**.

### 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

When a case of pre-eclampsia is admitted, a comprehensive laboratory and fetomaternal monitoring panel is organized:

#### A. Maternal Laboratory Workup

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Peripheral Blood Smear**:
    - **Hemoconcentration**: Elevated hematocrit.
    - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL** indicates severe pre-eclampsia.
    - **Peripheral Smear**: Presence of **schistocytes / fragmented RBCs** confirms microangiopathic hemolysis in HELLP syndrome.
2. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5 mg/dL** (classic hallmark correlating with severity).
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL**.
3. **Urinalysis for Protein**:
    - 24-hour urine collection for protein (≥0.3 g/24 hr) or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio >0.3 mg/mg or dipstick ≥1+.
4. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**:
    - Serum transaminases: **AST/SGOT and ALT/SGPT elevated to ≥2× normal**.
    - **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)**: Elevated **>600 U/L** (marker of hemolysis and cellular necrosis).
5. **Coagulation Profile**:
    - Prothrombin Time (PT), APTT, Fibrinogen, and D-dimers (indicated if platelets <100,000/μL to rule out DIC).

#### B. Maternal Clinical & Imaging Evaluations

1. **Fundoscopic / Ophthalmoscopic Examination**: Evaluates retinal arteriolar vasospasm (vein:arteriole ratio altered from 3:2 to 3:1), retinal edema, hemorrhages, exudates, or retinal detachment.
2. **Neuro-imaging (MRI Brain)**:
    - **Investigation of choice** for persistent severe headache, visual loss, or seizures.
    - Confirms **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** in parieto-occipital white matter.

#### C. Fetal Wellbeing & Surveillance Panel

1. **Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC / Kick Chart)**.
2. **Non-Stress Test (NST) & Cardiotocography (CTG)**: Daily or twice weekly.
3. **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Twice weekly.
4. **Ultrasonography (USG Biometry)**: Evaluates Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW) and detects FGR every 2–3 weeks.
5. **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler Velocimetry**:
    - Monitors placental vascular resistance: S/D ratio ≥3, Absent End-Diastolic Velocity (AEDV at 33–34 wks), or Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity (REDV at 31–32 wks).

### 8. Treatment & Management of Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Objectives of Management

1. Stabilize blood pressure and prevent progression to severe PE, HELLP, or eclampsia.
2. Prevent maternal and fetal complications.
3. Prevent eclamptic convulsions.
4. Deliver a healthy baby at optimal time.
5. Restore maternal health in the postpartum period.

##### 1. Gestational Hypertension & Mild Pre-eclampsia (PE without severe features)

- **Outpatient / Daycare Management**: Managed on OPD basis with close surveillance.
- **Monitoring**: Twice daily BP monitoring, weekly CBC, platelets, LFT, RFT, USG fetal growth every 3 weeks, daily DFMC.
- **Antihypertensives**: Started only if BP is persistently ≥150/100 mmHg (Oral Labetalol, Methyldopa, or Nifedipine).
- **Definitive Delivery**: **Delivery at 37 completed weeks of gestation** (Induction of Labor).

##### 2. Severe Pre-eclampsia (PE with severe features)

- **Inpatient Admission**: Compulsory admission in a tertiary care unit with HDU/NICU facilities.
- **Rest**: Left-lateral position in bed (reduces vena caval compression → increases renal blood flow → diuresis; increases uterine blood flow → improves placental perfusion).
- **Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** is the drug of choice (Magpie Trial 2002) for seizure prophylaxis in severe PE, imminent eclampsia, and HELLP syndrome.
- **Antihypertensive Therapy**: Indicated when SBP is **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP is **≥110 mmHg** (Target BP: SBP 130–140 mmHg, DBP 80–90 mmHg).
    - Parenteral 1st line drugs: **IV Labetalol**, **IV Hydralazine**, or **Oral Nifedipine**.
- **Corticosteroids for Fetal Lung Maturation**: Administered if gestation is **≤34 weeks** (Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM, 2 doses 24 hours apart).
- **Timing of Delivery**:
    - **At ≥34 Completed Weeks**: Proceed with planned delivery after stabilizing BP and completing corticosteroid course.
    - **At <34 Weeks**: Expectant management in a tertiary unit IF maternal and fetal conditions remain completely stable.
    - **Emergency Indications for Immediate Delivery (Irrespective of Gestational Age)**:
        1. Imminent eclampsia or eclamptic convulsions.
        2. HELLP syndrome.
        3. Uncontrolled severe hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg) despite dual drugs.
        4. Severe fetal distress / Abnormal Doppler (AEDV/REDV).
        5. Placental abruption.
        6. Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
        7. Pulmonary edema or severe renal failure.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**: Surgical induction by Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Low Rupturing) + Oxytocin infusion if cervix is ripe. Cervical priming with PGE₂ gel (500 μg) if cervix is unripe. Shorten 2nd stage with forceps or ventouse.
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Reserved for obstetrical indications, unfavorable cervix in rapidly deteriorating mother, or severe fetal distress. Neuraxial (epidural/spinal) anesthesia is preferred.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Neurological**: Eclampsia (convulsions), cerebral hemorrhage/infarction, PRES, cortical blindness.
- **Hematological**: HELLP syndrome, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), severe thrombocytopenia.
- **Placental**: **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**.
- **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), acute tubular/cortical necrosis, oliguria, anuria.
- **Cardiopulmonary**: Pulmonary edema, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), acute left ventricular failure.
- **Hepatic**: Subcapsular liver hematoma, liver rupture, jaundice.
- **Obstetric**: Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH), maternal death.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications

- Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR / FGR) due to chronic placental insufficiency.
- Spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm labor and prematurity.
- Oligohydramnios.
- Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
- Birth asphyxia & NICU admission.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Proteinuria is NO LONGER Mandatory for Diagnosis**: According to ACOG guidelines, pre-eclampsia can be diagnosed in the complete absence of proteinuria if new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) is accompanied by any systemic end-organ feature (thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, pulmonary edema, or severe visual/cerebral symptoms).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Giving sublingual Nifedipine for severe hypertension or withholding the loading dose of MgSO₄ to evaluate renal function.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Nifedipine must ALWAYS be swallowed whole with water**; sublingual administration causes a rapid, uncontrolled precipitate drop in BP, resulting in cerebral ischemia and acute fetal distress.
    - **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IRRESPECTIVE of renal status!** Renal function (urine output ≥30 mL/hr) is evaluated only before administering the 4-hourly _maintenance_ doses.

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-R-E-E-C-L-A-M-P-S-I-A (Diagnostic & Severity Checklist)

- **P**: **Proteinuria (≥0.3 g/24 hr or P/C ratio >0.3)**
- **R**: **Rise in BP (≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks)**
- **E**: **End-organ dysfunction** (Liver, Kidney, Brain, Platelets)
- **E**: **Epigastric / RUQ pain** (severe feature)
- **C**: **Cerebral & visual symptoms** (severe persistent headache, scotoma)
- **L**: **Low platelets (<100,000/μL - HELLP syndrome)**
- **A**: **Anti-angiogenic imbalance** (↑ sFlt-1, ↑ sEng, ↓ PlGF)
- **M**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** for seizure prophylaxis
- **P**: **Pulmonary edema**
- **S**: **Systolic BP ≥160 or Diastolic BP ≥110 mmHg** (severe PE)
- **I**: **Ischemia of placenta** → FGR & Abruption
- **A**: **Antihypertensives** (Labetalol, Hydralazine, Nifedipine)

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: New BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks with proteinuria (≥0.3 g/24 hr) OR end-organ damage.
- **Severe Features**: BP **≥160/110 mmHg**, severe headache, visual loss, RUQ/epigastric pain, platelets **<100,000/μL**, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**, or pulmonary edema.
- **Symptoms of Imminent Eclampsia**: Persistent frontal headache, visual blurring/scotoma, epigastric pain, and marked oliguria.
- **Drug of Choice for Seizure Prophylaxis**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hourly). Antidote: 10% Calcium Gluconate 10 mL IV.
- **Antihypertensives**: IV Labetalol, IV Hydralazine, or Oral Nifedipine (swallowed whole) when BP ≥160/110 mmHg.
- **Definitive Cure**: Delivery (IOL at 37 wks for mild PE; at 34 wks for severe PE after steroids).

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Pre-eclampsia is a multisystem gestational disorder characterized by new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) and proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction developing after 20 weeks of gestation, driven by defective spiral artery remodeling, placental ischemia, and systemic endothelial vasospasm.**
