# Gestational Hypertension & Pre-eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Gestational Hypertension (GH)

- **Gestational Hypertension**: Defined as a sustained rise in blood pressure to **≥140/90 mmHg** (systolic BP ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥90 mmHg) measured on at least two occasions at least 4 hours apart, developing for the first time after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman, **without proteinuria** and without systemic end-organ dysfunction.
- **Diagnostic Criteria & Features**:
    1. Blood pressure returns to normal within **12 weeks postpartum** (it is a retrospective final diagnosis).
    2. Unassociated with hemoconcentration, thrombocytopenia, hyperuricemia, or hepatic/renal dysfunction.
    3. Approximately **25% of women** initially presenting with gestational hypertension subsequently develop proteinuria and evolve into pre-eclampsia.

#### B. Pre-eclampsia (PE)

- **Pre-eclampsia**: Defined as the new onset of hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks of gestation) accompanied by **proteinuria** OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**, in a previously normotensive woman. It is a progressive multisystem gestational syndrome.
- **Diagnostic Thresholds**:
    1. **Hypertension**: Absolute SBP **≥140 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥90 mmHg** on 2 occasions at least 4 hours apart.
    2. **Proteinuria**: Excretion of **≥0.3 g protein in a 24-hour urine specimen**, or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio **>0.3 mg/mg**, or dipstick reading of **≥1+** (persistent 30 mg/dL) in clean-catch random samples.
    3. **End-Organ Dysfunction (In the absence of proteinuria)**:
        - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL**.
        - **Renal Insufficiency**: Serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** or doubling of baseline.
        - **Impaired Liver Function**: Serum transaminases (AST and ALT) elevated to **≥2 times the upper limit of normal** (>70 U/L).
        - **Pulmonary Edema**.
        - **New-onset persistent cerebral or visual symptoms**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Major Risk Factors

- **Primigravida**: First-time maternal exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens).
- **Maternal Age**: Extremes of reproductive age (young primigravidae or age >35–40 years).
- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive exposure to chorionic villi (twin/multiple pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, diabetes mellitus).
- **Family or Personal History**: Pre-eclampsia in a prior pregnancy or in a first-degree female relative (mother or sister).
- **Interpregnancy Interval**: Long interval **≥7 years**.
- **Obesity & Metabolic Factors**: BMI **>35 kg/m²**, insulin resistance, Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (PCOS).
- **Pre-existing Medical Conditions**: Chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes, chronic kidney disease, antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APSA), and inherited thrombophilias (Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S deficiency).
- **New Paternity & Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART/IVF)**.

1. **Gestational Hypertension**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks without proteinuria or end-organ damage.
2. **Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia**:
    - **Pre-eclampsia without severe features (Mild PE)**: BP 140–159/90–109 mmHg with proteinuria.
    - **Pre-eclampsia with severe features (Severe PE)**: BP ≥160/110 mmHg OR presence of end-organ damage.
    - **Eclampsia**: Pre-eclampsia complicated by generalized grand mal tonic-clonic seizures and/or coma.
3. **Chronic Hypertension**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg present before pregnancy or before 20 weeks of gestation, or persisting >12 weeks postpartum.
4. **Chronic Hypertension with Superimposed Pre-eclampsia / Eclampsia**: New-onset proteinuria (>0.5 g/24 hr) or sudden end-organ damage developing after 20 weeks in a woman with chronic hypertension.

#### Sub-classification by Onset:

- **Early-Onset Pre-eclampsia**: Develops **<34 weeks of gestation** (associated with severe placental malperfusion, high fetal morbidity).
- **Late-Onset Pre-eclampsia**: Develops **≥34 weeks of gestation** (more common, milder clinical course).

### 4. Etiopathogenesis & Pathophysiology (Cascade Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Basic Pathology

The underlying fundamental pathology of pre-eclampsia is **endothelial dysfunction and intense vasospasm**, resulting from abnormal placentation.

#### B. Two-Stage Etiopathogenetic Sequence

1. **Stage 1 — Failure of Endovascular Trophoblastic Invasion**:
    - Normally, cytotrophoblasts invade spiral arterioles up to the myometrial segment (16–18 weeks), replacing musculoelastic media with fibrinoid tissue to create high-flow, low-resistance vessels.
    - In pre-eclampsia, endovascular invasion **fails to extend beyond the decidua-myometrial junction**. Spiral arterioles remain narrow and muscular, hyper-responsive to pressors, causing chronic **uteroplacental ischemia**.
2. **Stage 2 — Systemic Endothelial Syndrome & Angiogenic Imbalance**:
    - The ischemic placenta secretes excess anti-angiogenic factors:
        - **sFlt-1 (Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1)**: Binds VEGF and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF), inhibiting endothelial cell signaling and angiogenesis.
        - **sEng (Soluble Endoglin)**: Inhibits TGF-β1 signaling.
    - **Vasoactive Mediator Shift**: Increased Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂ - vasoconstrictor) with decreased Prostacyclin (PGI₂ - vasodilator); deficiency of Nitric Oxide (NO) and elevated Endothelin-1.
    - **Systemic Manifestations**: Vasospasm, increased capillary permeability (leak), activation of coagulation cascade (thrombocytopenia, DIC), and multi-organ hypoperfusion.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Triad of Classical Signs

1. **Hypertension**: SBP ≥140 mmHg or DBP ≥90 mmHg.
2. **Pathological Edema**: Pitting edema over ankles on rising from bed after 12 hours rest, or sudden gain in weight **>4 lb (1.8 kg) in a week** in late pregnancy.
3. **Proteinuria**: Dipstick ≥1+ or 24-hour urine protein ≥0.3 g.

#### B. Symptoms & Signs of Severe Pre-eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Severe Blood Pressure Elevation**: SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg**.
2. **Cerebral Symptoms**: Severe persistent frontal or occipital headache unresponsive to standard analgesics.
3. **Visual Disturbances**: Blurring of vision, scotoma, diplopia, or cortical blindness (amaurosis fugax) due to occipital subcortical vasogenic edema (PRES).
4. **Epigastric or Right Upper Quadrant Pain**: Acute continuous pain caused by stretching of Glisson's capsule from hepatic subcapsular edema, focal necrosis, or hematoma.
5. **Renal Dysfunction & Oliguria**: Urine output **<400–500 mL in 24 hours**, serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**.
6. **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL**.
7. **Hepatic Dysfunction**: AST and ALT **>70 U/L (≥2× normal)**, serum LDH **>600 U/L**.
8. **Pulmonary Edema**: Secondary to capillary leak and decreased oncotic pressure.
9. **HELLP Syndrome**: Triad of Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, and Low Platelets.
10. **Symptoms of Imminent Eclampsia**: Severe headache, epigastric/RUQ pain, visual loss, and marked oliguria signal an impending eclamptic fit.

#### A. Maternal Diagnostic Laboratory Workup

1. **Urine Analysis**: 24-hour urine collection for total protein (**≥0.3 g/24 hr**), urine P/C ratio (>0.3 mg/mg), or dipstick (≥1+).
2. **Complete Blood Count & Peripheral Smear**:
    - Hemoconcentration (elevated hematocrit).
    - Thrombocytopenia (**<100,000/μL**).
    - Schistocytes and burr cells on peripheral smear confirming microangiopathic hemolysis in HELLP.
3. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL** (normal <4 mg/dL); indicates distal tubular reabsorption changes and correlates with disease severity.
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL**.
4. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**:
    - Serum transaminases: **AST/SGOT and ALT/SGPT >70 U/L (≥2× normal)**.
    - **Serum LDH**: Elevated **>600 U/L**.
    - Total Serum Bilirubin: **>1.2 mg/dL**.
5. **Coagulation Profile**: PT, APTT, Fibrinogen, and D-dimer (indicated if platelets <100,000/μL to exclude DIC).
6. **Ophthalmoscopy**: Evaluates retinal arteriolar vasospasm (V:A ratio altered from 3:2 to 3:1), retinal edema, hemorrhages, exudates, or retinal detachment.
7. **Neuro-imaging (MRI Brain)**: Diagnostic tool for persistent severe headache, visual loss, or seizures; confirms **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** in parieto-occipital subcortical white matter.

#### B. Antenatal Fetal Wellbeing Panel

1. **Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC / Kick Chart)**.
2. **Non-Stress Test (NST) & Cardiotocography (CTG)**: Performed weekly in mild PE; daily/alternate days in severe PE.
3. **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Score calculated twice weekly.
4. **Ultrasonography (USG Biometry)**: Evaluates Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW), detects FGR every 2–3 weeks, and measures Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).
5. **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler Velocimetry**: Monitors for elevated placental vascular resistance (S/D ratio ≥3, Absent End-Diastolic Flow at 33–34 wks, or Reversed End-Diastolic Flow at 31–32 wks).

### 8. Treatment & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Objectives of Management

1. To stabilize blood pressure and prevent progression to severe PE, HELLP, or eclampsia.
2. To prevent maternal and fetal complications.
3. Delivery of a healthy baby at optimal time.
4. Restoration of maternal health in the postpartum period.

##### 1. Management of Gestational Hypertension & Mild Pre-eclampsia

- **Outpatient / Daycare Setting**: Patients without severe features are managed on an outpatient or daycare basis.
- **Bed Rest**: Left-lateral posture in bed increases renal blood flow (promoting diuresis), increases uterine blood flow (improving placental perfusion), and reduces BP.
- **Monitoring**: BP measured twice daily; daily DFMC; weekly CBC, platelet count, LFT, serum creatinine; USG fetal growth every 3 weeks.
- **Antihypertensive Indications**: Antihypertensives are **NOT routinely indicated** in mild PE unless BP persistently reaches **≥150/100 mmHg** or severe range **≥160/110 mmHg**. First-line oral drugs: **Labetalol**, **Methyldopa**, or **Nifedipine**.
- **Definitive Delivery**: **Delivery at 37 completed weeks of gestation** (Induction of Labor).

##### 2. Management of Severe Pre-eclampsia (PE with Severe Features)

- **Inpatient Admission**: Hospitalization is mandatory in a tertiary unit with HDU/NICU facilities.
- **Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis — Drug of Choice: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)**: Recommended antepartum, intrapartum, and postpartum for seizure prophylaxis (Magpie Trial 2002).
    - **Pritchard Regimen**: Loading dose = **4 g IV (20% solution over 5–10 min) + 10 g IM (50% solution, 5 g deep IM in each buttock)**; Maintenance dose = **5 g IM (50%) every 4 hours** in alternate buttocks.
    - **Zuspan Regimen**: Loading 4–6 g IV in 100 mL fluid over 15–20 min, followed by 1–2 g/hour continuous IV infusion.
    - **Mandatory 4-Hourly Pre-Requisite Checks Before Maintenance Dose**:
        1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
        2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute**.
        3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL in 4 hours).
    - **Therapeutic Range & Antidote**: Therapeutic level = **4 to 7 mEq/L**. Specific antidote = **10 mL of 10% Calcium Gluconate IV slowly over 10 minutes**.
- **Antihypertensive Therapy for Hypertensive Crisis (BP ≥160/110 mmHg)**:
    - Goal: Target SBP **130–140 mmHg**, DBP **80–90 mmHg** (prevent stroke while avoiding fetal hypoperfusion).
    - **Inj. Labetalol**: 20 mg IV bolus over 2 min; repeat 40–80 mg IV every 10–15 min up to max cumulative **300 mg**.
    - **Inj. Hydralazine**: 5–10 mg IV diluted in 10 mL NS over 20 min; repeat every 20–30 min up to max **20–30 mg**.
    - **Tab. Nifedipine**: 10 mg PO (swallowed whole, **NEVER sublingual**); repeat 10–20 mg PO after 30 min if needed.
- **Corticosteroid Therapy**: Administered if gestation is **≤34 weeks** (**Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM, 2 doses 24 hours apart**) to accelerate fetal lung maturity and reduce RDS, IVH, NEC, and maternal thrombocytopenia.
- **Timing of Delivery**:
    - **At ≥34 Completed Weeks**: Proceed to delivery after stabilizing BP.
    - **At <34 Weeks**: Expectant management in a tertiary care setting IF maternal and fetal conditions remain completely stable.
    - **Emergency Indications for Immediate Delivery (Irrespective of Gestational Age)**:
        1. Imminent eclampsia or eclamptic convulsions.
        2. HELLP syndrome.
        3. Uncontrolled severe hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg) despite dual drugs.
        4. Pulmonary edema or PaO₂ <95%.
        5. Severe renal failure (oliguria <500 mL/24 hr or serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL).
        6. Non-reassuring fetal status / Umbilical artery Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity (REDV).
        7. Placental abruption or DIC.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**: Method of choice if cervix is ripe. Performed via **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Low Rupturing) + Oxytocin infusion**. Unripe cervix is primed with **PGE₂ gel 0.5 mg**intracervically. Shorten second stage with forceps or ventouse.
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Reserved for obstetrical indications, unfavorable cervix in a rapidly deteriorating mother, or severe fetal distress. Neuraxial (epidural/spinal) anesthesia is preferred.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neurological**: Eclampsia (fits), cerebral hemorrhage, PRES, cortical blindness.
- **Hematological**: HELLP syndrome, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), severe thrombocytopenia.
- **Placental & Vascular**: **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**.
- **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), acute tubular/cortical necrosis, oliguria, anuria.
- **Cardiopulmonary**: Pulmonary edema, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), acute heart failure.
- **Hepatic**: Subcapsular liver hematoma, liver rupture, jaundice.
- **Obstetric**: Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH), maternal death.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR / FGR) due to chronic placental insufficiency.
- Oligohydramnios.
- Spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm labor and prematurity.
- Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
- Intrapartum fetal hypoxia, birth asphyxia, and NICU admission.

---

### 10. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- Prognosis is excellent when pre-eclampsia is detected early and managed appropriately; maternal and perinatal morbidity rise significantly with delayed hospital referral, progression to eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, or abruption.

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Proteinuria is NO LONGER Mandatory for Diagnosis**: According to current ACOG guidelines, pre-eclampsia can be diagnosed even in the total absence of proteinuria if new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) is accompanied by any systemic end-organ feature (thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, pulmonary edema, or severe visual/cerebral symptoms).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering sublingual Nifedipine for acute hypertension or withholding the loading dose of MgSO₄ to evaluate kidney function.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Nifedipine must ALWAYS be swallowed whole with water**; sublingual administration causes a rapid, uncontrolled precipitate drop in BP, resulting in cerebral ischemia and acute fetal distress.
    - **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IRRESPECTIVE of renal status!** Renal function (urine output ≥30 mL/hr) is evaluated only before administering the 4-hourly _maintenance_ doses.

### 13. Diagram Guides

### Diagram 1: Retinal Arteriolar Changes on Fundoscopy in Severe Pre-eclampsia

- **Type**: Fundoscopic diagnostic schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Circular fundoscopic viewing field showing optic disc, retinal vessels, and macula.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Optic Disc** — showing disc edema / papilledema
    2. **Retinal Arterioles** — localized segmental arteriolar constriction / vasospasm
    3. **Altered Vein-to-Arteriole Ratio** — altered from normal 3:2 to pathological 3:1 or 4:1
    4. **Flame-shaped Retinal Hemorrhages & Cotton Wool Exudates** — vascular ischemic leaks
    5. **Serous Retinal Detachment** — subretinal fluid accumulation
- **Annotation**: _"Retinal arteriolar vasospasm (3:1 V:A ratio) and flame hemorrhages reflect systemic multi-organ vascular endothelial injury in severe pre-eclampsia."_

### Quick Revision

- **Gestational Hypertension**: New BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks without proteinuria or end-organ damage, resolving <12 weeks postpartum.
- **Pre-eclampsia Definition**: New BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks with proteinuria (≥0.3 g/24 hr) OR end-organ damage.
- **Etiopathogenesis**: Failure of endovascular trophoblastic invasion beyond decidua-myometrial junction → placental ischemia → sFlt-1/sEng release → systemic endothelial vasospasm.
- **Drug of Choice for Seizure Prophylaxis**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hourly). Antidote: 10% Calcium Gluconate 10 mL IV.
- **Definitive Cure**: Delivery (Induction of labor at 37 wks for mild PE; at 34 wks for severe PE after Betamethasone steroids).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Gestational hypertension is a transient pregnancy-induced elevation of blood pressure (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) without proteinuria developing after 20 weeks, whereas pre-eclampsia is a multisystem syndrome driven by defective spiral artery remodelling, placental ischemia, anti-angiogenic imbalance (sFlt-1), and systemic endothelial vasospasm, requiring timed delivery, antihypertensives, and Magnesium Sulfate prophylaxis.**
