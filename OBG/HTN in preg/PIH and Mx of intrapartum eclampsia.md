# Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension & Management of Intrapartum Eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)

- **PIH**: Defined as hypertension that develops as a direct result of pregnancy after **20 weeks of gestation** (or earlier in gestational trophoblastic disease/hydatidiform mole or multifetal pregnancy) in a previously normotensive woman, and resolves within **12 weeks postpartum**.
- **Diagnostic Threshold for Hypertension**:
    - Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on **two occasions at least 4 hours apart** after 20 weeks of gestation.
    - Severe Hypertension: SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg**, confirmed on repeat measurement within 15–30 minutes.

#### B. Pre-eclampsia

- **Pre-eclampsia**: A progressive multisystem gestational disorder characterized by new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) arising after 20 weeks of gestation accompanied by **proteinuria** (≥0.3 g/24 hours or urine protein-to-creatinine ratio ≥0.3 mg or dipstick ≥1+) OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**(thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, renal insufficiency with serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, impaired liver function, pulmonary edema, or persistent cerebral/visual symptoms).

#### C. Eclampsia

- **Eclampsia**: Defined as the occurrence of **new-onset generalized tonic-clonic convulsions (grand mal seizures) and/or coma** in a woman with pre-eclampsia (or gestational hypertension), in the absence of any other identifiable neurological or medical cause (e.g., epilepsy, intracranial hemorrhage, meningitis).

1. **Gestational Hypertension (GH)**: New-onset BP ≥140/90 mmHg occurring after 20 weeks of gestation **without**proteinuria or systemic end-organ damage. BP normalizes within 12 weeks postpartum (retrospective final diagnosis).
2. **Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia**:
    - **Pre-eclampsia without severe features**: SBP 140–159 mmHg or DBP 90–109 mmHg with proteinuria.
    - **Pre-eclampsia with severe features**: SBP ≥160 mmHg or DBP ≥110 mmHg, OR severe end-organ features (headache, visual disturbances, epigastric/RUQ pain, ALT/AST >2 times upper limit, platelets <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, or pulmonary edema).
    - **Eclampsia**: Pre-eclampsia associated with tonic-clonic convulsions or coma.
3. **Chronic Hypertension**: SBP ≥140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg antedating pregnancy or identified prior to 20 weeks of gestation, or persisting beyond 12 weeks postpartum.
4. **Chronic Hypertension with Superimposed Pre-eclampsia / Eclampsia**: Development of new-onset proteinuria (≥0.5 g/24 hours) or sudden worsening of hypertension or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks in a woman with known pre-existing chronic hypertension.

#### Clinical Classification of Eclampsia by Timing of Onset [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Antepartum Eclampsia (50%)**: Seizures occur during pregnancy prior to the onset of labor (most common type, carries worst prognosis).
- **Intrapartum Eclampsia (30%)**: Seizures occur during the course of labor (as in the present case of a 25-year-old primigravida).
- **Postpartum Eclampsia (20%)**: Seizures occur after delivery, typically within the first 48 hours postpartum.

### 3. Etiopathogenesis & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Risk Factors

- **Primigravida**: First-time maternal exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens).
- **Maternal Age**: Young primigravidae (<20 years, as in this 25-year-old case) or elderly primigravidae (>35 years).
- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive exposure to trophoblastic tissue (twin/multiple pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, diabetes mellitus).
- **Family / Personal History**: History of pre-eclampsia/eclampsia in mother or sister.
- **Pre-existing Medical Diseases**: Chronic hypertension, renal disease, pre-gestational diabetes, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²), antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APLA), and inherited thrombophilias.

#### B. Etiopathogenesis (Two-Stage Disease Process) [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                       STAGE 1: ABNORMAL PLACENTATION (10–18 Weeks)
       Defective Trophoblastic Invasion → Failure of Remodeling of Uterine Spiral Arterioles
                                           │
                                           ▼
                            Placental Ischemia & Hypoxia
                                           │
                                           ▼
                       STAGE 2: MATERNAL SYSTEMIC ENDOTHELIAL SYNDROME
             Release of Anti-angiogenic Factors (sFlt-1, sEng) & Inflammatory Cytokines
                                           │
                                           ▼
                        Systemic Endothelial Dysfunction & Vasospasm
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
   Hypertension                   Increased Permeability             Organ Ischemia
(↑ Endothelin-1, TXA₂,         (Proteinuria, Pathological Edema,     (Eclampsia / PRES,
 ↓ PGI₂, Nitric Oxide)            Pulmonary & Cerebral Edema)         HELLP, Oliguria)
```

1. **Stage 1 — Failure of Endovascular Trophoblastic Invasion**:
    
    - In normal pregnancy, endovascular cytotrophoblasts invade the maternal uterine spiral arterioles in two successive waves: 1st wave (10–12 weeks, invading up to decidual segment) and 2nd wave (16–18 weeks, invading up to myometrial segment). This replaces muscular and elastic tissue with fibrinoid material, converting narrow, high-resistance vessels into distended, low-resistance, high-flow conduits.
    - In pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, the **2nd wave of trophoblastic invasion fails** to occur beyond the decidua-myometrial junction. Spiral arterioles remain narrow, muscular, and hyper-responsive to pressor agents, resulting in **chronic uteroplacental ischemia**.
2. **Stage 2 — Angiogenic Imbalance & Systemic Endothelial Dysfunction**:
    
    - Hypoxic syncytiotrophoblasts release excess anti-angiogenic soluble factors into maternal circulation:
        - **Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase 1 (sFlt-1)**: Binds and neutralizes circulating Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF).
        - **Soluble Endoglin (sEng)**: Inhibits Transforming Growth Factor-beta (TGF-β1) signaling.
    - **Vasoactive Mediator Imbalance**:
        - Increased Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) (platelet vasoconstrictor/aggregator) with relative deficiency of Prostacyclin (PGI₂) (endothelial vasodilator).
        - Deficient synthesis of endothelial **Nitric Oxide (NO)** from L-arginine.
        - Increased production of **Endothelin-1** and reactive oxygen species (ROS / oxidative stress).
    - **Pathological Consequences**: Systemic arteriolar vasospasm, leaky capillary endothelium, microvascular thrombosis, and organ ischemia.

- **Brain Pathology in Eclampsia**: Severe cerebral vasospasm, breakdown of cerebral blood flow autoregulation, capillary hyperperfusion, and microvascular leakage result in **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**. MRI reveals bilateral, symmetrical vasogenic edema predominantly affecting the parieto-occipital subcortical white matter.
- **Renal Pathology**: **Glomerular capillary endotheliosis** (swollen endothelial cells encroaching on capillary lumens), causing reduced Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR), elevated serum uric acid (>4.5 mg/dL due to diminished tubular secretion), elevated serum creatinine (>1.1 mg/dL), and proteinuria.
- **Hepatic Pathology**: Periportal hemorrhagic necrosis, focal ischemic infarcts, and subcapsular hepatic hematoma formation, manifesting as elevated liver transaminases (AST/ALT) and severe right upper quadrant/epigastric pain.
- **Hematological Pathology**: Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, intravascular platelet aggregation, and consumption resulting in **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)** and **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Premonitory (Alarming) Symptoms of Imminent Eclampsia

Before convulsive fits occur, patients exhibit warning symptoms of acute fulminant pre-eclampsia:

- **Severe Persistent Headache**: Located over frontal or occipital regions, unresponsive to standard analgesics.
- **Visual Disturbances**: Blurring of vision, scotomata, photopsia, diplopia, or sudden cortical blindness.
- **Epigastric or Right Upper Quadrant Pain**: Acute severe pain caused by stretching of Glisson's capsule from hepatic subcapsular edema/hematoma.
- **Oliguria**: Urinary output <400 mL in 24 hours.

#### B. Four Clinical Stages of an Eclamptic Fit (Grand Mal Seizure) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Premonitory Stage (lasts ≈30 seconds)**:
    - Patient loses consciousness.
    - Involuntary twitching of facial muscles, eyelids, tongue, and upper extremity muscles.
    - Eyeballs roll upward or turn laterally and become fixed.
2. **Tonic Stage (lasts ≈30 seconds)**:
    - The entire body undergoes severe tonic muscular spasm: trunk in opisthotonus posture, limbs flexed, hands clenched into fists.
    - Respiration completely ceases due to spasm of intercostal muscles and diaphragm, producing cyanosis.
    - Jaw firmly closes; tongue protrudes between teeth.
3. **Clonic Stage (lasts 1 to 4 minutes)**:
    - All voluntary muscles undergo rapid, alternating violent contractions and relaxations.
    - Convulsive twitching starts in the face, spreads to extremities, and involves the whole body.
    - **Severe tongue bite injury** occurs as the jaw snaps shut (Diagram 1).
    - Respiration resumes with deep, stertorous gasping; blood-stained frothy mucus exudes from mouth and nose. Cyanosis gradually clears.
4. **Stage of Coma (variable duration)**:
    - Following the fit, the patient passes into a deep comatose state.
    - Coma may last for minutes to hours; when multiple seizures occur in rapid succession, it is termed **status eclampticus**.
    - On regaining consciousness, the patient exhibits confusion and total anterograde amnesia of the event.
    - Hyperpyrexia (>102°F / >38.9°C), tachycardia (>120/min), tachypnea, and severe hypertension persist.

### 6. Diagnosis & Diagnostic Workup [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Diagnostic Criteria for Eclampsia

- Presence of grand mal generalized tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma in a pregnant woman with hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) and proteinuria/end-organ damage after 20 weeks of gestation.

#### B. Essential Laboratory Investigations

- **Complete Blood Count & Peripheral Blood Smear**: Hemoconcentration (elevated hematocrit), **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)**, and schistocytes/burr cells (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia in HELLP).
- **Renal Function Tests**:
    - Serum Uric Acid **>4.5 mg/dL** (classic hallmark correlating with disease severity).
    - Serum Creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**.
    - Blood Urea and Electrolytes.
- **Urinalysis**: 24-hour urine collection for protein (≥0.3 g/24 hr) or urine dipstick (≥2+ to 3+).
- **Liver Function Tests**: Serum AST, ALT, and LDH **≥2 times upper limit of normal**, serum bilirubin >1.2 mg/dL.
- **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT), APTT, Fibrinogen (<150 mg/dL), and D-dimer (done to rule out DIC).

#### C. Neuro-imaging (MRI / CT Brain)

- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Brain)**: **Investigation of choice** for atypical presentations, focal neurological deficits, persistent blindness, or prolonged coma.
- **Key Finding**: Hyperintense T2/FLAIR lesions demonstrating bilateral **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** in parieto-occipital subcortical white matter.

#### STEP 1: Emergency General & Nursing Care (Airway & Safety) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Call for Help**: Mobilize obstetrician, anesthetist, neonatologist, and intensive care team.
- **Airway & Resuscitation (ABC)**:
    - Turn patient to **left lateral position** to prevent aspiration and relieve inferior vena cava compression.
    - Insert a padded **mouth gag** between teeth during premonitory/tonic stage to prevent tongue bite (do NOT force open clenched teeth during tonic contraction).
    - Clear air passages by suctioning blood-stained frothy secretions.
    - Administer **100% Oxygen via face mask** at 8–10 L/min.
- **Prevent Maternal Injury**: Raise padded bed side-rails to prevent fall from bed; avoid rigid physical restraint.
- **Catheterization**: Insert a Foley catheter connected to a urometer to monitor hourly urine output.

#### STEP 2: Anticonvulsant Therapy — Drug of Choice: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)** is the **gold-standard drug of choice** to control eclamptic fits and prevent recurrence (superior to Diazepam, Phenytoin, or Lytic Cocktail).

##### A. Pritchard Intramuscular (IM) Regimen (Widely Followed in India) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose (Total 14 g MgSO₄)**:
    - **IV Loading Dose**: **4 g MgSO₄** (8 mL of 50% solution diluted with 12 mL Normal Saline to yield **20% concentration**) given **IV slowly over 5–10 minutes** (at 1 g/minute).
    - **IM Loading Dose**: **10 g MgSO₄** (5 g of 50% solution given deep IM into the upper outer quadrant of **each buttock** using a 3-inch long needle).
- **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g MgSO₄ (50% solution) deep IM every 4 hours** in alternate buttocks.
- **Duration**: Continued for **24 hours after delivery OR 24 hours after the last eclamptic convulsion**, whichever is later.

##### B. Zuspan / Sibai Intravenous (IV) Regimen (ACOG Recommended) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose**: **4 to 6 g MgSO₄ IV** diluted in 100 mL IV fluid administered over 15–20 minutes.
- **Maintenance Dose**: **1 to 2 g/hour** continuous IV infusion.

##### C. Mandatory Pre-Requisite Clinical Checks Before Each Maintenance Dose [⭐ High-Yield]

Before administering each 4-hourly maintenance dose of MgSO₄, verify these **3 mandatory clinical parameters**:

1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute** (or ≥16/min).
3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL in 4 hours) over the preceding 4 hours.

##### D. Serum Therapeutic Level & Toxicity Management [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Normal Plasma Level**: 1.5–2.2 mEq/L.
- **Therapeutic Range**: **4 to 7 mEq/L** (or 4.8–8.4 mg/dL).
- **Toxicity Progression**:
    - **7–10 mEq/L**: Loss of patellar tendon reflexes (earliest clinical sign of toxicity).
    - **12 mEq/L**: Respiratory depression.
    - **15 mEq/L**: Cardiac conduction block / arrhythmias.
    - **24 mEq/L**: Cardiac arrest.
- **Antidote for MgSO₄ Toxicity**: **Injection Calcium Gluconate 10% — 10 mL IV given slowly over 10 minutes**.

#### STEP 3: Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when persistent BP is **≥160/110 mmHg**.
- **Target Blood Pressure**: SBP **140–150 mmHg** and DBP **90–100 mmHg** (avoid over-rapid reduction to prevent placental hypoperfusion).
- **First-Line Antihypertensive Regimens**:
    - **Inj. Labetalol**: 20 mg IV bolus over 2 minutes; repeat 40–80 mg IV at 10-minute intervals up to a maximum cumulative dose of **300 mg**.
    - **Inj. Hydralazine**: 5 to 10 mg IV diluted in 10 mL NS over 20 minutes; repeat 5–10 mg IV every 20–30 minutes up to maximum **20 mg**.
    - **Tab. Nifedipine**: 10 mg PO (swallowed whole, not sublingual); repeat 10–20 mg PO after 30 minutes if needed.

#### STEP 4: Definitive Obstetric Management (Termination of Pregnancy) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden Rule**: **Delivery is the only definitive cure for eclampsia.** Once eclamptic fits are controlled and maternal BP is stabilized, delivery MUST be expedited without unnecessary delay.
- **Time Target**: Delivery should ideally be achieved **within 6 to 8 hours** of the onset of eclamptic fits.
- **Gestational Age Considerations at 36 Weeks (Late Preterm)**:
    - At **36 weeks of gestation**, fetal pulmonary maturity is advanced; expectant management is contraindicated in eclampsia.

##### Management in Intrapartum Labor at 36 Weeks:

1. **Favorable Cervix (Bishop Score ≥6) & Reassuring Fetal Status**:
    - Proceed with **Vaginal Delivery**.
    - Perform **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Low Rupturing)** combined with **Oxytocin infusion** to accelerate labor progress.
    - Maintain continuous intrapartum electronic fetal monitoring (EFM).
    - Shorten the 2nd stage of labor using **Pudendal block and Forceps / Ventouse extraction** to avoid maternal bearing-down exertion.
2. **Unfavorable Cervix (Bishop Score <6) OR Fetal Distress OR Uncontrolled Convulsions**:
    - Perform prompt **Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS)** under neuraxial (epidural/spinal) or general anesthesia.

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neurological**: Cerebral hemorrhage (leading cause of death), PRES, cerebral infarction, cerebral edema, tentorial tears, and transient cortical blindness.
- **Cardiopulmonary**: Acute Left Ventricular Failure (LVF), pulmonary edema, aspiration pneumonia, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS).
- **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN), oliguria, and cortical necrosis.
- **Hepatic**: HELLP syndrome, subcapsular liver hematoma, liver rupture, and jaundice.
- **Obstetric & Coagulation**: **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)** (15%), Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Traumatic**: Severe tongue bite injury (Diagram 1), fractures, and bed sores.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR) and severe oligohydramnios.
- Intrapartum fetal hypoxia, asphyxia, and meconium aspiration.
- Preterm delivery and complications of prematurity (RDS, IVH, NEC).
- **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth** and high perinatal mortality.

---

### 11. Prognosis & Poor Prognostic Indicators [⭐ High-Yield]

Maternal mortality in eclampsia ranges between 2% and 20% in developing regions. Eden's criteria outline the major poor prognostic factors in eclampsia:

1. Late hospital referral (delay in initiating anticonvulsant treatment).
2. High number of eclamptic fits (**>10 fits**).
3. Deep persistent coma between convulsions.
4. Hyperpyrexia (>102°F / >38.9°C) and persistent pulse rate >120/minute.
5. SBP **>200 mmHg**.
6. Presence of pulmonary edema or jaundice.
7. Anuria or severe oliguria (<400 mL/24 hours) with heavy proteinuria (>5 g/24 hours).

---

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Delivery Rule in Eclampsia**: Delivery is the only definitive treatment for eclampsia. Once eclamptic fits are controlled with MgSO₄ and blood pressure is stabilized with IV antihypertensives, **delivery MUST be carried out within 6 to 8 hours**, regardless of the period of gestation.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Treating eclamptic convulsions with standard antiepileptic drugs (e.g., Phenytoin or Diazepam) or administering sublingual Nifedipine for acute hypertension.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) is the drug of choice** for eclampsia (Phenytoin and Diazepam have higher seizure recurrence rates and maternal mortality). **Sublingual Nifedipine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** due to the risk of precipitous uncontrolled hypotension, cerebral infarction, and fetal distress; Nifedipine must always be swallowed whole.

- **U**: **Urine output must be ≥30 mL/hour** before every 4-hourly IM maintenance dose

### Quick Revision

- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension**: New BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks gestation.
- **Eclampsia**: Pre-eclampsia + generalized tonic-clonic seizures and/or coma.
- **Four Seizure Stages**: Premonitory (30s) → Tonic (30s) → Clonic (1–4 mins with tongue bite) → Coma.
- **Pritchard Regimen Dose**: Loading = 4 g IV (20%) + 10 g IM (50%); Maintenance = 5 g IM 4-hourly in alternate buttocks.
- **MgSO₄ Toxicity Checks**: Knee jerk present, RR ≥12/min, Urine output ≥30 mL/hr. Antidote = 10 mL 10% Calcium Gluconate IV.
- **Definitive Delivery in Intrapartum Eclampsia at 36 Weeks**: Deliver within 6–8 hours. Favorable cervix → ARM + Oxytocin; Unfavorable / Fetal distress → LSCS.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Eclampsia is an acute, life-threatening obstetric emergency defined as the occurrence of grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions or coma in a pre-eclamptic pregnant woman, driven by defective spiral artery remodelling, systemic endothelial dysfunction, and vasogenic cerebral edema (PRES), requiring immediate airway stabilization, Magnesium Sulfate seizure control, IV antihypertensives, and prompt delivery within 6 to 8 hours.**
