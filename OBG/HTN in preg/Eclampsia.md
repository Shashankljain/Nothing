### 1. Definition & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition

- **Eclampsia**: Defined as the occurrence of **new-onset generalized grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma** in a woman with pre-eclampsia (or gestational hypertension), in the absence of any other identifiable neurological or medical cause (e.g., epilepsy, meningitis, or cerebral venous thrombosis).

#### B. Classification by Timing of Onset [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Antepartum Eclampsia (50%)**: Convulsions occur during pregnancy prior to the onset of labor (most common type, carries worst maternal-fetal prognosis).
- **Intrapartum Eclampsia (30%)**: Convulsions occur during the course of labor.
- **Postpartum Eclampsia (20%)**: Convulsions occur after delivery, typically within the first 48 hours postpartum.

### 2. Four Clinical Stages of an Eclamptic Convulsion [⭐ High-Yield]

An eclamptic seizure progresses sequentially through four distinct clinical stages:

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │   Four Stages of Eclamptic Convulsion  │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│  1. Premonitory Stage   │               │     2. Tonic Stage      │               │     3. Clonic Stage     │
│       (≈30 Seconds)     │               │       (≈30 Seconds)     │               │     (1 to 4 Minutes)    │
│ • Loss of consciousness │               │ • Generalized tonic     │               │ • Alternate contraction │
│ • Facial & limb twitching│              │   spasm & opisthotonus  │               │   and relaxation        │
│ • Eyeballs roll & fix   │               │ • Cessation of respiration│              │ • Severe tongue biting  │
└─────────────────────────┘               │   and cyanosis          │               │ • Stertorous breathing  │
                                          └─────────────────────────┘               │   & frothy bloody saliva│
                                                                                    └────────────┬────────────┘
                                                                                                 │
                                                                                                 ▼
                                                                                    ┌─────────────────────────┐
                                                                                    │    4. Stage of Coma     │
                                                                                    │ (Variable Duration)     │
                                                                                    │ • Deep coma following   │
                                                                                    │   fit; stertorous respiration│
                                                                                    └─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Premonitory Stage (≈30 seconds)**: Sudden loss of consciousness, twitching of facial muscles, eyelids, and upper extremity muscles; eyeballs roll upward and fix.
2. **Tonic Stage (≈30 seconds)**: Generalized tonic muscular spasm with opisthotonus posture; respiration ceases due to spasm of intercostal muscles and diaphragm, producing cyanosis; jaw firmly closes.
3. **Clonic Stage (1 to 4 minutes)**: Rapid, alternating violent contractions and relaxations of all voluntary muscles; **severe tongue bite injury** occurs as jaws snap shut; deep stertorous gasping resumes with blood-stained frothy mucus exuding from mouth.
4. **Stage of Coma (variable duration)**: Deep comatose state following the fit; patient regains consciousness with complete amnesia of the event. Multiple rapid seizures without intervening consciousness is termed **status eclampticus**.

### 4. Complications of Eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

Eclampsia affects virtually every major organ system, resulting in severe maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality:

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neurological**: Cerebral hemorrhage (leading cause of death), Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES), cerebral edema, tentorial tears, and transient cortical blindness.
- **Cardiopulmonary**: Acute Left Ventricular Failure (LVF), pulmonary edema, aspiration pneumonia, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), and cardiopulmonary arrest.
- **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN), oliguria (<400 mL/24 hr), and anuria.
- **Hepatic**: HELLP syndrome (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets), subcapsular liver hematoma, liver rupture, and jaundice.
- **Obstetric & Coagulation**: **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH), and puerperal sepsis.
- **Traumatic**: Severe tongue bite injury, fractures due to fall from bed, and bed sores.
- **Maternal Mortality**: Overall maternal mortality ranges between 2% and 20%.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR) due to chronic placental insufficiency.
- Spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm labor and prematurity complications.
- Intrapartum fetal hypoxia, severe birth asphyxia, and low Apgar scores.
- **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth** and high perinatal mortality.

### 5. Diagnosis & Diagnostic Investigations

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Platelet Count**: Detects hemoconcentration and thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL).
2. **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Identifies **schistocytes / burr cells** (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia in HELLP syndrome).
3. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL** (correlates directly with disease severity).
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL** (indicates renal impairment).
4. **Urinalysis**: 24-hour urine protein collection (≥0.3 g/24 hr) or urine dipstick (≥2+ to 3+).
5. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**: Serum transaminases (AST/SGOT and ALT/SGPT) elevated to ≥2× normal (>70 U/L) and serum LDH **>600 U/L**.
6. **Coagulation Profile**: PT, APTT, Fibrinogen (<150 mg/dL), and D-dimers (to rule out DIC).
7. **Neuro-imaging (MRI / CT Brain)**: **Investigation of choice** for focal neurological deficits, persistent blindness, or prolonged coma; reveals bilateral **parieto-occipital vasogenic edema (PRES)**.

#### STEP 1: Emergency Care & Stabilization (ABC)

- Position patient in **left lateral position** to prevent aspiration and relieve inferior vena cava compression.
- Insert a padded **mouth gag** between teeth during the premonitory/tonic stage to prevent tongue bite injury.
- Clear air passages by suctioning frothy blood-stained secretions; administer **100% Oxygen via face mask** at 8–10 L/min.
- Raise padded bed side-rails to prevent falls from bed. Catheterize with a Foley catheter to monitor hourly urine output.

#### STEP 2: Anticonvulsant Therapy — Drug of Choice: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)** is the **gold-standard drug of choice** to control eclamptic fits and prevent recurrence (Magpie Trial 2002).

##### A. Pritchard Intramuscular (IM) Regimen (Standard in India) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose (Total 14 g MgSO₄)**:
    - **IV Loading Dose**: **4 g MgSO₄** (20% solution given IV slowly over 5–10 minutes).
    - **IM Loading Dose**: **10 g MgSO₄** (5 g of 50% solution deep IM into upper outer quadrant of **each buttock**).
- **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g MgSO₄ (50% solution) deep IM every 4 hours** in alternate buttocks.
- **Recurrence of Fits**: Administer an additional **2 g IV bolus** (20% solution) slowly over 3–5 minutes.

##### B. Zuspan Intravenous (IV) Regimen [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Loading Dose**: **4 to 6 g MgSO₄ IV** in 100 mL fluid administered over 15–20 minutes.
- **Maintenance Dose**: **1 to 2 g/hour** continuous IV infusion.

##### C. 3 Mandatory Pre-Requisite Clinical Checks Before Maintenance Dose [⭐ High-Yield]

Before administering each 4-hourly maintenance dose, verify:

1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute**.
3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL over 4 hours).

- **Therapeutic Serum Level**: **4 to 7 mEq/L** (4.8–8.4 mg/dL).
- **Specific Antidote for MgSO₄ Toxicity**: **Injection Calcium Gluconate 10% — 10 mL IV given slowly over 10 minutes**.

#### STEP 3: Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when persistent SBP is **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP is **≥110 mmHg**.
- **Target Blood Pressure**: SBP **140–160 mmHg** and DBP **90–100 mmHg**.
- **First-Line Regimens**:
    - **Inj. Labetalol**: 20 mg IV bolus over 2 minutes; repeat 40–80 mg IV every 10–15 minutes up to max cumulative **300 mg**.
    - **Inj. Hydralazine**: 5 to 10 mg IV diluted in 10 mL NS over 20 minutes; repeat every 20–30 minutes up to max **20–30 mg**.
    - **Tab. Nifedipine**: 10 mg PO (swallowed whole, **NEVER sublingual**); repeat 10–20 mg PO after 30 minutes if needed.

#### STEP 4: Definitive Obstetric Management (Timing & Delivery Mode) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden Rule**: **Delivery is the only definitive cure for eclampsia.**
- **Timing of Delivery**: Once maternal BP and convulsions are controlled and stabilized, delivery MUST be expedited without delay, ideally **within 6 to 8 hours** of the fit, **irrespective of gestational age**.
- **Corticosteroids**: Administer **Betamethasone 12 mg IM (2 doses 24 hours apart)** if gestation is **<34 weeks**.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**: Preferred if cervix is favorable (Bishop score ≥6). Performed via **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Amniotomy) + Oxytocin infusion**. If cervix is unfavorable, perform cervical ripening with **PGE₂ gel 0.5 mg** intracervically. Shorten the 2nd stage using **pudendal block and forceps / ventouse extraction**.
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Indicated for obstetrical reasons (malpresentation, CPD), unfavorable cervix in an unstable mother, or severe fetal distress. **Neuraxial (epidural/spinal) anesthesia** is preferred over general anesthesia.
- **Postpartum Duration**: Continue prophylactic **MgSO₄ for 24 hours postpartum OR 24 hours after the last eclamptic seizure**, whichever is later.

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Delivery Timeline in Eclampsia**: Delivery is the only definitive treatment for eclampsia. Once eclamptic fits are arrested with MgSO₄ and BP is stabilized with IV antihypertensives, **delivery MUST be achieved within 6 to 8 hours**, regardless of the period of gestation.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Treating eclamptic fits with standard antiepileptic drugs (Phenytoin or Diazepam) or administering sublingual Nifedipine for severe hypertension.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) is the drug of choice** for eclampsia; Phenytoin and Diazepam have higher seizure recurrence rates and maternal mortality.
    - **Sublingual Nifedipine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** due to the risk of precipitous uncontrolled hypotension, cerebral infarction, and acute fetal distress; Nifedipine must always be swallowed whole.

### Quick Revision

- **Eclampsia Definition**: Pre-eclampsia + generalized grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma.
- **Seizure Stages**: Premonitory (30s) → Tonic (30s) → Clonic (1–4 mins with tongue bite) → Coma.
- **Drug of Choice**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hourly).
- **MgSO₄ Toxicity Checks**: Knee jerk present, RR ≥12/min, Urine output ≥30 mL/hr. Antidote: 10 mL 10% Calcium Gluconate IV.
- **Obstetric Management**: Expedite delivery within **6 to 8 hours** irrespective of gestational age once stabilized. Vaginal delivery preferred (ARM + Oxytocin); continue MgSO₄ for 24 hours postpartum.
