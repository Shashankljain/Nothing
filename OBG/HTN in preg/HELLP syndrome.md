# HELLP Syndrome: Definition, Pathophysiology, Diagnostic Criteria, and Management at 32 Weeks [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition of HELLP Syndrome

- **HELLP Syndrome**: HELLP is an acronym for **H**emolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, and **L**ow **P**latelet count. It represents a severe, life-threatening multisystem variant and complication of severe pre-eclampsia, driven by microvascular endothelial activation, intravascular fibrin deposition, and liver parenchymal necrosis.
- **Incidence & Special Features**:
    - Occurs in **10–15%** of women with severe pre-eclampsia.
    - Develops predominantly in the third trimester (70% antepartum) or within 48 hours postpartum (30%).
    - **Atypical Presentation**: Up to **15–20%** of patients with HELLP syndrome may present without significant hypertension or proteinuria.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Risk Factors

- **Multigravida Patients**: More common in multiparous women aged >25 years.
- **Prior History**: Previous pre-eclampsia or HELLP syndrome in prior pregnancy (recurrence risk 3–19%).
- **Underlying Conditions**: Antiphospholipid Antibody Syndrome (APSA), inherited thrombophilias (Factor V Leiden mutation), pre-existing vascular or chronic renal disease.
- **Defective Placentation**: Abnormal endovascular trophoblastic invasion and systemic release of anti-angiogenic factors (sFlt-1, sEng).

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Tennessee Diagnostic Criteria (Broadly Accepted) [⭐ High-Yield]

All 3 criteria must be present for a definitive diagnosis of complete HELLP syndrome:

1. **Hemolysis**: Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia with abnormal peripheral blood smear (**schistocytes, burr cells, helmet cells**), serum LDH **>600 U/L**, and total serum bilirubin **≥1.2 mg/dL**.
2. **Elevated Liver Enzymes**: Serum AST (SGOT) and ALT (SGPT) elevated to **≥2 times upper limit of normal** (>70 U/L).
3. **Low Platelet Count**: Thrombocytopenia with platelet count **<100,000/μL** (<1 lakh/mm³).

#### B. Mississippi Triple-Class Classification (Based on Platelet Nadir) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Class 1 (Severe)**: Platelets **≤50,000/μL**, AST/ALT ≥70 U/L, LDH >600 U/L (highest maternal-fetal morbidity).
- **Class 2 (Moderate)**: Platelets **>50,000/μL to ≤100,000/μL**, AST/ALT ≥70 U/L, LDH >600 U/L.
- **Class 3 (Mild / Partial)**: Platelets **>100,000/μL to ≤150,000/μL**, AST/ALT ≥40 U/L, LDH >600 U/L.

#### Core Mechanisms:

1. **Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia**: Endothelial damage leads to microvascular fibrin mesh deposition. Transiting RBCs undergo mechanical shear stress and fragmentation, forming **schistocytes and burr cells**.
2. **Hepatic Sinusoidal Obstruction**: Fibrin deposition in hepatic sinusoids causes periportal ischemic necrosis, elevation of hepatic transaminases (AST/ALT), and stretching of Glisson's capsule (causing severe epigastric pain).
3. **Consumptive Thrombocytopenia**: Widespread microvascular thrombosis consumes platelets, precipitating rapid thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL) and risk of Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) or Epigastric Pain (85–90%)**: Constant, severe aching pain due to Glisson's capsule stretch from subcapsular hepatic edema or hematoma.
- **Nausea and Vomiting (45–80%)**.
- **Malaise & Flu-like Symptoms (80%)**.
- **Visual Disturbances & Headache (30–60%)**: Blurring of vision, scotoma.
- **Jaundice (5–15%)**: Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

#### B. Signs

- **Right Upper Quadrant Tenderness**: Marked liver tenderness on palpation.
- **Hypertension**: SBP ≥140 mmHg or DBP ≥90 mmHg present in 80–85% of cases.
- **Petechiae, Purpura, or Mucosal Bleeding**: Indicates severe platelet depletion (<20,000/μL) or DIC.

#### A. Essential Laboratory Investigations

1. **Peripheral Blood Smear (Gold Standard for Hemolysis)**: Confirms presence of **schistocytes, burr cells, and helmet cells** (fragmented RBCs).
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Demonstrates **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)** and hemoconcentration.
3. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**:
    - Serum transaminases: **AST/SGOT and ALT/SGPT >70 U/L (≥2× upper limit of normal)**.
    - **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)**: Elevated **>600 U/L** (sensitive marker of cellular necrosis and hemolysis).
    - Total Serum Bilirubin: Elevated **≥1.2 mg/dL**.
4. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL**.
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL**.
5. **Coagulation Profile**: PT, APTT, Serum Fibrinogen (<150 mg/dL), and D-dimer to evaluate for Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

#### B. Imaging Studies

1. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) / CT Scan**:
    - Evaluates for **subcapsular hepatic hematoma**, parenchymal infarction, or acute hemoperitoneum resulting from liver rupture.
2. **Antenatal Fetal Surveillance Panel at 32 Weeks**:
    - Cardiotocography (NST), Biophysical Profile (BPP), and Umbilical Artery Doppler velocimetry to assess uteroplacental vascular resistance.

#### STEP 1: Emergency Hospitalization & Stabilization

- **Tertiary Care Transfer**: Patient must be managed in a High Dependency Unit (HDU) / Intensive Care Unit (ICU) under a multidisciplinary team (Obstetrician, Intensivist, Hematologist, Neonatologist).
- **Hemodynamic & Fluid Balance**:
    - Infusion of crystalloids (Ringer Saline) maintaining strict fluid balance to prevent pulmonary edema.
    - Plasma volume expansion using **5% Albumin** or Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) if hypoalbuminemia or DIC is present.
- **Platelet Transfusion Rules**:
    - Transfuse pooled platelets if platelet count is **<20,000/μL** (regardless of delivery mode) or **<50,000/μL** prior to surgical delivery (Cesarean section) or if active mucosa/wound bleeding is present.

#### STEP 2: Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis — Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Administer **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** for seizure prophylaxis, as HELLP syndrome carries a high risk of progression to eclampsia.
- **Loading Dose**: **4 g IV (20% solution over 5–10 min) + 10 g IM (50% solution, 5 g in each buttock)**.
- **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g IM (50% solution) 4-hourly** in alternate buttocks.
- **Monitoring Checks**: Knee jerk present, Respiratory Rate ≥12/min, Urine output ≥30 mL/hr. Antidote: **10 mL 10% Calcium Gluconate IV**.

#### STEP 3: Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when SBP is ≥160 mmHg and/or DBP is ≥110 mmHg to prevent intracranial hemorrhage.
- **Target BP**: SBP 130–140 mmHg, DBP 80–90 mmHg.
- First-line drugs: **IV Labetalol** (20 mg IV bolus every 10–15 min up to 300 mg), **IV Hydralazine** (5–10 mg IV every 20 min), or **Oral Nifedipine** (10 mg PO swallowed whole).

#### STEP 4: High-Dose Corticosteroids at 32 Weeks Gestation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mandatory Administration at 32 Weeks**:
    - **Fetal Benefits**: Accelerates fetal lung maturity, drastically reducing neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS), Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH), and Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC).
    - **Maternal Benefits**: High-dose corticosteroid therapy stabilizes endothelial membranes, reduces hepatic inflammation, increases maternal platelet count, and improves urinary output.
- **Regimen Options**:
    - **Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM**, 2 doses given 24 hours apart.
    - **Inj. Dexamethasone 10 mg IV**, every 12 hours for 2–4 doses until clinical recovery.

#### STEP 5: Timing & Mode of Delivery at 32 Weeks Gestation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden Rule**: **Delivery is the only definitive cure for HELLP syndrome.**.
- **Timing Decision at 32 Weeks**:
    - **Primary Approach**: **Immediate delivery after initial dose of corticosteroids and stabilization**, irrespective of gestational age.
    - **Selective Short-Term Delay (24–48 Hours)**: If maternal and fetal parameters are completely stable in a tertiary ICU, pregnancy may be delayed for **24 to 48 hours ONLY** to complete the 2-dose Betamethasone course for fetal lung maturity.
    - **Absolute Indications for Immediate Delivery Without Delay**:
        - Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
        - Subcapsular liver hematoma or liver rupture.
        - Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) or oliguria (<400 mL/24 hr).
        - Pulmonary edema or ARDS.
        - Eclampsia or imminent eclampsia.
        - Non-reassuring fetal status / Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity (REDV) on Doppler.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery**: Preferred if cervix is favorable (ripe) and gestation is ≥32 weeks. Induction with **ARM + Oxytocin**.
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Preferred mode of choice in majority of HELLP cases at 32 weeks due to unripe cervix, severe maternal compromise, or fetal distress.
    - **Anesthesia**: Neuraxial (epidural/spinal) anesthesia is safe **ONLY if platelet count is >70,000/μL**; general anesthesia is required if platelets are ≤70,000/μL or DIC is present.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) (20%)**.
- **Placental Abruption (16%)**.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) (7–15%)**.
- **Pulmonary Edema & ARDS (6%)**.
- **Subcapsular Hepatic Hematoma & Liver Rupture** (presents with sudden hypotension, right-sided chest/shoulder pain, and acute hemoperitoneum).
- **Eclampsia and Cerebral Stroke**.
- **Maternal Mortality**: Ranging from 1% up to **25%** in severe multi-organ dysfunction.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR).
- Spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm delivery.
- Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
- High Perinatal Mortality (5–60%) secondary to birth asphyxia and prematurity.

---

### 10. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- Maternal mortality is ~1–2% in specialized tertiary units, rising up to 25% with multi-organ failure.
- Recurrence risk of HELLP syndrome in subsequent pregnancies is **3–19%**, while overall pre-eclampsia recurrence is **25%**.

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Atypical HELLP Syndrome**: HELLP syndrome can develop in up to 15–20% of cases without overt hypertension or proteinuria. Any pregnant woman in the 3rd trimester presenting with severe epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, nausea, and malaise must have an immediate CBC, platelet count, and LFT panel to rule out HELLP syndrome.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing HELLP syndrome with Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP).
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **HELLP syndrome** presents with microangiopathic hemolysis (schistocytes), elevated LFTs, and marked **thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)** with normal blood glucose and near-normal PT/APTT initially.
    - **AFLP** presents with **profound hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL)**, severe early coagulopathy (prolonged PT/APTT), and hyperbilirubinemia with minimal or secondary thrombocytopenia.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: HELLP = **H**emolysis (schistocytes, LDH >600 U/L), **E**levated **L**iver enzymes (AST/ALT >70 U/L), and **L**ow **P**latelets (<100,000/μL).
- **Tennessee Criteria**: Schistocytes + LDH >600 U/L + AST/ALT ≥2× normal + Platelets <100,000/μL.
- **Management at 32 Weeks**: Admit to ICU, start **MgSO₄ prophylaxis**, control BP with **IV Labetalol**, give **Betamethasone (12 mg IM 24h apart)** for lung maturity, and **proceed to delivery**.
- **Indications for Immediate Delivery**: DIC, liver hematoma/rupture, AKI, ARDS, eclampsia, or severe fetal distress.
- **Platelet Transfusion**: Required if platelets <20,000/μL or <50,000/μL prior to Cesarean delivery.
