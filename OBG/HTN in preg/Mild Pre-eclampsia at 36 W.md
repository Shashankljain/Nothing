# Case Management: Mild Pre-eclampsia at 36 Weeks Gestation [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Diagnosis of Clinical Case [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Impression**: A 20-year-old primigravida at 36 weeks of gestation presenting with a blood pressure of 140/90 mmHg and urine dipstick proteinuria of 1+ is diagnosed as a case of **Mild Pre-eclampsia (Pre-eclampsia without severe features)**.
- **Justification**:
    1. **Blood Pressure**: SBP = 140 mmHg and DBP = 90 mmHg meets the diagnostic threshold for gestational hypertension (≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks) but remains below the severe range (<160/110 mmHg).
    2. **Proteinuria**: Dipstick 1+ corresponds to approximately 0.3 g/L (30 mg/dL) of protein in urine, establishing the diagnosis of pre-eclampsia.
    3. **Absence of Severe Features**: There are no documented symptoms of imminent eclampsia (severe headache, visual blurring, epigastric pain) or systemic end-organ damage (thrombocytopenia, renal failure, pulmonary edema).

---

### 2. Definition & Diagnostic Criteria [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pre-eclampsia (PE)

- **Definition**: Pre-eclampsia is defined as the new onset of hypertension accompanied by **proteinuria** OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**, arising after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    1. **Hypertension**: Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on two occasions at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation.
    2. **Proteinuria**: Urinary excretion of **≥0.3 g protein in a 24-hour urine collection**, or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio **>0.3 mg/mg**, or dipstick reading of **≥1+** (persistent 30 mg/dL) in clean-catch random samples.
    3. **End-Organ Dysfunction Criteria (In the absence of proteinuria)**:
        - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelets **<100,000/μL**.
        - **Renal Insufficiency**: Serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**.
        - **Impaired Liver Function**: Serum transaminases (AST/SGOT, ALT/SGPT) elevated to **≥2 times upper limit of normal**.
        - **Pulmonary Edema**.
        - **New-onset persistent cerebral or visual symptoms**.

---

### 3. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Risk Factors Identified in this Patient

- **Young Primigravida (20 Years Old)**: First-time maternal exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens) is a major predisposing factor.

#### B. General Risk Factors for Pre-eclampsia

- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive trophoblastic mass (multifetal pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, diabetes mellitus).
- **Maternal Age**: Extremes of reproductive age (<20 years or >35 years).
- **Family / Personal History**: Prior pre-eclampsia or family history in mother/sister.
- **Pre-existing Vascular or Medical Diseases**: Chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes, chronic kidney disease, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²), antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APSA), and inherited thrombophilias.

#### Core Mechanisms:

1. **Failure of 2nd Wave Trophoblastic Invasion**: Endovascular cytotrophoblasts fail to invade the myometrial segment of uterine spiral arterioles (16–18 weeks). The vessels remain narrow and muscular, causing chronic **uteroplacental ischemia**.
2. **Angiogenic Imbalance**: Ischemic placenta releases **sFlt-1** and **sEng**, which neutralize VEGF and PlGF, causing systemic maternal endothelial damage, vasoconstriction, increased vascular permeability, and microvascular thrombosis.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Signs Present in Mild Pre-eclampsia

- **Hypertension**: BP = 140/90 mmHg (SBP 140–159 mmHg or DBP 90–109 mmHg).
- **Proteinuria**: Dipstick 1+ (or 0.3 g/24 hours).
- **Pathological Edema**: Ankle/pedal edema that does not subside after 12 hours of bed rest, or rapid weight gain >4 lb/week in late pregnancy.

#### B. Symptoms of Imminent Eclampsia (To Rule Out at Admission) [⭐ High-Yield]

The patient must be specifically evaluated for premonitory alarming symptoms that signal disease progression toward eclampsia:

- Severe persistent frontal or occipital headache.
- Visual disturbances (blurring of vision, scotoma, diplopia, blindness).
- Epigastric or right upper quadrant abdominal pain (subcapsular hepatic stretch).
- Marked oliguria (<400 mL/24 hours).

#### A. Maternal Laboratory Baseline Tests

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Platelet Count**: To rule out hemoconcentration and thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL).
2. **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Checks for schistocytes/burr cells (microangiopathic hemolysis in HELLP syndrome).
3. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Normal <4 mg/dL; elevated **>4.5–5 mg/dL** indicates altered tubular reabsorption and disease progression.
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Normal <1 mg/dL; values >1.1 mg/dL indicate severe renal involvement.
4. **Urinalysis for Quantification**:
    - 24-hour urine protein estimation (gold standard) to confirm excretion ≥0.3 g/24 hr or urine protein-to-creatinine ratio >0.3 mg/mg.
5. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**:
    - Serum AST/SGOT, ALT/SGPT, and LDH (>600 U/L) to rule out hepatocellular injury and HELLP syndrome.
6. **Ophthalmoscopic Examination**: Evaluates retinal vessel caliber (arteriolar constriction).

#### B. Fetal Surveillance Panel

1. **Daily Fetal Movement Counting (DFMC / Kick Chart)**.
2. **Non-Stress Test (NST)**: Done once or twice weekly.
3. **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Done twice weekly.
4. **Ultrasonography (USG Biometry)**: Evaluates Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW), detects Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), and measures Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI).
5. **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler Velocimetry**: Checks for elevated placental vascular resistance (S/D ratio, presence of end-diastolic flow).

#### STEP 1: Setting of Care & Hospitalization Decision

- **Outpatient / Daycare or Brief Admission**: Patients with mild pre-eclampsia at 36 weeks can be managed on an outpatient / daycare basis or admitted briefly to a high-dependency unit to establish baseline laboratory values and confirm absence of severe features.
- **Rest**: Advise adequate rest in the **left-lateral posture** (minimizes aortocaval compression → increases renal blood flow → promotes diuresis; increases uteroplacental blood flow).

#### STEP 2: Maternal & Fetal Monitoring Schedule (36 to 37 Weeks)

- **Maternal Monitoring**:
    - Blood pressure measured at least twice daily.
    - Daily monitoring for premonitory symptoms (headache, epigastric pain, visual blurring).
    - Weekly laboratory testing: CBC, platelet count, LFT (AST, ALT), serum creatinine, serum uric acid, and 24-hour urine protein.
- **Fetal Monitoring**:
    - Daily Fetal Movement Counting (DFMC / Kick Chart).
    - Weekly Non-Stress Test (NST) and Biophysical Profile (BPP).
    - USG biometry for fetal growth, Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI), and Umbilical Artery Doppler velocimetry.

#### STEP 3: Antihypertensive & Drug Therapy Rules at 36 Weeks Mild PE

- **Antihypertensive Drugs**: **NOT routinely required** for mild pre-eclampsia when BP is <150/100 mmHg. Antihypertensive therapy (Oral Labetalol, Methyldopa, or Nifedipine) is initiated only if BP persistently reaches **≥150/100 mmHg** or severe range (**≥160/110 mmHg**).
- **Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis (MgSO₄)**: **NOT required** for mild pre-eclampsia. Magnesium Sulfate is reserved for severe pre-eclampsia, imminent eclampsia, or eclamptic convulsions.
- **Corticosteroids for Fetal Lung Maturation**: **NOT required at 36 weeks gestation**. Antenatal corticosteroids (Betamethasone) are indicated only at **≤34 weeks of gestation**.

#### STEP 4: Timing & Mode of Definitive Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden Rule**: **Delivery is the only definitive cure for pre-eclampsia.**.
- **Timing of Delivery for Mild Pre-eclampsia at 36 Weeks**:
    - The patient has reached **36 weeks gestation**. In mild pre-eclampsia with stable maternal-fetal parameters, pregnancy is allowed to continue under close surveillance until **37 completed weeks of gestation**.
    - **At 37 Completed Weeks**: Proceed with planned **Induction of Labor (IOL)**.
- **Indications for Immediate Delivery BEFORE 37 Weeks** (If disease progresses):
    - Development of severe hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg).
    - Appearance of imminent eclamptic symptoms (headache, epigastric pain, visual loss).
    - Laboratory deterioration: Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL), rising LFTs, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, or HELLP syndrome.
    - Fetal compromise: Non-reassuring NST, oligohydramnios, severe FGR, or reversed umbilical artery end-diastolic flow (REDV).
- **Mode of Delivery at 37 Weeks**:
    - **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**: Vaginal delivery is the method of choice.
    - **Cervical Priming**: If cervix is unripe (Bishop score <6), perform cervical ripening with **Prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) gel 0.5 mg** intracervically.
    - **Surgical Induction**: Perform **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Low Rupturing)** combined with **Oxytocin infusion** once cervix is favorable.
    - **Intrapartum Care**: Maintain continuous EFM. Shorten second stage of labor with prophylactic **forceps or ventouse extraction** if needed.
    - **Cesarean Delivery (LSCS)**: Reserved for standard obstetric indications (malpresentation, CPD, severe fetal distress, or failed induction).

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- Progression to severe pre-eclampsia, imminent eclampsia, or **Eclampsia (convulsions)**.
- **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)** (due to spiral artery thrombosis/atherosis).
- **HELLP Syndrome** (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets).
- Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Oliguria.
- Pulmonary edema.
- Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
- Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH).

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications

- Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR / FGR) due to chronic placental insufficiency.
- Oligohydramnios.
- Iatrogenic preterm delivery.
- Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
- Intrapartum fetal hypoxia and birth asphyxia.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Delivery at 37 Completed Weeks for Mild PE**: In mild pre-eclampsia (BP 140–159/90–109 mmHg, no end-organ damage), expectant management with intensive fetal-maternal surveillance is safe up to **37 completed weeks of gestation**, at which point delivery (IOL) is recommended to prevent maternal-fetal complications.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering antihypertensives, MgSO₄, or Betamethasone routinely for mild pre-eclampsia at 36 weeks.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Antihypertensives** are NOT required in mild PE unless BP is persistently ≥150/100 or ≥160/110 mmHg.
    - **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** is reserved for severe pre-eclampsia, imminent eclampsia, or eclampsia (NOT routine in mild PE).
    - **Betamethasone** is NOT needed at 36 weeks gestation (indicated only at ≤34 weeks).
