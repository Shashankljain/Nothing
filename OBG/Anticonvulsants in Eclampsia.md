### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition of Eclampsia

- **Eclampsia**: Defined as the occurrence of **new-onset generalized grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma** in a pregnant or postpartum woman with pre-eclampsia, in the absence of any other identifiable cause (e.g., epilepsy, meningitis, or cerebral venous thrombosis).

#### B. Primary Objective of Anticonvulsant Therapy

- The primary goal of anticonvulsant therapy in eclampsia is to **arrest active convulsions**, **prevent seizure recurrence**, and **protect maternal cerebral function** without causing significant respiratory or CNS depression in the mother or fetus.
- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)** is the **gold-standard drug of choice** for seizure control and prophylaxis in eclampsia and severe pre-eclampsia (Magpie Trial 2002).

- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Actions**:
    1. Blocks central **N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA)** glutamatergic receptors.
    2. Reduces presynaptic release of the excitatory neurotransmitter **glutamate**.
    3. Inhibits neuronal **calcium (Ca²⁺) influx**, acting as a natural calcium antagonist.
    4. Induces **cerebral vasodilation**, reducing cerebral ischemia and vasogenic edema.
- **Peripheral & Neuromuscular Actions**:
    1. Reduces presynaptic release of **acetylcholine (ACh)** at the neuromuscular junction.
    2. Reduces motor endplate sensitivity to acetylcholine, producing muscle relaxation.
- **Uteroplacental Vascular Action**:
    1. Decreases uterine vascular resistance and increases uteroplacental blood flow.
- **Unique Clinical Advantage**: Unlike Diazepam, Phenytoin, or Barbiturates, **Magnesium Sulfate DOES NOT depress maternal sensorium** or cause neonatal respiratory depression.

### 4. Magnesium Sulfate Regimens & Clinical Monitoring [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Standard Regimens

1. **Pritchard Intramuscular (IM) Regimen (Standard in India)**:
    
    - **Loading Dose (14 g Total)**:
        - **4 g IV**: Given as a **20% solution** (4 ampoules of 50% MgSO₄ [8 mL] diluted with 12 mL Normal Saline or sterile water) slowly over 5–10 minutes.
        - **10 g IM**: Given as a **50% solution** (5 g [10 mL] injected deep IM into the upper outer quadrant of **each buttock**).
    - **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g IM (50% solution) deep IM every 4 hours** in alternate buttocks.
    - **Recurrence of Seizure**: Administer an additional **2 g IV bolus** (20% solution) slowly over 3–5 minutes.
2. **Zuspan / Sibai Intravenous (IV) Regimen (ACOG Recommended)**:
    
    - **Loading Dose**: **4 to 6 g IV** diluted in 100 mL Normal Saline over 15–20 minutes.
    - **Maintenance Dose**: **1 to 2 g/hour** continuous IV infusion.

#### B. 3 Mandatory Pre-Requisite Clinical Checks Before Each Maintenance Dose [⭐ High-Yield]

Before administering every 4-hourly maintenance dose, the clinician must confirm all 3 parameters:

1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute** (or ≥16/min).
3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL over 4 hours).

#### C. Serum Magnesium Levels & Progressive Toxicity Spectrum [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Normal Plasma Magnesium**: **1.5 to 2.2 mEq/L**.
- **Therapeutic Range for Seizure Control**: **4 to 7 mEq/L** (4.8 to 8.4 mg/dL or 2 to 3.5 mmol/L).
- **Toxicity Progression**:
    - **7 to 10 mEq/L (>9 mg/dL)**: **Loss of Deep Tendon Reflexes (Patellar Reflex / Knee Jerk)** — _earliest clinical warning sign_.
    - **≥10 mEq/L (>12 mg/dL)**: **Respiratory Depression** and weakness.
    - **≥15 mEq/L (>17 mg/dL)**: **Altered Cardiac Conduction / Heart Block**.
    - **≥25 mEq/L (>30 mg/dL)**: **Cardiac Arrest** in diastole.

#### D. Management of Hypermagnesemic Toxicity [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Immediate Step**: **STOP Magnesium Sulfate administration**.
2. **Specific Antidote**: Administer **Injection Calcium Gluconate 10% — 10 mL IV given slowly over 10 minutes**.
3. **Supportive Care**: Administer 100% O₂ via face mask; if respiratory arrest occurs, perform endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation. Forced diuresis with IV fluids.

### 5. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Superiority of Magnesium Sulfate (Magpie Trial 2002)**: The landmark international Magpie Trial established that Magnesium Sulfate reduces seizure recurrence by >50% (9.4% vs 23.1%) and lowers maternal death rates (3% vs 4.8%) compared to Diazepam, Phenytoin, or Lytic Cocktail, without depressing maternal consciousness or neonatal Apgar scores.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Withholding the loading dose of MgSO₄ in an eclamptic patient with oliguria, OR administering 50% MgSO₄ directly via the IV route.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IMMEDIATELY on diagnosis of eclampsia, IRRESPECTIVE of renal status!** Renal function (urine output ≥30 mL/hr) is evaluated only prior to administering subsequent _maintenance_ doses.
    - **MgSO₄ for IV injection must NEVER exceed 20% concentration!** (Dilute 1 part 50% MgSO₄ with 1.5 parts sterile water/NS; e.g., 4 mL 50% MgSO₄ + 6 mL NS = 10 mL 20% solution) to prevent acute vascular collapse.
