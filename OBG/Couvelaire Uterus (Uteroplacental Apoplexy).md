### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Couvelaire Uterus (Uteroplacental Apoplexy)**: A severe pathological condition associated with severe concealed or mixed **abruptio placentae**, characterized by massive extravasation and intravasation of blood into the myometrium, beneath the visceral uterine peritoneum (serosa), and into the broad ligament.
- First described by the French obstetrician **Alexandre Couvelaire** in 1911.
- **Diagnostic Hallmark**: It is primarily an **intraoperative / gross pathological diagnosis** made during laparotomy (Cesarean section).

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Couvelaire uterus is an extreme manifestation of severe **concealed abruptio placentae**. Associated risk factors include:

- **Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy (10–50%)**: Pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, and chronic hypertension cause spiral artery vasospasm, endothelial damage, and decidual necrosis.
- **Sudden Uterine Decompression**: Rapid escape of liquor in polyhydramnios or delivery of the first twin in multiple gestation.
- **Abdominal Trauma**: Road traffic accidents, direct abdominal blows, or forceful external cephalic version.
- **High Parity (≥5) & Advanced Maternal Age (>35 years)**.
- **Thrombophilias & Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)**.
- **Substance Abuse**: Cocaine use and heavy maternal cigarette smoking (inducing placental vasospasm).

---

### 3. Pathophysiology & Mechanism [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Retroplacental Hematoma Formation**: Rupture of damaged, degenerate spiral arterioles in the decidua basalis leads to blood accumulation behind the placenta.
2. **High-Pressure Extravasation**: In concealed abruption, blood cannot escape through the cervix due to firm apposition of fetal membranes or head.
3. **Myometrial Intravasation**: High intrauterine pressure forces retroplacental blood to dissect between the smooth muscle fibers of the myometrium.
4. **Serosal & Subperitoneal Spread**: Blood percolates up to the outer serous coat, producing subperitoneal petechial hemorrhages, broad ligament hematomas, and bloody peritoneal fluid.
5. **Myometrial Dysfunction & DIC**: Extravasated blood causes mechanical distortion and tearing of muscle fibers, impairing post-delivery uterine contraction/retraction and triggering **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)** via tissue thromboplastin release.

### 5. Gross & Histopathological Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Naked Eye / Gross Appearance**:
    - The uterus exhibits a characteristic **dark port-wine, mottled, or dark bluish-purplish (bruised) appearance**.
    - Discoloration can be patchy or diffuse, typically initiating at the uterine cornua before spreading across the main body and placental site.
    - **Subperitoneal Petechial Hemorrhages**: Visible ecchymotic spots beneath the uterine serosa.
    - **Broad Ligament Involvement**: Extravasation of blood into the broad ligaments, often accompanied by dark serosanguineous fluid in the pouch of Douglas.
- **Microscopic Appearance**:
    - Widespread interstitial hemorrhage between myometrial smooth muscle bundles.
    - Edema, muscle fiber disorganization, and focal myofibrillar necrosis.

---

### 6. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- Severe, continuous, agonizing **abdominal pain**.
- Vaginal bleeding is continuous, dark red, and often minimal or absent in purely concealed cases (disproportionate to shock).

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Severe Shock & Pallor**: Shock is pronounced and **out of proportion to visible external blood loss**.
- **Uterine Examination**:
    - Uterus is **tense, tender, rigid, and woody-hard** on palpation.
    - Fundal height may be greater than expected for gestational age and progressively rising.
    - Fetal parts are difficult or impossible to palpate due to myometrial rigidity.
- **Fetal Status**: Fetal heart sounds (FHS) are usually **absent (fetal demise)** in severe concealed cases.
- **Intraoperative Sign**: Direct visual confirmation of a **dark port-wine bluish uterus** upon opening the peritoneal cavity during C-section.

---

### 7. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: Mainly clinical, based on the classic triad of a **woody-hard tender uterus, severe shock out of proportion to external bleeding, and absent FHS**.
- **Ultrasonography (TVS / TAS)**: Demonstrates a large retroplacental hyperechoic or heterogenous clot with concealed intra-uterine hemorrhage.
- **Coagulation Profile (DIC Screen)**:
    - **Serum Fibrinogen**: Reduced (<150 mg/dL; critical level <100 mg/dL).
    - **Platelet Count**: Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL).
    - **D-Dimer & FDPs**: Markedly elevated.
    - **Prothrombin Time (PT) & aPTT**: Prolonged.
- **Definitive Diagnosis**: **Laparotomy** (visualizing port-wine dark bluish discoloration of the uterus).

### 9. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Fundamental Management Principle

- **Couvelaire Uterus is NOT an absolute indication for Hysterectomy!**
- Once the baby and placenta are delivered and hypovolemia/DIC are corrected with blood products, the myometrium usually regains its tone and retracts effectively.

#### B. Emergency Resuscitation & DIC Correction

- **Airway, Breathing, Circulation (ABC)**: Oxygen inhalation and continuous vitals monitoring.
- **Fluid & Blood Component Therapy**:
    - Establish two large-bore IV lines (14G / 16G) and infuse crystalloids (Ringer's lactate).
    - **Transfusion Protocol**: Fresh Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) to correct anemia and hypovolemia.
    - **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)**: 4 units for every 6 units PRBC to replenish clotting factors.
    - **Cryoprecipitate**: Indicated if serum fibrinogen remains **<100–150 mg/dL**.
    - **Platelet Concentrates**: Transfused if platelet count drops **<50,000/μL**.
    - **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: 1 g IV over 10 minutes to inhibit hyperfibrinolysis.

#### C. Obstetric Management & Delivery

- **Expeditious Delivery**: Immediate delivery is mandatory to remove the source of tissue thromboplastin.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**: Preferred in severe concealed cases, presence of live fetus with distress, or an unfavorable cervix.
    - **Vaginal Delivery**: Favored if fetus is dead and maternal condition is stabilized, accelerated by early **Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM)** ± Oxytocin drip.

#### D. Intraoperative Atony Management (If PPH Occurs)

1. **Uterine Massage & Bimanual Compression**.
2. **Uterotonics**: Oxytocin drip, Inj. Methergine 0.2 mg IV/IM, Inj. Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) 250 μg IM, and Tab Misoprostol 800–1000 μg rectally.
3. **Uterine Compression Sutures**: B-Lynch or Hayman compression sutures.
4. **Surgical Devascularization**: Stepwise uterine devascularization (bilateral uterine artery ligation, utero-ovarian anastomotic ligation, or internal iliac artery ligation).
5. **Hysterectomy**: Performed **only as a last resort** if uncorrected myometrial atony leads to life-threatening, uncontrollable hemorrhage.

### 11. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Severe atonic bleeding due to intra-myometrial blood disruption.
- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**: Consumptive coagulopathy from tissue factor release.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Cortical Necrosis**: Driven by hypovolemic shock and serotonin-induced renal vasospasm.
- **Sheehan Syndrome**: Postpartum ischemic necrosis of the anterior pituitary gland.
- **Fetal Demise / IUFD**: Exceeds **50–100%** in severe concealed abruption.

---

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Couvelaire Uterus Does NOT Mean Hysterectomy**: Despite its alarming, bruised, dark port-wine appearance, the Couvelaire uterus usually regains myometrial contractility once hypovolemia and DIC are aggressively corrected. Conservatism with oxytocics and blood components saves the uterus in >90% of cases.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing the cause of shock in Couvelaire uterus with external blood loss alone.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: In Couvelaire uterus, **shock is severely out of proportion to visible external bleeding**because a massive volume of blood is concealed retroplacentally and extravasated within the myometrium and broad ligament.

### 14. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: C-O-U-V-E-L-A-I-R-E

- **C**: **Concealed abruptio placentae** (primary underlying cause).
- **O**: **Out of proportion shock** relative to visible external bleeding.
- **U**: **Uteroplacental apoplexy** (synonym).
- **V**: **Visceral subperitoneal ecchymoses** & broad ligament hematomas.
- **E**: **Extravasation of blood** between smooth muscle fibers.
- **L**: **Laparotomy diagnosis** (visualizing port-wine appearance).
- **A**: **Atony and PPH risk** post-delivery.
- **I**: **Is NOT an absolute indication for hysterectomy**.
- **R**: **Rigid, woody-hard, tender uterus** on abdominal palpation.
- **E**: **Emergency resuscitation** with PRBCs, FFP, Cryoprecipitate, and Oxytocics.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Massive intramyometrial blood extravasation in severe concealed abruption causing a dark port-wine uterus.
- **Diagnostic Sign**: **Dark port-wine / purplish bruised uterus** with subperitoneal petechiae seen on laparotomy.
- **Clinical Hallmark**: Severe continuous abdominal pain with a **woody-hard, tender uterus** and shock out of proportion to external bleeding.
- **Golden Management Rule**: **Hysterectomy is NOT routinely required**; myometrial contractility returns once blood loss and DIC are corrected.
- **Key Complications**: Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), DIC (consumptive coagulopathy), and acute renal cortical necrosis.
