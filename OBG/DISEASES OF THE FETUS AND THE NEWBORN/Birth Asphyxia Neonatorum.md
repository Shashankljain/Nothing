# Birth Asphyxia Neonatorum: Causes, Assessment, and Resuscitation [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Birth Asphyxia (Perinatal Asphyxia)**: A condition resulting from impaired placental or pulmonary gaseous exchange during labor or delivery, leading to progressive fetal/neonatal **hypoxemia**, **hypercarbia**, and **metabolic acidosis**.
- **Diagnostic Criteria (Essential AAP / ACOG Criteria)**:
    1. **Umbilical Arterial Blood Acidemia**: Cord blood pH <7.0 or base deficit ≥12 mmol/L.
    2. **Depressed Apgar Score**: Persistence of an Apgar score <5 at 5 minutes and 10 minutes.
    3. **Neurological Manifestations**: Clinical signs of **Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE)** (e.g., hypotonia, altered sensorium, early neonatal seizures within 12–24 hours).
    4. **Multiorgan Dysfunction**: Evidence of acute organ injury in the cardiovascular, renal, gastrointestinal, or hematological systems in the immediate neonatal period.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Approximately **90% of asphyxial events** originate in the antepartum or intrapartum periods as a consequence of uteroplacental insufficiency, while 10% occur postnatally.

#### A. Antepartum / Maternal Causes

- Maternal hypertensive disorders (pre-eclampsia, eclampsia) causing chronic uteroplacental insufficiency.
- Maternal hypotension or severe hemorrhage (antepartum hemorrhage - abruption/previa).
- Severe maternal anemia, cyanotic heart disease, or respiratory failure.
- Intrauterine infection (chorioamnionitis) and maternal fever.

#### B. Placental & Umbilical Cord Causes

- **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**: Sudden premature separation reducing gas exchange.
- **Umbilical Cord Accidents**: Cord prolapse, tight nuchal cord, cord compression, true knot of the cord, or vasa previa rupture.
- Placental infarction, calcification, or extensive thrombosis.

#### C. Fetal Causes

- Preterm labor (<34 weeks) and low birth weight (LBW).
- Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR).
- Fetal hydrops, congenital cardiac anomalies, or severe congenital infections.
- Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) with thick meconium aspiration.

#### D. Intrapartum & Delivery Causes

- Prolonged or obstructed labor.
- Tetanic uterine contractions induced by oxytocin hyperstimulation.
- Malpresentations (breech delivery, transverse lie).
- Traumatic instrumental deliveries (difficult forceps or ventouse extraction).
- Maternal administration of heavy sedatives or narcotic analgesics (e.g., Pethidine) close to delivery.

#### E. Postnatal Causes

- Severe Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS / HMD).
- Congenital cyanotic heart disease or persistent pulmonary hypertension (PPHN).
- Severe neonatal sepsis or airway obstruction.

### 3. Pathophysiology (Cascade Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

```

A[Impaired Gas Exchange: Hypoxia + Hypercarbia]:::primary --> B[Primary Apnea: Initial Rapid Breathing -> Apnea & Transient Bradycardia]:::warning
B --> C[Gasping Phase: Deep Involuntary Respiratory Efforts]:::warning
C --> D[Secondary Apnea: Cessation of Breathing, HR Drops <100 bpm, Flaccidity & Cyanosis]:::danger
D --> E[Switch to Anaerobic Metabolism -> Lactic Acid Accumulation -> Metabolic Acidosis pH <7.0]:::danger
E --> F[Loss of Cerebral Vascular Autoregulation -> Hypotension & Reduced Cardiac Output]:::danger
F --> G[Cellular Energy Failure -> Necrosis/Apoptosis -> Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy HIE]:::danger
G --> H[Delivery Room Resuscitation Protocol: T-ABC Steps + PPV + Chest Compressions]:::success
```

- **Primary vs Secondary Apnea**:
    - **Primary Apnea**: Initial oxygen deprivation causes rapid breathing followed by a temporary cessation of respiration and mild bradycardia. Responds to tactile stimulation and oxygen.
    - **Secondary Apnea**: Persistent hypoxia leads to gasping, followed by complete respiratory failure, progressive bradycardia, hypotension, and flaccidity. **Requires immediate Positive Pressure Ventilation (PPV)** to restore breathing.
- **Cerebral Blood Flow Redistribution**: Hypoxia initially triggers catecholamine release, redistributing blood flow to vital organs (brain, myocardium, adrenals) while sacrificing renal and splanchnic beds. Prolonged hypoxia causes failure of autoregulation, leading to diffuse cortical and subcortical ischemic brain injury.

### 4. Clinical Features & Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms & Physical Signs

- **Cardiovascular**: Persistent bradycardia (heart rate <100 bpm or <60 bpm), weak pulses, poor capillary refill (>3 seconds), hypotension.
- **Respiratory**: Apnea, irregular gasping respirations, or severe intercostal retractions.
- **Neurological**: Hypotonia ("floppy baby"), absent neonatal reflexes (Moro, sucking), altered sensorium (lethargy/coma), and focal or generalized seizures within 12–24 hours.
- **Integumentary**: Pallor, central cyanosis, or meconium staining of skin, cord, and nails.

### 5. Diagnosis & Assessment Tools [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Apgar Scoring System (Dr. Virginia Apgar, 1953) [⭐ High-Yield]

Assessed routinely at **1 minute** and **5 minutes** after birth (and repeated at 10, 15, and 20 minutes if depressed):

|Apgar Parameter|Score 0|Score 1|Score 2|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**A - Appearance (Color)**|Blue, pale completely|Body pink, extremities blue (Acrocyanosis)|Completely pink|
|**P - Pulse (Heart Rate)**|Absent|<100 bpm|>100 bpm|
|**G - Grimace (Reflex Irritability)**|No response|Slight grimace|Cough, sneeze, or cry|
|**A - Activity (Muscle Tone)**|Flaccid / Limp|Some flexion of extremities|Active movements, well-flexed|
|**R - Respiration (Breathing Effort)**|Absent (Apnea)|Slow, irregular, shallow|Good, strong crying|

- **Score Interpretation**:
    - **8–10**: Normal (No depression).
    - **5–7**: Mild depression.
    - **3–4**: Moderate depression.
    - **0–2**: Severe depression / Severe Birth Asphyxia.

#### B. Sarnat and Sarnat Clinical Staging of HIE [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Stage I (Mild)|Stage II (Moderate)|Stage III (Severe)|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Level of Consciousness**|Hyperalert, irritable|Lethargic, obtunded|Comatose, stuporous|
|**Muscle Tone**|Normal or slightly hypertonic|Hypotonia (flexor weakness)|Flaccid|
|**Pupils & Reflexes**|Mydriasis; exaggerated Moro|Miosis; weak Moro & sucking|Fixed/dilated; absent Moro|
|**Seizures**|Absent|**Common (within 12–24 hrs)**|Frequent / Status epilepticus|
|**EEG Pattern**|Normal|Low voltage / burst suppression|Isoelectric / flat|
|**Prognosis**|Good (100% recovery)|20–30% neurological deficit|High mortality (50–80%); severe sequelae|

#### C. Laboratory & Imaging Studies

- **Umbilical Cord Blood Gas**: Cord arterial pH <7.0, PaO₂ <30 mmHg, PaCO₂ >60 mmHg, Base Deficit >12 mmol/L.
- **Biochemical Profile**: Serum glucose (<40 mg/dL indicates hypoglycemia), serum calcium (<7 mg/dL), BUN, creatinine (prerenal/renal failure), hepatic transaminases.
- **Neuro-imaging & Neuro-monitoring**:
    - **Cranial Ultrasonography (CUS)**: Detects intraventricular hemorrhage and diffuse cerebral edema.
    - **Brain MRI**: Gold standard for evaluating hypoxic-ischemic basal ganglia and cortical injury.
    - **Amplitude-integrated EEG (aEEG)**: Evaluates background cerebral electrical activity.

### 7. Resuscitation Protocol (NRP Guidelines - AHA / AAP / NNF India) [⭐ High-Yield]

Neonatal resuscitation follows the standardized **T-ABC approach** (Thermoregulation, Airway, Breathing, Circulation). **Do NOT wait for the 1-minute Apgar score to start resuscitation!**


#### Step 1: Initial Steps (First 30 Seconds) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **T - Thermoregulation**: Place baby under a pre-heated radiant warmer (36.5–37.5°C). For preterm babies <28 weeks, wrap in a polyethylene plastic bag without drying.
- **A - Airway Positioning & Clearing**: Position the infant on their back with the neck slightly extended in the **"sniffing position"** (avoid hyperextension or hyperflexion).
    - Suction the mouth first, then the nose using a bulb syringe or suction catheter (negative pressure <100 mmHg) **only if secretions obstruct the airway**.
- **D - Dry & Stimulate**: Thoroughly dry the baby with warm towels and discard wet linen; provide gentle tactile stimulation (flicking the soles of feet or rubbing the back).

#### Step 2: Evaluation & Breathing (Positive Pressure Ventilation - PPV) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Assess respiration and heart rate (auscultate precordium for 6 seconds).
- **Indication for PPV**: Apnea, gasping respiration, or **heart rate <100 bpm**.
- **PPV Technique**:
    - Deliver PPV using a self-inflating bag (Ambu bag) with a well-fitting cushioned face mask covering the nose and chin.
    - **Ventilation Rate**: **40–60 breaths/minute** (mneumonic: _"Breathe, two, three, breathe..."_).
    - **Initial Inflation Pressure**: 20–25 cm H₂O (up to 30–40 cm H₂O for the first few breaths in term infants).
    - **Oxygen Concentration**: Initial FiO₂ = **0.21 (21% room air)** for term infants; **0.30 (30% O₂)** for preterm infants (<28 weeks), adjusted based on target pre-ductal pulse oximetry (SpO₂).
- **Ventilation Corrective Steps (MR. SOPA)**: If chest is not rising during PPV:
    - **M**: Mask readjustment
    - **R**: Reposition head (sniffing position)
    - **S**: Suction mouth and nose
    - **O**: Open mouth slightly
    - **P**: Pressure increase (by 5 cm H₂O increments up to 40 cm H₂O)
    - **A**: Alternate airway (Endotracheal intubation or laryngeal mask airway)

#### Step 3: Circulation (Chest Compressions) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Heart rate **<60 bpm** despite **30 seconds of effective PPV** that moves the chest.
- **Technique**:
    - **Two-Thumb Technique (Preferred)**: Thumbs placed over the lower third of the sternum (just below the intermammary line), with fingers encircling the torso supporting the back.
    - **Depth**: Compress the sternum to **one-third of the anteroposterior diameter** of the chest.
    - **Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio**: **3:1 ratio** (3 compressions coordinated with 1 ventilation = 90 compressions + 30 breaths = 120 events/minute).
    - Increase FiO₂ to **1.00 (100% Oxygen)** when chest compressions are initiated.
    - Perform compressions for **60 seconds** before re-evaluating heart rate.

#### Step 4: Medications & Volume Expansion [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Heart rate remains **<60 bpm** despite 60 seconds of coordinated chest compressions and 100% O₂ PPV.
- **Epinephrine (Adrenaline)**:
    - Concentration: **1:10,000 dilution** (0.1 mg/mL).
    - Route: Intravenous (IV) or Intraosseous (IO) via Umbilical Venous Catheter (UVC).
    - Dose: **0.02 to 0.03 mg/kg** (0.2 to 0.3 mL/kg of 1:10,000 solution). Endotracheal dose is higher (0.5 to 1.0 mL/kg) if UVC is not yet established.
    - Frequency: Repeat every **3–5 minutes** if HR remains <60 bpm.
- **Volume Expansion**:
    - Indication: Evidence of acute blood loss, pallor, poor perfusion, or shock.
    - Solution: **Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)** or cross-matched O-negative packed RBCs.
    - Dose: **10 mL/kg** IV over 5–10 minutes via UVC.

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Acute Multiorgan System Complications

- **Central Nervous System**: Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE), cerebral edema, intracranial hemorrhage (IVH/SAH), neonatal seizures.
- **Cardiovascular**: Cardiogenic shock, myocardial ischemia, papillary muscle dysfunction, severe hypotension.
- **Respiratory**: Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS), Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN), pulmonary hemorrhage.
- **Renal**: Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN), oliguria/anuria, renal vein thrombosis.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC), stress ulceration, ischemic intestinal perforation.
- **Hematological & Metabolic**: Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), thrombocytopenia, hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia, severe metabolic acidosis.

#### B. Delayed / Long-Term Neurological Sequelae

- **Choreoathetoid or Spastic Cerebral Palsy (CP)**.
- Epilepsy and recurrent seizure disorder (up to 30% cases).
- Mental retardation, intellectual disability, and microcephaly.
- Sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) and visual impairment.

---

### 9. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

Prognosis depends on the severity and duration of asphyxia, 5-minute and 10-minute Apgar scores, time to establish spontaneous respiration, and extent of multiorgan involvement.

- An Apgar score of **0–3 at 20 minutes** or persistent oliguria >24 hours predicts high mortality (>80%) or severe permanent neurological deficits.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 5-Minute Apgar Rule**: While the 1-minute Apgar score guides the immediate decision to initiate resuscitation, the **5-minute and 10-minute Apgar scores are far superior predictors of long-term neurodevelopmental outcome and infant survival**.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Delaying Resuscitation to Calculate the Apgar Score.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **NEVER delay the start of neonatal resuscitation to calculate the 1-minute Apgar score!**Resuscitation must begin instantly at birth based on the initial rapid assessment (gestational age, tone, breathing/crying, and heart rate). Apgar scores are assigned retrospectively while resuscitation is actively ongoing.

### Diagram 1: Correct Position of Head for Airway Management (Sniffing Position)

- **Annotation**: _"The two-thumb technique provides superior coronary and cerebral perfusion pressure compared to the two-finger method."_

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: T-A-B-C-D (Neonatal Resuscitation Steps)

- **T**: **Temperature / Thermoregulation** (Radiant warmer, warm towels, plastic wrap for preterms)
- **A**: **Airway** (Sniffing position, clear mouth then nose suction)
- **B**: **Breathing** (Bag-and-mask PPV for 30s if HR <100 bpm or apnea)
- **C**: **Circulation** (Chest compressions 3:1 ratio with 100% O₂ if HR <60 bpm)
- **D**: **Drugs** (IV Epinephrine 1:10,000 and Normal Saline volume expanders via UVC)

### Quick Revision

- **Diagnostic Acidemia Threshold**: Umbilical arterial cord blood **pH <7.0** and base deficit ≥12 mmol/L.
- **Resuscitation Trigger for PPV**: Apnea, gasping, or **HR <100 bpm**.
- **Resuscitation Trigger for Chest Compressions**: **HR <60 bpm** despite 30 seconds of effective PPV.
- **Compressions Ratio**: **3:1 ratio** (90 compressions + 30 breaths/min = 120 events/min).
- **Epinephrine Dose**: **0.1–0.3 mL/kg of 1:10,000 dilution IV** via Umbilical Venous Catheter.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Birth asphyxia neonatorum is a critical perinatal emergency characterized by impaired gas exchange leading to hypoxemia, hypercarbia, and umbilical cord arterial metabolic acidemia (pH <7.0), managed urgently via the standardized T-ABC Neonatal Resuscitation Protocol involving positive pressure ventilation, chest compressions, and epinephrine.**
