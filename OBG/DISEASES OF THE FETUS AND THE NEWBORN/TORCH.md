# TORCH Infections in Pregnancy: Etiology, Pathophysiology, Diagnosis, and Management [⭐ High-Yield]

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **TORCH Infections**: A collective acronym designating a group of congenitally acquired infectious pathogens that cross the placenta or lower genital tract to infect the fetus, causing abortion, intrauterine fetal demise (IUFD), fetal growth restriction (FGR), non-immune hydrops fetalis, and severe structural congenital anomalies.
- **Acronym Components**:
    - **T**: **Toxoplasmosis** (_Toxoplasma gondii_).
    - **O**: **Others** (Syphilis, Varicella Zoster Virus, Parvovirus B19, HIV, Hepatitis B/C, Zika Virus).
    - **R**: **Rubella** (German Measles Virus).
    - **C**: **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)** — _Most common congenital viral infection_.
    - **H**: **Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV-1 & HSV-2)**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Causative Agents & Transmission Routes

1. **Toxoplasmosis**: Intracellular protozoan _T. gondii_; transmitted via ingestion of cysts in raw/undercooked meat, unpasteurized milk, or oocysts from infected cat feces.
2. **Rubella**: Togaviridae family single-stranded RNA virus; transmitted via respiratory droplets.
3. **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**: Human Herpesvirus-5 (dsDNA virus); transmitted via bodily fluids (saliva, urine, cervical secretions), sexual contact, or transplacental route.
4. **Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV)**: dsDNA virus (HSV-1 oral/genital, HSV-2 predominantly genital); transmitted via direct contact with active genital lesions or viral shedding during vaginal birth.
5. **Others**: _Treponema pallidum_ (Syphilis), Varicella Zoster Virus (Chickenpox), Parvovirus B19 (Slapped-cheek disease), and Zika Virus.

#### B. Key High-Risk Factors

- Consumption of raw/undercooked meat or contact with stray cats/cat litter.
- Non-immunized status in women of childbearing age (for Rubella and Varicella).
- Occupational exposure in daycare centers or healthcare settings with young children shedding CMV.
- Multiple sexual partners, history of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), or active primary genital herpes lesions near term.

---

### 3. Classification & Gestational Timing Effects [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Gestational Timing Rule of Vertical Transmission

- **First Trimester Infection**:
    - **Lowest Vertical Transmission Rate** (~15–25%), BUT causes the **highest severity of fetal damage and structural malformations** because infection occurs during active embryonic organogenesis.
- **Third Trimester Infection**:
    - **Highest Vertical Transmission Rate** (~60–90%), BUT usually results in **milder or subclinical fetal manifestations** (e.g., SGA, neonatal organomegaly, or late-onset sensorineural hearing loss).
    - _Exception_: CMV damages fetal neural tissue across all trimesters.

### 5. Clinical Features & Pathognomonic Manifestations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Presentation

- Frequently **asymptomatic (80%)** or manifests as non-specific, mild flu-like illness (low-grade fever, malaise, myalgia, cervical lymphadenopathy, or maculopapular skin rash).

#### B. Fetal & Neonatal Congenital Syndromes [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Congenital Toxoplasmosis**:
    - Classic Clinical Triad: (1) **Chorioretinitis**, (2) **Hydrocephalus / Ventriculomegaly**, and (3) **Intracerebral / Diffuse Ring-like Calcifications**.
    - _Note_: Sensorineural hearing loss is typically ABSENT in toxoplasmosis.
2. **Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)** (Teratogenic insult <16 weeks):
    - Classic Gregg's Triad:
        - **Eye Anomalies**: Congenital cataracts, glaucoma, microphthalmia, salt-and-pepper retinopathy.
        - **Ear Anomalies**: **Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL)** — _Most common individual feature (90%)_.
        - **Cardiac Anomalies**: **Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)** and Pulmonary Artery Stenosis.
    - Dermal extramedullary hematopoiesis ("Blueberry muffin rash").
3. **Congenital Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**:
    - Classic Features: **Periventricular intracranial calcifications**, **Microcephaly**, **Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL — most common non-genetic cause)**, chorioretinitis, hepatosplenomegaly, and petechial rash.
4. **Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV)**:
    - Manifests in neonates as: (a) Localized Skin-Eye-Mouth (SEM) vesicles, (b) Central Nervous System disease (encephalitis, microcephaly, seizures), or (c) Disseminated multiorgan failure with high mortality.
5. **Other Pathogens**:
    - **Varicella**: Cicatricial skin scarring, limb hypoplasia, cataracts.
    - **Parvovirus B19**: Erythrocyte precursor destruction → severe fetal anemia, high-output cardiac failure, and **Non-Immune Hydrops Fetalis**.
    - **Syphilis**: Early: Rhinitis (snuffles), maculopapular rash, osteochondritis; Late: **Hutchinson's teeth**, saddle nose, saber shins, 8th nerve deafness.

### 6. Diagnosis & Diagnostic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Serological Testing (TORCH Panel)

- **IgM Antibody**: Indicates acute, recent, or active primary infection (appears within 1–2 weeks).
- **IgG Antibody**: Indicates past exposure and immunity if present alone.
- **IgG Avidity Test (Crucial Diagnostic Discriminator)**:
    - **High IgG Avidity**: Indicates primary infection occurred **>3–4 months prior** (reassuring; low risk to fetus in early pregnancy).
    - **Low IgG Avidity**: Confirms acute primary infection acquired within the preceding **3 months** (high risk of vertical transmission).

#### B. Invasive Prenatal Fetal Diagnosis (Gold Standard)

- **Amniocentesis**: Performed at **≥16–18 weeks** of gestation (at least 6–8 weeks following maternal primary infection).
- **Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) Analysis**: Quantitative PCR amplification of pathogen-specific DNA/RNA (e.g., _T. gondii_ B1 gene, CMV DNA, Rubella RNA) in amniotic fluid provides **100% positive predictive value** for fetal infection.

#### C. Ultrasonography (Targeted Level II USG / Neurosonogram)

- Serial USG performed every 2–4 weeks to detect fetal stigmata:
    - Ventriculomegaly, microcephaly, or intracranial calcifications (periventricular in CMV vs diffuse in Toxoplasmosis).
    - Hepatosplenomegaly, fetal ascites, echogenic bowel, or frank **Hydrops Fetalis**.
    - **Middle Cerebral Artery Peak Systolic Velocity (MCA-PSV) Doppler**: Assesses severity of fetal anemia in Parvovirus B19 infection.

#### A. Toxoplasmosis Management Protocol

- **Maternal Primary Acute Infection (Fetus Uninfected / Status Unknown)**:
    - Administer **Spiramycin 3 g/day PO** continuously until delivery to prevent transplacental parasite transfer.
- **Confirmed Fetal Infection (Positive Amniotic Fluid PCR / USG Anomalies)**:
    - Switch after 18 weeks to **Pyrimethamine (25–50 mg/day PO) + Sulfadiazine (1 g QID PO) + Folinic Acid / Leucovorin (10–25 mg/day PO)** to eliminate fetal tissue cysts and prevent bone marrow suppression.

#### B. Rubella Management

- No effective antiviral treatment exists.
- If primary maternal infection is documented **<12–16 weeks**, counsel parents regarding high risk (>50–90%) of severe Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS) and offer **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)**.
- **Prevention**: Administer live attenuated **MMR Vaccine** to non-immune women in the **postpartum period** (strictly contraindicated during pregnancy; advise delaying pregnancy for 1 month post-vaccination).

#### C. Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV) Management

- **Primary / Recurrent Genital Episode**: Administer **Acyclovir 400 mg PO TID** for 7–10 days.
- **Suppressive Therapy**: Administer **Acyclovir 400 mg PO TID** or **Valacyclovir 500 mg PO BD** from **36 weeks gestation** until delivery to reduce viral shedding and recurrences.
- **Delivery Decision**: **Elective Cesarean Section** is strongly indicated if active primary genital herpes lesions or prodromal symptoms are present at the onset of labor or membrane rupture to prevent catastrophic neonatal transmission.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal**: Severe psychological distress, risk of disseminated disease in immunocompromised mothers (e.g., varicella pneumonia, HSV hepatitis).
- **Fetal / Perinatal**: Miscarriage, severe FGR, non-immune hydrops fetalis, intrauterine fetal death (IUFD), premature delivery.
- **Long-term Neonatal Sequelae**: Microcephaly, severe intellectual disability, epilepsy/seizures, permanent sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL), blindness/chorioretinitis, and cerebral palsy.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Amniocentesis Timing Rule for TORCH PCR**: To avoid a false-negative amniotic fluid PCR result for TORCH pathogens (CMV, Toxoplasma, Rubella), amniocentesis MUST be performed at **≥16–18 weeks of gestation AND at least 6–8 weeks after the estimated time of primary maternal infection**. This delay allows sufficient time for the pathogen to infect the placenta, cross into the fetal circulation, replicate in fetal kidneys, and be excreted into the amniotic fluid.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing the **Pattern of Intracranial Calcifications** in Congenital Toxoplasmosis versus Congenital CMV.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Congenital CMV**: Produces **PERIVENTRICULAR intracranial calcifications** (due to subependymal germinal matrix necrosis around ventricles).
    - **Congenital Toxoplasmosis**: Produces **DIFFUSE / INTRACEREBRAL scattered ring-like calcifications**throughout the brain parenchyma.

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: T-O-R-C-H (Key Diagnostic & Clinical Distinctions)

- **T**: **Toxoplasmosis** — Cat litter / undercooked meat; **Diffuse calcifications + Chorioretinitis + Hydrocephalus**(No SNHL).
- **O**: **Others** — **Parvovirus B19** (Hydrops fetalis/anemia); **Syphilis** (Penicillin G Rx).
- **R**: **Rubella** — **Gregg's Triad** (Cataracts + SNHL + PDA); MTP indicated if <12–16 wks.
- **C**: **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)** — **Most common congenital infection**; **Periventricular calcifications + SNHL + Microcephaly**.
- **H**: **Herpes Simplex (HSV)** — Active genital lesions at term require **Elective C-Section**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Most Common Congenital Viral Infection**: **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**.
- **Most Teratogenic Infection**: **Rubella** (<12 weeks = >80–90% CRS risk).
- **Diagnostic Gold Standard for Fetal Infection**: **Amniocentesis PCR** performed at ≥16–18 weeks.
- **Calcification Distinction**: **CMV = Periventricular**; **Toxoplasmosis = Diffuse parenchymal**.
- **Key Treatment Regimens**: Spiramycin for maternal Toxoplasmosis; Benzathine Penicillin G for Syphilis; Acyclovir for HSV; Elective C-section for active genital HSV at term.
