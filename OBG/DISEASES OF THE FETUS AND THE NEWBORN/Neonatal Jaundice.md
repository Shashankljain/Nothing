### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neonatal Jaundice (Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia)**: Yellowish discoloration of the skin, sclera, and mucous membranes in a newborn infant caused by the accumulation of excess total serum bilirubin (TSB >5–7 mg/dL or >85–120 μmol/L) in tissues and plasma.
- **Incidence**: Clinical jaundice occurs in approximately **60–80% of term infants** and up to **80–90% of preterm infants** during the first week of life.
- **Clinical Distinction**: Visible yellow staining of tissues appears when total serum bilirubin exceeds **5–7 mg/dL**, whereas in adults, icterus becomes visible only when serum bilirubin exceeds **2 mg/dL**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Classification of Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

Neonatal jaundice is broadly classified based on physiological mechanisms, time of onset, and the chemical state of circulating bilirubin into **Physiological Jaundice**, **Pathological Unconjugated (Indirect) Hyperbilirubinemia**, and **Pathological Conjugated (Direct) Hyperbilirubinemia**.

#### A. Physiological Jaundice (Non-Pathological) [⭐ High-Yield]

Appears after **24 hours of life** (Day 2 or 3), peaks on Day 3–5 at **≤12 mg/dL** in term infants (≤15 mg/dL in preterm infants), and resolves spontaneously by **Day 7–10** in term infants (up to 14 days in preterm infants).

- **Causes & Physiological Mechanisms**:
    1. **Increased Bilirubin Production**: Higher circulating red blood cell (RBC) volume per kg body weight combined with a shorter fetal RBC lifespan (**80–90 days** vs 120 days in adults), releasing 35 mg of bilirubin per gram of hemoglobin broken down.
    2. **Deficient Hepatic Uptake**: Low intracellular transport proteins, specifically **Ligandin (Y-protein)**, inside hepatocytes.
    3. **Immaturity of Glucuronidation**: Developmental enzyme deficiency of **Uridine Diphosphoglucuronate Glucuronosyltransferase (UGT1A1)** in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum of liver cells.
    4. **Exaggerated Enterohepatic Circulation**: Sterile neonatal intestinal lumen rich in mucosal **β-glucuronidase**enzyme that deconjugates bilirubin, allowing reabsorption of fat-soluble unconjugated bilirubin back into portal blood.
    5. **Perinatal / Delivery Factors**: Birth trauma (cephalhematoma), maternal oxytocin infusion, delayed cord clamping, and mild intrapartum hypoxia.

---

#### B. Pathological Unconjugated (Indirect) Hyperbilirubinemia [⭐ High-Yield]

Characterized by onset **within the first 24 hours of life**, rapid rise in serum bilirubin (**>5 mg/dL/day or >0.2 mg/dL/hour**), TSB exceeding **12 mg/dL in term** or **15 mg/dL in preterm** infants, or persistence beyond 10–14 days.

1. **Hemolytic Causes (Increased RBC Destruction)**:
    
    - **Isoimmune Alloimmunization**:
        - **Rh Incompatibility (Anti-D alloimmunization)**: Rh-negative mother carrying Rh-positive fetus; causes severe hemolysis, _Icterus Gravis Neonatorum_, and _Hydrops Fetalis_.
        - **ABO Blood Group Incompatibility**: Mother Group O carrying fetus with Group A or B (IgG anti-A / anti-B antibodies cross placenta); commonest cause of day-1 jaundice.
        - **Minor Blood Group Incompatibilities**: Anti-Kell (K1), Anti-c, Anti-E, Anti-Duffy, Anti-Kidd.
    - **RBC Enzymopathies**: **Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency** (X-linked recessive; precipitated by oxidative stress/infection/drugs), Pyruvate Kinase deficiency.
    - **RBC Membrane Defects**: Congenital Spherocytosis, Hereditary Elliptocytosis.
    - **Extravascular Blood Extravasation**: Cephalhematoma, subgaleal hematoma, extensive cutaneous ecchymosis/bruising, intraventricular hemorrhage.
    - **Polycythemia**: Infant of Diabetic Mother (IDM), Twin-to-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS recipient twin), delayed cord clamping (>60 seconds).
    - **Infections & Sepsis**: Intrauterine **TORCH infections** (Toxoplasmosis, Rubella, Cytomegalovirus, HSV, Syphilis) and severe neonatal bacterial sepsis (_E. coli_, Group B Streptococcus) causing microangiopathic hemolysis.
2. **Non-Hemolytic Causes (Defective Conjugation / Clearance)**:
    
    - **Congenital Enzymatic Deficiencies**:
        - **Crigler-Najjar Syndrome Type I**: Autosomal recessive; complete absence of UGT1A1 enzyme; severe untreatable jaundice (TSB 20–45 mg/dL) leading to fatal kernicterus.
        - **Crigler-Najjar Syndrome Type II**: Autosomal dominant/recessive; partial UGT1A1 deficiency; responds to enzyme induction with Phenobarbitone.
        - **Gilbert Syndrome**: Autosomal dominant mutation in _UGT1A1_ promoter; mild, benign hyperbilirubinemia.
    - **Breast Milk Jaundice (Late-Onset)**: Appears between **Day 7–14** (TSB up to 20–30 mg/dL); caused by substances in maternal breast milk—specifically **3α, 20β-pregnanediol** and non-esterified free fatty acids—that competitively inhibit hepatic UGT1A1 enzyme activity.
    - **Breastfeeding Jaundice (Early-Onset)**: Appears on **Day 2–4**; caused by **inadequate breast milk intake/dehydration**, leading to delayed meconium passage and increased enterohepatic reabsorption of bilirubin.
    - **Metabolic & Endocrine Disorders**: Congenital Hypothyroidism (decreased hepatic glucuronidating maturation), Hypopituitarism.
    - **Increased Enterohepatic Circulation**: Intestinal obstruction (Duodenal atresia, Pyloric stenosis, Meconium ileus, Hirschsprung disease) slowing gut transit and accentuating β-glucuronidase activity.
    - **Drugs Displacing Bilirubin from Albumin**: Sulfonamides, aspirin, ceftriaxone, and free fatty acids increase free, unbound toxic unconjugated bilirubin.

---

#### C. Pathological Conjugated (Direct) Hyperbilirubinemia [⭐ High-Yield]

Defined as **direct (conjugated) bilirubin >2 mg/dL** (or >20% of Total Serum Bilirubin). **Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia is NEVER physiological** and indicates severe hepatobiliary disease. Direct bilirubin is water-soluble and excreted in urine.

1. **Extrahepatic Biliary Obstruction**:
    - **Extrahepatic Biliary Atresia (EHBA)**: Progressive idiopathic fibrotic obliteration of extrahepatic bile ducts; presents with persistent jaundice, **dark yellow urine**, and **clay-colored (acholic/pale) stools** beyond 2 weeks of life.
    - **Choledochal Cyst**: Congenital cystic dilation of the common bile duct.
2. **Intrahepatic Cholestasis & Neonatal Hepatitis**:
    - **Idiopathic Neonatal Hepatitis Syndrome**: Inflammation and giant cell transformation of hepatocytes.
    - **Congenital Infections**: TORCH spectrum (CMV, Rubella, Syphilis, Hepatitis B, Toxoplasmosis).
3. **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**:
    - **Galactosemia**: Deficiency of _Galactose-1-Phosphate Uridyltransferase (GALT)_; ingestion of milk lactose causes accumulation of toxic galactose derivatives, periportal hepatic cirrhosis, hepatomegaly, cataracts, and reducing substances in urine.
    - **α₁-Antitrypsin Deficiency**: Misfolded protein accumulation in hepatocytes.
    - **Cystic Fibrosis**: Inspissated secretions obstructing bile canaliculi.
    - **Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis (PFIC)** & Tyrosinemia.

### 4. Clinical Features & Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Signs

- **Cephalocaudal Progression (Kramer's Dermal Zones)**: Jaundice appears first on the face and progresses downward to the trunk, limbs, and finally palms and soles as serum bilirubin levels rise.
    - _Zone 1 (Head & Neck)_: TSB ≈ 5 mg/dL.
    - _Zone 2 (Upper Trunk to Umbilicus)_: TSB ≈ 10 mg/dL.
    - _Zone 3 (Lower Abdomen & Thighs)_: TSB ≈ 12 mg/dL.
    - _Zone 4 (Arms & Lower Legs)_: TSB ≈ 15 mg/dL.
    - _Zone 5 (Palms & Soles)_: TSB >15 mg/dL.

#### B. Systemic Features Based on Etiology

- **Hemolytic Disease (Rh/ABO)**: Rapidly developing jaundice within <24 hours, pallor, hepatosplenomegaly, and hydrops.
- **Sepsis / TORCH**: Sick infant, lethargy, poor feeding, purpura, petechiae, temperature instability.
- **Conjugated Cholestasis (Biliary Atresia / Galactosemia)**: **Dark urine (staining diapers yellow)**, **pale/clay-colored (acholic) stools**, hepatomegaly, failure to thrive.

### 5. Diagnostic Approach & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Serum Bilirubin Measurement**:
    - Total, Direct (conjugated), and Indirect (unconjugated) fractions.
    - **Direct Bilirubin >2 mg/dL** confirms conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.
2. **Hematological Workup**:
    - **Blood Grouping (ABO & Rh)** of mother and neonate.
    - **Direct Coombs Test (DCT)** on infant's cord blood (Positive in Rh/ABO isoimmunization).
    - **Complete Blood Count (CBC)** & **Reticulocyte Count** (>6% indicates active hemolysis).
    - **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Microspherocytes (characteristic of ABO incompatibility), fragmented RBCs/schistocytes (DIC/sepsis), or bite cells (G6PD deficiency).
3. **Specialized Diagnostic Tests**:
    - **G6PD Enzyme Assay**: Evaluated in unexplained severe hemolysis.
    - **Thyroid Function Test (TSH, Free T4)**: Rules out congenital hypothyroidism.
    - **Urine Test for Reducing Substances (Clinistix / Benedict's Test)**: Positive for galactose in Galactosemia (while glucose dipstick remains negative).
    - **Sepsis Screen**: Blood culture, CRP, Absolute Neutrophil Count.
    - **Abdominal Ultrasonography & HIDA Scan**: Visualizes "triangular cord sign" or absent gallbladder non-visualization of biliary excretion in Biliary Atresia.

### 7. Management Summary & Interventions [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Phototherapy (Blue Light 460–490 nm)**:
    - First-line non-invasive therapy for unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia.
    - **Mechanisms**: Converts toxic unconjugated bilirubin into water-soluble **lumirubin** (via structural isomerization) and **4Z,15E photoisomers**, excreted in bile and urine without requiring hepatic conjugation.
2. **Exchange Transfusion (Double Volume = 160 mL/kg)**:
    - Indicated when TSB approaches critical neurotoxic thresholds (**>20 mg/dL** in term infants) or rapidly rises at **>1 mg/dL/hour** despite intensive phototherapy. Replaces 85% of circulating sensitized fetal RBCs and removes 50% of circulating bilirubin.
3. **Surgical Intervention**:
    - **Kasai Portoenterostomy**: Performed before **60 days of age** for Biliary Atresia to re-establish bile flow.

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Kernicterus / Bilirubin-Induced Neurologic Dysfunction (BIND)**: Occurs when unconjugated, free (unbound) lipid-soluble bilirubin exceeds the blood-brain barrier threshold (**>20 mg/dL / 340 μmol/L** in term infants), causing irreversible neuronal necrosis of the basal ganglia (globus pallidus), subthalamic nuclei, and brainstem auditory nuclei.
    - _Clinical Features_: Lethargy, poor feeding, hypotonia followed by extensor hypertonia, retrocollis, opisthotonos, seizures, and high-pitched cry.
    - _Permanent Sequelae_: Choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL), upward gaze palsy, and dental enamel dysplasia.
- **Hepatic Cirrhosis & Portal Hypertension**: Outcome of uncorrected biliary atresia or metabolic storage diseases.

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Conjugated Bilirubin is NEVER Physiological**: Any newborn with a **direct (conjugated) bilirubin level >2 mg/dL** or >20% of TSB must be immediately evaluated for structural or infectious hepatobiliary disease (e.g. **Extrahepatic Biliary Atresia** or TORCH neonatal hepatitis). Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia is NEVER benign or physiological!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Breastfeeding Jaundice** with **Breast Milk Jaundice**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Breastfeeding Jaundice (Early Onset, Day 2–4)**: Caused by **NOT ENOUGH MILK INTAKE**(dehydration and delayed meconium clearance increasing enterohepatic circulation). Treatment is to **INCREASE feeding frequency**.
    - **Breast Milk Jaundice (Late Onset, Day 7–14)**: Caused by **SUBSTANCES IN THE MILK ITSELF**(3α,20β-pregnanediol inhibiting UGT1A1 enzyme). Infant is thriving and well-nourished.

### Quick Revision

- **Physiological Jaundice**: Appears **after 24 hours** (Day 2–3); peaks ≤12 mg/dL in term; resolves by **Day 7–10**.
- **Day-1 Jaundice (<24 Hours)**: ALWAYS pathological; primary cause is **Rh or ABO Isoimmunization**.
- **Conjugated Hyperbilirubinemia**: Direct bilirubin **>2 mg/dL**; hallmark of **Biliary Atresia** (clay-colored stools, dark urine).
- **Kramer Zone 5**: Jaundice on **palms and soles = TSB >15 mg/dL**.
- **Kernicterus Cut-off**: TSB **>20 mg/dL (340 μmol/L)** in term infants; damages basal ganglia (globus pallidus).
- **Key Drug Therapies**: **IVIG (0.5–1 g/kg)** for Rh/ABO hemolysis; **Phenobarbitone** for UGT1A1 induction; **Ursodeoxycholic acid** for cholestasis.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Neonatal jaundice is the clinical yellow staining of skin and sclera occurring when total serum bilirubin exceeds 5–7 mg/dL, categorized into benign physiological jaundice (appearing after 24 hours due to high RBC turnover and UGT1A1 immaturity) and pathological hyperbilirubinemia caused by immune hemolysis (Rh/ABO), enzyme defects (G6PD), structural biliary obstruction (biliary atresia), or metabolic disorders.**
