# Cephalhematoma [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cephalhematoma**: A localized subperiosteal accumulation of blood between the skull bone and its overlying pericranium (periosteum), resulting from the rupture of small emissary veins following mechanical birth trauma [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Anatomical Boundary Rule**: It is strictly **limited by the suture lines** of the involved skull bone because the pericranium is firmly anchored to the sutural margins of individual cranial bones [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Predominant Site**: Most commonly unilateral and situated over the **parietal bone** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Etiology

- Caused by shearing traction forces and severe friction between the fetal skull and the maternal bony pelvis, or by operative instrumentation, leading to the rupture of subperiosteal emissary blood vessels [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

#### B. Risk Factors

- **Operative Vaginal Delivery**: High association with **vacuum extraction (ventouse)** and **forceps delivery** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455; Ch 37, p. 535].
- **Mechanical Factors**: Cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD), prolonged or obstructed second stage of labor, and rigid maternal perineum.
- **Fetal Factors**: Macrosomia, fetal malpositions (e.g., persistent occiput posterior), and primigravid birth.

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Onset & Progression

- **Delayed Appearance**: **Never present at birth**; appears gradually **12 to 24 hours after delivery** as slow subperiosteal venous bleeding accumulates [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 79; Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Size Trajectory**: Progressively increases in size over the first 2–3 days of life before stabilizing.

#### B. Physical Examination Signs

- **Boundaries**: Strictly confined to the margins of a single cranial bone (does **NOT** cross suture lines) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Consistency**: Soft, circumscribed, fluctuant, and **incompressible** swelling [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Mobility**: Fixed to one site; does not shift with changes in infant head posture [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 79].
- **Palpation Edge**: As the outer margins begin to organize and calcify, a **firm raised bony ridge** is felt at the periphery with a soft central depression.

---

### 5. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Clinical Physical Examination**: Diagnostic confirmation based on a fluctuant parietal swelling appearing 12–24 hours post-birth that respects suture boundaries [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Hematocrit**: Assesses the extent of internal blood loss and checks for anemia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
3. **Serum Bilirubin Levels**: Serial monitoring of total serum bilirubin (TSB) to detect early unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia from extravasated red blood cell breakdown [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
4. **Skull Radiograph (X-Ray) / CT Scan**: Indicated if a depressed contour is felt, to rule out an underlying **linear or depressed skull bone fracture** (present in 5–15% of cases) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
5. **Cranial Ultrasonography (CUS) or MRI**: Indicated if focal neurological deficits or signs of raised intracranial pressure develop [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

### 7. Treatment & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Conservative Observation**: Primary management relies on observation and gentle handling; the subperiosteal hematoma reabsorbs spontaneously over **6 to 8 weeks** leaving an entirely normal skull [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION ON NEEDLE ASPIRATION**: **Needle aspiration or surgical drainage is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** because it introduces skin flora into the closed hematoma, converting a sterile blood collection into a life-threatening infection (**suppurative cephalhematoma / osteomyelitis**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Management of Hyperbilirubinemia**: Initiate phototherapy (or exchange transfusion if severe) if total serum bilirubin exceeds risk thresholds [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 452, 455].
- **Management of Anemia**: Administer packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusion if significant internal blood loss produces symptomatic anemia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Infection Management**: If secondary suppuration occurs (fever, erythema, extreme local warmth), administer systemic antibiotics and obtain neurosurgical consultation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

---

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Unconjugated Hyperbilirubinemia & Kernicterus**: Breakdown of trapped extravasated hemoglobin releases bilirubin into circulation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
2. **Neonatal Anemia**: Significant subperiosteal blood accumulation can deplete circulating blood volume [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
3. **Suppurative Cephalhematoma**: Secondary bacterial infection forming an abscess (usually following unnecessary needle aspiration) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
4. **Underlying Skull Fracture**: Linear or depressed skull fracture present in up to 15% of cases [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
5. **Dystrophic Calcification**: Peripheral calcification of the elevated pericranium, forming a temporary bony helmet or ridge that remodels over months.

---

### 9. Prognosis

- **Excellent**: Spontaneous complete resolution occurs in the vast majority of infants within 6–8 weeks without long-term cranial deformation or neurological deficit [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

---

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Suture Line Rule**: Because the pericranium (periosteum) is firmly glued to the sutural ligaments between skull bones, subperiosteal bleeding in a cephalhematoma can **NEVER cross a suture line**. If a scalp swelling freely crosses suture lines, it is either **Caput Succedaneum** (subcutaneous edema) or **Subgaleal Hemorrhage** (bleeding under the galea aponeurotica) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 79; Ch 33, p. 455].

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing a needle aspiration to "relieve pressure" or "speed up resolution" of a tense cephalhematoma.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **NEVER needle-aspirate a cephalhematoma!** Aspiration converts a benign, self-resolving sterile blood collection into an infected **suppurative cephalhematoma or skull osteomyelitis**. Treatment is strictly conservative observation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

### Quick Revision

- **Anatomical Site**: **Subperiosteal** blood collection over flat skull bone (usually parietal) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Suture Boundary**: **Does NOT cross suture lines** due to firm pericranial sutural anchoring [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Onset**: Appears **12 to 24 hours after birth** (never present at birth) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 79; Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Primary Complications**: **Hyperbilirubinemia** and **Anemia** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].
- **Golden Rule**: **Strictly conservative management**; needle aspiration is contraindicated [DC Dutta 1, Ch 33, p. 455].

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Cephalhematoma is a traumatic subperiosteal blood collection over a fetal skull bone (most commonly parietal) that appears 12–24 hours after birth, is strictly demarcated by cranial suture lines, and resolves spontaneously over 6–8 weeks with conservative management.**
