# Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

- **Clinical Definition**: Any amount of blood loss following delivery of the fetus that results in hemodynamic instability (evidenced by a fall in Systolic Blood Pressure [SBP] by **>30 mmHg**, a rise in heart rate by **>30 beats/minute**, a drop in hemoglobin by **>30% / >3 g/dL**, or urine output **<30 mL/hour**).
- **Quantitative Definition**: Blood loss **≥500 mL** following a vaginal birth or **≥1000 mL** following a Cesarean section.

#### B. Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage

- **Definition**: Postpartum hemorrhage resulting from the failure of the uterine myometrium to contract and retract effectively after the separation and expulsion of the placenta.
- **Clinical Significance**: Atonicity of the uterus is the single most common cause of primary PPH, accounting for approximately **80% of all PPH cases**.

#### A. Based on Timing of Onset

1. **Primary (Early) PPH**: Hemorrhage occurring within **24 hours** following the delivery of the baby.
    - _Third-Stage PPH_: Bleeding occurring before the expulsion of the placenta.
    - _Postpartum Proper PPH_: Bleeding occurring after the expulsion of the placenta.
2. **Secondary (Late / Delayed) PPH**: Hemorrhage occurring beyond **24 hours and up to 12 weeks postpartum**during the puerperium.

#### B. Etiological Classification — The "4 Ts" (RCOG) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Tone (80%)**: Uterine atony (failure of myometrial contraction and retraction).
- **Tissue (5–10%)**: Retained placental bits, cotyledons, or blood clots.
- **Trauma (10–15%)**: Lacerations of the cervix, vagina, perineum, or lower uterine segment.
- **Thrombin (1–2%)**: Coagulopathy (DIC, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, HELLP syndrome).

### 3. Etiology & Predisposition for Atonic PPH [⭐ High-Yield]

Myometrial atony occurs when physiological mechanisms interfering with muscle contraction and retraction are present:

#### A. Overdistension of the Uterus

- **Multiple Pregnancy (Twins / Triplets)**: Excessive muscle fiber stretching reduces contractility.
- **Polyhydramnios**: Rapid loss of large volume liquor amnii impairs post-delivery retraction.
- **Fetal Macrosomia (Birth Weight >4 kg)**.

#### B. Myometrial Muscle Exhaustion & Dysfunction

- **Prolonged Labor (>12 hours)**: Muscle glycogen depletion and amnionitis impair retraction.
- **Precipitate Labor**: Rapid delivery leaves insufficient time for physiological retraction to keep pace.
- **High Parity (Grand Multipara)**: Fibrotic changes replace myometrial muscle fibers.
- **Uterine Fibroids (Leiomyomas)**: Mechanically distort muscle fibers and prevent uniform retraction.

#### C. Pharmacological & Anesthetic Agents

- **Tocolytic Drugs**: Isoxsuprine, Nifedipine, Terbutaline, and **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** administered prior to delivery.
- **Deep General Anesthesia**: Halothane, ether, or halogenated agents cause profound uterine relaxation.
- **Abrupt Stoppage of Prolonged Oxytocin**: Down-regulation of oxytocin receptors following prolonged induction/augmentation.

#### D. Mismanagement of the Third Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

- Pulling the umbilical cord before placental separation.
- Kneading, squeezing, or fiddling with the uterine fundus before separation.
- Sudden forceful expulsion / fundal pressure.

#### E. Maternal & Constitutional Factors

- **Severe Anemia (Hb <10 g/dL)**: Reduced tissue oxygenation lowers myometrial tone.
- **Obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²)** and **Maternal Age >40 years**.
- **Previous History of PPH**: Recurrence risk is **5–15%**.

#### Core Mechanisms:

1. **The "Living Ligature" Concept**: Under normal conditions, middle layer myometrial fibers are arranged in a criss-cross "figure-of-8" pattern surrounding uterine blood vessels. Their contraction and retraction act as anatomical ligatures, obliterating lumen of torn sinuses.
2. **Pathology of Atony**: When retraction fails, torn uterine sinuses remain wide open, permitting direct blood flow from uterine arteries at a rate of **500–600 mL/minute**, causing massive hemorrhage within minutes.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- Continuous vaginal bleeding presenting as a steady trickle or sudden massive gush of dark red blood following delivery of the baby.
- Symptoms of acute hypovolemia: Faintness, dizziness, extreme thirst, restlessness, cold clammy extremities, and air hunger.

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Abdominal Palpation of the Uterus [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - The uterus feels **soft, flabby, boggy, and ill-defined**, occupying a high level well above the umbilicus.
    - **Diagnostic Sign**: The uterus becomes **firm and hard upon abdominal massaging**, with blood and clots expressing out through the vagina during massage.
- **Hemodynamic Signs of Shock (Rule of 30 & Shock Index)**:
    - **Pulse Rate**: Tachycardia (**>100–120 bpm** or rise by >30 bpm).
    - **Blood Pressure**: Fall in Systolic BP (**<90 mmHg** or drop by >30 mmHg).
    - **Shock Index (SI = Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP)**: Normal is **0.5–0.7**; a value **>0.9 to 1.0** indicates major blood loss (>20–30% volume loss) requiring rapid resuscitation.
    - **Respiration**: Tachypnea (**>30 breaths/minute**).
    - **Renal**: Oliguria (**<30 mL/hour**).

### 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Diagnosis

- Clinical diagnosis is made immediately by combining **visible vaginal bleeding** with an **absent/flabby uterine tone on abdominal palpation**.

#### B. Emergency Laboratory Investigation Panel

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Hemoglobin and hematocrit estimation (monitors drop >30%).
2. **Blood Grouping & Cross-Matching**: Immediate ABO and Rh D typing; cross-match **at least 2–4 units of Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)**.
3. **Bedside Clotting Test (Lee-White)**: 2 mL of venous blood in a glass tube should clot within 6–10 minutes. A soft clot that breaks easily or failure to clot indicates DIC.
4. **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Serum Fibrinogen level (**target >2 g/L**), D-dimer, and Platelet count (**target >50,000/μL**).
5. **Ultrasound Examination (USG Bedside)**: Excludes retained placental tissue, blood clots, or uterine inversion.

### 8. Prevention & Active Management of Third Stage (AMTSL) [⭐ High-Yield]

AMTSL is a proven evidence-based protocol that **reduces the incidence of PPH by 60%**.

1. **Uterotonic Administration**: Administer **Injection Oxytocin 10 IU IM** (or 5–10 IU slow IV) to the mother within **1 minute of birth of the baby** (after ruling out a second twin). Alternatively, Tab. Misoprostol **600 μg PO/PR** or Inj. Methergine 0.2 mg IM.
2. **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews Maneuver)**: Counter-pressure is applied abdominally upward on the lower uterine segment while applying gentle traction downward and backward on the clamped cord during a contraction.
3. **Uterine Massage**: Massage the uterine fundus immediately after delivery of the placenta until hard, and palpate every 15 minutes for the first 2 hours postpartum.
4. **Delayed Cord Clamping**: Clamping the cord **1 to 3 minutes after birth** (or after pulsations cease) improves neonatal iron stores.

### 9. Treatment & Management Protocol (22-Year-Old Primipara) [⭐ High-Yield]

In a **22-year-old primiparous woman**, preserving future fertility is of critical importance. Management follows a strict stepwise approach from medical to uterine-sparing surgical interventions, reserving hysterectomy as an absolute last resort.

#### STEP 1: Immediate Resuscitation & Initial Measures ("Golden 1 Hour") [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Call for Assistance**: Activate multidisciplinary team (Obstetrician, Anesthetist, Hematologist, ICU team).
- **Airway & Oxygenation**: Administer 100% Oxygen via face mask at 6–8 L/minute.
- **Venous Access & Circulation**:
    - Insert two large-bore IV cannulae (**14G or 16G**).
    - Rapidly infuse warmed isotonic crystalloids (**Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate up to 2 L**) and colloids.
    - Transfuse cross-matched Fresh Whole Blood or **Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)** combined with Fresh Frozen Plasma (**FFP: 4 units for every 6 units PRBC**) to prevent dilutional coagulopathy.
- **Bladder Decompression**: Insert Foley catheter to keep bladder empty and monitor hourly urine output (**target >30 mL/hr**).
- **Fundal Massage**: Perform continuous abdominal uterine massage to stimulate natural contraction.

#### STEP 2: Pharmacological / Medical Management (Uterotonics & Anti-fibrinolytics) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Oxytocin**: **10–20 IU in 500 mL Normal Saline IV drip** at 40–60 drops/min (or up to 40 IU/L).
2. **Methylergometrine (Methergine)**: **0.2 mg IV slowly** over 5 minutes (or IM); excites tetanic contractions. _Contraindicated in hypertension or pre-eclampsia_.
3. **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α)**: **250 μg IM or intramyometrial**; repeat every 15 to 90 minutes (maximum 8 doses = 2 mg). _Contraindicated in bronchial asthma_.
4. **Misoprostol (PGE₁)**: **800 to 1000 μg per rectum (PR)** or sublingual.
5. **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: **1 g IV slowly over 10 minutes** (WHO recommendation); repeat 1 g IV after 30 minutes if bleeding continues.

#### STEP 3: Mechanical & Uterine Tamponade Methods (Uterine-Sparing) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Bimanual Uterine Compression (Hamilton Maneuver)**:
    - Introduce gloved right hand into vagina, clenched as a fist in the anterior fornix.
    - Place left hand abdominally behind the uterine fundus, squeezing the uterus firmly against the vaginal fist. Maintain until tone is restored.
2. **Uterine Balloon Tamponade (First-Line Conservative Surgical Action)**:
    - Insert a **Bakri Balloon**, **Foley Catheter**, or **Condom Catheter** into the uterine cavity under aseptic conditions.
    - Inflate balloon with **200 to 500 mL of warm sterile normal saline**.
    - Exerts direct internal hydrostatic pressure on open sinuses; **avoids hysterectomy in 78% of cases**. Retained for 4 to 24 hours under prophylactic antibiotic cover.
3. **Intrauterine Packing**: Pack a 5-meter long, 8-cm wide gauze strip soaked in antiseptic cream tightly into the uterine cavity from fundus downward. Removed after 24 hours.

#### STEP 4: Conservative Surgical Methods (Uterine Devascularization & Compression Sutures) [⭐ High-Yield]

If medical and tamponade methods fail in this 22-year-old primipara, proceed to laparotomy for fertility-preserving surgical procedures:

1. **B-Lynch Uterine Compression Sutures**:
    - A continuous bracing suture using **No. 1 Chromic Catgut or Vicryl** passed from lower segment over fundus, compressing anterior and posterior uterine walls against each other. Success rate **~80%**.
2. **Stepwise Uterine Devascularization**:
    - _Bilateral Uterine Artery Ligation (O'Leary)_: Ligated at junction of upper and lower uterine segment 2 cm medial to vessels (75–90% success).
    - _Utero-Ovarian Vessel Ligation_: Ligated just below ovarian ligament anastomosis.
    - _Bilateral Internal Iliac (Hypogastric) Artery Ligation_: Ligated at anterior division below origin of posterior division. Reduces pelvic arterial pulse pressure by **85%**, converting high-pressure arterial flow to low-pressure venous flow to allow clot formation. Does not cause pelvic necrosis due to collateral circulation.
3. **Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)**: Fluoroscopic gelfoam embolization by interventional radiologist (success **>90%**, preserves future fertility).

#### STEP 5: Life-Saving Obstetric Hysterectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated as a last resort when all uterine-sparing surgical measures fail and maternal life is in jeopardy. Perform subtotal or total hysterectomy promptly.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden 1 Hour Rule**: Survival in major PPH drops sharply if resuscitation and hemostasis are delayed beyond **1 hour** from onset. Immediate activation of resuscitation, shock index monitoring (>0.9), and step-by-step uterotonic management within this window saves lives.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering Inj. Methergine to a PPH patient with co-existing severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, OR giving Carboprost to an asthmatic patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Methergine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in pre-eclampsia/hypertension** (causes fatal cerebrovascular accidents or eclamptic fits); use **Oxytocin** or **Misoprostol** instead.
    - **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α) is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in bronchial asthma** (causes fatal bronchospasm); use **Misoprostol** instead.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: PPH = Blood loss ≥500 mL (vaginal) or ≥1000 mL (CS) or SBP drop >30 mmHg / HR rise >30 bpm. Atonic accounts for **80% of PPH**.
- **AMTSL**: Oxytocin 10 IU IM + Controlled Cord Traction + Fundal Massage (reduces PPH by 60%).
- **First-line Drugs**: Oxytocin 10–20 IU IV, Methergine 0.2 mg IV (avoid in HTN), Carboprost 250 μg IM (avoid in asthma), Misoprostol 800–1000 μg PR, Tranexamic Acid 1 g IV.
- **Conservative Tamponade**: Bakri Balloon (200–500 mL saline) or Bimanual Compression.
- **Fertility-Preserving Surgery (22-Year-Old)**: B-Lynch compression sutures, bilateral uterine/internal iliac artery ligation.
