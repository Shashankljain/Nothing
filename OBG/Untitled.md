# Diagnosis of Pregnancy in First Trimester [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **First Trimester Pregnancy**: Spans from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP) up to **12 completed weeks** (0–12 weeks / first 84 days) of gestation.
- **Diagnostic Foundation**: Diagnosis is established through a combination of **presumptive subjective symptoms**, **probable objective physical signs** (breast and pelvic changes), **immunological pregnancy tests** (detecting human chorionic gonadotropin - β-hCG), and **definitive ultrasonographic evidence** (transvaginal sonography).

---

### 2. Clinical Features & Chronological Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Presumptive Subjective Symptoms (Felt by Patient)

1. **Amenorrhea**: Abrupt cessation of menses in a healthy woman of reproductive age with previously regular 28-day cycles.
2. **Morning Sickness (Nausea & Vomiting)**: Appears following the 1st or 2nd missed period (around **6th week**), peaks at **8–10 weeks** (coinciding with peak serum β-hCG, estrogen, and progesterone levels), and subsides spontaneously by **12–16 weeks**.
3. **Frequency of Micturition**: A prominent symptom between **8–12 weeks** caused by:
    - Rest of the bulky, exaggerated anteverted uterine fundus on the urinary bladder.
    - Vascular congestion of the bladder mucosa.
    - Altered maternal osmoregulation causing increased thirst and polyuria.
    - _Note_: Disappears after 12 weeks as the uterus rises out of the pelvis into the abdomen.
4. **Breast Discomfort**: Feeling of fullness, heaviness, and "pricking sensation" evident as early as **6–8 weeks** (most marked in primigravidae).
5. **Fatigue & Mood Changes**: Frequent early symptom driven by high progesterone.

#### B. Probable Objective Physical Signs (Elicited by Examiner)

- **Breast Changes (6–8 Weeks)**:
    - Enlargement with vascular engorgement (delicate subepidermal veins visible).
    - Pronounced pigmentation of the **primary areola and nipple**.
    - Prominence of **Montgomery's tubercles** (hypertrophied sebaceous glands).
    - Expression of thick yellowish secretion (**colostrum**) by **12th week**.

- **Pelvic & Uterine Signs**:
    - **Goodell's Sign (6 Weeks)**: Softening of the cervix (first sign to become positive). The pregnant cervix feels soft like the **lips of the mouth**, whereas a non-pregnant cervix feels firm like the **tip of the nose**.
    - **Chadwick's / Jacquemier's Sign (6–8 Weeks)**: Dusky bluish or purplish discoloration of the vestibule, anterior vaginal wall, and portio vaginalis of the cervix due to local venous congestion.
    - **Osiander's Sign (8 Weeks)**: Increased arterial pulsations felt through the lateral vaginal fornices due to hypervascularity of uterine arteries.
    - **Piskacek's Sign (8 Weeks)**: Asymmetrical enlargement of the uterus caused by lateral implantation of the blastocyst over one cornua; symmetry is restored by 12 weeks.
    - **Palmer's Sign (4–8 Weeks)**: Regular, rhythmic, painless uterine contractions elicited during bimanual pelvic examination.
    - **Hegar's Sign (6–10 Weeks - Present in 2/3rd cases)**: Based on three anatomical variations: (1) upper body of uterus is enlarged by conceptus, (2) lower segment (isthmus) is empty and extremely soft, and (3) cervix is comparatively firm. On bimanual examination (two fingers in anterior vaginal fornix and abdominal fingers behind uterus), the fingers seem to **approximate / appose** below the upper body of the uterus.
- **Uterine Size & Shape Progression**:
    - 6–8 weeks: Size of a **hen's egg** (pyriform).
    - 8–10 weeks: Size of a **cricket ball / orange**.
    - 10–12 weeks: Size of a **fetal head / grapefruit** (globular; palpable per abdomen at pubic symphysis at 12 weeks).

### 4. Investigations & Diagnostic Criteria [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Immunological Pregnancy Tests (β-hCG Testing)

- **Principle**: Detects the glycoprotein hormone **human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)** secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast.
- **Structure**: Composed of an α-subunit (identical to LH, FSH, TSH) and a distinct, specific **β-subunit**. Antibodies against the β-subunit prevent cross-reactivity.
- **Sensitivity & Limits**:
    - **Serum β-hCG (ELISA / RIA / FIA)**: Detects levels as low as **1–2 mIU/mL** as early as **8–9 days post-fertilization** (5 days before missed period). Plasma half-life = **36 hours**. Doubling time in early pregnancy = **1.4–2 days**.
    - **Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT - Sandwich ELISA Card Test)**: Detects minimum values of **20–60 mIU/mL**. Positive on the day of missed period (first morning voided urine sample preferred).
- **Peak & Critical Titers**:
    - **Peak Level**: Reaches **100,000–200,000 IU/L** (10⁵ IU/L) between **60–80 days (10 weeks)** of pregnancy, then declines to 10,000–20,000 IU/L by 16 weeks.
    - **Discriminatory Zone (Critical Titer)**: Serum β-hCG level at which an intrauterine gestational sac MUST be visualized on USG:
        - **TVS Discriminatory Zone**: **1000–2000 IU/L**.
        - **TAS Discriminatory Zone**: **6500 IU/L**.

#### B. Ultrasonography (TVS vs TAS) - Gold Standard

Transvaginal Sonography (TVS) is superior to Transabdominal Sonography (TAS), visualizing fetal structures **1 week earlier**.

|Menstrual Age|TVS Finding|Clinical Significance|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**4.5–5.0 Weeks**|**Gestational Sac (GS)** (MSD = 5 mm)|First structure seen; shows **Intradecidual Sign** & **Double Decidual Sac Sign**.|
|**5.5 Weeks**|**Yolk Sac** (MSD = 8–10 mm)|**First diagnostic sign of Intrauterine Pregnancy**; excludes pseudosac.|
|**5.5–6.0 Weeks**|**Fetal Pole / Embryo** (CRL = 1–5 mm)|First appearance of embryonic tissue adjacent to yolk sac.|
|**6.0–6.5 Weeks**|**Fetal Cardiac Activity**|Visualized on M-mode TVS (FHR = 110–130 bpm at 6 wks; 160–170 bpm at 8 wks).|
|**7.0–12.0 Weeks**|**Crown-Rump Length (CRL)**|**Gold standard parameter for gestational age dating** (accuracy ±5 days).|
|**10.0 Weeks**|**Doppler Fetal Heart Tones**|Audible using handheld Doppler device.|

### 5. Differential Diagnosis

|Condition|Amenorrhea|Uterine Enlargement|Goodell / Hegar Sign|Pregnancy Test (β-hCG)|Ultrasonography Findings|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Normal 1st Trimester Pregnancy**|**Present**.|Soft, progressive enlargement.|**Present**.|**Positive (+)**.|Gestational sac, yolk sac, CRL, & cardiac activity.|
|**Uterine Fibroid (Leiomyoma)**|Usually absent (menorrhagia).|Slow growing, firm/hard, nodular.|Absent.|Negative (-).|Well-circumscribed hypoechoic mass in myometrium.|
|**Cystic Ovarian Tumor**|Absent.|Uterus felt separate from pelvic cyst.|Absent.|Negative (-).|Anechoic/cystic adnexal mass; empty uterus.|
|**Pseudocyesis (Phantom Pregnancy)**|Present (psychogenic).|Normal non-pregnant size.|Absent.|Negative (-).|Empty uterine cavity.|
|**Hematometra**|Present (primary).|Tense, tender, pelvic mass.|Absent.|Negative (-).|Blood accumulation in uterine cavity; imperforate hymen.|

### 6. Indicators of Early Pregnancy Loss / Failed Pregnancy on TVS

- **Anembryonic Pregnancy (Blighted Ovum)**: Mean Sac Diameter (MSD) **≥25 mm** with no embryo visible.
- **Missed Abortion / Embryonic Demise**: Crown-Rump Length (CRL) **≥7 mm** with no fetal cardiac activity.
- **Abnormal Cardiac Motion**: Fetal heart rate **<85 bpm** at 6 weeks gestation indicates poor prognosis.

---

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Yolk Sac Confirms Intrauterine Location**: The appearance of a **Yolk Sac** inside a gestational sac at **5.5 weeks on TVS** is the earliest definitive sign that confirms a true **intrauterine pregnancy**, reliably differentiating it from a fluid-filled **pseudosac** seen in ectopic pregnancy.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing a rough bimanual examination while eliciting **Hegar's Sign**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Gentle palpation is mandatory when eliciting Hegar's sign!** Because the lower uterine segment (isthmus) is extremely soft and thin between 6–10 weeks, vigorous or rough compression during bimanual examination can easily compress the gestational sac or trigger uterine irritability, precipitating an unwanted **abortion**.

### 9. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: H-E-G-A-R (Pelvic Signs of Early Pregnancy)

- **H**: **Hegar sign** (6–10 weeks: soft isthmus allowing finger apposition)
- **E**: **Egg-like size** (6–8 weeks uterus = hen's egg)
- **G**: **Goodell sign** (6 weeks: softening of cervix like lips of mouth)
- **A**: **Asymmetric uterus / Piskacek sign** (8 weeks: lateral implantation site)
- **R**: **Reddish-blue / Chadwick sign** (6–8 weeks: dusky vascular discoloration of vagina & cervix)

### Quick Revision

- **First Sign of Pregnancy**: **Goodell's sign** (cervical softening at 6 weeks).
- **Hegar's Sign Timing**: Present between **6 and 10 weeks** due to extreme softening of the isthmus.
- **Immunological Gold Standard**: **Serum β-hCG** detected **8–9 days post-fertilization** (1–2 mIU/mL).
- **TVS Discriminatory Zone**: β-hCG **1000–2000 IU/L** (gestational sac must be visible).
- **TVS Timeline**: Gestational Sac (5 wks) → Yolk Sac (5.5 wks) → Fetal Pole & Cardiac Activity (6 wks) → CRL Dating (7–12 wks).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Diagnosis of first-trimester pregnancy is established by synthesizing presumptive symptoms (amenorrhea, morning sickness, micturition frequency), probable pelvic signs (Goodell's, Chadwick's, Hegar's signs), positive β-hCG immunological tests, and definitive transvaginal sonography demonstrating a gestational sac at 5 weeks and fetal cardiac activity at 6 weeks.**
