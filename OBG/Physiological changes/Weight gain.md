# Weight Gain During Pregnancy — Significance [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks**

---

### Definition

- **Gestational Weight Gain (GWG)** is the progressive increase in maternal body mass during singleton pregnancy, averaging **11–12 kg (25–35 lb)** in a healthy woman with a normal pre-pregnancy Body Mass Index (BMI 18.5–24.9 kg/m²).
- Distribution: **1 kg** in the 1st trimester, and **5 kg each** in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters (~0.5 kg/week in the 2nd half). Nearly 60% of total gain is contributed by the fetoplacental unit (fetus ~3.3 kg, placenta ~0.6 kg, liquor ~0.8 kg) and maternal tissue adaptations (uterus ~0.9 kg, breasts ~0.4 kg, blood volume ~1.2 kg, ECF ~1.3 kg, fat stores ~3.5 kg).

---

### Significance / Clinical Implications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Screening for Pre-Eclampsia (Excessive Gain)**: Rapid weight gain **>0.5 kg/week or >2 kg/month** in the late 2nd or 3rd trimester is the earliest clinical indicator of pathological fluid retention (occult edema) in **Pre-eclampsia / Gestational Hypertension**, appearing before visible pitting edema or overt hypertension.
- **Screening for Fetal Growth Restriction (Inadequate Gain)**: Stationary, falling, or subnormal weight gain during the 2nd half of pregnancy strongly alerts to **Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR)**, oligohydramnios, severe maternal malnutrition, or **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD)**.
- **Predictor of Perinatal Morbidity (Macrosomia vs SGA)**:
    - **Excessive Weight Gain**: Directly correlates with **GDM**, **fetal macrosomia** (birth weight >4.0 kg), shoulder dystocia, soft tissue lacerations, PPH, and increased risk of operative Cesarean delivery.
    - **Inadequate Weight Gain**: Significantly increases the incidence of **Small-for-Gestational-Age (SGA)**infants, low birth weight (LBW <2500 g), and preterm birth.
- **Pre-Pregnancy BMI Calibration (Target Weight Gain Rules)**:
    - **Underweight (BMI <18.5 kg/m²)**: Target weight gain = **12.5–18 kg (28–40 lb)**.
    - **Normal Weight (BMI 18.5–24.9 kg/m²)**: Target weight gain = **11–12.5 kg (25–35 lb)**.
    - **Overweight (BMI 25.0–29.9 kg/m²)**: Target weight gain = **7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb)**.
    - **Obese (BMI ≥30.0 kg/m²)**: Target weight gain = **5–9 kg (11–20 lb)**.
- **Long-Term Maternal Metabolic Risk**: Excessive gestational weight gain leads to persistent postpartum weight retention, increasing the long-term risk of maternal obesity, Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, and metabolic syndrome.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Average Total Weight Gain**: **11–12 kg (24–25 lb)** for a normal BMI singleton pregnancy (1 kg in 1st trimester; 5 kg each in 2nd and 3rd trimesters).
- **Rapid Gain (>0.5 kg/wk or >2 kg/month)**: Earliest sign of **Pre-eclampsia** (occult edema) and macrosomia.
- **Stationary / Falling Weight**: Key clinical indicator of **FGR / IUGR** or **IUFD**.
- **BMI Targets**: Underweight = 12.5–18 kg; Normal = 11–12.5 kg; Overweight = 7–11.5 kg; Obese = 5–9 kg.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Monitoring maternal weight gain during pregnancy is a fundamental, non-invasive antenatal screening tool where an average total gain of 11–12 kg reflects normal fetoplacental development, while rapid gain (>0.5 kg/week) signals pre-eclampsia or macrosomia and stationary gain indicates fetal growth restriction.**
