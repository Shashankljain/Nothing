# Braxton Hicks Contraction [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Braxton Hicks Contractions** are spontaneous, irregular, infrequent, spasmodic, and painless myometrial contractions occurring throughout pregnancy that do not cause cervical effacement or dilatation.

---

### Key Features & Clinical Significance [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Onset & Clinical Progression**: Appears in early pregnancy (perceptible from around 16 weeks) and increases in frequency and intensity near term, where it may cause mild discomfort before merging into true labor pains.
- **Intrauterine Pressure**: Basal tone remains low, with intrauterine pressure generated during contractions staying strictly **<8 mmHg**.
- **Elicitability & Cervical Effect**: Can be excited or exaggerated by abdominal palpation or gentle rubbing of the uterus, but **does not produce cervical effacement or dilatation**.
- **Diagnostic Importance**: Serves as a clinical sign of intrauterine pregnancy; characteristically, **Braxton Hicks contractions are absent in abdominal pregnancy**, pelvic fibroids, and ovarian cysts.
- **Distinction from True Labor Pains**: Unlike true labor, Braxton Hicks contractions are **painless, non-rhythmic, do not progressively increase in frequency or duration, and cause no descent of the fetus or cervical change**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Nature**: Spontaneous, irregular, infrequent, spasmodic, and **painless**.
- **Pressure**: Intrauterine pressure remains **<8 mmHg**.
- **Cervix**: **No cervical effacement or dilatation**.
- **Absence**: **Absent in abdominal pregnancy**.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Braxton Hicks contractions are physiological, irregular, and painless myometrial contractions present throughout pregnancy with an intrauterine pressure <8 mmHg that cause no cervical dilatation and are characteristically absent in extrauterine (abdominal) pregnancy.**
