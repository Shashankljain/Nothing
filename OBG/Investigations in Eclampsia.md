# Diagnostic Investigations in Eclampsia [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Eclampsia**: Defined as the occurrence of **new-onset generalized grand mal tonic-clonic convulsions and/or coma** in a pregnant woman with pre-eclampsia (or gestational hypertension), in the absence of any other identifiable neurological cause (e.g., epilepsy, meningitis, or cerebral venous thrombosis).
- **Diagnostic Criteria for Pre-eclampsia**: Hypertension (SBP ≥140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg after 20 weeks) accompanied by **proteinuria** (≥0.3 g/24 hours or urine protein-to-creatinine ratio >0.3 mg/mg) OR new-onset **end-organ dysfunction** (thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT >70 U/L, pulmonary edema, or visual/cerebral symptoms).

### 3. Maternal Laboratory Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Hematological Workup [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Hematocrit**: Demonstrates **hemoconcentration** (elevated hematocrit) due to systemic capillary endothelial leakage and intravascular plasma volume contraction.
2. **Platelet Count**: **Thrombocytopenia (platelets <100,000/μL)** is the most common hematological abnormality, reflecting microvascular platelet activation and consumption; the degree of drop directly correlates with disease severity.
3. **Peripheral Blood Smear**: Presence of fragmented red blood cells (**schistocytes, burr cells, and helmet cells**) confirms microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, the hallmark of **HELLP syndrome**.
4. **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Serum Fibrinogen, and D-dimers are indicated when platelet count is <100,000/μL to rule out **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)**.

#### B. Renal Function Tests (RFTs) & Urinalysis [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL** (normal <4.0 mg/dL); hyperuricemia is an early hallmark caused by diminished distal tubular secretion and increased tubular reabsorption, correlating directly with disease severity and fetal jeopardy.
2. **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL** (or doubling of baseline), signaling impaired Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) secondary to **glomerular capillary endotheliosis**.
3. **Blood Urea & Electrolytes**: Serum urea is elevated in severe cases; serum electrolytes are evaluated to manage fluid shifts and rule out hypermagnesemic toxicity during MgSO₄ therapy.
4. **Urinalysis for Protein**:
    - **24-Hour Urine Protein Collection (Gold Standard)**: Excretion of **≥0.3 g/24 hours** (or >5 g/24 hours in severe fulminant renal involvement).
    - **Urine Protein-to-Creatinine (P/C) Ratio**: **>0.3 mg/mg**.
    - **Dipstick Testing**: Persistent **≥2+ to 3+** protein on clean-catch random urine samples.

#### C. Hepatic Function Tests (LFTs) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Serum Transaminases (AST/SGOT & ALT/SGPT)**: Elevated **>70 U/L (≥2 times upper limit of normal)** due to periportal hemorrhagic necrosis and hepatic parenchymal ischemia.
2. **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)**: Elevated **>600 U/L**, indicating intravascular red cell hemolysis and cellular tissue breakdown in HELLP syndrome.
3. **Total Serum Bilirubin**: Elevated **>1.2 mg/dL** (unconjugated fraction predominance due to microangiopathic hemolysis).

### 4. Neuro-imaging & Ophthalmic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Ophthalmoscopy / Fundoscopic Examination [⭐ High-Yield]

Evaluates the grade of hypertensive retinopathy and cerebral microvascular vasospasm:

- **Grade 1–2**: Segmental retinal arteriolar vasospasm; alteration of normal vein-to-arteriole (V:A) diameter ratio from **3:2 to 3:1 or 4:1**.
- **Grade 3**: Retinal edema, flame-shaped hemorrhages, and cotton-wool exudates.
- **Grade 4**: Papilledema and serous retinal detachment.

#### B. Neuro-imaging Studies (MRI & CT Brain) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Brain)**: **Investigation of choice** for eclampsia, persistent severe headache, visual disturbances, status eclampticus, focal neurological deficits, or prolonged coma.
- **Key MRI Finding**: Bilateral, symmetrical hyperintense T2/FLAIR subcortical white matter vasogenic edema in parieto-occipital lobes, establishing the diagnosis of **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**.
- **Computed Tomography (CT Brain)**: Performed in emergencies when MRI is unavailable; detects acute cerebral cortical hypodensities, cerebral edema, infarction, or intraventricular/parenchymal hemorrhage.
- **Electroencephalogram (EEG)**: Indicated in prolonged post-ictal coma or suspected status eclampticus to differentiate eclamptic encephalopathy from underlying epileptogenic focus.

---

### 5. Fetal Surveillance & Wellbeing Panel [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Daily Fetal Movement Counting (DFMC / Kick Chart)**: Maternal monitoring of fetal activity.
2. **Non-Stress Test (NST) & Cardiotocography (CTG)**: Performed daily or on alternate days to detect non-reactive tracing, loss of baseline variability, or late decelerations.
3. **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Assesses fetal tone, movements, breathing, and amniotic fluid volume twice weekly.
4. **Ultrasonography (USG Biometry & AFI)**: Evaluates Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW), detects Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), and measures Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI / oligohydramnios) every 2 weeks.
5. **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler Velocimetry**: Measures placental vascular resistance; abnormal findings include elevated S/D ratio (≥3), **Absent End-Diastolic Flow (AEDV at 33–34 wks)**, or **Reversed End-Diastolic Flow (REDV at 31–32 wks)**.

### 8. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Serum Uric Acid as Disease Hallmark**: Serum uric acid elevation **>4.5–5.0 mg/dL** is one of the earliest and most consistent biochemical markers of eclampsia; it reflects distal tubular reabsorption changes and renal ischemia, correlating directly with volume contraction, disease severity, and fetal jeopardy.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Delaying emergency loading dose of Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) to wait for renal or liver laboratory investigation results.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IMMEDIATELY on clinical diagnosis of eclampsia, IRRESPECTIVE of renal status!** Laboratory RFT results and urine output checks (≥30 mL/hr) are evaluated only prior to administering the 4-hourly _maintenance_ doses.

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: T-O-X-I-C-S (Eclampsia Investigation Checklist)

- **T**: **Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL) & Transaminases (AST/ALT >70 U/L)**
- **O**: **Ophthalmoscopy (retinal vasospasm V:A ratio 3:1 & papilledema)**
- **X**: **X-ray / MRI Brain (PRES vasogenic edema in parieto-occipital lobes)**
- **I**: **Ischemia markers (Serum LDH >600 U/L for microangiopathic hemolysis)**
- **C**: **Creatinine (>1.1 mg/dL) & Coagulation profile (PT, APTT, Fibrinogen, D-dimer)**
- **S**: **Serum Uric Acid (>4.5–5.0 mg/dL) & 24-hr Urine Protein (≥0.3 g)**

### Quick Revision

- **Hematology**: Hemoconcentration, Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL), Smear for schistocytes (HELLP).
- **Renal & Liver Panel**: Uric acid >4.5 mg/dL, Creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT >70 U/L, LDH >600 U/L.
- **Urinalysis**: 24-hour urine protein ≥0.3 g, P/C ratio >0.3, or dipstick ≥2+.
- **Neuro-imaging**: **MRI Brain is Investigation of Choice**, showing bilateral parieto-occipital vasogenic edema (**PRES**).
- **Fetal Monitoring**: Daily DFMC, NST, BPP, USG growth, and Umbilical Artery Doppler (AEDV/REDV).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Diagnostic investigations in eclampsia involve an urgent maternal multi-organ laboratory evaluation (CBC, platelets <100,000/μL, peripheral smear for HELLP, uric acid >4.5 mg/dL, creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT >70 U/L, 24-hr urine protein ≥0.3 g), neuro-imaging (MRI Brain for PRES), fundoscopy, and continuous fetal surveillance (NST, BPP, Doppler).**
