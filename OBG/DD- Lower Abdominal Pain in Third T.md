# Differential Diagnosis of Lower Abdominal Pain in Third Trimester [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks**

---

### Definition / Clinical Context

- **Lower Abdominal Pain in the Third Trimester (28–40 Weeks)**: Acute or subacute pelvic/abdominal discomfort occurring in late pregnancy, requiring prompt clinical differentiation between life-threatening obstetric emergencies and non-obstetric surgical or medical conditions.

---

### Differential Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Obstetric Causes (Uteroplacental)**:
    - **Abruptio Placentae**: Characterized by severe continuous dark red vaginal bleeding, uterine rigidity ("woody-hard" tender uterus), and fetal distress.
    - **True Labor Pains / Preterm Labor**: Regular, rhythmic uterine contractions accompanied by progressive cervical effacement, dilatation, and "show".
    - **Uterine Rupture**: Sudden, agonizing bursting pain followed by cessation of contractions, loss of uterine contour, and loss of fetal station (predominantly in a previously scarred uterus).
    - **Chorioamnionitis**: Uterine tenderness accompanied by maternal fever (≥38°C), maternal/fetal tachycardia, and foul-smelling amniotic fluid.
2. **Gynecological Causes**:
    - **Red Degeneration of Leiomyoma (Fibroid)**: Acute focal abdominal pain and localized uterine tenderness caused by hemorrhagic ischemia within a fibroid.
    - **Torsion of Ovarian Cyst / Adnexal Mass**: Sudden, severe unilateral colicky pain associated with nausea, vomiting, and an adnexal mass.
3. **Surgical Causes**:
    - **Acute Appendicitis**: Most common non-obstetric surgical acute abdomen; presents with abdominal pain and rebound tenderness (shifted superiorly and laterally due to the enlarging gravid uterus).
    - **Intestinal Obstruction / Volvulus**: Severe colicky pain, abdominal distension, obstipation, and persistent vomiting.
4. **Medical & Urological Causes**:
    - **Acute Pyelonephritis / Severe UTI**: Flank/hypogastric pain, fever with chills/rigors, dysuria, and costovertebral angle tenderness.
    - **Severe Pre-Eclampsia / HELLP Syndrome / Acute Fatty Liver (AFLP)**: Epigastric or right upper quadrant/lower abdominal pain due to hepatic subcapsular stretch or liver microvascular ischemia.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Obstetric Emergencies**: Abruptio placentae (woody-hard tender uterus), uterine rupture (loss of uterine contour/station), and preterm labor (progressive cervical change).
- **Gynecological Emergencies**: Red degeneration of fibroid and twisted ovarian cyst.
- **Surgical Emergency**: Acute appendicitis (pain displaced upward and outward by gravid uterus).
- **Medical Emergency**: Acute pyelonephritis (fever + flank/hypogastric pain) and HELLP syndrome.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Differential diagnosis of lower abdominal pain in the third trimester encompasses life-threatening obstetric conditions (abruptio placentae, uterine rupture, labor), gynecological pathologies (red degeneration of fibroid, ovarian torsion), and non-obstetric surgical/medical emergencies (acute appendicitis, pyelonephritis).**
