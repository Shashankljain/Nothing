# Hyperemesis Gravidarum: Investigations, Treatment, and Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hyperemesis Gravidarum (HG)**: A severe, severe-type vomiting of pregnancy (pernicious vomiting) characterized by intractable nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy that deleteriously affects maternal health and incapacitates day-to-day activities.
- **Classic RCOG / ACOG Diagnostic Triad**:
    1. **Weight Loss**: >5% loss of pre-pregnancy body weight.
    2. **Dehydration**: Clinical signs of volume depletion.
    3. **Electrolyte Imbalance & Ketonuria**: Detection of urine ketone bodies (+ to +++) with altered serum electrolytes.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. High-Risk Factors

- **Parity & Age**: More common in primigravidae and younger women.
- **Body Mass Index (BMI)**: Low pre-pregnancy body mass index (LBMI).
- **Associated Obstetric Conditions**: Multiple pregnancy (twins/triplets) and Hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy) due to markedly elevated human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) titers.
- **Personal & Family History**: History of motion sickness, migraine, hyperemesis in previous pregnancy (15% recurrence), or family history (mother/sisters).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Co-existing _Helicobacter pylori_ infection.

#### B. Pathogenetic Theories

- **Hormonal Factors**:
    - **Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**: Peak levels around 8–10 weeks coincide with peak symptom severity.
    - **Estrogen & Progesterone**: High estrogen levels and progesterone-induced relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter with delayed gastric emptying.
    - **Thyrotoxicosis**: Transient gestational hyperthyroidism due to structural homology between hCG and TSH.
- **Neurogenic & Psychological Factors**: Aggravated by stress, anxiety, or unplanned pregnancy.

- **Dehydration & Electrolyte Loss**: Loss of water and hydrochloric acid (HCl) leads to hypovolemia, hyponatremia (<135 mEq/L), hypokalemia (<3.5 mEq/L), hypochloremia, and early metabolic alkalosis.
- **Starvation & Ketosis**: Exhaustion of hepatic glycogen stores forces the body to metabolize fat reserves, resulting in accumulation of acetoacetic acid and beta-hydroxybutyric acid, leading to **ketonuria** and late metabolic acidosis.
- **Hemoconcentration**: Reduced plasma volume causes hemoconcentration, elevated hematocrit, and prerenal azotemia, increasing thromboembolism risk.
- **Vitamin Deficiencies**: Depletion of **Thiamine (Vitamin B1)** causes neurotoxicity, while Vitamin K deficiency induces hypoprothrombinemia.

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- Persistent, continuous vomiting occurring throughout the day accompanied by severe retching.
- Inability to retain any oral food or fluids.
- Epigastric pain or burning sensation due to reflux retching.
- Constipation and marked oliguria (scanty, dark urine).

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Features of Dehydration**: Dry, coated tongue, sunken eyes, dry loss of skin turgor, and weight loss >5%.
- **Vital Signs**: Tachycardia (pulse >100 bpm), hypotension (systolic BP <100 mmHg), and low-grade pyrexia.
- **Ketoacidosis Sign**: Distinct **acetone (fruity) smell in breath**.
- **Late Unfavorable Signs**: Jaundice (hepatic microvascular damage), confusion, nystagmus, and altered sensorium.

---

### 5. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Urine Examination

- **Urine Acetone / Ketones**: Positive for ketone bodies (+ to +++) using dipstick/nitroprusside test.
- **Specific Gravity & Volume**: Elevated specific gravity (>1.030) and oliguria.
- **Urine Chloride & Protein**: Reduced urine chlorides; trace proteinuria from renal hypoperfusion.

#### B. Blood & Biochemical Studies

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Raised hemoglobin (>14 g/dL) and hematocrit (>45%) secondary to hemoconcentration.
- **Serum Electrolytes**: Hyponatremia (<135 mEq/L), Hypokalemia (<3.5 mEq/L), and Hypochloremia (<95 mEq/L).
- **Renal Function Tests (RFT)**: Elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine (>1.0 mg/dL) due to prerenal azotemia.
- **Liver Function Tests (LFT)**: Transient, mild elevation of serum transaminases (AST and ALT up to 200 U/L) and serum bilirubin (>1.2 mg/dL) in severe cases.
- **Thyroid Function Test (TFT)**: Transient gestational thyrotoxicosis with suppressed serum TSH (<0.1 mIU/L) and elevated free T4/T3 levels in up to 60% of cases (resolves spontaneously by 18 weeks).
- **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Early metabolic alkalosis (from loss of gastric HCl) evolving into late metabolic acidosis (from starvation ketoacidosis).

#### C. Imaging & Diagnostic Modalities

- **Ultrasonography (USG Pelvis - Gold Standard)**: Mandatory to:
    1. Confirm viable intrauterine pregnancy and gestational age.
    2. Rule out **Hydatidiform Mole (snowstorm appearance)**.
    3. Exclude **multiple pregnancy** (twins/triplets).
- **Electrocardiogram (ECG)**: Indicated in severe hypokalemia (reveals T-wave flattening/inversion, ST depression, and U waves).

#### A. Criteria for Hospitalization

- Protracted nausea and vomiting despite oral antiemetics.
- Continued vomiting with ketonuria and/or weight loss >5%.
- Clinical signs of severe dehydration, hemodynamic instability, or co-existing medical complications.

#### B. Supportive & Environmental Measures

- **NPO (Nil Per Os)**: Oral feeding is completely withheld for at least 24 hours after the cessation of vomiting to rest the gastrointestinal tract.
- Patient is placed in a quiet, dark, well-ventilated room; sympathetic but firm nursing care and psychological reassurance are extended.

#### C. Intravenous Fluid & Electrolyte Replacement Protocol

- **Total Fluid Requirement**: **2.5 to 3 Liters in 24 hours**.
- **Fluid Selection**: **Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)** or **Ringer's Lactate (RL)** to correct volume depletion and hyponatremia.
- **CRITICAL RULE ON DEXTROSE**: **Never administer IV Dextrose solutions alone without prior Thiamine administration!** Infusing glucose into a thiamine-depleted state precipitates acute Wernicke's Encephalopathy.
- **Potassium Chloride (KCl) Replacement**: Add 20–40 mEq KCl per liter of IV fluid if serum potassium is <3.5 mEq/L (monitored with urine output >30 mL/hr).

#### D. Pharmacotherapy (Antiemetic Regimen)

1. **First-Line Antiemetics (ACOG)**:
    - **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)** 10–25 mg TID alone or combined with **Doxylamine succinate** 10 mg at bedtime/BD.
2. **Second-Line Antiemetics**:
    - **Promethazine** 12.5–25 mg IV/IM or **Prochlorperazine** 5–10 mg IV/IM every 6–8 hours.
    - **Metoclopramide** 10 mg IV/IM every 8 hours (prokinetic and central D2 antagonist).
3. **Third-Line Antiemetics**:
    - **Ondansetron** 4–8 mg IV/oral every 8 hours (5-HT3 receptor antagonist; highly effective, used cautiously at <10 weeks due to minor potential cardiac/cleft risks).
4. **Refractory / Intractable Cases**:
    - **Corticosteroid Therapy**: **Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV BD** or **Methylprednisolone 16 mg IV TID** for 3 days, followed by oral prednisolone taper. Reserves for intractable cases failing conventional antiemetics.
5. **Gastroprotection & Thromboprophylaxis**:
    - **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPI)**: Inj. Pantoprazole 40 mg IV OD to prevent stress ulcers and Mallory-Weiss mucosal bleeding.
    - **Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH)**: Prophylactic Enoxaparin 40 mg SC OD indicated in severe hemoconcentration to prevent DVT.

#### E. Diet Restoration Protocol

- Once vomiting ceases and urine is free of acetone for 24 hours, oral fluids are started (clear liquids in small sips).
- Progress to dry carbohydrate foods (biscuits, dry toast, puffed rice) in small, frequent feeds (every 2 hours).
- Avoid fatty, fried, spicy, and strongly odorous foods. Gradually restore normal balanced diet.

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

1. **Neurological Complications (Most Serious)**:
    - **Wernicke's Encephalopathy**: Caused by severe **Thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency**. Presents with the classic clinical triad of:
        - (a) **Ophthalmoplegia / Nystagmus**,
        - (b) **Ataxia (gait unsteadiness)**, and
        - (c) **Mental confusion / Memory loss**.
    - **Korsakoff's Psychosis**: Irreversible confabulation and amnesia following uncorrected Wernicke's.
    - **Central Pontine Myelinolysis**: Caused by rapid, overly aggressive correction of severe hyponatremia.
    - **Peripheral Neuritis** and optic neuritis.
2. **Gastrointestinal Complications**:
    - **Mallory-Weiss Syndrome**: Longitudinal mucosal tears at the gastroesophageal junction causing hematemesis.
    - **Stress Ulceration** and severe esophagitis.
3. **Hepatic & Renal Complications**:
    - Jaundice, hepatic microvesicular steatosis, and hepatic failure.
    - **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** secondary to severe hypovolemic prerenal azotemia.
4. **Hematological & Metabolic Complications**:
    - **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)** and pulmonary embolism from severe hemoconcentration.
    - **Hypoprothrombinemia** secondary to Vitamin K deficiency.

#### B. Fetal Complications

- **Small for Gestational Age (SGA) / Low Birth Weight (LBW)**: Increased risk if severe maternal weight loss persists.
- **Preterm Birth (18%)**: Secondary to maternal metabolic stress.
- _Note_: Fetal loss or congenital malformations are **not increased** if maternal hydration and nutrition are restored promptly.

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden Thiamine Rule**: ALWAYS administer **Parenteral Thiamine (Vitamin B1 100 mg IV)** BEFORE or alongside starting IV Dextrose infusions in a malnourished hyperemesis patient. IV glucose acts as a substrate for carbohydrate metabolism that rapidly consumes residual thiamine stores, instantly precipitating fatal or irreversible **Wernicke's Encephalopathy**!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Assuming Ultrasonography (USG) in Hyperemesis Gravidarum is performed solely for confirming gestational age.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **USG is MANDATORY in Hyperemesis to rule out Molar Pregnancy (Hydatidiform Mole) and Multiple Gestation (Twins)**, both of which produce hyper-elevated beta-hCG levels that trigger severe refractory hyperemesis.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Triad**: Severe NVP with **weight loss >5%, dehydration, and ketonuria**.
- **Mandatory First Rule**: **Administer Parenteral Thiamine (100 mg IV) BEFORE starting Dextrose fluids** to prevent Wernicke's Encephalopathy.
- **Investigation of Choice (USG)**: USG Pelvis to rule out **molar pregnancy and twin gestation**.
- **Fluid Management**: **1.5 to 3 L/day of Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate** with KCl replacement.
- **Key Antiemetics**: First-line: Pyridoxine + Doxylamine; Second-line: Metoclopramide / Ondansetron; Refractory: Hydrocortisone IV.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Hyperemesis gravidarum is a severe form of nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy characterized by weight loss >5%, dehydration, ketonuria, and electrolyte imbalance, diagnosed by clinical presentation and sonography (to exclude molar/twin gestation), and managed by hospital admission, rapid IV normal saline rehydration, mandatory thiamine supplementation prior to dextrose, and stepwise antiemetic therapy.**
