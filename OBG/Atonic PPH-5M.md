# Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) — Causes & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

- **Clinical Definition**: Any amount of bleeding from or into the genital tract occurring within 24 hours following delivery of the fetus that adversely affects the general condition of the patient, as evidenced by a rising pulse rate and falling blood pressure.
- **Quantitative Definition (WHO & ACOG)**: Cumulative blood loss **≥500 mL** following a vaginal delivery or **≥1000 mL** following a Cesarean section.

#### B. Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage

- **Definition**: Postpartum hemorrhage resulting from the failure of the uterine myometrium to contract and retract effectively after the separation and expulsion of the placenta.
- **Incidence & Significance**: Atonicity of the uterus is the single most common cause of primary PPH, accounting for approximately **80% of all PPH cases**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors (Causes of Atonicity) [⭐ High-Yield]

Myometrial atony occurs when physiological mechanisms interfering with muscle contraction and retraction are present. The causes are categorized under **Tone (Atonicity)** in the RCOG "4 Ts" framework:

```

         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Uterine Overdistension  │               │ Myometrial Exhaustion   │               │ Iatrogenic & Drugs      │
│ • Multiple pregnancy    │               │ • Prolonged labor (>12h)│               │ • Deep anesthesia       │
│ • Polyhydramnios        │               │ • Precipitate labor     │               │ • Tocolytics / MgSO₄    │
│ • Fetal Macrosomia (>4kg│               │ • Grand multiparity     │               │ • Sudden oxytocin stop  │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Uterine Overdistension** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **Multiple Pregnancy (Twins / Triplets)**: Excessive muscle fiber stretching reduces contractility.
    - **Polyhydramnios**: Rapid loss of large volume liquor amnii impairs post-delivery retraction.
    - **Fetal Macrosomia (Birth Weight >4 kg)**.
2. **Myometrial Exhaustion & Dysfunction** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **Prolonged Labor (>12 hours)**: Muscle glycogen depletion, exhaustion, and intra-amniotic infection (amnionitis) impair retraction.
    - **Precipitate Labor**: Rapid delivery leaves insufficient time for physiological retraction to keep pace.
    - **High Parity (Grand Multipara)**: Fibrotic replacement of myometrial muscle fibers impairs uniform contraction.
    - **Uterine Leiomyomas (Fibroids)**: Mechanically distort muscle fibers and prevent uniform retraction.
3. **Pharmacological & Anesthetic Agents** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - **Tocolytic Therapy**: Isoxsuprine, Nifedipine, Terbutaline, and **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** administered prior to delivery.
    - **Deep General Anesthesia**: Halothane, ether, or halogenated agents cause profound uterine muscle relaxation.
    - **Abrupt Stoppage of Prolonged Oxytocin**: Down-regulation of oxytocin receptors following prolonged labor induction or augmentation.
4. **Mismanagement of the Third Stage of Labor** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    
    - Pulling the umbilical cord before placental separation.
    - Kneading, squeezing, or fiddling with the uterine fundus before separation.
    - Rapid delivery of the baby preventing swift uterine adaptation.
5. **Maternal & Constitutional Factors**:
    
    - **Severe Pre-existing Anemia (Hb <10 g/dL)**: Reduces tissue oxygenation, lowering myometrial tone and ability to withstand blood loss.
    - **Obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²)** and **Maternal Age >40 years**.
    - **Previous History of PPH**: Recurrence risk is **5–15%**.

#### Core Mechanism — The "Living Ligature" Concept:

- Under normal physiological conditions, middle layer myometrial fibers are arranged in a criss-cross "figure-of-8" pattern surrounding uterine blood vessels. Their contraction and retraction act as anatomical ligatures, obliterating the lumen of torn sinuses.
- In **atonic PPH**, failure of retraction leaves these torn sinuses wide open, permitting direct blood flow from uterine vessels at a rate of **500–600 mL/minute**, causing massive hemorrhage within minutes.

### 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Physical Findings & Abdominal Palpation

- **Abdominal Palpation [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - The uterus feels **soft, flabby, boggy, and ill-defined**, occupying a high level well above the umbilicus.
    - **Diagnostic Hallmark**: The flabby uterus becomes **firm and hard upon abdominal massaging**, expressing blood and dark clots through the vagina during massage.
- **Vaginal Bleeding**: Continuous trickle or sudden massive gush of dark red blood following delivery of the baby.

#### B. Hemodynamic Signs of Shock (Rule of 30 & Shock Index) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rule of 30 for PPH Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Fall in Systolic Blood Pressure by **≥30 mmHg**.
    - Rise in Heart Rate by **≥30 beats/minute**.
    - Respiratory Rate **>30 breaths/minute**.
    - Drop in Hemoglobin / Hematocrit by **≥30%**.
    - Fall in Urine Output to **<30 mL/hour**.
- **Shock Index (SI = Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP)**: Normal is **0.5–0.7**; a value **>0.9 to 1.0** indicates major blood loss (>20–30% volume deficit) requiring urgent blood resuscitation.

---

### 5. Diagnosis & Emergency Investigation Panel [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Clinical Diagnosis**: Made immediately at the bedside by combining **visible vaginal bleeding** with an **absent/flabby uterine tone on abdominal palpation**.
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Hematocrit**: Monitors drop in hemoglobin and hematocrit (>30% fall).
3. **ABO & Rh D Blood Grouping and Cross-Matching**: Immediate ABO/Rh D typing; cross-match at least **2 to 4 units of Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)**.
4. **Bedside Clotting Test (Lee-White)**: 2 mL of venous blood in a glass tube should clot within 6–10 minutes. Failure to clot or a soft fragile clot indicates DIC.
5. **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Serum Fibrinogen level (**target >2 g/L**), D-dimer, and Platelet count (**target >50,000/μL**).
6. **Bedside Pelvic Ultrasonography (USG)**: Excludes retained placental fragments, membranes, echogenic intrauterine blood clots, or broad ligament hematoma.

### 7. Prevention — Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL) [⭐ High-Yield]

AMTSL is a mandatory routine for all delivering women that **reduces the incidence of PPH by 60%**:

1. **Uterotonic Administration**: Administer **Injection Oxytocin 10 IU IM** (or slow IV) to the mother within **1 minute of birth of the baby** (after ruling out a second twin). _(Alternatively, Tab. Misoprostol 600 μg PO/PR or Inj. Methergine 0.2 mg IM)_.
2. **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews Maneuver)**: Counter-pressure is applied abdominally upward on the lower uterine segment while applying gentle traction downward and backward on the clamped cord during a contraction.
3. **Uterine Massage**: Massage the uterine fundus immediately after delivery of the placenta until hard, and reassess every 15 minutes for the first 2 hours postpartum.

#### STEP 1: Immediate Resuscitation & Golden 1 Hour Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden 1 Hour**: Immediate resuscitation initiated within the first hour ensures maximum survival; mortality rises sharply if delayed.
- **Call for Extra Assistance**: Activate multidisciplinary emergency team (Obstetrician, Anesthetist, Hematologist, ICU team).
- **Airway & Oxygenation**: Administer 100% Oxygen via face mask at 10–15 L/minute.
- **Intravenous Access & Resuscitation**:
    - Insert two large-bore IV cannulae (**14G or 16G**).
    - Rapidly infuse up to **2 L of warmed isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate)** and **1–2 L colloids**.
    - Transfuse cross-matched PRBCs as soon as available; maintain a balanced transfusion ratio of **4 units Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) for every 6 units PRBC** to prevent dilutional coagulopathy. Transfuse platelets if <50,000/μL and cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen <1 g/L.
- **Bladder Decompression**: Insert Foley catheter connected to a urometer to keep bladder empty and monitor hourly urine output (**target >30 mL/hr**).
- **Fundal Massage**: Perform continuous abdominal uterine massage to stimulate natural contraction.

#### STEP 2: Pharmacological / Medical Management (Uterotonics & Anti-fibrinolytics) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Oxytocin**: **10 to 40 IU in 1 L Normal Saline IV drip** at 40–60 drops/min.
2. **Methylergometrine (Methergine)**: **0.2 mg IV slowly** over 5 minutes; excites tetanic contractions. _Strictly contraindicated in pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, hypertension, and cardiac disease_.
3. **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α)**: **250 μg IM or intramyometrial**; repeat every 15 to 90 minutes (maximum 8 doses = 2 mg). _Strictly contraindicated in active bronchial asthma_.
4. **Misoprostol (PGE₁)**: **800 to 1000 μg per rectum (PR)** or sublingual single dose.
5. **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: **1 g IV slowly over 10 minutes** (WHO recommendation); repeat 1 g IV after 30 minutes if bleeding continues.

#### STEP 3: Mechanical & Tamponade Interventions [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Bimanual Uterine Compression (Hamilton Maneuver)**:
    - Introduce gloved right hand into vagina as a fist in the anterior fornix.
    - Place left hand abdominally behind the uterine fundus, squeezing the uterus firmly against the vaginal fist until tone is restored.
2. **Uterine Balloon Tamponade (First-Line Conservative Surgical Action)**:
    - Insert a **Bakri Balloon**, **Foley Catheter**, or **Condom Catheter** into the uterine cavity.
    - Inflate balloon with **200 to 500 mL of warm sterile normal saline**.
    - Exerts direct internal hydrostatic pressure on open sinuses; **avoids hysterectomy in 78% of cases**. Retained for 4 to 24 hours under antibiotic cover.
3. **Tight Intrauterine Packing**: Pack a 5-meter long, 8-cm wide gauze strip tightly into the uterine cavity from fundus downward (removed after 24 hours).

#### STEP 4: Conservative Surgical Methods (Uterine Devascularization & Compression Sutures) [⭐ High-Yield]

If medical and tamponade methods fail, proceed to laparotomy for fertility-preserving surgical procedures:

1. **B-Lynch Compression Sutures**:
    - Vertical bracing sutures using **No. 1 Chromic Catgut or Vicryl** passed from lower segment over fundus, compressing anterior and posterior uterine walls against each other. Success rate **~80%**.
2. **Stepwise Uterine Devascularization**:
    - _Uterine Artery Ligation (O'Leary)_: Bilateral ligation at upper and lower uterine segment medial to vessels (75–90% success).
    - _Utero-Ovarian Vessel Ligation_: Ligated just below ovarian ligament anastomosis.
    - _Internal Iliac (Hypogastric) Artery Ligation_: Bilateral ligation of anterior division below posterior origin reduces pelvic pulse pressure by **85%**, facilitating stable clot formation without pelvic tissue necrosis.
3. **Angiographic Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)**: Fluoroscopic gelfoam embolization by interventional radiologist (success >90%).

#### STEP 5: Life-Saving Obstetric Hysterectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated as a last resort when all uterine-sparing surgical measures fail and maternal life is in jeopardy. Perform subtotal or total hysterectomy promptly involving a second consultant.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Immediate Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemorrhagic / Hypovolemic Shock & Cardiac Arrest**.
- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)** secondary to washout of clotting factors and tissue factor release.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** due to renal cortical/tubular necrosis from prolonged hypotension.
- **Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) & Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)**.

#### B. Remote / Delayed Maternal Sequelae [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sheehan's Syndrome (Postpartum Ischemic Pituitary Necrosis)**: Caused by severe intrapartum hypotension resulting in pituitary insufficiency, failure of lactation, amenorrhea, and hypothyroidism.
- **Puerperal Sepsis & Pelvic Thrombophlebitis**.
- **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections** and broad ligament hematoma formation.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden 1 Hour & Shock Index**: Survival in primary PPH is directly tied to initiating balanced resuscitation within the **Golden 1 Hour**. A **Shock Index (Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP) >0.9** serves as an immediate bedside indicator of severe volume loss (>20–30%), mandating rapid blood transfusion.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering Inj. Methergine to a PPH patient with co-existing severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, OR giving Carboprost to an asthmatic patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Methergine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in pre-eclampsia/hypertension** (precipitates hypertensive crisis or eclamptic fits); use **Oxytocin** or **Misoprostol** instead.
    - **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α) is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in bronchial asthma** (precipitates severe bronchospasm); use **Misoprostol** instead.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Primary PPH = Blood loss ≥500 mL (vaginal) or ≥1000 mL (CS) within 24 hours of delivery. Atonicity accounts for **80% of cases**.
- **Causes**: Uterine overdistension (twins/polyhydramnios), labor exhaustion, grand multiparity, deep GA, tocolytic/MgSO₄ drugs, and mismanaged 3rd stage.
- **AMTSL Prevention**: Oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 min of birth + Controlled Cord Traction + Fundal Massage (reduces PPH by 60%).
- **First-line Medical Drugs**: Oxytocin 10–40 IU IV, Methergine 0.2 mg IV (avoid in HTN), Carboprost 250 μg IM (avoid in asthma), Misoprostol 800–1000 μg PR, Tranexamic Acid 1 g IV.
- **Conservative Tamponade & Surgery**: Bakri Balloon (200–500 mL saline, 78% success), Bimanual compression, B-Lynch compression sutures (80% success), Bilateral uterine/internal iliac artery ligation (85% pulse pressure reduction).
