# Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks** (Definition + Concise, structured subheadings covering screening, criteria, management at 32 weeks, and complications)

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)** is defined as **carbohydrate intolerance** of variable severity with onset or first recognition during the present pregnancy.
- It usually presents late in the **second trimester** or during the **third trimester** (typically at **24–28 weeks**) due to the peak surge of placental contrainsulin hormones.
- It excludes women who had pre-existing overt diabetes prior to pregnancy, although it may resolve or persist after delivery.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors

- **Diabetogenic State of Pregnancy**: Driven by placental hormones causing peripheral **insulin resistance** and **β-cell dysfunction**:
    - **Human Placental Lactogen (hPL)** (primary contrainsulin hormone)
    - **Estrogen** and **Progesterone**
    - **Free Cortisol** and **Prolactin**
    - **Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α)** (inversely correlated with insulin sensitivity)
- **Maternal Risk Factors**:
    - **Family history of diabetes** (parents, siblings, uncles, aunts, or grandparents)
    - **Previous birth of a macrosomic baby** (≥4.0 kg)
    - **Previous GDM**, unexplained **stillbirth**, or **intrauterine fetal death (IUFD)**
    - **Maternal obesity** (BMI ≥25 or ≥30 kg/m²) and maternal age **>30 years**
    - **Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (PCOS)** and pre-existing **hypertension** (≥140/90 mmHg)
    - **Polyhydramnios**, **recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis**, or persistent **glycosuria** in present pregnancy

---

### 3. Classification

- **Priscilla White's Classification (Gestational Subset)**:
    - **Class A1**: GDM controlled on **Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) / Diet** alone (Fasting plasma glucose <105 mg/dL, 2-hr PP <120 mg/dL).
    - **Class A2**: GDM requiring **Pharmacotherapy** (**Insulin** or **Metformin**) (Fasting plasma glucose >105 mg/dL, 2-hr PP >120 mg/dL).

---

### 4. Pathophysiology (Pedersen Hypothesis) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal Hyperglycemia**: Contrainsulin hormones induce peripheral insulin resistance.
- **Placental Transport**: **Maternal glucose crosses the placenta** via **facilitated diffusion (GLUT-1 transporters)**. Maternal insulin **does NOT cross the placenta**.
- **Fetal Hyperinsulinemia**: Excessive glucose influx causes **fetal hyperglycemia**, which stimulates hypertrophy and hyperplasia of **fetal pancreatic β-islet cells**.
- **Anabolic Growth Surge**: High **fetal insulin** and **Insulin-like Growth Factors (IGFs)** act as potent growth factors, increasing glucose utilization, glycogen storage, and fat deposition.
- **Selective Adiposity**: Fat accumulates selectively around the fetal shoulders and trunk, leading to **fetal macrosomia**and **shoulder dystocia**.
- **Absence of Malformations**: In true GDM, hyperglycemia develops **after organogenesis** (>20–24 weeks), so **gross congenital anomalies (GCA) do NOT occur**.

### 5. Diagnosis / Investigations (Screening & Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Diagnostic Strategy & Timing

- **Universal Screening**: Recommended for all pregnant women between **24–28 weeks of gestation**.
- **High-Risk Screening**: Done at the **first antenatal visit**; if normal, repeat at **24–28 weeks** (minimum time gap: 4 weeks).

#### DIPSI Criteria (Government of India Recommended Single-Step Test)

- **Procedure**: Single-step non-fasting test.
    - Patient is given **75 g oral anhydrous glucose** dissolved in **300 mL water**, swallowed over **5–10 minutes**, **irrespective of the time of the last meal**.
    - Venous blood sample drawn at **2 hours** and measured using a plasma-calibrated glucometer.
    - If patient vomits within 30 minutes, repeat test on another day; if vomits after 30 minutes, continue test.
- **Diagnostic Cut-Off Values (2-Hour Postprandial Blood Glucose)**:
    - **<140 mg/dL**: Normal glucose tolerance (repeat at **24–28 weeks**).
    - **≥140 mg/dL to 199 mg/dL**: Diagnosed as **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)**.
    - **≥200 mg/dL**: Diagnosed as **Pre-Gestational / Overt Diabetes**.

#### Target Glycemic Values in Pregnancy

- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)**: **<95 mg/dL**
- **1-Hour Postprandial (PP)**: **<140 mg/dL**
- **2-Hour Postprandial (PP)**: **<120 mg/dL**
- **Glycosylated Hemoglobin (HbA1c)**: **<6.0%**

### 6. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **During Pregnancy**:
    - **Pre-eclampsia / Gestational Hypertension** (25% risk)
    - **Polyhydramnios** (25–50% risk due to fetal osmotic diuresis) → increases risk of **Preterm Labor (PTL)**, **PROM**, **Cord Prolapse**, and **PPH**
    - **Infections**: Vulvovaginal **candidiasis**, **asymptomatic bacteriuria** / UTI, and **puerperal sepsis**
    - **Ketoacidosis** (rare in GDM compared to Pre-GDM)
- **During Labor & Postpartum**:
    - Increased rate of **Cesarean Section** and **operative vaginal delivery**
    - **Shoulder dystocia**, **perineal lacerations**, and **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**
- **Long-Term Effects**:
    - **50% risk of developing Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)** within 5–20 years
    - **50% recurrence risk** in subsequent pregnancies

#### B. Fetal & Neonatal Complications

- **Fetal Macrosomia** (birth weight ≥4.0 kg)
- **Shoulder Dystocia** and birth injuries (**Erb's palsy [C5–C6]**, clavicular fractures)
- **Neonatal Hypoglycemia** (<40 mg/dL or <35 mg/dL within 2 hours of birth due to sudden loss of maternal glucose with persistent fetal hyperinsulinemia)
- **Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)**: Fetal hyperinsulinemia suppresses cortisol-mediated activation of type II pneumocytes, inhibiting **surfactant** synthesis.
- **Polycythemia & Hyperbilirubinemia**: Fetal hypoxia increases erythropoietin → **polycythemia** → RBC breakdown → jaundice.
- **Neonatal Hypocalcemia** (<7 mg/dL) and **Hypomagnesemia** (<7 mg/dL)
- **Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM)**: Interventricular septal hypertrophy driven by insulin excess
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD)** / Stillbirth in late pregnancy (due to severe hypoxia, lactic acidosis, and placental villous edema)
- **Important Note**: **NO Congenital Structural Malformations** occur in true GDM.

#### Antenatal Fetal Monitoring Protocol at 32 Weeks Gestation

- **Weekly Non-Stress Test (NST)** starting at 32 weeks.
- **Weekly Biophysical Score (BPS / BPP)** starting at 32 weeks.
- **Serial Growth Ultrasound**: Done every 3 weeks (specifically at **28–30 weeks** and **34–36 weeks**) with a minimum gap of 3 weeks to rule out **polyhydramnios** and **macrosomia**.
- **Weekly Capillary Blood Glucose Checks** throughout the third trimester.
- **Umbilical Artery Doppler**: Indicated only if complicated by **PIH** or **diabetic vasculopathy**.
- _Note_: **Fetal Echocardiography is NOT required in GDM** (no risk of CVS anomalies).

#### Non-Pharmacological Management (Medical Nutrition Therapy & Exercise)

- **Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT)**:
    - Caloric ratio: **40% Complex Carbohydrates**, **40% Fats**, and **20% Proteins**.
    - Caloric allowance:
        - Normal weight: **30 kcal/kg/day** (add **+350 kcal/day** in pregnancy).
        - Overweight (BMI >25): **Subtract 500 kcal/day** (24 kcal/kg/day).
    - Meal pattern: **3 main meals** (Breakfast 25%, Lunch 30%, Dinner 30%) + **3 snacks**.
    - MNT trial given for **2 weeks**.
- **Exercise**: Aerobic exercise / brisk walking for **30 minutes daily** or **10–15 minutes after each meal** to control postprandial glucose surges.

#### Pharmacological Management Guidelines

- **Indications**: Failure of MNT to achieve targets after 2 weeks, or initial 2-hour PP blood sugar ≥200 mg/dL.
- **First-Line Oral Agent (Government of India Guidelines)**: **Metformin** (preferred when diagnosed >20 weeks).
- **Gold Standard Drug**: **Human Insulin**.

#### Obstetric Management & Delivery Timing

- **Class A1 GDM (Controlled on Diet)**: Delivery at **≥39 weeks**.
- **Class A2 GDM (Controlled on Drugs - Well Controlled)**: Delivery at **>39 weeks**.
- **Class A2 GDM (Suboptimally Controlled)**: Delivery at **>37 weeks**.
- **Mode of Delivery**: **Vaginal delivery** is preferred.
- **Indications for Cesarean Section**: Estimated fetal weight **≥4.5 kg** (to prevent shoulder dystocia).

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Fasting Glycosuria**: Physiological glycosuria occurs in mid-pregnancy due to raised GFR and a lower renal threshold. However, **fasting glycosuria** or **glycosuria on ≥2 occasions after 20 weeks** is ominous and mandates immediate diagnostic testing for GDM.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing the occurrence of **Congenital Malformations** between Pre-Gestational Diabetes (Pre-GDM) and Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM).
- **Distinguishing Fact**: Pre-GDM causes gross congenital anomalies (VSD, TGA, NTD, Caudal Regression) because hyperglycemia is present during **first-trimester organogenesis**. True GDM develops **after 24 weeks** (after organogenesis is complete), so it causes **macrosomia, RDS, and neonatal hypoglycemia**, but **NO congenital structural malformations**.

- **D**: **DIPSI criteria** single-step test using **75 g glucose** non-fasting (≥140 mg/dL)
- **I**: **Insulin resistance** mediated by **hPL**, progesterone, cortisol, and TNF-α

### Quick Revision

- **DIPSI Diagnosis**: **75 g non-fasting oral glucose**; 2-hour plasma glucose **≥140 mg/dL**.
- **Target Glycemic Values**: Fasting **<95 mg/dL**, 1-hr PP **<140 mg/dL**, 2-hr PP **<120 mg/dL**, HbA1c **<6.0%**.
- **32 Weeks Surveillance**: Weekly **NST** and **BPP**; serial growth scan every 3 weeks to check for macrosomia and polyhydramnios.
- **32 Weeks Management**: 2-week trial of **MNT** (40:40:20 ratio) + **exercise**; if targets fail, start **Metformin** (up to 2 g/day) or **Human Premix Insulin 30:70**.
- **Key Complications**: **Macrosomia**, **Shoulder Dystocia**, **Neonatal Hypoglycemia**, **RDS**, **Polyhydramnios**, **PIH**(NO congenital malformations in GDM).

### ### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) is carbohydrate intolerance with onset or first recognition during pregnancy, diagnosed via DIPSI single-step testing (2-hour plasma glucose ≥140 mg/dL after 75 g non-fasting glucose), and managed at 32 weeks through strict glycemic targets, medical nutrition therapy, metformin/insulin, and weekly fetal monitoring to prevent macrosomia and neonatal hypoglycemia.**
