# Immunological Tests for Diagnosis of Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Immunological Pregnancy Tests**: Diagnostic immunoassays designed to detect the presence of **human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**—specifically its antigenically unique **β-subunit**—in maternal urine or serum using monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies.
- **Diagnostic Role**: Provides the earliest non-invasive biochemical confirmation of pregnancy, with modern test accuracy ranging between **98.6% and 99%**.

---

### 2. Biological Basis & Structure of hCG [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Biochemical Nature

- **Glycoprotein Hormone**: Secreted by the syncytiotrophoblasts of the developing placenta with a molecular weight of **36,000 to 40,000 Daltons** and a plasma half-life of **36 hours**.

#### B. Subunit Composition

- **α-Subunit (92 amino acids)**: Biochemically identical to the α-subunits of pituitary LH, FSH, and TSH.
- **β-Subunit (145 amino acids)**: Structurally distinct and unique to hCG.
- **Immunological Specificity**: Commercial pregnancy test kits utilize specific **anti-β-hCG antibodies** to eliminate cross-reactivity with circulating pituitary LH, FSH, or TSH.

#### C. Secretory Dynamics & Doubling Time in Early Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Earliest Blood Detection**: Detected in maternal serum by radioimmunoassay as early as **8 to 9 days post-fertilization / post-ovulation** (3 to 5 days before the expected missed period).
- **Doubling Time**: In a normal viable intrauterine pregnancy, serum β-hCG levels double every **48 hours (1.4 to 2 days)**, rising by **33% to 66%** every 48 hours.
- **Peak Level**: Reaches a maximum concentration of **100,000 to 200,000 IU/L (100–200 IU/mL)** between **60 and 80 days (8 to 10 weeks)** of gestation.
- **Nadir / Trough**: Decreases slowly to a baseline plateau of **10,000 to 20,000 IU/L** by **100 to 130 days (16 to 20 weeks)**, remaining constant until term.
- **Postpartum Clearance**: Clears completely from maternal circulation within **2 weeks post-delivery** (or 2 to 4 weeks post-abortion).

#### A. Two-Site Sandwich Immunoassay (Membrane ELISA / Card Test / UPT) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sample**: First voided morning urine sample (contains the highest concentration of hCG) collected in a clean container without soap residue.
- **Principle & Mechanism**:
    - The test card membrane contains mobile dye-conjugated monoclonal **anti-β-hCG antibodies** at the sample well and fixed immobilized antibodies at the test and control windows.
    - When urine containing hCG is added, it binds to the mobile dye-antibody complex, migrates by capillary action, and is trapped at the test line by fixed antibodies, forming a visible **colored band**.
- **Sensitivity Threshold**: **20 to 60 mIU/mL** of urine.
- **Earliest Positive Timing**: On the **first day of the missed period** (28th day of a 28-day cycle / 14 days post-ovulation).
- **Turnaround Time**: **4 to 5 minutes**.

#### B. Serum Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sample**: Maternal venous blood / serum.
- **Sensitivity Threshold**: **1.0 to 2.0 mIU/mL** of serum.
- **Earliest Positive Timing**: **5 days before the first missed period** (8–9 days post-fertilization).

#### C. Quantitative Radioimmunoassay (RIA) & Fluorescent Immunoassay (FIA) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Principle**: Measures exact concentration of serum β-hCG using radiolabeled or fluorescent tracer antibodies.
- **Sensitivity**: Detection limit of **<1.0 mIU/mL** (Fluorescent Immunoassay [FIA] > Radioimmunoassay [RIA]).
- **Primary Indications**: Monitoring serial doubling time in suspected ectopic pregnancy, early pregnancy loss, and trophoblastic neoplasia.

#### D. Classical Agglutination Inhibition Tests (Historical Interest)

- **Direct Agglutination / Latex Agglutination Inhibition**:
    - Mixes urine with anti-hCG antibodies and latex particles coated with hCG.
    - **Positive Result (Pregnant)**: hCG in urine neutralizes anti-hCG antibodies → **NO agglutination** occurs.
    - **Negative Result (Non-Pregnant)**: Free anti-hCG antibodies agglutinate latex particles → **Agglutination present**.

#### Discriminatory Zone of β-hCG [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition**: The threshold serum β-hCG level above which a normal intrauterine gestational sac must be visualized on ultrasound:
    - **Transvaginal Sonography (TVS)**: **1500 to 2000 IU/L**.
    - **Transabdominal Sonography (TAS)**: **6500 IU/L**.

### 5. Differential Diagnosis & Diagnostic Limitations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Clinical Causes of False-Positive and False-Negative hCG Tests

|Test Result Category|Clinical Condition / Cause|Underlying Mechanism / Explanation|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**False-Positive hCG** [⭐ High-Yield]|**Exogenous hCG Injections**.|Administered for ovulation induction / luteal support in ART.|
||**Trophoblastic Neoplasms**.|Hydatidiform mole & Choriocarcinoma produce massive β-hCG (>100,000 mIU/mL).|
||**Non-Trophoblastic Tumors**.|Ectopic hCG secretion by ovarian, lung, bladder, or GI carcinomas.|
||**Renal Failure**.|Impaired renal clearance leads to accumulation of physiological hCG.|
||**Pituitary hCG & Heterophile Antibodies**.|Physiological pituitary secretion in perimenopause or serum heterophile antibodies.|
||**Hematuria / Proteinuria**.|Presence of albumin or red cell proteins interfering with membrane binding.|
|**False-Negative hCG** [⭐ High-Yield]|**Test Performed Too Early**.|Testing prior to 8–9 days post-ovulation when hCG is <20 mIU/mL.|
||**Dilute Urine Sample**.|Low specific gravity from high fluid intake lowers concentration below detection threshold.|
||**The "Hook Effect"**.|Extremely high hCG levels (molar pregnancy) saturate assay antibodies without cross-linking.|
||**Ectopic / Dying Pregnancy**.|Impaired trophoblastic mass produces low, subnormal hCG levels.|

### 6. Other Clinical Applications of hCG Testing [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Diagnosis & Surveillance of Ectopic Pregnancy**: Suboptimal β-hCG rise (<33% over 48 hours) with an empty uterus above the discriminatory zone (1500–2000 IU/L) confirms ectopic gestation.
2. **Follow-Up of Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasia (GTN)**: Weekly serum β-hCG monitoring post-suction evacuation until 3 consecutive negative reports, then monthly for 6 to 12 months to detect persistent trophoblastic disease.
3. **First & Second Trimester Aneuploidy Screening**:
    - **First Trimester Combined Screening (11–14 weeks)**: Raised free β-hCG combined with low PAPP-A and increased Nuchal Translucency (NT) detects **Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome)**.
    - **Second Trimester Quadruple Screen (15–20 weeks)**: Elevated total hCG and Dimeric Inhibin-A with low MSAFP and uE3 indicates Down Syndrome.
4. **Monitoring IVF & Assisted Reproduction**: Serial serum assays monitor successful blastocyst implantation following embryo transfer (EDD calculated by adding 266 days to date of ET).

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **First Morning Voided Urine Rule**: Always instruct the patient to use the **first voided morning urine sample** in a clean, soap-free container for point-of-care UPT cards. Soap residues, proteinuria, or heavy fluid intake before testing cause false results by interfering with antibody-binding kinetics.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Assuming a positive UPT card test always confirms a healthy intrauterine pregnancy, OR assuming a negative test completely excludes pregnancy.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - A positive UPT confirms **trophoblastic activity** (hCG present) but **cannot determine anatomical location**; it could represent an ectopic pregnancy, hydatidiform mole, or blighted ovum.
    - A single negative UPT test in a symptomatic patient with amenorrhea does not rule out early pregnancy or ectopic gestation if performed before the detection threshold is reached.

### 8. Diagram Guide

### Diagram : Two-Site Sandwich Immunoassay (Urine Pregnancy Test Card)

- **Type**: Schematic membrane immunochromatography diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Rectangular plastic cassette showing sample well (S), test line (T), and control line (C).
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Sample Well (S)** — site of urine application containing mobile dye-conjugated anti-β-hCG monoclonal antibody.
    2. **Nitrocellulose Membrane** — matrix facilitating capillary flow of hCG-antibody complex.
    3. **Test Line (T)** — immobilized anti-α-hCG antibody zone; forms pink/blue band when hCG is present (Positive).
    4. **Control Line (C)** — immobilized anti-mouse IgG antibody zone; forms mandatory band to confirm valid capillary flow (Quality Control).
- **Interpretation Annotations**:
    - _Two Bands (T + C)_ = Positive for Pregnancy.
    - _One Band (C only)_ = Negative for Pregnancy.
    - _No Control Band (C absent)_ = Invalid Test (Repeat with new card).

### 9. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: H-C-G-T-E-S-T (Clinical & Diagnostic Essentials)

- **H**: **Heterophile antibodies & Hydatidiform mole** cause false-positive / high hCG levels.
- **C**: **Card test sensitivity = 20–60 mIU/mL** (urine).
- **G**: **Glycoprotein hormone** with identical α-subunit and unique β-subunit.
- **T**: **Two-site sandwich ELISA mechanism**.
- **E**: **Earliest detection = 8–9 days post-ovulation** in serum (1–2 mIU/mL).
- **S**: **Serial 48-hour doubling time** (33% to 66% rise in normal early pregnancy).
- **T**: **TVS Discriminatory Zone = 1500–2000 IU/L**.

### Quick Revision

- **Principle**: Detection of unique **β-subunit of hCG** in urine or serum using monoclonal antibodies.
- **Sensitivities**: Card UPT (Urine) = **20–60 mIU/mL**; Serum ELISA = **1–2 mIU/mL**; Serum FIA = **<1.0 mIU/mL**.
- **Earliest Positive Time**: Serum ELISA at **8–9 days post-fertilization** (5 days before missed period); Urine Card test at **28 days of cycle** (day of missed period).
- **hCG Kinetics**: Half-life = **36 hours**; doubles every **48 hours**; peaks at **8–10 weeks** (100,000–200,000 IU/L).
- **TVS Discriminatory Zone**: Gestational sac must be visible at β-hCG **≥1500–2000 IU/L**.
- **False-Positive Causes**: Exogenous hCG, trophoblastic/non-trophoblastic tumors, renal failure, pituitary hCG, heterophile antibodies.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Immunological pregnancy tests are highly specific immunoassays that detect the unique β-subunit of human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG) in maternal urine (sensitivity 20–60 mIU/mL) or serum (sensitivity 1–2 mIU/mL), serving as the definitive early biochemical diagnostic tool from 8 to 9 days post-fertilization onward.**.
