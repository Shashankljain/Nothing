# Diabetes in Pregnancy: Fetal and Neonatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition

Diabetes in pregnancy (encompassing Gestational Diabetes Mellitus and Pre-gestational Overt Diabetes) is a state of maternal hyperglycemia that leads to exaggerated transplacental glucose transfer, triggering fetal hyperinsulinemia and a spectrum of adverse perinatal outcomes.

---

### Fetal and Neonatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fetal Macrosomia & Birth Trauma**: Maternal hyperglycemia drives fetal hyperinsulinemia and elevated Insulin-like Growth Factors (IGFs), inducing excessive lipogenesis and fat deposition selectively around the fetal shoulders and trunk. This results in macrosomia (birth weight >4.0 kg or >90th percentile), increasing the risk of **shoulder dystocia**, birth asphyxia, and brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy [C5–C6]).
- **Congenital Structural Malformations**: Seen in **6–10% of pre-gestational diabetes** due to teratogenic oxidative stress during first-trimester organogenesis; **absent in true GDM**. Cardiovascular anomalies are most common (**VSD** is most frequent; **TGA** is most specific), followed by CNS/skeletal defects (**Neural Tube Defects**, **Caudal Regression Syndrome**).
- **Intrauterine Fetal Death (IUFD) / Stillbirth**: Uncontrolled hyperglycemia increases fetal oxygen consumption, generating lactic acidosis, fetal hypoxia, placental villous edema, and hyperviscosity, which significantly elevates the risk of late-gestation stillbirth.
- **Neonatal Metabolic Disturbance (Hypoglycemia, Hypocalcemia)**: **Neonatal Hypoglycemia** (<40 mg/dL) develops rapidly within 2 hours of birth because umbilical cord clamping abruptly cuts off maternal glucose supply while endogenous fetal hyperinsulinemia persists. It is frequently accompanied by **hypocalcemia** (<7 mg/dL) and **hypomagnesemia** (<7 mg/dL).
- **Respiratory Distress & Polycythemia**: High fetal insulin levels antagonize cortisol-mediated activation of type II pneumocytes, suppressing **surfactant synthesis** and causing Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS). Chronic intrauterine hypoxia stimulates erythropoietin production, leading to **polycythemia**, hyperviscosity, **neonatal hyperbilirubinemia/jaundice**, and **Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM)**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Pedersen Hypothesis**: Maternal Hyperglycemia → Fetal Hyperglycemia → Fetal Pancreatic β-cell Hyperplasia → Fetal Hyperinsulinemia → Macrosomia & Neonatal Hypoglycemia.
- **Congenital Malformations**: Restricted to Pre-GDM (6–10% risk; CVS > CNS; VSD most common, Caudal Regression Syndrome most specific); absent in GDM.
- **Key Neonatal Metabolic Triad**: Hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL), Hypocalcemia (<7 mg/dL), and Hypomagnesemia (<7 mg/dL).
- **Respiratory & Hematological Impact**: RDS (insulin inhibits surfactant), Polycythemia, Hyperbilirubinemia, and HOCM.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Fetal and neonatal complications in diabetic pregnancies arise primarily from maternal hyperglycemia driving fetal hyperinsulinemia (Pedersen Hypothesis), leading to macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, neonatal hypoglycemia, respiratory distress syndrome, and organogenesis-stage congenital malformations in pre-gestational diabetes.**
