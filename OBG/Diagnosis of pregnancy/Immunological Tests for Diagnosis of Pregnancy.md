# Immunological Tests for Diagnosis of Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition & Fundamental Principle [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Immunological Tests for Pregnancy**: Laboratory and point-of-care diagnostic assays designed to confirm pregnancy by detecting the presence of **human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)** in maternal urine or serum using specific monoclonal or polyclonal anti-hCG antibodies.
- **Basic Principle**: Immunological tests depend on an **antigen-antibody reaction**, where hCG acts as the target **antigen** present in maternal fluids, binding specifically to commercially produced antibodies.
- **Modern Evolution**: Historical biological bioassays (using frogs/mice) have been universally replaced by rapid, highly specific, and sensitive immunological techniques that eliminate cross-reactivity with other pituitary glycoprotein hormones.

---

### 2. Nature and Kinetics of the Target Molecule (hCG) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Structure of hCG**: A glycoprotein hormone (molecular weight 36,000–40,000 Daltons) synthesized by the **syncytiotrophoblast**.
    - **α-Subunit (92 amino acids)**: Biochemically identical to the α-subunits of Luteinizing Hormone (LH), Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), and Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH).
    - **β-Subunit (145 amino acids)**: Structurally unique to hCG, conferring specific biological and immunological identity.
- **Basis of Specificity**: Monoclonal antibodies are developed specifically against the **β-subunit (β-hCG)** to prevent false-positive cross-reactivity with physiological LH or FSH.
- **Secretory Timeline**:
    - Appears in maternal serum and urine as early as **8–9 days post-ovulation / fertilization** (3–4 days before the expected date of menses).
    - **Doubling Time**: In early viable pregnancy, serum β-hCG concentration doubles every **1.4 to 2 days (48 hours)**, rising by 33–66%.
    - **Peak Titer**: Reaches a maximum peak of **100,000 to 200,000 IU/L** (100–200 IU/mL) between **60 and 70 days (10 weeks)** of gestation, subsequently falling to a nadir of 10,000–20,000 IU/L by 100–130 days.

### 3. Types of Immunological Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) & Membrane Card Tests (UPT)

1. **Qualitative Urine Card Test (Direct / Sandwich ELISA)**:
    
    - **Mechanism**: Employs a **two-site sandwich immunoassay** on a nitrocellulose membrane. The card contains immobilized anti-β-hCG antibodies in the test window and control antibodies in the control window.
    - **Sensitivity**: Detects minimum urine hCG levels of **20–60 mIU/mL**.
    - **Timing**: Positive on the **first day of the missed period** (28th day of a regular menstrual cycle).
    - **Sample**: First morning voided urine sample is preferred because it is concentrated and possesses the highest specific gravity.
    - **Interpretation**: Appearance of **two colored bands** (test + control) indicates a positive result (pregnant); a single control band indicates a negative result.
2. **Quantitative Serum ELISA**:
    
    - **Sensitivity**: Highly sensitive, detecting serum levels up to **1.0 to 2.0 mIU/mL**.
    - **Timing**: Detects pregnancy **5 days before the first missed period** (8–9 days post-fertilization).

#### B. Radioimmunoassay (RIA) and Fluorescent Immunoassay (FIA)

- **Fluorescent Immunoassay (FIA)** & **Radioimmunoassay (RIA)** represent the most sensitive quantitative blood tests available.
- Detect serum β-hCG levels as low as **1.0 mIU/mL**.
- Primarily utilized for tracking serial β-hCG titers in ectopic pregnancy, threatened abortion, and Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasia (GTN).

#### C. Agglutination Inhibition Tests (Historical / Slide Tests)

- **Latex Agglutination Inhibition Test**: Urine is mixed with known anti-hCG antibodies, followed by the addition of latex particles coated with hCG.
    - **If Pregnant**: Urine hCG neutralizes the anti-hCG antibodies → **No agglutination** occurs (Positive result).
    - **If Not Pregnant**: Anti-hCG antibodies remain free to bind latex particles → **Agglutination occurs** (Negative result).

### 5. Other Clinical Applications of Immunological Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Diagnosis & Monitoring of Ectopic Pregnancy**:
    - A single β-hCG confirms pregnancy, but serial measurements every 48 hours assess location and viability.
    - Failure of serum β-hCG to double (rise <33–66%) over 48 hours strongly suggests an ectopic or abnormal pregnancy.
    - **Discriminatory Zone**: The critical serum β-hCG level at which an intrauterine gestational sac MUST be visualized on ultrasound:
        - **TVS Discriminatory Zone**: **2000 IU/L** (1000–2000 IU/L).
        - **TAS Discriminatory Zone**: **6500 IU/L**.
2. **Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasia (GTN / Hydatidiform Mole)**:
    - Serum β-hCG levels are pathologically elevated (**>100,000 mIU/mL**).
    - Essential for post-evacuation surveillance to rule out choriocarcinoma.
3. **Follow-up in Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)**:
    - Monitors early implantation success following In Vitro Fertilization and Embryo Transfer (IVF-ET).
4. **Antenatal Aneuploidy Screening (Down Syndrome)**:
    - Elevated maternal serum β-hCG in the first trimester (Dual Test) or second trimester (Quadruple Test) is associated with **Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome)**.

### 6. Clinical Causes of False Test Results [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Causes of False-Positive Results

- **Exogenous hCG Administration**: Infertility therapy (hCG trigger injections given for ovulation induction) within 10–14 days.
- **Heterophile Antibodies**: Human anti-animal antibodies in maternal serum interfering with the immunoassay.
- **hCG-Producing Neoplasms**: Choriocarcinoma, hydatidiform mole, or non-gestational tumors (ovarian, lung, bladder, or GI tumors).
- **Renal Failure**: Impaired excretion causing delayed plasma clearance of endogenous hCG.
- **Physiological Pituitary hCG**: Elevated gonadotropins in perimenopausal or postmenopausal women.

#### B. Causes of False-Negative Results

- **Test Performed Too Early**: Testing prior to the minimum threshold sensitivity (before 8–9 days post-ovulation).
- **Dilute Urine Sample**: Low specific gravity due to high fluid intake prior to testing (minimized by using first morning urine).
- **Hook Effect**: Extremely high hCG concentrations (>1,000,000 mIU/mL in molar pregnancy) saturating antibody binding sites without sandwich formation.
- **Proteinuria or Hematuria**: Interferences from heavy albuminuria or microscopic hematuria.

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **First Morning Void for UPT**: The first morning voided urine sample is ideal for immunological pregnancy card tests because overnight urinary concentration provides the highest hCG density per unit volume, preventing false-negative results in early pregnancy.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing the **α-subunit** and **β-subunit** of hCG in diagnostic testing.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Tests MUST specifically target the β-subunit!** Testing against the α-subunit causes massive false-positive cross-reactivity with LH, FSH, and TSH, which share an identical α-amino acid sequence.

### 8. Diagram Guide

### Diagram : Principle of Two-Site Sandwich ELISA (UPT Card Test)

- **Type**: Diagnostic immunoassay mechanism guide
- **Outline shape to draw**: Membrane strip with Sample Well, Test Line (T), and Control Line (C).
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Sample Well** — application point for urine containing hCG antigen
    2. **Mobile Conjugate Area** — contains dye-conjugated anti-β-hCG antibodies
    3. **Test Line (T)** — immobilized anti-α-hCG antibodies capturing the hCG-antibody complex (forms colored band if hCG is present)
    4. **Control Line (C)** — immobilized anti-species antibodies capturing excess conjugated antibodies (forms colored band to validate test functionality)
- **Arrows/connections**: Sample Well → Capillary Flow along Membrane → Test Line (T) → Control Line (C).
- **Annotation**: _"Two colored bands (T + C) indicate a positive pregnancy test; a single control band (C) indicates a negative test."_

### Quick Revision

- **Target Antigen**: Specific **β-subunit of hCG** (prevents cross-reactivity with LH/FSH/TSH).
- **Urine Card Test (UPT)**: Two-site sandwich ELISA; sensitivity **20–60 mIU/mL**; positive on **day 1 of missed period**.
- **Serum Immunoassay**: ELISA/RIA/FIA; sensitivity **1–2 mIU/mL**; positive **8–9 days post-ovulation**.
- **Ectopic Pregnancy Doubling Time**: Normal viable pregnancy β-hCG doubles every **48 hours** (rise <33–66% suggests ectopic).
- **Discriminatory Zone**: Serum β-hCG level of **2000 IU/L** on TVS at which an intrauterine gestational sac must be seen.
