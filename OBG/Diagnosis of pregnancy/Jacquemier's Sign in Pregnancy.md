[[C7-Diagnosis of Pregnancy]]
# Jacquemier's Sign in Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Jacquemier's Sign (Chadwick's Sign)**: Defined as the characteristic dusky bluish, violet, or purplish discoloration of the vestibule, anterior vaginal wall, and portio vaginalis of the cervix, serving as an objective pelvic sign of early pregnancy.

---

### Key Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Timing of Appearance**: Becomes clinically detectable on pelvic speculum examination at around the **8th week** of gestation.
- **Etiology & Mechanism**: Result of local hypervascularity, edema, and marked venous congestion of the subepithelial venous plexus surrounding the vaginal mucosa and cervix under the influence of elevated maternal estrogen levels.
- **Historical Eponym**: First described by French physician Jacquemier in 1836 and later by James Read Chadwick in 1887; therefore, the terms **Jacquemier's sign** and **Chadwick's sign** are used synonymously in clinical practice.
- **Diagnostic Category**: Classified as a **probable sign** of early pregnancy (an objective pelvic physical sign elicited by the clinician).
- **Clinical Pitfall / Differential**: While highly suggestive of early pregnancy, false positives can occasionally occur in non-pregnant conditions associated with marked pelvic vascular congestion, such as large uterine fibroids, pelvic mass/tumors, or severe pelvic organ congestion.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Jacquemier's (Chadwick's) Sign**: Dusky bluish or purplish discoloration of the vestibule, anterior vaginal wall, and cervix.
- **Time of Appearance**: 8th week of gestation.
- **Underlying Cause**: Estrogen-driven local pelvic venous congestion and increased vascularity.
- **Classification**: Probable pelvic sign of early pregnancy.

---
