[[C7-Diagnosis of Pregnancy]]
# Goodell's Sign and Osiander's Sign [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Goodell's Sign**: Defined as the marked **softening of the cervix** appearing as early as **6 weeks** of gestation (the first probable pelvic sign to become positive).
- **Osiander's Sign**: Defined as **increased arterial pulsations felt through the lateral vaginal fornices** appearing at **8 weeks** of gestation.

---

### Key Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Goodell's Sign (Cervical Softening) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Timing & Status**: Detectable at **6 weeks** of gestation; recognized clinically as the **1st sign to become positive**among all early pregnancy pelvic signs.
- **Anatomical & Physiological Basis**: Caused by estrogen-induced hypertrophy and hyperplasia of cervical mucous glands, stromal edema, and increased vascularity beneath the squamous epithelium of the portio vaginalis.
- **Physical Texture Comparison**: The pregnant cervix feels soft like the **lips of the mouth**, whereas the non-pregnant cervix feels firm like the **tip of the nose**.
- **Classification**: Probable pelvic sign of early pregnancy.

#### B. Osiander's Sign (Vaginal Pulsations) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Timing**: Detectable at **8 weeks** of gestation.
- **Anatomical & Physiological Basis**: Caused by hyperdynamic blood flow through markedly dilated uterine arteries running adjacent to the lateral vaginal fornices.
- **Elicitation**: Elicited on digital per-vaginal examination as distinct, regular arterial pulsations felt through the lateral fornices.
- **Classification**: Probable pelvic sign of early pregnancy.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Goodell's Sign**: Cervical softening at **6 weeks** (first sign to become positive); feels like lips of mouth vs tip of nose.
- **Osiander's Sign**: Pulsations felt through lateral vaginal fornices at **8 weeks** due to increased uterine arterial blood flow.
- **Classification**: Both are **probable pelvic signs** of first-trimester pregnancy.
- **Etiology**: Result of estrogen-driven pelvic hypervascularity and venous congestion.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Goodell's sign (cervical softening appearing at 6 weeks; the first positive sign) and Osiander's sign (increased uterine arterial pulsations felt through the lateral fornices at 8 weeks) are classic probable pelvic signs of early pregnancy resulting from estrogen-induced pelvic hypervascularity.**
