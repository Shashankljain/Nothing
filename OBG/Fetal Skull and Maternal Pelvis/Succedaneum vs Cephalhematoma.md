# Caput Succedaneum vs Cephalhematoma [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition

- **Caput Succedaneum**: A benign, diffuse, boggy swelling of the fetal scalp caused by the stagnation of serous fluid (edema) in the subcutaneous tissue beneath the girdle of contact (dilating cervix or vulval ring) during labor.
- **Cephalhematoma**: A localized subperiosteal accumulation of blood between the pericranium and a cranial flat bone (most commonly parietal), resulting from birth trauma and rupture of small emissary blood vessels.

---

### Key Differentiating Features [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Pathology & Anatomical Site**: Caput is serous fluid accumulation in the subcutaneous scalp tissue above the periosteum; Cephalhematoma is subperiosteal hemorrhage beneath the pericranium.
2. **Relation to Suture Lines**: Caput is diffuse and **crosses suture lines** freely; Cephalhematoma is strictly **limited to a single cranial bone and NEVER crosses suture lines** (as the pericranium is firmly attached to bone margins).
3. **Time of Appearance**: Caput is **present at birth**; Cephalhematoma is **never present at birth**, appearing 12 to 24 hours after delivery as blood gradually accumulates.
4. **Consistency & Feel**: Caput feels soft, boggy, and pit on pressure (compressible); Cephalhematoma is soft, fluctuant, and incompressible, often surrounded by a firm bony edge.
5. **Clinical Course & Resolution**: Caput subsides rapidly and **disappears spontaneously within 24 to 48 hours**without treatment; Cephalhematoma **persists for weeks to months (6 to 8 weeks)** and may predispose the neonate to hyperbilirubinemia or anemia.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Caput Succedaneum**: Subcutaneous scalp edema present at birth, crosses suture lines, disappears within 24–48 hours.
- **Cephalhematoma**: Subperiosteal blood collection appearing 12–24 hours after birth, strictly limited by suture lines, resolves over 6–8 weeks.
- **Primary Diagnostic Hallmark**: Extension across suture lines (Caput = Yes; Cephalhematoma = No).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Caput succedaneum is a benign subcutaneous scalp edema present at birth that crosses cranial suture lines and resolves rapidly within 24 hours, whereas cephalhematoma is a traumatic subperiosteal hemorrhage appearing hours after birth that is strictly bounded by suture lines and resolves slowly over weeks.**
