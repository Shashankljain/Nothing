# Sagittal Suture: Anatomical & Obstetrical Significance [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

### 1. Definition & Anatomical Relations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sagittal Suture (Longitudinal Suture)**: It is a major anteroposterior membranous articulation of the fetal skull vault situated in the midline between the two parietal bones.
- **Anatomical Boundaries & Extent**:
    - **Anterior Boundary**: Extends forward to join the **anterior fontanelle (bregma)**, where it meets the frontal suture and the two coronal sutures.
    - **Posterior Boundary**: Extends backward to join the **posterior fontanelle (lambda)**, where it meets the two lambdoid sutures.
    - **Lateral Boundaries**: Flanked on either side by the right and left parietal bones, bounded laterally by the parietal eminences.

- **Sagittal Fontanelle**: An inconsistent membranous gap located on the sagittal suture at the junction of its anterior two-thirds and posterior one-third (clinically non-significant).
- **Biparietal Diameter (9.5 cm)**: The greatest transverse diameter of the fetal skull, extending between the two parietal eminences bisected by the sagittal suture.

### 3. Obstetrical Importance during Labor & Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

Digital palpation of the sagittal suture during vaginal examination provides indispensable clinical information regarding the course and progress of labor:

#### A. Permits Gliding Movement & Molding of Fetal Head [⭐ High-Yield]

- During the passage of the fetal head through the rigid bony pelvis, the unossified membranous sagittal suture permits the **gliding movement of one parietal bone over the other**.
- In first vertex position (LOA), the right parietal bone overrides the left, reducing the engaging transverse diameter of the skull without causing brain injury.

#### B. Assessment of Synclitism & Asynclitism (Parietal Presentation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Synclitism**: Occurs when the sagittal suture lies in the available transverse or oblique diameter of the pelvic inlet, placed equidistant from the symphysis pubis and sacral promontory.
- **Asynclitism**: Lateral deflection of the sagittal suture away from the midline plane of the pelvis:
    
    1. **Anterior Asynclitism (Negele's Obliquity)**:
        - Sagittal suture is deflected **posteriorly toward the sacral promontory**.
        - Anterior parietal bone becomes the leading presenting part; more common in multiparae due to lax abdominal wall.
    2. **Posterior Asynclitism (Litzmann's Obliquity)**:
        - Sagittal suture is deflected **anteriorly toward the symphysis pubis**.
        - Posterior parietal bone becomes the leading presenting part; more common in primigravidae due to tight abdominal tone.
    
    - **Clinical Significance**: Mild asynclitism allows the smaller super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm) to cross the brim one eminence at a time. Persistent severe asynclitism indicates **Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD)**.

#### C. Identification of Fetal Presentation, Position, & Degree of Flexion [⭐ High-Yield]

- Tracing the sagittal suture to its terminals identifies the fontanelles:
    - **Y-shaped triangular end** = Posterior fontanelle (Lambda) → indicates **Occiput**.
    - **Diamond-shaped end** = Anterior fontanelle (Bregma) → indicates **Sinciput**.
- **Orientation**: Alignment of the sagittal suture in the pelvic quadrants determines fetal position (transverse, oblique, or anteroposterior).
- **Flexion**: In a well-flexed head, the sagittal suture is easily traced toward the posterior fontanelle, which lies at a lower level than the anterior fontanelle.

#### D. Tracking Internal Rotation & Progress of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

- During normal labor, internal rotation carries the sagittal suture from the transverse or oblique diameter into the **anteroposterior diameter** of the pelvic outlet.
- **Deep Transverse Arrest (DTA)**: Arrest of labor characterized by the sagittal suture remaining trapped in the bispinous/transverse diameter at station 0 or lower.

#### E. Differentiation of Scalp Swellings [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Caput Succedaneum**: Diffuse, boggy serosanguinous fluid accumulation that **crosses suture lines** (including the sagittal suture).
- **Cephalhematoma**: Subperiosteal blood collection that is **strictly limited by suture lines** and never crosses the sagittal suture.

#### F. Essential Landmark in Operative Obstetric Deliveries [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Obstetric Forceps Application**: Correct biparietal-bimalar application requires the sagittal suture to be strictly in the midline between both forceps blades.
- **Ventouse (Vacuum Extractor) Application**: The center of the vacuum cup must be placed over the **flexion point**, located on the sagittal suture **3 cm anterior** to the posterior fontanelle.

### 6. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Flexion Point Landmark**: The single most critical landmark for successful vacuum extraction (ventouse) is the **flexion point**, located directly over the sagittal suture in the midline, **3 cm anterior to the posterior fontanelle**(or 6 cm posterior to the anterior fontanelle). Placing the cup here promotes maximum head flexion and minimizes traction force.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing Negele's obliquity with Litzmann's obliquity or confusing Caput Succedaneum with Cephalhematoma across the sagittal suture.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Negele's (Anterior) Asynclitism** = Sagittal suture points **posteriorly** (toward sacrum), anterior parietal bone leads.
    - **Litzmann's (Posterior) Asynclitism** = Sagittal suture points **anteriorly** (toward pubis), posterior parietal bone leads.
    - **Caput succedaneum** crosses the sagittal suture; **Cephalhematoma** never crosses the sagittal suture.

### Quick Revision

- **Sagittal Suture**: Midline membranous suture between parietal bones running from anterior (bregma) to posterior (lambda) fontanelle.
- **Molding Role**: Permits overlapping of parietal bones during head compression in labor.
- **Asynclitism**: Anterior (Negele) = suture shifted posteriorly; Posterior (Litzmann) = suture shifted anteriorly.
- **Diagnostic Role**: Determines position, degree of flexion, internal rotation, and station.
- **Operative Landmark**: Ensures correct symmetry in forceps application and marks flexion point for ventouse placement.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **The sagittal suture is the central anteroposterior membranous articulation of the fetal skull vault lying between the parietal bones, serving as a vital clinical landmark in labor to assess head molding, synclitism, degree of flexion, internal rotation, and proper placement of operative delivery instruments.**
