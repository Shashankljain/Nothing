# Placenta Previa: Diagnosis and Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Placenta Previa**: An obstetric condition where the placenta is implanted partially or completely over the lower uterine segment (over, adjacent to, or within 2 cm of the internal cervical os) in front of the fetal presenting part.
- **Antepartum Hemorrhage (APH)**: Bleeding from or into the genital tract occurring after the 20th week (or 28th week) of gestation up to the birth of the baby. Placenta previa accounts for approximately 35% of all APH cases.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Prior Uterine Scarring (Major Risk Factor)**: History of previous **Cesarean Delivery (CD)**, myomectomy, or curettage. The risk of placenta previa increases with the number of prior C-sections (0.9% with 1 prior CD up to 3% with ≥3 prior CDs).
- **Multiparity & Advanced Maternal Age (>35 years)**: Atrophic endometrial changes from repeated pregnancies favor lower segment implantation (4-fold risk elevation).
- **Hyperplacentosis**: Multiple gestation (twins) or large placental size encroaches into the lower uterine segment.
- **Maternal Smoking & Substance Use**: Carbon monoxide-induced hypoxemia triggers compensatory placental hypertrophy.

---

### 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Revised Classification (ACOG / ACR Criteria)

- **True Placenta Previa**: The placenta directly covers the internal cervical os.
- **Low-Lying Placenta**: The lower placental edge lies within **2 cm** of the internal cervical os but does not cover it.

#### B. Morphological Types (Types I–IV)

- **Type 1 (Low-Lying / Lateral)**: Placental edge is in the lower segment within 2–5 cm of the internal os.
- **Type 2 (Marginal)**: Placental edge reaches the margin of the internal os. (_Type 2 Posterior is the "Dangerous Variety"_ due to cord compression against the sacral promontory).
- **Type 3 (Incomplete / Partial Central)**: Placenta covers the internal os partially.
- **Type 4 (Complete / Total Central)**: Placenta completely covers the internal os.

- **Mechanism of Bleeding**: Lower uterine segment expansion (from 0.5 cm at 20 weeks to >5 cm at term) creates shearing forces against the inelastic placenta, disrupting uteroplacental sinuses and causing **inevitable, painless hemorrhage**.

---

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Painless, Causeless, Bright Red, Recurrent Vaginal Bleeding**: Hallmark symptom. Often wakes the patient from sleep ("warning hemorrhage").

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Abdominal Examination**: Uterus is **soft, relaxed, and non-tender**. High floating head or malpresentation (transverse lie/breech) is common.
- **Stallworthy's Sign**: Slowing of fetal heart rate on pressing the floating head into the pelvis, recovering when pressure is released (suggests posterior low-lying placenta).
- **STRICT CONTRAINDICATION**: **Digital Vaginal Examination (P/V) is strictly prohibited** as it can provoke fatal, torrential hemorrhage!

---

### 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transvaginal Ultrasonography (TVS - Investigation of Choice)**:
    - **Gold Standard** imaging modality (100% accuracy and safe).
    - Measures the exact distance from the lower placental edge to the internal cervical os.
- **Transabdominal Ultrasonography (TAS)**: Initial rapid screening tool; requires a moderately full bladder.
- **Color Doppler USG & MRI**:
    - Essential to rule out **Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS)** in women with prior C-sections (signs: loss of retroplacental clear zone <1 mm, intraplacental lacunae, hypervascularity).
    - **MRI**: Superior for confirming posterior placenta previa and deep organ invasion.
- **Lab Profile**: Complete blood count, ABO/Rh grouping, cross-matching PRBC, coagulation profile.

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

|**Shock Status**|Proportionate to visible blood loss.|Out of proportion to visible loss (in concealed).|
|**Placental Site (USG)**|Lower uterine segment (within 2 cm of os).|Upper uterine segment (retroplacental clot).|

#### A. Emergency Resuscitation

1. Establish two large-bore IV lines (14G / 16G); infuse Ringer's lactate.
2. Draw blood for Hb, ABO/Rh grouping, cross-matching 2–4 units PRBC, and coagulation profile.
3. Strictly avoid digital vaginal examination.

#### B. Expectant Management (MacAfee & Johnson Protocol)

- **Selection Criteria (ALL must be met)**: Maternal vitals stable, active bleeding absent/minor, gestational age **<37 weeks**, and reassuring FHR (CTG reactive).
- **Protocol**:
    - Complete hospital bed rest.
    - **Antenatal Corticosteroids (<34 weeks)**: Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM 24 hours apart (2 doses) or Inj. Dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12 hours apart (4 doses) to promote fetal lung maturity.
    - **Neuroprotection (<32 weeks)**: Inj. Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) loading & maintenance infusion.
    - **Rh Immunoglobulin**: Administer Anti-D 300 µg IM within 72 hours if Rh-negative unsensitized.
    - **Elective Delivery**: Plan elective C-section at **36°/7 to 37°/7 weeks**.

#### C. Active / Definitive Management (Immediate Delivery)

- **Indications**: Gestational age **≥37 weeks**, active/heavy bleeding, maternal shock, or fetal distress.
- **Mode of Delivery**:
    - **Cesarean Delivery (LSCS)**: Mandatory for True Placenta Previa or when the placental edge is **≤2 cm** from the internal os.
    - **Trial of Vaginal Delivery**: Allowed ONLY if TVS confirms placental edge is **>2 cm** away from the os.

---

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal**: Hypovolemic shock, Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH due to poor LUS retraction), Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS), and puerperal sepsis.
- **Fetal**: Prematurity (leading cause of mortality), birth asphyxia, fetal growth restriction (FGR), and fetal demise.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Poor Hemostatic Retraction in LUS**: The lower uterine segment lacks the middle interlacing "Figure-of-8" muscle fibers ("living ligatures") present in the upper segment, leading to high risks of atonic **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)** following placental separation.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing a digital vaginal examination (P/V) in a patient presenting with third-trimester bleeding before obtaining an ultrasound.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Digital P/V examination is strictly CONTRAINDICATED** in suspected APH as it can tear placental sinuses and trigger fatal, torrential hemorrhage. Always perform a **screening TVS** first.

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-R-E-V-I-A

- **P**: **Painless, causeless, bright red, recurrent bleeding**
- **R**: **Relaxed, soft, non-tender uterus** on palpation
- **E**: **Expectant management (MacAfee Protocol)** for POG <37 weeks if stable
- **V**: **Vaginal examination is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**
- **I**: **Investigation of Choice = Transvaginal Sonography (TVS)**
- **A**: **Active delivery via C-section** if edge ≤2 cm from internal os or at ≥37 weeks

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Lower uterine segment implantation within 2 cm of or covering internal os.
- **Clinical Triad**: **Painless, causeless, bright red recurrent bleeding** with a **soft, non-tender uterus**.
- **Golden Contraindication**: **Never perform digital P/V examination**.
- **Investigation of Choice**: **Transvaginal Sonography (TVS)**.
- **Management Thresholds**: Expectant management (MacAfee) if <37 wks + stable; **LSCS** if ≥37 wks, active bleeding, or edge **≤2 cm** from internal os.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Placenta previa is the abnormal implantation of the placenta in the lower uterine segment within 2 cm of or covering the internal cervical os, presenting with painless, bright red recurrent hemorrhage, diagnosed definitively via transvaginal ultrasonography (TVS) and managed expectantly before 37 weeks or actively via Cesarean section.**
