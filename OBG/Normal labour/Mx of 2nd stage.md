# Management of Second Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks** (Definition + Structured subheadings covering clinical features, principles, step-by-step conduction of delivery, and complications)

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Second Stage of Labor**: The period of labor that begins with **complete/full dilatation of the cervix (10 cm)** and ends with the **complete expulsion of the fetus** from the birth canal into the external world.
- **Average Duration**:
    - **Primigravidae**: Average **2 hours** (ACOG/WHO upper limit: 3 hours without epidural, 4 hours with epidural).
    - **Multiparae**: Average **30 minutes** (ACOG/WHO upper limit: 2 hours without epidural, 3 hours with epidural).

---

### 2. Phases of Second Stage of Labor

- **Propulsive (Passive) Phase**: Extends from full cervical dilatation until the fetal presenting part descends to touch the pelvic floor.
- **Expulsive (Active) Phase**: Characterized by voluntary maternal bearing-down efforts (triggered by the **Ferguson reflex** set up by stretching of the lower vagina and pelvic floor) and ends with the birth of the baby.

---

### 3. Clinical Features & Diagnosis of Onset [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Increased Contraction Intensity**: Contractions become more frequent, intense, and prolonged (occurring every **2–3 minutes** and lasting **60–90 seconds**).
- **Urge to Bear Down**: Irresistible reflex desire to push ("straining at stool") accompanied by grunting.

#### B. Signs

- **Perineal Distension & Crowning**:
    - The descending head distends the pelvic floor and perineum; the anal sphincter dilates, and stool may be expelled.
    - Vulval opening becomes circular, and scalp hair becomes visible during contractions.
    - **Crowning**: Occurs when the widest transverse diameter of the head (biparietal diameter = **9.5 cm**) stretches the vulval outlet and remains permanently visible at the introitus without receding between contractions.
- **Vaginal Examination (PV)**: Cervix is no longer palpable (fully dilated to **10 cm**), and the station of the head is at or below the level of the ischial spines (**Station ≥0 to +3**).

### 5. Management & Conduction of Delivery (Step-by-Step) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Principles of Management

1. Assist in the natural expulsion of the fetus slowly and steadily.
2. Prevent maternal soft tissue / perineal injuries and fetal birth trauma.

#### B. General Measures & Monitoring

- **Positioning**: Dorsal position with a **15° left lateral tilt** is preferred to prevent aortocaval compression (Supine Hypotension Syndrome). Upright, squatting, or lateral positions chosen by the woman are encouraged based on WHO guidelines.
- **Fetal Heart Rate (FHR) Monitoring**: Continuous electronic monitoring or intermittent auscultation recorded **every 5 minutes** or immediately after every uterine contraction.
- **Aseptic Preparation**: Vulva, perineum, and thighs are cleansed with antiseptic solution, and sterile drapes are applied.
- **Bladder Care**: Empty the bladder using a sterile catheter if distended.

#### C. Conduction Phase 1: Delivery of the Head

- **Maintaining Flexion**:
    - As the head descends, the left hand presses the occiput downward and backward while the right hand supports the perineum using a sterile pad.
    - _Objective_: Keeps the head well-flexed so that the smaller **suboccipitofrontal diameter (10.0 cm)** distends the vulval introitus rather than the larger occipitofrontal diameter (11.5 cm).
- **Modified Ritgen's Maneuver**:
    - When the subocciput hinges under the symphysis pubis, the delivery of the head is regulated **in between contractions**.
    - The clinician places sterile towel-covered fingers over the anococcygeal region behind the anus and applies upward and forward pressure on the fetal chin.
    - The other hand exerts gentle downward pressure on the occiput.
    - The head emerges slowly by extension: vertex, forehead, nose, mouth, and chin are born successively over the perineum.
- **Selective Episiotomy**:
    - Routine episiotomy is **not recommended**.
    - A **selective mediolateral episiotomy** is performed under local infiltration with **10 mL of 1% Lignocaine**only when the perineum is fully thinned out at crowning and threatens to tear.
- **Checking for Cord Loop**:
    - Once the head is delivered, pass a finger along the neck to check for a loop of the umbilical cord (present in 20–25% of cases).
    - If loose, slip it over the head; if tight, clamp with two Kocher forceps 2.5 cm apart and cut in between.

#### D. Conduction Phase 2: Delivery of the Shoulders

- Wait for the head to undergo **restitution and external rotation**.
- The head is held in both hands placed over the parietal bones.
- **Anterior Shoulder**: Gentle downward and backward traction is applied until the anterior shoulder escapes from under the symphysis pubis.
- **Posterior Shoulder**: The head is then lifted upward and forward toward the mother's abdomen to deliver the posterior shoulder over the perineum.

#### E. Conduction Phase 3: Delivery of Trunk & Immediate Care

- The rest of the body is expelled rapidly by **lateral flexion** of the fetal spine.
- Clear airway/pharyngeal secretions gently using a bulb syringe/mucus sucker.
- **Delayed Cord Clamping**: Delay clamping for **1 to 3 minutes** post-delivery (or until pulsations cease) to optimize neonatal iron stores.

### 7. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- **Genital Tract Lacerations**: 1st to 4th-degree perineal tears, cervical tears, and periurethral sulcus tears.
- **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: From uterine atony or soft tissue trauma.
- **Maternal Exhaustion, Dehydration, and Bladder Atony**.

#### B. Fetal Complications

- **Birth Asphyxia & Hypoxia**: Secondary to prolonged compression or cord accidents.
- **Shoulder Dystocia**: Impaction of anterior shoulder behind symphysis pubis.
- **Intracranial Hemorrhage & Cephalhematoma**: Secondary to rapid compression-decompression or prolonged pelvic pressure.

---

### 8. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Delivering Between Contractions**: Regulating the delivery of the fetal head **slowly in between contractions** using Ritgen's maneuver reduces the rate of severe 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears significantly compared to allowing explosive birth during peak contractions.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Applying **manual fundal pressure** (Kristeller maneuver) to expedite delivery in the second stage.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Manual fundal pressure is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** by WHO and ACOG guidelines. It does not shorten the second stage and dramatically increases the risk of uterine rupture, severe perineal tears, and further impaction of shoulder dystocia.

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: H-E-A-D-S (Steps of Second Stage Conduction)

- **H**: **Head flexion maintained** — push occiput down until subocciput hinges under pubic arch
- **E**: **Extension delivery via Ritgen's** — apply upward chin pressure in between contractions
- **A**: **Assess neck for cord loop** — slip over head or clamp and cut if tight
- **D**: **Downward traction for anterior shoulder** — until anterior humerus clears pubic arch
- **S**: **Upward Sweep for posterior shoulder** — followed by lateral flexion of trunk

---

### Quick Revision

- **Second Stage Boundaries**: Full cervical dilatation (10 cm) to complete expulsion of fetus.
- **Two Phases**: Propulsive phase (passive descent) and Expulsive phase (active bearing-down).
- **Ritgen's Maneuver**: Maintains flexion, regulates slow head delivery **between contractions**, and protects perineum.
- **Selective Episiotomy**: Performed at crowning under 1% Lignocaine infiltration only when perineum is thinned out and threatens to tear.
- **Shoulder Delivery**: Downward traction for anterior shoulder first; upward traction for posterior shoulder second.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Management of the second stage of labor centers on continuous fetal heart monitoring, controlled delivery of the fetal head between contractions using Ritgen's maneuver to preserve perineal integrity, selective episiotomy, and sequential delivery of the shoulders to avoid birth trauma.**
