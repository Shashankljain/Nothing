# Mechanism of Normal Labor: Phases, Mechanics, and Reasons [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mechanism of Labor**: The series of continuous, passive adaptive movements undergone by the fetal head and body during its journey through the maternal birth canal to accommodate its longest diameters to the largest diameters of the pelvic planes.
- **Normal Labor (Eutocia)**: Labor is designated as normal when it is spontaneous in onset at term (37–42 weeks), with vertex presentation, completed without undue prolongation, resulting in spontaneous vaginal delivery without maternal or fetal complications.
- **Engaging Diameters in Normal Vertex Presentation**:
    - **Anteroposterior**: Suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm) when well-flexed, or Suboccipitofrontal (10.0 cm) in slight deflexion.
    - **Transverse**: Biparietal diameter (9.5 cm).

---

### 2. Prerequisites & Factors Influencing Mechanism

1. **Passenger (Fetal Factors)**: Normal fetal size, flexed attitude, vertex presentation, and absence of malformations.
2. **Passage (Maternal Pelvis & Soft Tissue)**: Adequate gynecoid pelvic dimensions (inlet, cavity, outlet) and elastic, relaxing pelvic floor muscles (levator ani).
3. **Powers (Uterine Expulsive Forces)**: Coordinated, rhythmic uterine contractions with fundal dominance, progressive retraction, and voluntary maternal bearing-down efforts.

---

### 3. Classification of Movements / Phases [⭐ High-Yield]

Although described as distinct sequential phases for clinical clarity, several movements occur simultaneously during labor:

1. **Engagement**
2. **Descent**
3. **Flexion**
4. **Internal Rotation of the Head**
5. **Crowning**
6. **Extension**
7. **Restitution**
8. **External Rotation**
9. **Expulsion of the Shoulders and Trunk**

---

#### Phase 1: Engagement [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: The passage of the widest transverse diameter of the fetal head (Biparietal diameter = 9.5 cm) through the plane of the pelvic inlet.
- **Timing**: Occurs in the late weeks of pregnancy (38–42 weeks) in primigravidae, but during early labor or after membrane rupture in multiparae.
- **Mechanics (Asynclitism)**: Prior to engagement, the head enters the inlet with lateral tilt (asynclitism) rather than absolute synclitism:
    - **Anterior Asynclitism (Naegele's Obliquity)**: The sagittal suture is tilted posteriorly toward the sacral promontory, making the anterior parietal bone the leading part.
    - **Posterior Asynclitism (Litzmann's Obliquity)**: The sagittal suture is tilted anteriorly toward the symphysis pubis, making the posterior parietal bone leading.
- **Reason for Asynclitism**:
    - Asynclitism allows the two parietal eminences to cross the pelvic brim **one at a time**.
    - This substitutes the smaller **super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm)** for the larger biparietal diameter (9.5 cm), allowing easier negotiation of the pelvic inlet.

---

#### Phase 2: Descent [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: The progressive downward movement of the presenting part through the birth canal.
- **Course**: Slow in the first stage of labor, becoming maximum during the second stage.
- **Reasons for Descent**:
    1. **Uterine Contraction and Retraction**: Permanent myometrial shortening pushes the fetus downward.
    2. **Bearing-Down Efforts**: Voluntary abdominal muscle contraction increases intra-abdominal pressure.
    3. **Straightening of Fetal Ovoid**: Contractions reduce transverse uterine diameter, causing fetal spine extension and transmitting **fetal axis pressure** directly through the podalic pole to the head.
    4. **Liquor Rupture**: Rupture of membranes reduces uterine volume, allowing direct fundal force transmission.

---

#### Phase 3: Flexion [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: Bending of the fetal neck so that the chin comes into direct contact with the fetal chest.
- **Reason for Flexion (Two-Arm Lever Theory)**:
    - Flexion is explained by the **Two-Arm Lever Principle**:
        - The occipito-atlantal joint acts as the **fulcrum**.
        - The **short arm** extends from the fulcrum to the occiput.
        - The **long arm** extends from the fulcrum to the chin.
    - When the descending head meets resistance from the unyielding cervix, pelvic walls, or pelvic floor, equal force is applied to both lever arms.
    - By laws of mechanics, the short arm descends while the long arm ascends, forcing the chin toward the chest.
- **Anatomical Advantage**: Replaces the larger engaging **occipitofrontal diameter (11.5 cm)** with the much smaller **suboccipitobregmatic diameter (9.5 cm)**, reducing the cross-sectional area by over 20%.

---

#### Phase 4: Internal Rotation of the Head [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: Turning of the fetal head inside the pelvic cavity so that the occiput rotates **1/8th of a circle (45°)** anteriorly (in oblique positions) or **2/8ths of a circle (90°)** anteriorly (in transverse positions) to lie directly behind the symphysis pubis.
- **Level of Occurrence**: Occurs predominantly when the head reaches the pelvic floor (levator ani) in the mid-pelvis.
- **Reasons for Internal Rotation**:
    1. **Slope of Pelvic Floor (Hart's Rule)**: The two halves of the levator ani form a V-shaped gutter sloping downward and forward. The well-flexed occiput strikes one slope first; during relaxation, elastic recoil pushes the occiput forward toward the midline.
    2. **Pelvic Architecture**: The long anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic outlet accommodates the long AP diameter of the flexed head, while the narrow bispinous diameter accommodates the narrower transverse diameter.
    3. **Law of Unequal Flexibility (Sellheim and Moir)**: The fetal axis rotates to align its direction of easiest bending with the curve of the birth canal.

---

#### Phase 5: Crowning [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: The state where the largest transverse diameter of the head (Biparietal diameter = 9.5 cm) stretches the vulval ring and remains permanently visible at the introitus without receding between contractions.
- **Reason for Crowning**:
    - The subocciput becomes fixed underneath the pubic arch, acting as a fulcrum.
    - The widest part of the head has passed through the bony pelvic outlet and is now restrained only by the elastic vulval outlet.

---

#### Phase 6: Extension [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: The delivery of the fetal head through the vulval introitus by an upward and forward sweeping movement.
- **Sequence of Born Parts**: Vertex → Forehead → Face → Chin.
- **Reason for Extension (Couple of Force Theory)**:
    - Extension is governed by two opposing physical forces:
        1. **Downward driving force**: Generated by uterine contractions and maternal pushing acting through the fetal spine.
        2. **Upward and forward resistance**: Offered by the muscular pelvic floor and perineal body.
    - These two forces form a "couple of forces" whose resultant vector acts in an **upward and forward direction**, causing the head to extend around the subocciput fulcrum under the pubic arch.

---

#### Phase 7: Restitution [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: A visible, passive untwisting movement of the head through **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the direction opposite to internal rotation.
- **Reason for Restitution**:
    - During internal rotation of the head (45°), the fetal shoulders inside the pelvis rotate by only 22.5°, creating a **neck torsion of 45°**.
    - Once the head is freed outside the vulva, it passively untwists to realign itself at right angles with the bisacromial shoulder line.

---

#### Phase 8: External Rotation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: Further movement of the born head through **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the same direction as restitution.
- **Reason for External Rotation**:
    - This visible external head movement is the direct reflection of **internal rotation of the fetal shoulders** inside the maternal pelvis.
    - The bisacromial diameter of the shoulders rotates 45° from the oblique diameter into the anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic outlet to prepare for birth.

---

#### Phase 9: Expulsion of Shoulders and Trunk [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition / Movement**: The complete birth of the fetal body.
- **Mechanics & Reason**:
    - Descent brings the **anterior shoulder** under the symphysis pubis, where it hinges.
    - The **posterior shoulder** sweeps over the perineum first by a movement of **lateral flexion** of the fetal spine.
    - Following shoulder release, the rest of the trunk is expelled rapidly by lateral flexion along the axis of the birth canal.

### 6. Clinical Features & Diagnosis of Progress

- **Abdominal Palpation**:
    - **Engagement Assessment (Fifth System)**: Head is engaged when ≤2/5ths of the head is palpable above the pelvic brim.
    - **Sincipital Prominence**: Felt at a higher level than the occiput, confirming a flexed head.
- **Vaginal Examination (PV)**:
    - **Station**: Position of the leading bony part of the head relative to the ischial spines (Station 0 = engaged at ischial spines).
    - **Sagittal Suture Alignment**: Identifies position (e.g., left oblique in L.O.A.).
    - **Fontanelle Levels**: Posterior fontanelle is lower than the anterior fontanelle due to flexion.

---

### 7. Diagnosis / Investigations (Partograph & Station Assessment)

- **Partograph (WHO Labor Care Guide)**:
    - Plots cervical dilatation (active phase starting at 5 cm) and station of descent against time.
    - **Alert Line / Action Line**: Protraction (<1 cm/hr dilatation) alerts to mechanical dystocia or poor uterine action.
- **Ultrasound Assessment**: Confirms occiput location, angle of progression, and midline distance in difficult cases.

---

### 9. Management of Labor & AMTSL

#### A. First & Second Stage Conduction

- **First Stage**: Ambulatory support, left lateral tilt to avoid aortocaval compression, partographic tracking, and oral hydration.
- **Second Stage**: Voluntary bearing-down pushing during contractions. Perform **Ritgen's maneuver** (perineal protection) during head extension and selective mediolateral episiotomy at crowning.

#### B. Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL) [⭐ High-Yield]

Reduces PPH risk by 60% and shortens third stage to 5 minutes:

1. **Uterotonic**: **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM** within 1 minute of birth.
2. **Delayed Cord Clamping**: Wait **1 to 3 minutes** post-delivery.
3. **Controlled Cord Traction (Brandt-Andrews Technique)**: Pull cord downward and backward while applying suprapubic counter-traction upward.
4. **Fundal Massage**: Massage uterus immediately post-delivery until firm.

### 11. Complications of Mechanical Failure / Arrest

- **Deep Transverse Arrest (DTA)**: Arrest of internal rotation at the bispinous plane in occipito-transverse position.
- **Persistent Occipito-Posterior / Face-to-Pubis Delivery**: Increased perineal tears due to larger distending diameters (11.5 cm).
- **Obstructed Labor & Uterine Rupture**: Caused by uncorrected fetopelvic disproportion or malpresentation.
- **Birth Trauma**: Severe molding (Grade 3), cephalhematoma, or brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy).

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Living Ligatures**: The middle layer of the myometrium contains interlacing criss-cross muscle fibers in a Figure-of-8 arrangement. As these fibers contract and permanently retract in the third stage, they act as **"living ligatures"** that physically clamp the tortuous blood vessels passing through them to achieve hemostasis.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Restitution** with **External Rotation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Restitution** is the initial 45° passive untwisting of the _fetal neck_ outside the vulva. **External Rotation** is the subsequent 45° turning of the _head_ in the same direction caused by the internal rotation of the _fetal shoulders_ inside the pelvis.

### 13. Diagram Guides

### Diagram 1: Two-Arm Lever Theory of Flexion

- **Type**: Labeled mechanical schematic diagram
- **Outline shape to draw**: Side profile of fetal head resting on pelvic floor with a lever balance overlay.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Fulcrum** — Occipito-atlantal joint

    3. **Long Lever Arm** — Fulcrum to Chin

- **Arrows/connections**: Upward Resistance → Short Arm Descends / Long Arm Ascends → Chin Touches Chest (Flexion).
- **Annotation**: _"Equal resistance on unequal lever arms forces short arm descent, converting 11.5 cm occipitofrontal diameter to 9.5 cm suboccipitobregmatic diameter."_

### Diagram 2: Couple of Force Theory of Extension

- **Type**: Vector force diagram
- **Outline shape to draw**: Sagittal section of maternal pelvis showing fetal head emerging under symphysis pubis.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Subocciput Fulcrum** — Fixed under pubic arch
    2. **Downward Expulsive Vector** — Contractions + Pushing
    3. **Upward/Forward Resistance Vector** — Pelvic floor and perineum
    4. **Resultant Forward Extension Vector** — Sweeps head upward
- **Arrows/connections**: Downward Force + Upward Resistance → Resultant Forward Extension Vector.
- **Annotation**: _"The head is born by extension as the resultant of downward uterine push and upward pelvic floor resistance."_

### Quick Revision

- **Core Principle**: Mechanism of labor is a series of passive adaptive head movements accommodating fetal diameters to pelvic dimensions.
- **Flexion Mechanics**: Explained by the **Two-Arm Lever Theory**; substitutes 11.5 cm OF diameter with 9.5 cm SOB diameter.
- **Internal Rotation Mechanics**: Driven by **Hart's Rule** (levator ani slope) and AP pelvic outlet shape, rotating occiput 45° anteriorly.
- **Extension Mechanics**: Driven by the **Couple of Force Theory** (downward uterine push + upward perineal resistance) around the subocciput hinge.
- **Restitution vs External Rotation**: Restitution = neck untwisting (45°); External rotation = shoulder internal rotation (45°).
