#  [⭐ High-Yield]

### Definition

- **Asynclitism** is the lateral inclination or deflection of the fetal head in relation to the pelvic inlet plane during engagement, causing the sagittal suture to lie deflected either anteriorly toward the symphysis pubis or posteriorly toward the sacral promontory rather than in the central plane.

---

### Key Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Anterior Asynclitism (Naegele's Obliquity)**: The sagittal suture is deflected posteriorly toward the sacral promontory, making the **anterior parietal bone** the leading presenting part. It is more commonly seen in **multiparae** due to a lax abdominal wall.
- **Posterior Asynclitism (Litzmann's Obliquity)**: The sagittal suture is deflected anteriorly toward the symphysis pubis, making the **posterior parietal bone** the leading presenting part. It is more frequently encountered in **primigravidae** owing to good uterine tone and a tight abdominal wall.
- **Physiological Mechanical Advantage**: Mild asynclitism enables the two parietal eminences to negotiate and cross the pelvic brim one at a time, allowing the smaller **super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm)** to cross the inlet instead of the larger biparietal diameter (9.5 cm).
- **Correction to Synclitism**: During descent, lateral flexion glides the leading parietal bone past the bony landmark (symphysis or promontory), restoring **synclitism** as engagement completes.
- **Pathological Significance**: Mild asynclitism is present in ~75% of normal engagements, but **marked and persistent asynclitism** is pathological and indicates **cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)** or a flat pelvis.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Anterior Asynclitism (Naegele's)**: Anterior parietal bone leads; sagittal suture near sacral promontory; common in multiparae.
- **Posterior Asynclitism (Litzmann's)**: Posterior parietal bone leads; sagittal suture near symphysis pubis; common in primigravidae.
- **Mechanical Benefit**: Replaces biparietal diameter (9.5 cm) with super-subparietal diameter (**8.5 cm**) so parietal eminences cross the brim sequentially.
- **Clinical Warning**: Persistent, uncorrected asynclitism indicates **cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)**.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Asynclitism is the physiological lateral deflection of the fetal head during engagement that allows the parietal eminences to cross the pelvic brim sequentially via the smaller 8.5 cm super-subparietal diameter, categorized into Naegele's (anterior) and Litzmann's (posterior) obliquities.**
