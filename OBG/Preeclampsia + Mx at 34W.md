### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pre-eclampsia (PE)

- **Pre-eclampsia**: Defined as the new onset of hypertension accompanied by **proteinuria** OR, in the absence of proteinuria, new-onset **end-organ dysfunction**, arising after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman. It is a progressive multisystem disorder peculiar to pregnancy.
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:
    1. **Hypertension**: Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) **≥140 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP) **≥90 mmHg**, measured on two occasions at least 4 hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation. (Severe hypertension: SBP ≥160 mmHg and/or DBP ≥110 mmHg confirmed on repeat measurement within 15–30 minutes).
    2. **Proteinuria**: Urinary excretion of **≥0.3 g protein in a 24-hour urine collection**, or urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio **>0.3 mg/mg**, or dipstick reading of **≥1+** (persistent 30 mg/dL) in clean-catch random samples.
    3. **End-Organ Dysfunction (In the absence of proteinuria)**:
        - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL**.
        - **Renal Insufficiency**: Serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** or doubling of baseline serum creatinine.
        - **Impaired Liver Function**: Serum transaminases (AST and ALT) elevated to **≥2 times the upper limit of normal**.
        - **Pulmonary Edema**.
        - **New-onset persistent cerebral or visual symptoms**.

### 2. Etiopathogenesis & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. High-Risk Factors

- **Primigravida**: First-time maternal exposure to chorionic villi (paternal antigens).
- **Maternal Age**: Primigravidae at extremes of age (<20 years or >35 years).
- **Placental Hyperplacentosis**: Excessive trophoblastic mass (multifetal pregnancy, hydatidiform mole).
- **Previous History or Family History**: Prior pre-eclampsia, family history of pre-eclampsia in mother/sister.
- **Pre-existing Medical Conditions**: Chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, obesity (BMI >35 kg/m²), antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APSA), and inherited thrombophilias.

#### B. Etiopathogenesis (Two-Stage Model) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Stage 1 — Defective Endovascular Trophoblastic Invasion**:
    - In normal pregnancy, endovascular cytotrophoblasts invade uterine spiral arteries up to the myometrial segment (16–18 weeks), replacing musculoelastic media with fibrinoid material to transform narrow, high-resistance vessels into wide, low-resistance channels.
    - In pre-eclampsia, trophoblastic invasion **fails beyond the decidua-myometrial junction**. The myometrial spiral arterioles remain narrow, muscular, and hyper-responsive to vasoconstrictors, leading to chronic **uteroplacental ischemia and hypoxia**.
2. **Stage 2 — Systemic Endothelial Dysfunction & Angiogenic Imbalance**:
    - Hypoxic placenta releases anti-angiogenic factors into maternal circulation:
        - **sFlt-1 (Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1)**: Binds and depletes Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF).
        - **sEng (Soluble Endoglin)**: Inhibits TGF-β1 signaling.
    - **Vasoactive Mediator Imbalance**: Increased Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂ - vasoconstrictor) and Endothelin-1 with decreased Prostacyclin (PGI₂ - vasodilator) and Nitric Oxide (NO).
    - **Maternal Syndrome**: Intense systemic vasospasm, increased vascular permeability (capillary leak), microvascular thrombosis, and multi-organ hypoperfusion.

### 4. Clinical Features & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Features of Severe Pre-eclampsia (Pre-eclampsia with Severe Features) [⭐ High-Yield]

Diagnosed when pre-eclampsia presents with **one or more** of the following clinical or laboratory features:

1. **Severe Blood Pressure Elevation**: Systolic BP **≥160 mmHg** and/or Diastolic BP **≥110 mmHg** on 2 occasions at least 4 hours apart (or once if acute parenteral antihypertensives are administered).
2. **Persistent Cerebral Symptoms**: Severe, persistent frontal or occipital headache unresponsive to standard analgesics.
3. **Visual Disturbances**: Blurring of vision, scotoma, diplopia, photopsia, or cortical blindness (amaurosis fugax) due to occipital vasogenic edema (PRES) or retinal ischemia/detachment.
4. **Epigastric or Right Upper Quadrant Pain**: Severe, persistent pain due to stretching of Glisson's capsule from hepatic subcapsular edema, focal necrosis, or subcapsular hematoma.
5. **Impaired Liver Function**: Serum transaminases (AST/SGOT, ALT/SGPT) elevated to **≥2 times the upper limit of normal**.
6. **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL**.
7. **Renal Insufficiency / Oliguria**: Serum creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL** (or doubling of baseline) OR oliguria with urine output **<400–500 mL in 24 hours**.
8. **Pulmonary Edema**: Due to leaky capillaries, increased pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure, and reduced plasma oncotic pressure.
9. **HELLP Syndrome**: Triad of Hemolysis (schistocytes on peripheral smear, LDH >600 U/L), Elevated Liver enzymes (AST/ALT ≥2× normal), and Low Platelets (<100,000/μL).
10. **Fetal Compromise**: Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), oligohydramnios, or abnormal umbilical artery Doppler velocimetry (Absent or Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity - AEDV/REDV).

_Note: According to ACOG 2013 guidelines, the degree of proteinuria (e.g. massive proteinuria ≥5 g/24 hr) is NO LONGER considered a sole criterion for classifying pre-eclampsia as severe._

5. **Rapid, Generalized Pathological Edema**: Sudden excessive facial, vulval, or abdominal wall swelling.
6. **Central Nervous System Excitability**: Brisk patellar reflexes, ankle clonus, irritability, or somnolence.

#### A. Maternal Laboratory Workup

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Peripheral Blood Smear**:
    - **Hemoconcentration**: Elevated hematocrit due to capillary leak.
    - **Thrombocytopenia**: Platelet count **<100,000/μL** indicates severe pre-eclampsia.
    - **Peripheral Smear**: Presence of fragmented red cells (**schistocytes, burr cells**) confirms microangiopathic hemolytic anemia in HELLP syndrome.
2. **Renal Function Tests (RFTs)**:
    - **Serum Uric Acid**: Elevated **>4.5 mg/dL** (normal <4 mg/dL); hyperuricemia is a reliable hallmark indicating tubular reabsorption changes and disease severity.
    - **Serum Creatinine**: Elevated **>1.1 mg/dL** (indicates renal impairment).
    - Blood urea and non-protein nitrogen (NPN) levels.
3. **Urinalysis for Protein**:
    - 24-hour urine collection for protein (≥0.3 g/24 hr for pre-eclampsia; >5 g/24 hr historically termed severe).
    - Urine protein-to-creatinine (P/C) ratio >0.3 mg/mg.
4. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)**:
    - Serum transaminases: **AST/SGOT (>70 U/L)** and **ALT/SGPT** elevated to ≥2× normal.
    - **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)**: **>600 U/L** (marker of hemolysis and cellular necrosis).
    - Total Serum Bilirubin: **>1.2 mg/dL**.
5. **Coagulation Profile**:
    - Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Serum Fibrinogen, and D-dimers (indicated when platelet count is <100,000/μL to rule out DIC).

#### B. Maternal Clinical & Imaging Evaluations

1. **Fundal / Ophthalmoscopic Examination**: Evaluates retinal vessel caliber (vein:arteriole diameter ratio altered from normal 3:2 to 3:1), segmental arteriolar spasm, retinal edema, hemorrhages, exudates, or retinal detachment.
2. **Neuro-imaging (Brain MRI / CT)**:
    - **Indication**: Persistent severe headache, visual loss, hyperreflexia, focal neurological deficits, or status eclampticus.
    - **Finding**: Bilateral hyperintense T2/FLAIR subcortical white matter lesions confirming **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**.

#### C. Fetal Wellbeing & Surveillance Panel

1. **Daily Fetal Movement Counting (DFMCR / Kick Chart)**: Maternal monitoring of fetal movements.
2. **Non-Stress Test (NST) & Cardiotocography (CTG)**: Performed daily or twice weekly to detect non-reactive tracing or late decelerations.
3. **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: Assesses fetal tone, movements, breathing, and amniotic fluid volume.
4. **Ultrasonography (USG Biometry)**: Evaluates Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW) and detects FGR every 2–3 weeks.
5. **Doppler Velocimetry**:
    - **Umbilical Artery (UA) Doppler**: Monitors for increased placental vascular resistance (high S/D ratio, Absent or Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity - AEDV/REDV).
    - **Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) & Cerebroplacental Ratio (CPR)**: Assesses brain-sparing reflex.

#### A. Hospital Admission & Inpatient Stabilization Protocol

- **Compulsory Admission**: All patients with severe pre-eclampsia must be admitted to a tertiary care hospital equipped with a high-dependency unit (HDU) / ICU and a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU).
- **Bed Rest**: Left-lateral position in bed to prevent aortocaval compression, increase renal blood flow (promoting diuresis), and enhance uteroplacental perfusion.
- **Monitoring Schedule**:
    - Maternal BP checked 4 to 6-hourly.
    - Hourly urine output monitoring via Foley's catheter.
    - Daily weight and monitoring for ominous premonitory symptoms.
    - Serial CBC, platelet count, LFT, RFT, and 24-hr urine protein twice weekly.

---

#### B. Medical Management Regimens

##### 1. Anticonvulsant Prophylaxis — Drug of Choice: Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄·7H₂O)** is the drug of choice for seizure prophylaxis in severe pre-eclampsia and imminent eclampsia (Magpie Trial 2002).
- **Pritchard Intramuscular (IM) Regimen**:
    - **Loading Dose**: **4 g MgSO₄ IV** (20% concentration) slowly over 5–10 minutes PLUS **10 g MgSO₄ IM** (5 g of 50% solution deep IM in each buttock).
    - **Maintenance Dose**: **5 g MgSO₄ IM (50% solution)** every 4 hours in alternate buttocks.
- **Zuspan Intravenous (IV) Regimen**: Loading 4–6 g IV in 100 mL fluid over 15–20 minutes, followed by 1–2 g/hour continuous IV infusion.
- **Mandatory 4-Hourly Pre-Requisite Checks Before Maintenance Dose**:
    1. **Patellar Reflex (Knee Jerk)**: Must be **PRESENT**.
    2. **Respiratory Rate**: Must be **≥12 breaths/minute**.
    3. **Urinary Output**: Must be **≥30 mL/hour** (or ≥100 mL over 4 hours).
- **Therapeutic Range & Antidote**: Therapeutic serum level = **4 to 7 mEq/L**. Specific antidote for toxicity = **10 mL of 10% Calcium Gluconate IV slowly over 10 minutes**.

##### 2. Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when SBP is **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP is **≥110 mmHg**.
- **Target Blood Pressure**: SBP **140–150 mmHg** and DBP **90–100 mmHg**.
- **First-Line Agents**:
    - **Inj. Labetalol**: 20 mg IV bolus over 2 minutes; repeat 40–80 mg IV every 10 minutes up to max cumulative dose of **300 mg**.
    - **Inj. Hydralazine**: 5–10 mg IV diluted in 10 mL NS over 20 minutes; repeat 5–10 mg IV every 20–30 minutes up to max **20 mg**.
    - **Tab. Nifedipine**: 10 mg PO (swallowed whole with water, **NEVER sublingual**); repeat 10–20 mg PO after 30 minutes if needed.

##### 3. Antenatal Corticosteroid Therapy for Fetal Lung Maturation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mandatory Administration at 34 Weeks**: Antenatal corticosteroid therapy is strongly recommended for all cases of severe pre-eclampsia presenting **at or before 34 weeks of gestation**.
- **Regimen**: **Inj. Betamethasone 12 mg IM**, 2 doses given 24 hours apart (OR Inj. Dexamethasone 6 mg IM, 4 doses given 12 hours apart).
- **Neonatal & Maternal Benefits**: Dramatically reduces neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS), Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH), Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC), neonatal mortality, and improves maternal platelet count and urinary output.

#### C. Obstetric Management & Decision on Timing of Delivery at 34 Weeks [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Definitive Treatment Principle

- **Delivery is the only definitive cure for pre-eclampsia.**.
- **At 34 Completed Weeks of Gestation**: Once the gestational age reaches **34 completed weeks**, the risks of continuing a pregnancy with severe pre-eclampsia outweigh the risks of prematurity.
- **Management Plan at 34 Weeks**: Administer the complete course of antenatal corticosteroids (48 hours), stabilize blood pressure, initiate prophylactic MgSO₄, and **proceed with planned delivery**.

##### 2. Indications for Immediate Emergency Delivery at 34 Weeks (Irrespective of Corticosteroid Completion) [⭐ High-Yield]

If any of the following maternal or fetal emergency indications develop, immediate delivery is indicated without waiting for 34 weeks or corticosteroid completion:

- Imminent eclampsia or eclamptic convulsions.
- HELLP syndrome.
- Uncontrolled severe hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg) despite dual antihypertensives.
- Pulmonary edema or arterial oxygen desaturation (PaO₂ <95%).
- Severe renal failure (oliguria <500 mL/24 hr or serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL).
- Persistent severe headache or visual loss.
- Placental abruption.
- Non-reassuring fetal status on EFM or umbilical artery Doppler showing Absent or Reversed End-Diastolic Velocity (AEDV/REDV).

##### 3. Mode of Delivery at 34 Weeks [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vaginal Delivery (Preferred)**:
    - Vaginal delivery is the preferred mode if Bishop's score is favorable.
    - **Surgical Induction**: Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM / Amniotomy) combined with Oxytocin infusion.
    - **Cervical Ripening**: Prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) gel 0.5 mg intracervically or pessary if cervix is unripe.
    - **Intrapartum Care**: Continue continuous EFM, IV antihypertensives, and prophylactic MgSO₄ throughout labor. Shorten the second stage of labor using prophylactic **forceps or ventouse extraction**.
- **Cesarean Section (LSCS)**:
    - Performed for obstetric indications (malpresentation, cephalopelvic disproportion), unfavorable cervix in a rapidly deteriorating mother, or non-reassuring fetal status.
    - **Anesthesia**: **Neuraxial anesthesia (epidural or spinal)** is preferred over general anesthesia.

##### 4. Postpartum Management

- Continue prophylactic **MgSO₄ for 24 hours postpartum** (or 24 hours after the last seizure).
- Continue oral antihypertensives and monitor BP, fluid balance, and urine output for at least 48–72 hours.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neurological**: Eclampsia (fits), cerebral hemorrhage, PRES, cortical blindness.
- **Hematological**: HELLP syndrome, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), severe thrombocytopenia.
- **Placental & Vascular**: **Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae)**.
- **Renal**: Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), acute tubular/cortical necrosis, oliguria, anuria.
- **Cardiopulmonary**: Pulmonary edema, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), acute heart failure.
- **Hepatic**: Subcapsular liver hematoma, liver rupture, jaundice.
- **Obstetric**: Atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH), high maternal mortality.

#### B. Fetal & Perinatal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR / FGR) due to chronic uteroplacental insufficiency.
- Oligohydramnios.
- Spontaneous or iatrogenic preterm birth.
- Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD) / Stillbirth.
- Intrapartum fetal hypoxia, birth asphyxia, low Apgar scores, and NICU admission.

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Proteinuria is NO LONGER Mandatory**: According to current ACOG guidelines, pre-eclampsia can be diagnosed and classified as severe even in the total absence of proteinuria, provided new-onset hypertension is accompanied by any systemic end-organ feature (thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, pulmonary edema, or severe visual/cerebral symptoms).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Giving sublingual Nifedipine for severe hypertension or delaying the loading dose of MgSO₄ to check kidney function.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Nifedipine must ALWAYS be swallowed whole with water**; sublingual administration causes a rapid, uncontrolled precipitate drop in BP, resulting in cerebral ischemia and acute fetal distress.
    - **The loading dose of MgSO₄ is given IRRESPECTIVE of renal function!** Renal function (urine output ≥30 mL/hr) is evaluated only before administering the 4-hourly _maintenance_ doses.

#### Mnemonic: S-E-V-E-R-E (Features of Severe Pre-eclampsia)

- **S**: **Systolic BP ≥160 mmHg or Diastolic BP ≥110 mmHg**

- **H**: **Headache (severe, persistent, frontal/occipital)**
- **E**: **Epigastric / Right hypochondriac pain**
- **A**: **Altered vision (blurring, scotoma, amaurosis fugax)**
- **D**: **Decrease in urine output (marked oliguria) & Sudden generalized edema**

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: New BP **≥140/90 mmHg** after 20 weeks with proteinuria (≥0.3 g/24 hr) OR end-organ damage.
- **Severe Features**: BP **≥160/110 mmHg**, severe headache, visual loss, RUQ/epigastric pain, platelets **<100,000/μL**, AST/ALT ≥2× normal, creatinine **>1.1 mg/dL**, or pulmonary edema.
- **Imminent Eclampsia Symptoms**: Persistent frontal headache, visual scotomata/blurring, epigastric pain, and marked oliguria.
- **Drug of Choice for Seizure Prophylaxis**: **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hourly). Antidote: 10% Calcium Gluconate 10 mL IV.
- **Management at 34 Weeks**: Admit in tertiary care, give **MgSO₄ prophylaxis**, control BP with **IV Labetalol / Nifedipine**, administer **Betamethasone (12 mg IM 2 doses 24h apart)** for lung maturity, and **proceed to delivery**.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Pre-eclampsia is a multisystem gestational disorder defined by new-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) and proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction after 20 weeks of gestation, driven by defective spiral artery remodelling and systemic endothelial vasospasm, requiring close monitoring, Magnesium Sulfate seizure prophylaxis, antihypertensives, corticosteroids, and definitive delivery at 34 weeks.**
