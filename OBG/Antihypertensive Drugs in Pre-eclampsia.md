# Antihypertensive Drugs in Severe Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (Pre-eclampsia) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition & Diagnostic Thresholds [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Severe Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (Severe Pre-eclampsia)

- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)**: Sustained rise of blood pressure to **≥140/90 mmHg** after **20 weeks of gestation** in a previously normotensive woman, resolving within 12 weeks postpartum.
- **Severe PIH / Severe Pre-eclampsia**: Diagnosed when pre-eclampsia presents with **severe hypertension** (Systolic Blood Pressure [SBP] **≥160 mmHg** and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure [DBP] **≥110 mmHg**, confirmed on repeat measurement within 15 to 30 minutes) OR when hypertension is accompanied by systemic end-organ dysfunction (thrombocytopenia <100,000/μL, serum creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, elevated transaminases AST/ALT >70 U/L, pulmonary edema, or visual/cerebral symptoms).

---

### 2. Indications & Target Blood Pressure [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Indications for Acute Parenteral Antihypertensive Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Acute Severe Hypertension**: SBP **≥160 mmHg** and/or DBP **≥110 mmHg** (or Mean Arterial Pressure [MAP] ≥125 mmHg) measured on two occasions or once if acute parenteral drugs are required.
2. **Prevention of Maternal Cerebrovascular Accidents**: To protect the mother from cerebral hemorrhage, hypertensive encephalopathy, acute heart failure, and placental abruption.
3. **NICE Guidelines Threshold**: Persistent BP **≥150/100 mmHg** warrants oral antihypertensive treatment.

#### B. Target Blood Pressure Range [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Target SBP**: **130 to 140 mmHg** (or 140–150 mmHg).
- **Target DBP**: **80 to 90 mmHg** (or 90–100 mmHg).
- **Crucial Rule**: **Overcorrection of blood pressure must be strictly avoided!** Precipitous drop in maternal BP reduces uteroplacental perfusion, precipitating severe fetal hypoxia and distress.

### 5. Detailed Profiles of First-Line Antihypertensive Drugs [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Injection Labetalol [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mechanism**: Non-selective β-adrenergic and selective α₁-adrenergic receptor blocker (ratio 3:1 for oral, 7:1 for IV). Reduces systemic vascular resistance without altering cardiac output or uteroplacental blood flow.
- **Administration Protocol**:
    - Initial **20 mg IV bolus** over 2 minutes.
    - Check BP at 10 to 15-minute intervals.
    - If target BP is not achieved, administer **40 mg IV**, then **80 mg IV**, then **80 mg IV** every 10–15 minutes up to a maximum cumulative dose of **300 mg**.
- **Contraindications**: Bronchial asthma, severe sinus bradycardia, second/third-degree heart block, and overt congestive heart failure.

#### B. Injection Hydralazine [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mechanism**: Direct arterial smooth muscle relaxant; reduces peripheral vascular resistance and increases heart rate, stroke volume, cardiac output, and renal/uteroplacental blood flow.
- **Administration Protocol**:
    - Initial **5 to 10 mg IV** diluted in 10 mL Normal Saline given slowly over 20 minutes.
    - Repeat 5 to 10 mg IV every 20 to 30 minutes if SBP remains ≥160 or DBP ≥110 mmHg.
    - Maximum cumulative IV dose = **20 to 30 mg**.
- **Contraindications & Caution**: Avoid in patients with severe tachycardia, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), or aortic dissection.

#### C. Oral Nifedipine [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mechanism**: Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker; selectively inhibits voltage-gated L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle cells, producing potent arterial vasodilation.
- **Administration Protocol**:
    - **10 to 20 mg PO** (swallowed whole with water).
    - Repeat 10 to 20 mg PO after 30 minutes if BP remains elevated.
    - Maintenance: 10 to 20 mg PO every 6 to 8 hours (maximum 120–240 mg/day).
- **Mandatory Safety Rule**: **NEVER administer sublingually!** Sublingual administration causes rapid, uncontrolled hypotension, precipitating maternal cerebral ischemia and acute fetal distress.

### 6. Contraindicated Antihypertensives in Pregnancy [⭐ High-Yield]

The following antihypertensive classes are **strictly contraindicated** during pregnancy due to high teratogenic and fetotoxic risks:

1. **ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Captopril, Enalapril) & Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs - e.g., Losartan, Telmisartan)**:
    - **Fetal Hazards**: Causes **fetal renal tubular dysgenesis**, severe **oligohydramnios**, neonatal acute renal failure, pulmonary hypoplasia, intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), and calvarial (skull) hypoplasia.
2. **Atenolol / Propranolol (Non-selective β-blockers)**:
    - Associated with significant Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR), neonatal bradycardia, and hypoglycemia.
3. **Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide, Thiazides)**:
    - **Relative/Absolute Contraindication in PE**: Pre-eclampsia is already characterized by intravascular volume contraction and hemoconcentration; routine diuretic use further depletes plasma volume, compromising placental perfusion.
    - **Exceptions**: Indicated ONLY in cases of **pulmonary edema**, **acute left ventricular failure**, or co-existing chronic kidney disease.

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The First-Line Triad for Hypertensive Crisis**: **IV Labetalol**, **IV Hydralazine**, and **Oral Nifedipine (swallowed whole)** are the three gold-standard first-line drugs for managing acute severe hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg) in pre-eclampsia. Selection depends on drug availability, physician experience, and maternal co-morbidities (e.g., avoid labetalol in asthmatics).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering sublingual Nifedipine or starting Alpha Methyldopa for an acute hypertensive emergency (BP 180/120 mmHg).
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Nifedipine must ALWAYS be swallowed whole with water**; sublingual administration causes a rapid, uncontrolled precipitate drop in BP, resulting in maternal stroke and fetal demise.
    - **Alpha Methyldopa has a slow onset of action (4–6 hours)** and is unsuitable for acute hypertensive emergencies; it is reserved for maintenance therapy in mild-to-moderate pre-eclampsia.

- **L**: **Labetalol IV** (20 mg → 40 mg → 80 mg; avoid in asthma/bradycardia)
- **H**: **Hydralazine IV** (5–10 mg over 20 min; direct arteriolar vasodilator)
- **N**: **Nifedipine PO** (10–20 mg swallowed whole; **NO sublingual**)

#### Mnemonic: A-C-E (Contraindicated Drugs in Pregnancy)

- **A**: **ACE inhibitors & ARBs** (renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, skull defect)
- **C**: **Centrally acting beta-blockers like Atenolol** (severe FGR)
- **E**: **Excessive Diuretics** (worsens intravascular volume contraction)

### Quick Revision

- **Indication for Acute Antihypertensives**: BP **≥160/110 mmHg**.
- **Target BP**: SBP **130–140 mmHg**, DBP **80–90 mmHg** (avoid hypotension).
- **First-Line Emergency Drugs**: **IV Labetalol** (20–80 mg, max 300 mg), **IV Hydralazine** (5–10 mg, max 30 mg), and **Oral Nifedipine** (10–20 mg, swallowed whole).
- **Maintenance Drug**: **Alpha Methyldopa** (250 mg PO BD/TID; slow onset 4–6 hrs).
- **Strictly Contraindicated**: **ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Atenolol**, and **sublingual Nifedipine**.
