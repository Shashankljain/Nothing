# Hegar's Sign [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hegar's Sign**: Defined as the demonstrable softening of the lower uterine segment (isthmus) during early pregnancy, wherein on gentle bimanual pelvic examination, the abdominal and vaginal fingers seem to appose or touch each other below the enlarged body of the uterus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].

---

### Key Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Timing of Appearance**: Present in approximately two-thirds of pregnant women between **6 and 10 weeks** of gestation (demonstrated slightly earlier in multiparae) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
2. **Anatomical & Physiological Basis**: Based on three distinct structural consistency variations: (1) The upper part of the uterine body is enlarged and soft containing the growing fetus, (2) The lower part of the body (isthmus) is empty, thin, and extremely soft, and (3) The cervix is comparatively firm [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63].
3. **Elicitation Technique (Bimanual Examination)**: Performed by introducing two fingers into the anterior vaginal fornix while the fingers of the other hand press abdominally behind the uterine fundus; due to the extreme softening of the isthmus, the fingers feel as though they meet below the body of the uterus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
4. **Diagnostic Category**: Classified as a **probable sign** of early pregnancy (an objective pelvic physical sign elicited by the clinician) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
5. **Clinical Caution / Precaution**: Examination MUST be conducted with utmost gentleness to avoid triggering reflex myometrial contractions or dislodging early decidual attachment, which carries a risk of inducing **iatrogenic abortion** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63].

### Quick Revision

- **Hegar's Sign**: Softening of the lower uterine segment (isthmus) felt between **6 and 10 weeks** of gestation [DC Dutta 1, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
- **Anatomical Basis**: Soft fundal body + extremely soft empty isthmus + firm cervix [DC Dutta 1, p. 63].
- **Technique**: Two fingers in anterior fornix appose abdominal fingers pressed behind fundus [DC Dutta 1, p. 63].
- **Precaution**: Gentle bimanual examination required to prevent triggering iatrogenic abortion [DC Dutta 1, p. 63].
