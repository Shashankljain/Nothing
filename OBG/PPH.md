# Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH): Causes, Complications, and Surgical Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Definition**: Any amount of bleeding from or into the genital tract following the birth of the baby up to the end of the puerperium that adversely affects the general condition of the patient, as evidenced by a rising pulse rate and falling blood pressure.
- **Quantitative Definition (WHO / ACOG / RCOG)**: Cumulative blood loss **≥500 mL** following a vaginal delivery or **≥1000 mL** following a Cesarean section.
- **Severity Grading**:
    - **Minor PPH**: Blood loss **500 mL to 1000 mL (1 L)**.
    - **Major PPH**: Blood loss **>1000 mL (1 L)**.
    - **Severe PPH**: Blood loss **>2000 mL (2 L)**.

### 3. Etiology / Causes (The "4 Ts" Framework) [⭐ High-Yield]

Postpartum hemorrhage is systematically categorized into four basic underlying pathologies:

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │   Etiological Classification (4 Ts)    │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ TONE (Atonicity — 80%)  │               │ TISSUE (Retained — 5-10%)│               │ TRAUMA (10-15%) &       │
│ • Uterine overdistension│               │ • Retained cotyledons   │               │ THROMBIN (1-2%)         │
│ • Myometrial exhaustion │               │ • Succenturiate lobe    │               │ • Genital tract tears   │
│ • Tocolytics / MgSO₄    │               │ • Blood clots           │               │ • DIC / Coagulopathy    │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Tone (Uterine Atonicity — 80%) [⭐ High-Yield]

Failure of the myometrium to contract and retract effectively after placental separation.

- **Uterine Overdistension**: Multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, fetal macrosomia (**>4 kg**).
- **Myometrial Exhaustion & Dysfunction**: Prolonged labor (**>12 hours**), precipitate labor, grand multiparity, uterine fibroids.
- **Pharmacological & Anesthetic Agents**: Halothane/ether anesthesia, tocolytic drugs (Nifedipine, Terbutaline), and **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)**.
- **Third Stage Mismanagement**: Premature cord traction, fundal kneading/fiddling, sudden forced expulsion.
- **Maternal Factors**: Malnutrition, severe anemia (**Hb <9 g/dL**), obesity (**BMI >35 kg/m²**), maternal age **>40 years**, prior history of PPH.

#### B. Tissue (Retained Products — 5–10%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Retained placental cotyledons, succenturiate lobes, membranes, or blood clots preventing complete myometrial retraction.

#### C. Trauma (Genital Tract Injury — 10–15%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Lacerations of the cervix, vagina, perineum (episiotomy extension), lower uterine segment, or broad ligament/vulvovaginal hematomas.

#### D. Thrombin (Coagulopathy — 1–2%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Pre-existing or acquired coagulation failure: Abruptio placentae, severe pre-eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, amniotic fluid embolism, dead fetus syndrome, or IUD.

#### Core Mechanism — The "Living Ligature" Concept:

- Hemostasis following placental separation relies on **myometrial retraction**, wherein intermediate figure-of-8 muscle fibers contract and clamp tortuous blood vessels passing through them (**"living ligature"**).
- In **atonic PPH**, failure of retraction leaves torn sinuses wide open, resulting in rapid blood loss (**500–600 mL/minute**) directly from uterine vessels.

### 5. Clinical Features & Diagnostic Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Signs & Symptoms

- Continuous vaginal bleeding presenting as a dark red trickle or sudden massive gush.
- **Abdominal Palpation [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - _Atonic PPH_: Uterus is **soft, flabby, and boggy**, hardening upon massage with expression of blood clots.
    - _Traumatic PPH_: Uterus is felt **well-contracted and hard** despite bright red vaginal bleeding.

#### B. Bedside Clinical Assessment (Rule of 30 & Shock Index) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rule of 30 for PPH Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Fall in Systolic Blood Pressure by **≥30 mmHg**.
    - Rise in Pulse Rate by **≥30 beats/minute**.
    - Respiratory Rate **>30 breaths/minute**.
    - Drop in Hemoglobin / Hematocrit by **≥30%**.
    - Fall in Urine Output to **<30 mL/hour**.
- **Shock Index (SI = Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP)**: Normal **0.5–0.7**; an SI **>0.9 to 1.1** indicates major acute blood loss (**>20–30% volume deficit**) requiring urgent transfusion.

### 7. Surgical & Interventional Management of PPH [⭐ High-Yield]

Surgical management is indicated when medical uterotonics (Oxytocin, Methergine, Carboprost, Misoprostol, Tranexamic Acid) fail to arrest hemorrhage.

└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Mechanical & Intrauterine Tamponade Methods (First-Line Interventions) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Bimanual Uterine Compression (Hamilton Maneuver)**:
    - Gloved hand introduced into vagina as a fist in the anterior fornix; external hand placed abdominally behind fundus, squeezing the uterus firmly between both hands to compress open sinuses.
2. **Hydrostatic Balloon Tamponade (First-Line Conservative Surgical Action)**:
    - Insertion of a **Bakri Balloon**, **Foley Catheter**, or **Condom Catheter** into the uterine cavity, inflated with **200 to 500 mL of warm sterile normal saline** for 4 to 24 hours.
    - **Success Rate**: Avoids hysterectomy in **78% of cases**.
3. **Tight Intrauterine Packing**:
    - Uniform packing of the uterine cavity from fundus downward using a **5-meter long, 8-cm wide gauze strip**soaked in antiseptic cream. Exerts direct mechanical pressure on open sinuses; removed after 24 hours.

#### B. Compression Suture Techniques (Uterine-Sparing) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **B-Lynch Compression Suture (1997)**:
    - Continuous vertical bracing suture using **No. 1 Chromic Catgut or Vicryl** passed from lower segment over the fundus to compress anterior and posterior uterine walls against each other like a backpack strap.
    - **Success Rate**: Achieves hemostasis and avoids hysterectomy in **~80% of cases** while preserving fertility.
2. **Hayman & Cho Multiple Square Sutures**:
    - Full-thickness square compression sutures placed through anterior and posterior uterine walls to obliterate bleeding placental bed space.

#### C. Stepwise Uterine Devascularization Procedures [⭐ High-Yield]

Indicated when compression sutures fail or are technically unfeasible:

1. **Bilateral Uterine Artery Ligation (O'Leary)**:
    - Ascending branch of uterine artery ligated at lateral border between upper and lower uterine segments using No. 1 chromic catgut passed 2 cm medial to vessels. Effective in **75–90% of cases**.
2. **Low Uterine Artery Ligation**:
    - Ligation of uterine artery at lower segment level.
3. **Ligation of Utero-Ovarian Vessel Anastomosis**:
    - Ligated bilaterally just below the ovarian ligament.
4. **Bilateral Internal Iliac (Hypogastric) Artery Ligation**:
    - Ligation of the **anterior division** of the internal iliac artery below the origin of the posterior division using No. 1 silk.
    - **Hemodynamic Effect**: Reduces pelvic arterial pulse pressure by **85%**, converting arterial flow to low-pressure venous flow to facilitate stable clot formation. Avoids hysterectomy in **~50% of cases** without pelvic tissue necrosis due to extensive collateral blood supply.

#### D. Interventional Radiology — Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Fluoroscopic injection of **gelatin sponge (gelfoam)** particles into bleeding uterine or internal iliac vessels.
- **Success Rate**: **>90% success rate**, preserves uterine integrity and future reproductive capability with low complication rate (3–5%).

#### E. Life-Saving Obstetric Hysterectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications**: Absolute failure of conservative surgical devascularization, severe Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS / placenta accreta/percreta), or unrepairable uterine rupture.
- **Procedure**: Subtotal or total hysterectomy performed promptly involving a second consultant.

### 8. Complications of Postpartum Hemorrhage [⭐ High-Yield]

│ • Acute Kidney Injury   │                                                         │ • Puerperal Sepsis      │
│ • ARDS & TRALI          │                                                         │ • Secondary PPH         │
└─────────────────────────┘                                                         └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Immediate Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hypovolemic / Hemorrhagic Shock**: Severe tissue hypoperfusion and cardiovascular collapse.
2. **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC / Consumptive Coagulopathy)**: Triggered by tissue thromboplastin release and washout of clotting factors (fibrinogen <100–150 mg/dL, platelets <50,000/μL).
3. **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**: Ischemic acute tubular necrosis or renal cortical necrosis resulting from prolonged hypotension (oliguria <400 mL/24 hr).
4. **Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) & TRALI**: Alveolar capillary membrane damage following massive blood transfusion or endotoxemia.

#### B. Delayed / Remote Sequelae [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Sheehan's Syndrome (Postpartum Ischemic Pituitary Necrosis)**: Pituitary avascular necrosis secondary to severe intrapartum hypotension, presenting with agalactia, amenorrhea, and panhypopituitarism.
2. **Puerperal Sepsis & Pelvic Thrombophlebitis**: Increased susceptibility to pelvic infection secondary to anemia and intrauterine instrumentation.
3. **Secondary Postpartum Hemorrhage**: Late bleeding (8th–14th day) due to sloughing of retained cotyledons or subinvolution.
4. **Failing Lactation & Postpartum Depression**.

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden 1 Hour & Shock Index**: Survival in major PPH is directly tied to initiating balanced resuscitation within the **Golden 1 Hour**. A **Shock Index (Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP) >0.9 to 1.1** serves as an immediate bedside indicator of severe acute volume loss (**>20–30%**), mandating rapid blood transfusion.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering Inj. Methergine to a PPH patient with co-existing severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, OR giving Carboprost to an asthmatic patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Methergine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in pre-eclampsia/hypertension** (precipitates hypertensive crisis or eclamptic fits); use **Oxytocin** or **Misoprostol** instead.
    - **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α) is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in bronchial asthma** (precipitates fatal bronchospasm); use **Misoprostol** instead.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: S-U-R-G-I-C-A-L (PPH Surgical & Interventional Steps)

- **S**: **Stepwise Uterine Devascularization** (uterine → low uterine → utero-ovarian → internal iliac).
- **U**: **Uterine Balloon Tamponade** (Bakri balloon 200–500 mL saline, 78% success).
- **R**: **Radiographic Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)** (>90% success with gelfoam).
- **G**: **Gauze Intrauterine Packing** (5 m long, 8 cm wide pack for 24 hours).
- **I**: **Internal Iliac Artery Ligation** (anterior division; 85% pulse pressure drop).
- **C**: **Compression Sutures (B-Lynch / Cho / Hayman)** (~80% success).
- **A**: **Avoid Hysterectomy** via early conservative mechanical/surgical steps.
- **L**: **Life-Saving Obstetric Hysterectomy** (ultimate definitive recourse).

### Quick Revision

- **PPH Definition**: Blood loss ≥500 mL (vaginal) or ≥1000 mL (CS) within 24 hours of birth.
- **4 Ts Etiology**: Tone (80%), Tissue (5–10%), Trauma (10–15%), Thrombin (1–2%).
- **Tamponade Methods**: Bakri Balloon (200–500 mL saline, 78% success) & Intrauterine packing.
- **Surgical Compression**: B-Lynch bracing suture (~80% hemostatic success).
- **Devascularization Steps**: Bilateral Uterine Artery Ligation → Utero-Ovarian Ligation → Internal Iliac (Hypogastric) Artery Ligation (85% pulse pressure reduction).
- **Major Complications**: Hypovolemic shock, Consumptive DIC, Acute Kidney Injury, Sheehan's syndrome.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Postpartum hemorrhage is a major obstetric emergency defined by cumulative blood loss ≥500 mL in vaginal delivery or ≥1000 mL in Cesarean section within 24 hours of birth, primarily caused by myometrial atony (80%), requiring rapid fluid/blood resuscitation within the Golden 1 Hour, followed by uterine-sparing surgical interventions (balloon tamponade, B-Lynch compression sutures, stepwise devascularization) to prevent fatal shock, DIC, and Sheehan's syndrome.**.
