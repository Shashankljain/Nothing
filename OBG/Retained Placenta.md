# Retained Placenta: Definition, Etiology, Management, and Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Retained Placenta

- **Retained Placenta**: Defined as the non-expulsion of the placenta from the birth canal within **30 minutes** after the birth of the baby under active or expectant management (WHO threshold is 15–30 minutes) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Incidence**: Occurs in approximately **1% to 3%** of all vaginal deliveries [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Clinical Significance**: It is one of the major life-threatening emergencies of the third stage of labor, acting as a primary trigger for severe primary and secondary **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**, obstetric shock, and puerperal sepsis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Normal placental expulsion requires three distinct physiological phases: (1) Separation through the spongy layer of decidua basalis, (2) Descent into the lower uterine segment and vagina, and (3) Expulsion through the introitus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394]. Interference in any of these phases leads to retention [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394]:

#### A. Unseparated Simple Adherent Placenta (Commonest Cause - 90%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Myometrial Atony**: The primary reason for failure of placental separation is inadequate uterine contraction and retraction following fetal birth [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Risk Factors**:
    - Uterine overdistension (twin/multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, macrosomia) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388].
    - Grand multiparity (fibrotic replacement of myometrium) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].
    - Prolonged labor and uterine exhaustion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].
    - Uterine malformations (bicornuate or septate uterus) or fibroids [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
    - Tocolytic therapy (Isoxsuprine, Nifedipine) or Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388].

#### B. Incarcerated / Trapped Placenta (Separated but Retained) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Placenta is completely or partially detached from the uterine wall but remains trapped inside the uterine cavity [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Causes**:
    - **Constriction Ring (Hourglass Contraction)**: Spasm of the circular muscle fibers around the internal os or lower segment, often incited by premature manipulation or improper administration of ergometrine [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
    - Poor voluntary expulsive efforts or a full urinary bladder [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].

#### C. Morbidly Adherent Placenta (Placenta Accreta Spectrum - PAS) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Direct anchoring of chorionic villi to the myometrium due to partial or complete absence of **decidua basalis** and **Nitabuch's fibrinoid layer** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 395; Marrow Vol 2, p. 457].
- **Risk Factors**: Prior Cesarean delivery (risk increases from 3% with no prior CS to 61% with ≥3 prior CS), placenta previa, prior curettage, or myomectomy [DC Dutta 1, Ch 19, p. 237; Ch 28, p. 395; Marrow Vol 2, p. 458].

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Failure of Placental Expulsion**: Non-delivery of placenta **>30 minutes** after birth of baby [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Vaginal Bleeding**: Revealed bright or dark red bleeding if partially separated; may be concealed inside uterine cavity if incarcerated behind a constriction ring [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Systemic Hypovolemia**: Dizziness, air hunger, cold clammy extremities, and restlessness [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 390, 394].

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Abdominal Palpation**:
    - _Unseparated / Atonic_: Uterus is **soft, flabby, and boggy**, extending above umbilicus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].
    - _Separated / Incarcerated_: Uterus feels firm, globular, and elongated with fundal height elevated above umbilicus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 125; Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Vaginal Examination**:
    - Cord length outside introitus is lengthened if separated, or static if unseparated [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 125].
    - Internal os may reveal an **hourglass constriction ring** or a patulous os with unseparated placenta attached to fundus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
- **Hemodynamic Signs**: Tachycardia (pulse >100 bpm), hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg), elevated Shock Index (>0.9) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 390].

---

### 6. Diagnosis & Diagnostic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Time-Based Clinical Threshold**: Failure of placental delivery **>30 minutes** post-delivery [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].
2. **Clinical Signs of Placental Separation**:
    - _Fundal Sign_: Uterus becomes globular, firm, and rises above umbilicus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 125].
    - _Cord Sign (Ahfeld's Sign)_: Permanent elongation of umbilical cord without receding when uterus is pushed up [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 125].
    - _Suprapubic Pressure Sign (Küstner's Sign)_: Pushing uterus upward abdominally does NOT pull umbilical cord inward if separated [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 125].
3. **Bedside Pelvic Ultrasonography (USG)**:
    - Identifies exact location of placenta (upper vs lower segment), distinguishes separated trapped placenta from unseparated placenta, and screens for **Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS)** (loss of retroplacental hypoechoic clear zone <1 mm, intraplacental lacunae) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 395; Marrow Vol 2, p. 458].
4. **Emergency Laboratory Panel**:
    - Complete Blood Count (CBC & hematocrit) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 19, p. 239].
    - Blood Grouping & ABO/Rh D cross-matching (reserve 2–4 units PRBC) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 390].
    - Coagulation Profile (PT, APTT, Fibrinogen, Platelet count) to rule out DIC [DC Dutta 1, Ch 19, p. 239; Ch 28, p. 388].

#### STEP 1: Resuscitation & Watchful Expectancy (Golden 30 Minutes) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemodynamic Resuscitation**: Insert large-bore IV cannula (16G); rapidly infuse Ringer's Lactate / Normal Saline with **20 IU Oxytocin in 1 L** at 40–60 drops/min [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388].
- **Catheterization**: Empty bladder completely using a sterile catheter (full bladder inhibits myometrial retraction and descent) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 395].
- **Assisted Expulsion**: If placenta separates, deliver using **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews maneuver)** while uterus is hard and contracted [DC Dutta 1, Ch 13, p. 123; Ch 28, p. 388].

#### STEP 2: Definitive Surgical Management — Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP) under GA [⭐ High-Yield]

If placenta remains unseparated >30 minutes, perform **Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP)** in the operation theater under strict aseptic conditions [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 389]:

##### Step-by-Step Operative Protocol of MRP [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Anesthesia**: **General Anesthesia (GA)** is mandatory to ensure complete uterine relaxation and patient comfort [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389]. _(In extreme emergencies where anesthetist is unavailable, deep IV sedation with Diazepam 10 mg can be used)_ [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
2. **Patient Position & Preparation**: Place patient in **lithotomy position**, paint vulva with povidone-iodine, drape, and catheterize bladder [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
3. **Introduction of Hand**: Smear gloved hand with antiseptic. Hold cord taut with outside hand. Introduce internal hand into vagina and uterine cavity in a **cone-shaped manner** following the umbilical cord to locate placental margin [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
4. **Fundal Counter-pressure**: Place external hand abdominally over uterine fundus to provide firm counter-pressure, steadying fundus and guiding internal fingers [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
5. **Plane of Cleavage & Separation**: Insinuate fingers between placenta and uterine wall at margin with back of hand contacting uterine wall [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389]. Separate placenta using a **sideways slicing movement** until completely detached [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
6. **Extraction & Exploration**: Extract placenta by cord traction with external hand while internal hand re-explores uterine cavity to ensure no cotyledons or tissue bits remain behind [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
7. **Uterotonic & Antibiotic Cover**: Administer **Inj. Methylergometrine 0.2 mg IV** as internal hand is withdrawn to excite firm tetanic contraction [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 389]. Administer IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (**Ampicillin 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 389].
8. **Post-MRP Inspection**: Inspect expelled placenta for completeness and examine cervicovaginal canal to rule out lacerations [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].

#### STEP 3: Management of Morbidly Adherent Placenta (PAS) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **CRITICAL RULE**: If no plane of cleavage is identified during MRP, **STOP FORCIBLE REMOVAL IMMEDIATELY!** Forcible manual separation causes uncontrollable hemorrhage, resulting in 100% risk of emergency hysterectomy or death [DC Dutta 1, Ch 19, p. 238; Ch 28, p. 395].
- **Definitive Treatment**: Perform **Cesarean Hysterectomy** (in multiparae) OR adopt **Conservative Management**(leave placenta in situ, ligate cord close to insertion, close uterus, and administer prophylactic antibiotics ± intra-arterial embolization for aseptic autolysis) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 19, p. 238; Ch 28, p. 395; Marrow Vol 2, p. 459].

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Immediate Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Severe Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Primary atonic or tissue PPH resulting from open sinuses [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].
- **Obstetric Shock**: Hemorrhagic shock (due to blood loss) or **Neurogenic Shock** (due to peritoneal traction, cervical stretching, or prolonged retention >1 hour) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 388, 394].
- **Uterine Perforation / Laceration**: Occurs during difficult MRP [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389].
- **Acute Uterine Inversion**: Caused by traction on cord attached to an unseparated fundal placenta [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389, 396].

#### B. Remote / Delayed Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Puerperal Sepsis & Endometritis**: Bacterial contamination during manual intrauterine manipulation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 389, 394].
- **Secondary Postpartum Hemorrhage**: Retained cotyledons undergoing necrosis and sloughing between 8th and 14th day postpartum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 391].
- **Placental Polyp & Subinvolution** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 17, p. 206; Ch 28, p. 391].
- **Recurrence Risk**: **10–15% recurrence risk** of retained placenta in subsequent pregnancies [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 394].

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden Rule of MRP Technique**: During Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP), the external hand placed over the abdomen MUST maintain continuous firm counter-pressure on the fundus to steady the uterus, while the internal hand uses a **sideways slicing motion** along the natural cleavage plane. Immediately upon withdrawal of the hand, administer **Inj. Methergine 0.2 mg IV** to lock down uterine retraction.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Attempting to force manual separation when no plane of cleavage is felt during MRP.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - In **Simple Adherent Placenta**, a smooth natural cleavage plane between placenta and uterine wall is easily identified.
    - In **Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS)**, **NO plane of cleavage exists**. Any forceful attempt to peel the placenta tears deep myometrium and uterine vessels, resulting in catastrophic hemorrhage and high maternal mortality. **If no plane is felt, STOP MRP immediately and proceed to hysterectomy or conservative management!**

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: R-E-T-A-I-N-E-D (Diagnostic & Management Protocol)

- **R**: **Rule of 30 Minutes** (diagnostic threshold for retained placenta).
- **E**: **Empty Bladder** via Foley catheterization (restores myometrial tone).
- **T**: **Taut Cord Guidance** (internal hand enters in cone shape following taut cord).
- **A**: **Anesthesia (General Anesthesia preferred)** for complete uterine relaxation.
- **I**: **Insinuate fingers at margin** using sideways slicing movement.
- **N**: **No forced separation in Accreta / PAS** (prevents fatal hemorrhage).
- **E**: **External fundal counter-pressure** maintained throughout procedure.
- **D**: **Drugs post-MRP** (Methergine 0.2 mg IV + Ampicillin & Metronidazole).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Non-expulsion of placenta **>30 minutes** post-delivery.
- **Causes**: Simple adherent (atony - 90%), Incarcerated (constriction ring), Morbidly adherent (PAS).
- **Treatment of Choice**: **Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP) under General Anesthesia**.
- **MRP Technique**: Cone-shaped hand entry → fundal counter-pressure → sideways slicing separation → cavity re-exploration → IV Methergine 0.2 mg + broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- **PAS Warning**: If no cleavage plane is found, **STOP MRP immediately**; perform hysterectomy or leave placenta in situ.
- **Major Complications**: Primary/secondary PPH, neurogenic/hemorrhagic shock, puerperal sepsis, uterine inversion.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Retained placenta is a life-threatening third-stage obstetric emergency defined by failure of placental expulsion for more than 30 minutes after delivery, primarily caused by myometrial atony (90%), requiring emergency resuscitation, catheterization, and definitive Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP) under general anesthesia while avoiding forced separation in Placenta Accreta Spectrum.**
