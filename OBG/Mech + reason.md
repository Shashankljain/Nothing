
# Mechanism of Labor: Phases and Anatomical Reasons [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mechanism of Labor**: The series of passive adaptive movements undergone by the fetal presenting part during its journey through the maternal pelvic birth canal in order to adapt its smallest diameters to the principal dimensions of the pelvis.
- **Normal Labor (Eutocia)**: Spontaneous in onset at term (37°/7 to 41°/7 weeks), presenting with a vertex presentation, culminating in natural delivery without undue prolongation or maternal/fetal complications.

---

### 2. Driving Forces / Primary Motors of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Uterine Contractions and Retractions**: Primary propulsive force causing progressive cervical dilatation, effacement, and downward expulsion.
2. **Voluntary Bearing-Down Efforts**: Secondary expulsive force produced by increased intra-abdominal pressure during the second stage (Ferguson reflex).
3. **Fetal Axis Pressure**: Straightening of the fetal spine during contractions transmits fundal pressure directly down the long axis of the fetus to the presenting part.

### 4. Phase-by-Phase Description and Anatomical Reason for Each Phase [⭐ High-Yield]

|Phase / Movement|Anatomical Events & Diameters Involved|Anatomical & Mechanical Reason for the Phase|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1. Engagement**|The passage of the widest transverse diameter of the fetal head (**Biparietal Diameter = 9.5 cm**) through the plane of the pelvic inlet. Occurs in the transverse diameter (70%) or oblique diameter.|**Reason**: Downward gravitational and fundal pressure drives the head into the pelvic brim. **Asynclitism (lateral tilt)** allows the smaller **super-subparietal diameter (8.5 cm)** to cross the brim one parietal eminence at a time rather than the larger biparietal diameter (9.5 cm).|
|**2. Descent**|Continuous downward progress of the presenting part through the birth canal throughout the 1st and 2nd stages of labor.|**Reason**: Driven by three combined forces: (a) Uterine contractions and permanent muscle shortening (**retraction**), (b) maternal bearing-down abdominal efforts, and (c) fetal axis straightening.|
|**3. Flexion**|Bending of the fetal neck so the chin touches the chest, converting the head from a deflexed to a fully flexed attitude.|**Reason (Two-Arm Lever Theory)**: The occipitoatlantoid joint acts as a fulcrum. The short lever arm extends to the occiput, and the long lever arm extends to the chin. Resistance from the cervix, pelvic walls, or levator ani exerts equal upward force on both arms; by mechanical law, the short arm (occiput) descends and the long arm (chin) ascends, reducing the engaging diameter from **occipitofrontal (11.5 cm)** to **suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm)**.|
|**4. Internal Rotation**|Forward rotation of the leading flexed occiput by **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in oblique positions (or 2/8ths / 90° in transverse positions) to lie directly behind the symphysis pubis.|**Reason (Hart's Rule / Law of Pelvic Floor)**: The two halves of the levator ani form a downward-and-forward sloping gutter. The well-flexed occiput touches the pelvic floor first during a contraction; upon elastic recoil of the levator ani, the occiput is directed forward and inward toward the midline. Accommodates the long axis of the head into the widest anteroposterior diameter of the outlet.|
|**5. Crowning**|The maximum biparietal diameter (**9.5 cm**) stretches the vulval introitus without any backward recession occurring between contractions.|**Reason**: The subocciput becomes fixed beneath the lower border of the symphysis pubis (acting as a fulcrum), preventing backward recoil due to permanent uterine retraction holding the head advanced.|
|**6. Extension**|Delivery of the fetal head as the brow, face, and chin sweep sequentially over the distended perineum.|**Reason (Couple of Force Theory)**: The downward propulsive force of uterine contractions combined with the upward-and-forward resistance of the muscular pelvic floor/perineum produce a resultant forward vector. This forces the head to pivot around the suboccipital fulcrum under the pubic arch in a movement of extension.|
|**7. Restitution**|Visible passive movement of the born head outside the vulva, rotating **1/8th of a circle (45°)**in the direction opposite to internal rotation.|**Reason**: Spontaneous **untwisting of the fetal neck**. During internal rotation, the head rotated 45° while the shoulders remained in the oblique diameter, causing neck torsion. Once released from the vulva, the neck untwists to realign the head at right angles with the shoulders.|
|**8. External Rotation**|Further rotation of the head outside the vulva by **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the same direction as restitution.|**Reason**: Caused by the **internal rotation of the fetal shoulders** inside the pelvis as the bisacromial shoulder diameter (**12 cm**) rotates from an oblique diameter into the anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic outlet.|
|**9. Expulsion of Trunk**|Delivery of the anterior shoulder under the symphysis pubis, followed by the posterior shoulder over the perineum and the rest of the body.|**Reason**: Further descent brings the anterior shoulder to hinge under the pubic arch. **Lateral flexion of the fetal spine** allows the posterior shoulder to clear the curved perineum safely, followed rapidly by the trunk.|

### 6. Clinical Features & Signs of Each Movement

- **Engagement**: Diagnosed on abdominal examination when <2/5ths of the fetal head is palpable above the pelvic brim (Crichton's rule) and examining fingers diverge in the first pelvic grip.
- **Flexion**: Diagnosed on vaginal examination (PV) when the **posterior fontanelle (triangular)** is easily reachable near the center of the pelvis while the **anterior fontanelle (diamond)** is difficult to reach.
- **Internal Rotation**: PV reveals the **sagittal suture in the anteroposterior diameter** of the pelvis with the posterior fontanelle directly under the symphysis pubis.
- **Crowning**: The vulval ring remains widely distended by scalp tissue even during the relaxation phase between contractions.

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

#### Eutocia (Normal Mechanism) vs Dystocia (Faulty Mechanism)

|Feature|Normal Mechanism (Occipito-Anterior)|Abnormal Mechanism (Occipito-Posterior / Malposition)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Engaging Diameter**|Suboccipitobregmatic (**9.5 cm**).|Occipitofrontal (**11.5 cm**) due to persistent deflexion.|
|**Internal Rotation**|Short anterior rotation (**45° / 1/8th circle**).|Long anterior rotation (**135° / 3/8ths circle**) or malrotation (Deep Transverse Arrest).|
|**Delivery Mechanism**|Born smoothly by **extension**.|Face-to-pubis delivery by **flexion** then extension, or operative arrest.|
|**Perineal Trauma Risk**|Low (minimal distension diameter).|High (larger diameter distends introitus).|

### 8. Treatment / Management (Conduction of Delivery) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Delivery of the Head (Ritgen's Maneuver)**:
    - Maintain flexion of the head until the subocciput hinges under the symphysis pubis.
    - Apply forward pressure on the fetal chin through the perineum behind the anus with the right hand while controlling rapid extension with the left hand over the occiput.
2. **Selective Episiotomy**: Performed during crowning if the perineum is rigid or tearing is imminent.
3. **Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL)**:
    - Administer **Inj. Oxytocin 10 IU IM** within 1 minute of fetal delivery.
    - Perform **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews technique)** with suprapubic counter-traction.
    - Perform fundal uterine massage immediately post-delivery.

---

### 9. Complications

- **Maternal**: Perineal and vaginal lacerations, cervical tears, obstructed labor (if rotation fails), atonic Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH).
- **Fetal**: Caput succedaneum formation, cephalhematoma, intrapartum asphyxia, brachial plexus injury during shoulder dystocia.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Two-Arm Lever Principle**: Flexion is a pure mechanical consequence of resistance. Because the occipitoatlantoid joint is closer to the occiput than the chin, equal upward resistance against the head creates a greater turning moment on the longer chin arm, forcing the chin toward the chest and presenting the smallest suboccipitobregmatic diameter (**9.5 cm**).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Restitution** with **External Rotation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **Restitution** is a passive **untwisting of the fetal neck** (rotating 45° opposite to internal rotation). **External Rotation** is caused by the **internal rotation of the fetal shoulders** inside the pelvis (rotating another 45° in the same direction as restitution).

- **Labels in order**:

    2. **Short Arm (A)** — Condyles to Occiput (rear)
    3. **Long Arm (B)** — Condyles to Chin (front)
    4. **Upward Pelvic Resistance Arrows** — acting equally on both arms
    5. **Resultant Flexion Movement** — Chin moves toward chest, Suboccipitobregmatic diameter (9.5 cm) presents
- **Arrows/connections**: Upward Resistance → Long Arm Ascends ↑ + Short Arm Descends ↓ → Flexion.
- **One annotation worth extra marks**: _"Equal pelvic resistance on unequal lever arms forces the long chin arm to ascend, achieving complete head flexion."_

### Quick Revision

- **Engaging Diameter**: Suboccipitobregmatic (**9.5 cm**) in well-flexed vertex.
- **Flexion Mechanism**: **Two-Arm Lever Theory** (short arm occiput, long arm chin).
- **Internal Rotation Mechanism**: **Hart's Rule / Law of Pelvic Floor** (levator ani gutter directs occiput 45° anteriorly).
- **Extension Mechanism**: **Couple of Force Theory** (downward uterine thrust + upward pelvic floor resistance).
- **Restitution vs External Rotation**: Restitution = neck untwisting; External rotation = shoulder internal rotation.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **The mechanism of normal labor comprises a sequence of passive adaptive movements—engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, crowning, extension, restitution, external rotation, and expulsion—whereby the fetus adapts its smallest dimensions to navigate the maternal pelvic canal under the influence of mechanical resistive forces.**
