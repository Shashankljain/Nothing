# Diagnosis of Pregnancy in First Trimester [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

- **First Trimester**: Spans from the first day of the Last Menstrual Period (LMP) up to **12 completed weeks (12 weeks + 6 days)** of gestation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 302].
- **Diagnostic Categories**:
    1. **Presumptive Symptoms (Subjective)**: Symptoms felt and reported by the patient [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
    2. **Probable Signs (Objective)**: Physical and pelvic signs elicited by the clinician [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
    3. **Positive / Definitive Signs**: Diagnostic confirmation established by immunological biomarkers and ultrasonography [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 308].

1. **Amenorrhea**:
    - Primary presumptive trigger in a previously healthy woman with regular cycles [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
    - **Hartmann Sign ("Placental Sign")**: Scanty, brief cyclic bleeding around the expected period up to 12 weeks due to incomplete fusion of decidua capsularis and decidua vera [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 270].
2. **Morning Sickness (Emesis Gravidarum)**:
    - Nausea and vomiting on rising in the morning, appearing at **6 weeks**, peaking at **8–10 weeks** (driven by peak serum β-hCG and estrogen), and resolving spontaneously by **12–16 weeks** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 320].
3. **Frequency of Micturition**:
    - Troublesome symptom between **8 and 12 weeks** of gestation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].
    - **Etiology**: (1) Bulky anteflexed uterus resting on the bladder fundus, (2) Bladder mucosal congestion, and (3) Maternal osmoregulatory changes causing polyuria [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62].
    - _Disappears after 12 weeks_ as the uterus ascends into the abdomen [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 308].
4. **Breast Discomfort & Fatigue**:
    - Pricking sensation, fullness, and tenderness evident between **6 and 8 weeks** (prominent in primigravidae) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].

#### B. Probable Signs (Objective Physical & Pelvic Signs) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Breast Changes

- Vascular engorgement with visible delicate sub-venous plexus at **6–8 weeks** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62].
- Pigmentation of the **primary areola** and nipple; enlargement of **Montgomery's tubercles** (hypertrophied sebaceous glands) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62].
- Thick yellowish **colostrum** can be expressed by manual compression by **12 weeks** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 354].

##### 2. Pelvic & Vaginal Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Goodell's Sign**[⭐ High-Yield]|**6 weeks**|**Softening of the cervix** (feels like the lips of the mouth; non-pregnant cervix feels like the tip of the nose) [DC Dutta 1, p. 62].|First sign to become positive; due to increased cervical vascularity and stromal edema [DC Dutta 1, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].|
|**Goodell / Chadwick / Jacquemier Sign** [⭐ High-Yield]|**8 weeks**|**Dusky bluish / purplish discoloration** of the vestibule, anterior vaginal wall, and cervix [DC Dutta 1, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].|Caused by local venous congestion beneath the squamous epithelium [DC Dutta 1, p. 62].|
|**Osiander's Sign**[⭐ High-Yield]|**8 weeks**|**Increased arterial pulsations** felt through the lateral vaginal fornices [DC Dutta 1, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 307].|Reflects hyperdynamic blood flow through dilated uterine arteries [DC Dutta 1, p. 62].|

##### 3. Uterine Size Changes

- **6–8 weeks**: Size of a hen's egg / pear [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 308].
- **8–10 weeks**: Size of a cricket ball / orange [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 308].
- **10–12 weeks**: Size of a fetal head / grapefruit; becomes a pelvic-abdominal organ resting at the pubic symphysis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 62; Marrow Vol 2, p. 308].

#### A. Immunological Pregnancy Tests (β-hCG Detection) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Principle**: Detects the specific **β-subunit of human Chorionic Gonadotropin (β-hCG)** secreted by syncytiotrophoblasts [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 287].
- **Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT)**:
    - **Sandwich ELISA Card Test**: Minimum detectable threshold is **20–60 mIU/mL** of urine [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 287].
    - **Earliest Positive Time**: Day of missed period (or 3–4 days prior) using the first morning voided urine sample [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 287].
- **Serum β-hCG Assay**:
    - Highly sensitive ELISA detects **1–2 mIU/mL** in serum as early as **8–9 days post-ovulation** (5 days before missed period) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Marrow Vol 2, p. 287].
    - **Doubling Time**: Serum β-hCG doubles every **48 hours** in a viable early intrauterine pregnancy [DC Dutta 1, Ch 16, p. 174; Marrow Vol 2, p. 287].
- **Discriminatory Zone of β-hCG [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - The threshold serum β-hCG level at which a true intrauterine gestational sac must be visualized on USG:
        - **Transvaginal Sonography (TVS)**: **1500 to 2000 IU/L** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 16, p. 174; Marrow Vol 2, p. 288].
        - **Transabdominal Sonography (TAS)**: **6500 IU/L** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 16, p. 174; Marrow Vol 2, p. 288].

##### Sequential TVS Milestones [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Intradecidual Sign / Gestational Sac (GS)** (**4.5 to 5 weeks**):
    - First sonographic sign seen as an eccentric fluid collection with a hyper-echogenic rim in the fundal endometrium [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 324].
    - **Double Decidual Sac Sign**: Interface between decidua capsularis and decidua parietalis confirming true intrauterine pregnancy [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 327].
2. **Yolk Sac** (**5.5 weeks**):
    - Appears as a bright echogenic ring with an anechoic center inside the GS when Mean Sac Diameter (MSD) reaches **10 mm** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 324].
    - **Double Bleb Sign**: Co-existence of yolk sac and amniotic sac inside GS [Marrow Vol 2, p. 327].
3. **Fetal Pole & Cardiac Activity** (**6 weeks**):
    - Fetal pole visualized when CRL is **1–5 mm** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 325].
    - Cardiac flickering documented on M-mode (Normal FHR = **110–130 bpm** at 6 weeks) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 325].
4. **Crown-Rump Length (CRL)** (**7 to 12 weeks**):
    - **Single most accurate parameter for gestational age dating** in the entire pregnancy (variability **±5 days**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 7, p. 63; Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 327].

### 5. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Eliciting Hegar's Sign Safely**: When demonstrating Hegar's sign between **6 and 10 weeks**, the bimanual examination (two fingers in the anterior fornix and abdominal hand pressed behind the fundus) must be performed extremely gently. Excessive pressure risks triggering reflex uterine contractions or dislodging early decidual attachment, precipitating **iatrogenic abortion**.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing a **True Gestational Sac** with a **Pseudogestational Sac** on early USG, OR confusing **Piskacek's sign** with a uterine fibroid.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **True Gestational Sac**: Located **eccentrically** within endometrium, shows **double decidual sac sign**, contains a yolk sac/embryo, and grows at **1.1 mm/day** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 41, p. 599; Marrow Vol 2, p. 326].
    - **Pseudogestational Sac**: Located **centrally** in uterine cavity, lacks double decidual sign, empty, associated with **ectopic pregnancy** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 16, p. 174; Marrow Vol 2, p. 326].

### 7. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: G-H-O-C-P (Probable First Trimester Pelvic Signs)

- **G**: **Goodell's sign** (cervical softening at 6 weeks — first sign to become positive).
- **H**: **Hegar's sign** (isthmic softening at 6–10 weeks).
- **O**: **Osiander's sign** (lateral fornix arterial pulsations at 8 weeks).
- **C**: **Chadwick's / Jacquemier's sign** (bluish vaginal discoloration at 8 weeks).
- **P**: **Piskacek's sign** (asymmetric corneal bulge at 8 weeks) & **Palmer's sign** (rhythmic contractions at 4–8 weeks).

### Quick Revision

- **Presumptive Symptoms**: Amenorrhea, morning sickness (6–12 weeks), urinary frequency (8–12 weeks), breast tenderness.
- **Probable Pelvic Signs**: Goodell's (6 wks), Hegar's (6–10 wks), Chadwick's (8 wks), Osiander's (8 wks), Piskacek's (8 wks).
- **First Sign to be Positive**: Goodell's sign (cervical softening at 6 weeks).
- **β-hCG Discriminatory Zone**: Gestational sac visible on TVS at **≥1500–2000 IU/L** (TAS at ≥6500 IU/L).
- **TVS Milestones**: Gestational sac (4.5–5 wks), Yolk sac (5.5 wks), Fetal cardiac activity (6 wks).
- **Best Dating Parameter**: Crown-Rump Length (CRL) on 1st trimester TVS (variability **±5 days**).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Diagnosis of first-trimester pregnancy is established by evaluating presumptive symptoms (amenorrhea, morning sickness), characteristic pelvic signs of tissue hypervascularity and softening (Goodell's, Hegar's, and Chadwick's signs), and definitive confirmation via urine/serum β-hCG immunoassays and transvaginal ultrasonography documenting an intrauterine gestational sac, yolk sac, and crown-rump length (CRL).**
