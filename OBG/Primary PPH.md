# Primary Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Definition

- **Primary Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Any amount of bleeding from or into the genital tract occurring within **24 hours following the birth of the baby** (majority subsequent to the expulsion of the placenta), which adversely affects the general condition of the patient as evidenced by a rising pulse rate and falling blood pressure.

#### B. Quantitative & ACOG / RCOG Definitions

- **Quantitative Definition (WHO)**: Blood loss in excess of **500 mL** following the birth of the baby.
- **ACOG (2017) & RCOG (2016) Definition**: Cumulative blood loss **≥500 mL** following a vaginal delivery or **≥1000 mL** following a Cesarean delivery, accompanied by signs and symptoms of hypovolemia.
- **Average Physiological Blood Losses**:
    - Vaginal delivery = **500 mL**.
    - Cesarean delivery = **1000 mL**.
    - Cesarean hysterectomy = **1500 mL**.
- **Severity Grading**:
    - **Minor PPH**: Blood loss **500 mL to 1000 mL (1 L)**.
    - **Major PPH**: Blood loss **>1000 mL (1 L)**.
    - **Severe PPH**: Blood loss **>2000 mL (2 L)**.

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Etiological Classification — The "4 Ts" (RCOG) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Tone (Atonicity — 80%)**: Failure of the uterine myometrium to contract and retract effectively after placental separation.
2. **Tissue (Retained Products — 5–10%)**: Retained placental bits, succenturiate lobes, membranes, or blood clots interfering with complete uterine retraction.
3. **Trauma (Genital Tract Injury — 10–15%)**: Lacerations of the cervix, vagina, perineum, or lower uterine segment.
4. **Thrombin (Coagulopathy — 1–2%)**: Acquired or congenital blood coagulation failure.

#### B. Predisposing Risk Factors for Primary PPH [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Uterine Overdistension**: Multiple pregnancy (twins/triplets), polyhydramnios, fetal macrosomia.
- **Myometrial Exhaustion & Dysfunction**: Prolonged labor (**>12 hours**), precipitate labor, high parity (**grand multipara**), uterine leiomyomas (fibroids).
- **Iatrogenic & Mismanagement Factors**: Mismanaged third stage of labor (pulling the cord before separation, kneading/fiddling the fundus, premature attempts to deliver unseparated placenta), abrupt stoppage of prolonged oxytocin infusion, deep general anesthesia (halothane, ether).
- **Pharmacological Agents**: Tocolytic therapy (Isoxsuprine, Nifedipine, Terbutaline) and **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)**.
- **Maternal Constitutional Factors**: Severe pre-existing anemia (**Hb <10 g/dL**), maternal obesity (**BMI >35 kg/m²**), maternal age **>40 years**, previous history of PPH (5–15% recurrence risk).
- **Placental Abnormalities**: Placenta previa, morbidly adherent placenta (placenta accreta, increta, percreta), abruptio placentae.

#### Physiological Hemostasis — The "Living Ligature" Concept

- Following placental separation, numerous torn uterine sinuses carrying free blood flow from uterine and ovarian vessels must be obliterated.
- Occlusion is primarily effected by complete **retraction**, whereby arterioles passing tortuously through the interlacing intermediate layer of myometrium are literally clamped in a figure-of-8 pattern.
- This physiological mechanism is termed the **"living ligature"** of the uterus. Secondary thrombosis further seals the sinuses, supported by the hypercoagulable state of pregnancy.
- In **atonic PPH**, failure of muscle contraction and retraction leaves these sinuses wide open, resulting in rapid blood loss at **500–600 mL/minute**.

### 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- Continuous vaginal bleeding presenting as a steady dark red trickle or sudden massive gush following delivery of the baby.
- Symptoms of hypovolemia: Faintness, dizziness, extreme thirst, cold clammy skin, restlessness, and air hunger.

#### B. Physical Signs & Hemodynamic Assessment (Rule of 30) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Abdominal Palpation [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - **Atonic PPH**: The uterus is **soft, flabby, and boggy**, occupying a high abdominal position; it becomes **hard on massaging**, expressing blood and clots through the vagina.
    - **Traumatic PPH**: The uterus is felt **well-contracted and hard**, despite continuous bright red vaginal bleeding.
- **Rule of 30 for PPH Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Fall in Systolic Blood Pressure by **≥30 mmHg**.
    - Rise in Heart Rate by **≥30 beats/minute**.
    - Respiratory Rate **>30 breaths/minute**.
    - Drop in Hemoglobin / Hematocrit by **≥30%**.
    - Fall in Urine Output to **<30 mL/hour**.
- **Shock Index (SI = Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP)**: Normal is **0.5–0.7**; an SI **>0.9 to 1.0** indicates major blood loss (>20–30% volume deficit) requiring urgent blood transfusion.

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Hematocrit**: Monitors drop in hemoglobin and hematocrit (>30% fall).
2. **ABO & Rh D Blood Grouping and Cross-Matching**: Immediate ABO/Rh D typing; cross-match at least **2 to 4 units of Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)**.
3. **Bedside Clotting Test (Lee-White)**: 2 mL of venous blood in a glass tube should clot within 6–10 minutes. Failure to clot or a soft fragile clot indicates DIC.
4. **Coagulation Profile**: Prothrombin Time (PT), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT), Serum Fibrinogen level (**target >2 g/L**), D-dimer, and Platelet count (**target >50,000/μL**).
5. **Bedside Pelvic Ultrasonography (USG)**: Evaluates for retained placental fragments, membranes, echogenic intrauterine blood clots, or concealed supralevator/broad ligament hematomas.

### 8. Treatment & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL) — Preventive Management [⭐ High-Yield]

AMTSL is a mandatory routine for all delivering women that **reduces the incidence of PPH by 60%**:

1. **Uterotonic Administration**: Administer **Injection Oxytocin 10 IU IM** to the mother within **1 minute of birth of the baby** (after excluding a second twin). _(Alternatively, Tab. Misoprostol 600 μg PO/PR or Carbetocin 100 μg IV)_.
2. **Controlled Cord Traction (CCT / Brandt-Andrews Maneuver)**: Counter-pressure is applied abdominally upward on the lower uterine segment while applying gentle traction downward and backward on the clamped cord during a contraction.
3. **Uterine Massage**: Massage the uterine fundus immediately after delivery of the placenta until hard, and reassess every 15 minutes for the first 2 hours postpartum.

##### STEP 1: Immediate Resuscitation & Golden 1 Hour Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Golden 1 Hour**: Immediate resuscitation within the first hour ensures the highest survival probability; probability of survival drops sharply after 1 hour.
- **Call for Extra Assistance**: Activate multidisciplinary team (Obstetrician, Anesthetist, Hematologist, ICU team).
- **Airway & Oxygenation**: Administer 100% Oxygen via face mask at 10–15 L/minute.
- **Intravenous Access & Resuscitation**:
    - Insert two large-bore IV cannulae (**14G or 16G**).
    - Rapidly infuse up to **2 L of warmed isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate)** and **1–2 L colloids**.
    - Transfuse cross-matched PRBCs as soon as available; maintain a balanced transfusion ratio of **4 units FFP for every 6 units PRBC** to prevent dilutional coagulopathy. Transfuse platelets if <50,000/μL and cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen <1 g/L.
- **Bladder Decompression**: Insert Foley catheter connected to a urometer to keep bladder empty and monitor hourly urine output (**target >30 mL/hr**).
- **Fundal Massage**: Perform continuous abdominal uterine massage to stimulate natural contraction.

##### STEP 2: Pharmacological / Medical Management (Uterotonics & Anti-fibrinolytics) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Oxytocin**: **10 to 40 IU in 1 L Normal Saline IV drip** at 40 drops/min.
2. **Methylergometrine (Methergine)**: **0.2 mg IV slowly** over 5 minutes; excites tetanic contractions. _Strictly contraindicated in hypertension, severe pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, and cardiac disease_.
3. **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α)**: **250 μg IM or intramyometrial**; repeat every 15 to 90 minutes (maximum 8 doses = 2 mg). _Strictly contraindicated in active bronchial asthma_.
4. **Misoprostol (PGE₁)**: **800 to 1000 μg per rectum (PR)** or sublingual single dose.
5. **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: **1 g IV slowly over 10 minutes** (WHO recommendation); repeat 1 g IV after 30 minutes if bleeding continues.

##### STEP 3: Mechanical & Tamponade Interventions [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Bimanual Uterine Compression (Hamilton Maneuver)**:
    - Introduce gloved right hand into vagina, clenched as a fist in the anterior fornix.
    - Place left hand abdominally behind the uterine fundus, squeezing the uterus firmly against the vaginal fist until tone is restored.
2. **Uterine Balloon Tamponade (First-Line Conservative Surgical Action)**:
    - Insert a **Bakri Balloon**, **Foley Catheter**, or **Condom Catheter** into the uterine cavity.
    - Inflate balloon with **200 to 500 mL of warm sterile normal saline**.
    - Exerts direct internal hydrostatic pressure on open sinuses; **avoids hysterectomy in 78% of cases**. Retained for 4 to 24 hours under prophylactic antibiotic cover.
3. **Tight Intrauterine Packing**: Pack a 5-meter long gauze strip tightly into the uterine cavity from fundus downward (useful when balloon tamponade is unavailable).

##### STEP 4: Conservative Surgical Methods (Uterine Devascularization & Compression Sutures) [⭐ High-Yield]

If medical and tamponade methods fail, proceed to laparotomy for fertility-preserving surgical procedures:

1. **B-Lynch Compression Sutures**:
    - Vertical bracing sutures using **No. 1 Chromic Catgut or Vicryl** passed from lower segment over fundus, compressing anterior and posterior uterine walls against each other. Success rate **~80%**.
2. **Stepwise Uterine Devascularization**:
    - _Uterine Artery Ligation (O'Leary)_: Bilateral ligation at upper and lower uterine segment medial to vessels.
    - _Utero-Ovarian Vessel Ligation_: Ligated just below ovarian ligament anastomosis.
    - _Internal Iliac (Hypogastric) Artery Ligation_: Bilateral ligation of anterior division reduces pelvic pulse pressure by **85%**, facilitating stable clot formation without causing tissue necrosis due to collateral blood supply.
3. **Angiographic Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)**: Fluoroscopic gelfoam embolization by interventional radiologist.

##### STEP 5: Life-Saving Obstetric Hysterectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated as a last resort when all uterine-sparing surgical measures fail and maternal life is in jeopardy. Perform subtotal or total hysterectomy promptly involving a second consultant.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Immediate Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemorrhagic / Hypovolemic Shock & Cardiac Arrest**.
- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)** secondary to washout of clotting factors and tissue factor release.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** due to renal cortical/tubular necrosis from prolonged hypotension.
- **Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) & Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)**.

#### B. Remote / Delayed Maternal Sequelae [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sheehan's Syndrome (Postpartum Ischemic Pituitary Necrosis)**: Caused by severe intrapartum hypotension resulting in pituitary insufficiency, failure of lactation, amenorrhea, and hypothyroidism.
- **Puerperal Sepsis & Pelvic Thrombophlebitis**.
- **Transfusion-Transmitted Infections** and broad ligament hematoma formation.

---

### 10. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- Prognosis is excellent when primary PPH is managed within the **Golden 1 Hour** using AMTSL, prompt fluid/blood resuscitation, and stepwise uterotonics.
- Delayed resuscitation leads to rapid multi-organ failure, DIC, and maternal death.

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Golden 1 Hour & Shock Index**: Survival in primary PPH is directly tied to initiating balanced resuscitation within the **Golden 1 Hour**. A **Shock Index (Heart Rate ÷ Systolic BP) >0.9** serves as an immediate bedside indicator of severe volume loss (>20–30%), mandating rapid blood transfusion.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Administering Inj. Methergine to a PPH patient with co-existing severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, OR giving Carboprost to an asthmatic patient.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Methergine is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in pre-eclampsia/hypertension** (precipitates hypertensive crisis or eclamptic fits); use **Oxytocin** or **Misoprostol** instead.
    - **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF₂α) is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in bronchial asthma** (precipitates severe bronchospasm); use **Misoprostol** instead.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Primary PPH = Blood loss ≥500 mL (vaginal) or ≥1000 mL (CS) occurring within 24 hours of delivery. Atonicity accounts for **80% of cases**.
- **4 Ts Classification**: Tone (80%), Tissue (5–10%), Trauma (10–15%), Thrombin (1–2%).
- **AMTSL Prevention**: Oxytocin 10 IU IM + Controlled Cord Traction + Fundal Massage (reduces PPH by 60%).
- **First-line Drugs**: Oxytocin 10–40 IU IV, Methergine 0.2 mg IV (avoid in HTN), Carboprost 250 μg IM (avoid in asthma), Misoprostol 800–1000 μg PR, Tranexamic Acid 1 g IV.
- **Conservative Tamponade**: Bakri Balloon (200–500 mL saline, 78% success) or Bimanual Compression.
- **Surgical Uterine-Sparing**: B-Lynch compression sutures (80% success), bilateral uterine/internal iliac artery ligation (85% pulse pressure reduction).
