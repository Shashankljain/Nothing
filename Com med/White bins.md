III. Management Protocol (Bio-Medical Waste Rules, 2016) ★ Important Exam Point

Under the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016, sharps must be managed through a strict, closed-loop protocol from the point of generation to final disposal:

1. Point-of-Generation Segregation & Containment

- Container Type: Sharps must be collected in puncture-proof, leak-proof, and tamper-proof containers.
- Colour Coding: These containers are designated under the White (Translucent) category.
- Immediate Pre-treatment: Syringes must be mutilated, or their needles cut using a needle cutter or burner at the site of use, and then stored in the puncture-proof containers.

2. Treatment and Disposal Options

Once collected, waste sharps must undergo validated sterilization and physical destruction to prevent unauthorized reuse:

- Sterilization: Autoclaving or Dry Heat Sterilization. Alternatively, a combination of shredding-cum-autoclaving can be used.
- Physical Destruction: Shredding or mutilation to a degree that completely prevents unauthorized reuse.
- Final Disposal:

- Sent to authorized iron foundries (with consent from State Pollution Control Boards/Committees) for smelting.
- Disposed of in a sanitary landfill or a designated concrete waste sharp pit.
- Encapsulation in a metal container or cement concrete block to prevent toxic migration.

IV. Standard Precautions for Prevention of Needlestick Injuries ★

- Careful Handling: All sharps must be handled with extreme care during clinical procedures, cleaning, and disposal.
- No Recapping: Avoid recapping needles manually to prevent accidental punctures. Use a single-handed scoop technique or immediate mechanical needle cutters.
- Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): If an accidental percutaneous injury occurs, HIV PEP must be initiated ideally within hours of exposure and should not be delayed while waiting for source testing.
