[[Genetics]]

**Genetic counselling** is regarded as the most immediate and practical service that genetics can render to modern medicine and surgery. It serves as a vital bridge between complex genetic science and the needs of families affected by or at risk of hereditary disorders.

---

### **I. Definition of Genetic Counselling** ★ Important Exam Point

Genetic counselling is the **process of communication** by which patients or their relatives at risk of a disorder that may be hereditary are advised of:

- The **consequences** and nature of the disorder.
- The **probability** (risk) of developing or transmitting it to their offspring.
- The **ways** in which the disorder may be prevented, avoided, or ameliorated.

---

### **II. Objectives: Why is it done?**

The primary goal is to empower couples with the information needed to make informed decisions about parenthood and to help families adapt to the medical and psychological implications of genetic disease.

---

### **III. Types of Genetic Counselling** ★ Important Exam Point

Counselling is broadly classified into two categories based on when the intervention occurs:

| **Type**      | **Prospective Counselling**                                                                                    | **Retrospective Counselling**                                                  |
| :------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Timing**    | Provided **before** the birth of the first affected child.                                                     | Provided **after** a hereditary disorder has ==already== occurred in a family. |
| **Mechanism** | Identifying **heterozygous carriers** through screening and explaining the risks of marriage between carriers. | Advising parents on the risk of ==recurrence== in future pregnancies.          |
| **Examples**  | Sickle cell anaemia, Thalassaemia.                                                                             | Down’s syndrome, PKU, Inborn errors of metabolism.                             |
| **Goal**      | **True prevention** of the first occurrence.                                                                   | Prevention of recurrence.                                                      |

---

### **IV. Indications: Who and When to Counsel?** ★ Important Exam Point

Counselling and prenatal diagnosis are strongly recommended for the following high-risk groups:

1. **Advanced Maternal Age:** Mothers aged **35 years or older** due to the high risk of **Trisomy 21 (Down's syndrome)**.
2. **Previous History:** Parents who have already had one child with Down’s syndrome or other chromosomal aberrations.
3. **Carrier Status:** Parents known to have a **chromosomal translocation**.
4. **Metabolic/Biochemical Defects:** Parents who have had a child with a metabolic defect detectable by amniocentesis (e.g., neural tube defects like spina bifida).
5. **Sex-Linked Disorders:** When a family history of a sex-linked disease (e.g., Duchenne muscular dystrophy) necessitates sex determination.

---

### **V. Investigation Methods: How is it done?**

To provide accurate risk assessment, the counsellor utilizes various diagnostic tools:

#### **1. Prenatal Diagnosis (Amniocentesis)** ★ Important Exam Point

- **Timing:** Performed in early pregnancy, usually between **14 and 16 weeks**.
- **Procedure:** A sample of amniotic fluid is taken to culture foetal cells for **Karyotyping** (to visualize chromosomes) and biochemical analysis (to detect metabolic errors).
- **Significance:** It allows for the detection of abnormalities while **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP)** is still a feasible option.

#### **2. Carrier Detection**

Techniques to identify healthy individuals who carry one "bad" gene (heterozygotes).

- **Examples:** Enzyme assays for PKU or DNA diagnosis for Sickle cell anaemia.

#### **3. Screening of Newborns**

Early diagnosis of infants allows for immediate life-saving treatment.

- **Examples:** **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** (using the Guthrie test), Congenital Hypothyroidism, and Sickle cell disease.

---

### **VI. Management and Preventive Measures**

Based on the counsellor's advice, the family may choose:

- **Contraception** or **Sterilization** to prevent further pregnancies.
- **Pregnancy Termination (MTP)** if a defect is confirmed prenatally.
- **Medical/Surgical treatment** (e.g., low-phenylalanine diets for PKU children).
- **Rehabilitation** for those with existing physical or mental disabilities.

### **High-Yield Exam Points** ★

- **Maternal Age Risk:** The risk of Down's syndrome is **1 in 3,000** for a 20-year-old mother, rising sharply to **1 in 50**for a mother aged 45.
- **Amniocentesis Window:** Always mention **14–16 weeks** of gestation.
- **Point Mutation:** Single-gene diseases (Mendelian) are often caused by a "point mutation" and follow patterns shown in **Figure 1 (Classical Mendelian patterns)**.

### **Mnemonic for Indications**

**"A-B-C-D"**

- **A** – **A**dvanced Maternal Age (>35).
- **B** – **B**iochemical/Metabolic Disorders (e.g., PKU).
- **C** – **C**hromosomal Aberrations (previous child affected).
- **D** – **D**etectable Neural Tube Defects (e.g., Spina Bifida).

### **Frequently Asked Viva Questions**

- **Q: What is the most important "F" in genetic counselling?**
    - **A:** **Feedback** and follow-up, ensuring the family understands the risk and is supported in their decision-making.
- **Q: Why is maternal age a risk factor for Down's syndrome?**
    - **A:** Increased maternal age is associated with a higher frequency of **non-disjunction** during nuclear division, leading to an extra 21st chromosome.
- **Q: Can genetic counselling "cure" a disease?**
    - **A:** No, it is a **preventive service**. While it can guide early treatment for conditions like PKU, its primary role is risk assessment and prevention of the birth of affected children.

---

### **Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **Confusing Terms:** Using "Eugenics" and "Genetic Counselling" interchangeably. **Genetic Counselling** is a service to _individuals_; **Eugenics** is a social science aimed at improving the genetic makeup of the _entire population_.
- **Timing of Amniocentesis:** Stating it is done in the third trimester. It must be done early (14–16 weeks) so parents have time to consider legal options like MTP.
- **Ignoring the Communication Aspect:** Defining it only as a "test." It is first and foremost a **communication process**.
