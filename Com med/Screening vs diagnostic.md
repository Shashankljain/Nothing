[[Screening of disease]]
**I. Definitions** ★ Important Exam Point

- **Screening Test:** This is the "search for unrecognized disease or defect by means of rapidly applied tests, examinations or other procedures in **apparently healthy** individuals".
- **Diagnostic Test:** This is the "use of clinical and/or laboratory procedures to **confirm or refute** the existence of disease" in individuals who already have signs or symptoms suggestive of a condition.

---

**II. Screening vs. Diagnostic Tests: The Differences** ★ Important Exam Point

> CATTI BRA

According to the provided material, the two tests are contrasted based on eight specific criteria shown in the table below.

|**Feature**|**Screening Test**|**Diagnostic Test**|
|---|---|---|
|**1. Target Population**|Done on **apparently healthy** or asymptomatic individuals.|Done on those with **indications, signs, or symptoms** of illness.|
|**2. Group vs. Individual**|Applied to **groups** or large populations.|Applied to **single patients**; all relevant diseases are considered.|
|**3. Test Results**|Results are **arbitrary and final** (Positive or Negative).|Diagnosis is not final; it is **modified in light of new evidence**.|
|**4. Basis of Criteria**|Usually based on **one criterion** or a single cut-off point.|Based on an **evaluation of a number of symptoms**, signs, and lab findings.|
|**5. Accuracy**|**Less accurate** (aimed at high sensitivity to catch all likely cases).|**More accurate** (aimed at high specificity to confirm the disease).|
|**6. Cost**|**Less expensive** (designed for mass application).|**More expensive** (due to complexity and specialized equipment).|
|**7. Treatment Basis**|**Not a basis for treatment**; requires a confirmatory diagnostic test.|**Used as a basis for treatment**.|
|**8. Initiative**|The initiative comes from the **investigator** or health agency.|The initiative comes from the **patient** presenting with a complaint.|
