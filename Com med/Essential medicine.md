### **I. Introduction and Definition** ★ Important Exam Point

- **Definition:** **Essential medicines** are those that satisfy the priority healthcare needs of the population.
- **The Concept:** They are selected with due regard to public health relevance, evidence on efficacy and safety, and comparative cost-effectiveness.
- **Core Objective (TADAPI):** They are intended to be available within the functioning health systems at all ==times==, in ==adequate== ==amounts==, in appropriate ==dosage== forms, with assured ==quality== and ==adequate== information, and at a ==price== the individual and the community can afford.

---

### **II. Advantages of the Essential Medicines Concept** ★

Carefully selecting a limited range of essential medicines provides several advantages:

- **Higher Quality of Care:** Focuses clinical attention on well-established, highly effective treatments.
- **Better Drug Management:** Simplifies the procurement, storage, distribution, and overall stock management of medicines.
- **Cost-Effective Use of Resources:** Prevents waste on redundant or overpriced drugs, maximizing the utility of limited healthcare budgets.
- **Improved Prescribing Practices:** Helps doctors prescribe rational, high-quality, and evidence-based medications.

---

### **III. National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) of India** ★ Important Exam Point

India has adopted the essential medicines concept to address its own national circumstances and disease burden.

#### **1. Key Milestones**

- **1996:** The first National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) in India was prepared.
- **Subsequent Revisions:** The list was updated and revised in **2003, 2011, 2015, and 2022**.
- **Categorization:** Medicines are categorized by therapeutic use. A medicine may appear in more than one category if it has multiple indications.

#### **2. Categorization by Level of Healthcare**

The NLEM is divided according to the level of healthcare because treatment facilities, training, experience, and personnel availability vary:

1. **Primary (P):** Foundational drugs accessible at the primary care level (e.g., Primary Health Centres/Sub-centres).
2. **Secondary (S):** Intermediate-level drugs for community hospitals and specialist clinics.
3. **Tertiary (T):** Highly specialized medications reserved for tertiary/referral hospitals and medical colleges.

---

### **IV. Criteria for Inclusion of a Drug in NLEM** ★ Important Exam Point

For a drug to be included in the NLEM, it must satisfy the following strict criteria:
## AsPECTS TSH

```
										[Approved/Licensed in India] ──> [Useful for Public Health Problems] ──>.            [Proven Efficacy & Safety]                                                                           │
[Stable in Indian Storage] <── [Aligned with Treatment Guidelines] <── [Cost-Effective (Total Price)]
```

1. **Approval:** The medicine must be approved or licensed in India.
2. **Public Health Relevance:** It should be useful in treating diseases that are public health problems in India.
3. **Safety & Efficacy:** It must have a proven efficacy and safety profile based on valid scientific evidence.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness:** It must be highly cost-effective.
5. **Treatment Guidelines:** It must be aligned with current clinical treatment guidelines for the disease.
6. **Storage Stability:** It must remain stable under the specific storage conditions of India.
7. **Total Treatment Cost:** The price of the **total treatment** must be considered, rather than just the unit price of a single pill.
8. **Strict Rule on FDCs:** Fixed-Dose Combinations (FDCs) are generally **not included** unless the combination has an unequivocally proven advantage over individual ingredients separately (e.g., increased efficacy, reduced side effects, or improved compliance).
9. **Healthcare Level Fit:** It must align with the appropriate level of healthcare (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary).
