[[Health programmes in India]]
The **Revised National Tuberculosis Control Programme (RNTCP)** is the state-run initiative in India that applies the global DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment Short-course) strategy to control and eliminate tuberculosis (TB).

In 2006, the programme incorporated the global **Stop TB Strategy**, and in recent years, it has transitioned towards the **National Strategic Plan** with the vision of achieving an "End TB" status.

---

### **I. Introduction and Milestones**

TB is a chronic infectious disease caused by _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_, often referred to as the "barometer of social welfare". RNTCP represents a paradigm shift from previous TB control efforts by focusing on supervised treatment and quality-assured diagnosis.

**Important Milestones:**

- **1993:** RNTCP launched as a pilot project.
- **1997:** Expansion of the programme across the country.
- **2006 (March 24):** Entire country covered under RNTCP.
- **2006:** Adoption of the "Stop TB Strategy" (2006–2015).
- **2016:** Launch of the National Framework for Malaria Elimination, while TB efforts intensified under Phase II.

---

### **II. Aim and Objectives**

The basic goal of RNTCP is to decrease the incidence of TB to such a low level that it is no longer a major public health problem.

**Core Objectives:**

1. **Case Detection:** Detect at least **70%** of estimated smear-positive cases through quality sputum microscopy. ★ Important Exam Point
2. **Cure Rate:** Achieve at least an **85%** cure rate among newly detected sputum smear-positive infectious cases.

---

### **III. Strategy: DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment Short-course)**

DOTS is the heart of RNTCP. It is a community-based strategy that combines the benefits of supervised treatment with community support.

#### **Five Components of DOTS:** ★ Important Exam Point (PADDS)

1. **Political and Administrative Commitment:** Ensuring sustained funding (e.g., the US $142 million World Bank loan in 1997).
2. **Good Quality Diagnosis by Microscopy:** Shifting from X-ray-based diagnosis to sputum smear microscopy as the primary tool.
3. **Adequate Supply of Quality Drugs:** Providing free, patient-wise boxes (colour-coded) to ensure uninterrupted supply.
4. **Directly Observed Treatment (DOT):** A trained DOTS agent or health worker observes the patient swallowing every dose.
5. **Systematic Monitoring and Accountability:** Using a standardized recording and reporting system (e.g., the TB Register).

---

### **VI. Organizational Structure**

RNTCP has a decentralized, 5-tier organizational setup.

1. **National Level:** Central TB Division, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (Secretary/Director General).
2. **State Level:** State TB Office and State TB Training and Demonstration Centre.
3. **District Level:** **District TB Centre (DTC)**, led by the District TB Officer (DTO).
4. **Sub-district Level:** **Tuberculosis Unit (TU)**. One TU covers ~5 lakh population (2.5 lakh in hilly/tribal areas).
5. **Peripheral/Village Level:** **Designated Microscopy Centres (DMC)** (one per 1 lakh population) and **DOTS Agents** (ASHA, Anganwadi workers, etc.).
