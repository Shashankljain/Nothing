The **Millennium Development Goals (MDGs)** were a set of eight international development goals established following the Millennium Summit of the United Nations in September 2000. 
These goals represented a global commitment to ==reduce poverty and hunger==, and to tackle ==ill==-==health==, gender inequality, lack of education, and environmental degradation by the target year of **2015**.

---

### **I. The Eight Millennium Development Goals** ★ Important Exam Point

To remember these, use the mnemonic: **"P-E-G-C-M-D-E-D"**

1. **P** – Eradicate extreme **P**overty and hunger.
2. **E** – Achieve universal primary **E**ducation.
3. **G** – Promote **G**ender equality and empower women.
4. **C** – Reduce **C**hild mortality.
5. **M** – Improve **M**aternal health.
6. **D** – Combat HIV/AIDS, malaria, and other **D**iseases.
7. **E** – Ensure **E**nvironmental sustainability.
8. **D** – Develop a global partnership for **D**evelopment.

### **II. Critical Analysis of India’s Progress in MCH**

In India, the progress towards MDGs was "impressive in many ways," yet many targets remained unfinished by 2015, leading to their transition into the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

#### **1. Goal 4: Reduce Child Mortality**

- **Target:** Reduce the under-five mortality rate (U5MR) by two-thirds between 1990 and 2015.
- **Indicators Used:** Under-five mortality rate, Infant mortality rate (IMR), and proportion of 1-year-old children immunized against measles.
- **India's Achievement:**
    - India saw a significant decline in mortality. By 2020, the national IMR dropped to **28 per 1,000 live births**.
    - **The Disparity Gap:** A critical analysis reveals sharp regional inequalities. For instance, **Kerala** achieved an IMR of **6**, which is comparable to developed nations, while the national average lagged at 28.
    - **Tribal Health:** Tribal children (Scheduled Tribes) still face a higher burden, with an IMR of **41.7** and a U5MR of **50.3**, significantly higher than the general population.
    - **Success Story:** The **Universal Immunization Programme (UIP)** and **Pulse Polio** (India was declared polio-free in 2014) were major drivers of success in reducing child deaths.

#### **2. Goal 5: Improve Maternal Health**

- **Target:** Reduce the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) by three-quarters and achieve universal access to reproductive health.
- **Indicators Used:** Maternal mortality ratio and proportion of births attended by skilled health personnel.
- **India's Achievement:**
    - The MMR was estimated at **167 in 2013** and dropped to **103 by 2017-19**.
    - **Institutional Deliveries:** Schemes like **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)** and **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK)** successfully increased institutional deliveries (76% in 2009).
    - **Critique of Progress:** Despite the decline, the target of reducing MMR by three-quarters was not fully met nationwide by 2015. Disparities remain between urban (94% skilled attendance) and rural areas (81.8% skilled attendance).
    - **Maternal Tetanus:** A major achievement was the validation of **Maternal and Neonatal Tetanus Elimination (MNTE)** in India in May 2015.

### **III. Factors Influencing the "Unfinished Agenda"** ★ Important Exam Point

The reason India did not fully meet all MDG targets for MCH includes:

- **Poverty:** Roughly **22%** of the population lived below the poverty line (2011-12), limiting access to nutrition and care.
- **Social Barriers:** Caste, religion, and gender inequality (preference for male children) hindered the utilization of maternal services.
- **Healthcare Infrastructure:** Shortage of doctors in rural/tribal areas and a focus on "curative" rather than "preventive" care.

### **Frequently Asked Viva Questions**

- **Q: How many MDGs are there?**
    - **A:** 8 goals.
- **Q: Which MDGs are specifically related to health?**
    - **A:** Goal 4 (Child Health), Goal 5 (Maternal Health), and Goal 6 (Communicable Diseases).
- **Q: What succeeded the MDGs in 2016?**
    - **A:** The **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**, which include 17 goals and 169 targets.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make**

- **Confusing MDGs and SDGs:** Students often mix the number of goals. Remember: **MDGs = 8**, **SDGs = 17**.
- **Ignoring Regional Disparity:** Writing only national averages. In a critical analysis, you **must** mention the gap between states like Kerala and the rest of India to show where the programme fell short.
- **Forgetting Indicators:** Only mentioning "death rates." You must mention "skilled birth attendance" and "measles immunization" as key MDG indicators.
