### **Basic Concepts & Classification** 📚

#### **Definition**

A **Corrosive Poison** (or **Caustic**) is a highly reactive chemical substance that produces rapid ==functional, histologic, and structural damage== immediately upon contact with any tissue surface. 
They act locally by chemically destroying, inflating, or ulcerating the skin, mucous membranes, and gastrointestinal tract (GIT).

```
                             [CORROSIVE POISONS]
                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                         ▼
     [ACIDS]                                                   [ALKALIS]
  (Proton Donators)                                        (Proton Acceptors)
      pH < 3.0                                                  pH > 11.0
```

#### **Classification of Corrosives**

Corrosives are broadly classified into four major categories based on their chemical composition:

1. **Inorganic / Mineral Acids:**
    - Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\)) – _commercially known as "Oil of Vitriol"_.
    - Nitric Acid (\(HNO_3\)) – _commercially known as "Aqua Fortis"_.
    - Hydrochloric Acid (\(HCl\)) – _commercially known as "Spirit of Salt"_.
2. **Organic Acids:**
    - Carbolic Acid (Phenol).
    - Oxalic Acid.
    - Acetic Acid.
3. **Strong / Caustic Alkalis:**
    - Sodium Hydroxide (\(NaOH\) / Caustic Soda).
    - Potassium Hydroxide (\(KOH\) / Caustic Potash).
    - Ammonia (\(NH_3\)).
    - Sodium Carbonate (\(Na_2CO_3\)).
4. **Corrosive Metallic Salts:**
    - Zinc Chloride (\(ZnCl_2\)).
    - Ferric Chloride (\(FeCl_3\)).
    - Silver Nitrate (\(AgNO_3\)).

### **The "6-W" Analytical Framework** ❓

- **What?** Corrosives are chemical agents that cause rapid, irreversible, local chemical burns on contact with tissue.
- **Why?** They are commonly ingested for suicide (due to cheap and easy household availability) and are maliciously thrown on faces (Vitriolage) to cause permanent disfigurement.
- **How?** They destroy tissue integrity by extracting water, coagulating or liquefying cellular proteins, and causing rapid cellular death.
- **When?** Signs and symptoms manifest **immediately** upon ingestion, inhalation, or skin contact.
- **Where?** The primary damage occurs at the local site of contact (lips, mouth, esophagus, stomach, and larynx).
- **Who?** Mostly ingested by suicidal individuals or accidental victims (especially children mistaking chemicals for water or medicines). Assailants who throw acids are prosecuted under severe legal codes.

### **Topic-Specific Explanation: Pathophysiology & Mechanisms** 🔬

★ **Important Exam Point: Coagulative vs. Liquefactive Necrosis** The fundamental mechanism of tissue destruction differs dramatically between acids and alkalis:

```
                      [MECHANISM OF CAUSTIC INJURY]
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                     ▼
     [ACIDS]                                               [ALKALIS]
(Coagulative Necrosis)                                (Liquefactive Necrosis)
         │                                                     │
         ▼                                                     ▼
Precipitates proteins                                 Saponifies mucosal fats
         │                                                     │
         ▼                                                     ▼
Forms hard, dry eschar / scab                         Dissolves tissue proteins
         │                                                     │
         ▼                                                     ▼
Limits deep, lateral penetration                       Causes deep, liquefying,
                                                      uncontrolled tissue                                                                        damage
```

- **Acids (Coagulative Necrosis):** Acids act as proton donators. On tissue contact, they extract water and rapidly **coagulate and precipitate cellular proteins**, forming a hard, dry, leathery eschar or scab. This eschar acts as a physical barrier, which often protects the deeper muscular layers of the organ from further lateral or deep penetration.
- **Alkalis (Liquefactive Necrosis):** Alkalis act as proton acceptors. They do **not** form a protective eschar. Instead, they liquefy proteins, extract water, and **saponify tissue fats and lipids**. This results in a soft, wet, gelatinous, and liquefying necrosis that allows the chemical to penetrate deeply, extensively, and uncontrollably into the submucosal and muscular coats, leading to a high rate of immediate organ perforation.

- ★ **The Only Exception (==Hydrofluoric Acid - HF==):** HF is the only acid that causes **both liquefactive and coagulative necrosis**, enabling extremely deep, bone-destroying tissue injuries [Image "24. CORROSIVES POISONS"].

### **Clinical Features: Signs & Symptoms** 🩺

When a corrosive is ingested, the clinical presentation is immediate and distressing:

#### **1. General Symptoms of Corrosive Ingestion**

- **Oropharyngeal:** Intense, immediate ==burning== pain in the mouth, throat, and retrosternal region.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Severe abdominal pain (==epigastric distress==), marked difficulty in swallowing (**==dysphagia==**), and inability to speak (**==dysphonia==**).
- **Vomiting:** Persistent, painful vomiting. The vomitus contains altered blood, mucus, and mucosal shreds. It classically has a **"==coffee-ground" appearance==** due to the conversion of ==hemoglobin to acid-hematin== by gastric acid.
- **Systemic Shock:** Cold, clammy skin, a rapid, feeble pulse, and profound hypotension due to severe pain and intravascular fluid loss.
- **Respiratory Distress:** Hoarseness, stridor, and immediate **dyspnea** due to laryngeal spasm or edema of the glottis caused by the inhalation of fumes or accidental aspiration.

#### **II. Detailed Treatment Protocols**

1. **Airway Management (Highest Priority):** Inhalation of corrosive fumes or glottic edema requires immediate oxygenation. If stridor or respiratory obstruction is present, execute a life-saving **tracheostomy** or early cricothyrotomy [331, 355, Image "24. CORROSIVES POISONS"].
2. **Removal of Unabsorbed Poison (Decontamination):**
    - ★ **Important Exam Point (Contraindications):** Gastric lavage and emetics are **absolutely contraindicated**in mineral acid and caustic alkali ingestions. The structurally weakened esophago-gastric wall is highly prone to perforation.
    - ★ **The Carbolic Acid Exception:** Cautious gastric lavage **is indicated** in **Carbolic acid (Phenol)**poisoning. Phenol induces coagulation necrosis which hardens and thickens the gastric lining (**Leathery Stomach**), making it structurally resistant to instrumental perforation. Lavage is performed using warm water containing activated charcoal, olive oil, or castor oil (which dissolves phenol).
    - **No Carbonate/Bicarbonate Neutralizers:** Never administer alkaline carbonates or sodium bicarbonate (\(NaHCO_3\)) to neutralize acids. Their reaction releases massive volumes of Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\)) gas, causing sudden distension and **catastrophic stomach rupture**. They also generate exothermic heat, worsening deep tissue damage.
3. **Dilution & Demulcents:**
    - Dilute the corrosive immediately by administering a glass of **milk or water** (approximately 5 mL/kg body weight).
    - Administer **demulcents** such as egg white, starch water, olive oil, or butter to mechanically coat and protect the raw mucosal surfaces.
    - Give **aluminum hydroxide gel** (4 tablespoonfuls) to coat gastric tissue.
4. **Medical & Systemic Care:**
    - **Pain Relief:** Administer intravenous **Morphine** immediately to control severe pain and prevent neurogenic shock.
    - **Stricture Prevention:** Administer systemic **corticosteroids** (e.g., Prednisolone 60 mg/day in divided doses) to prevent subsequent esophageal stricture formation. _Note: Steroids are strictly contraindicated if gastric perforation is suspected._
    - **Supportive Care:** Maintain the patient strictly **Nothing by Mouth (NPO)**. Support nutrition via total parenteral nutrition (IV route) for at least a week. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics to prevent secondary infection or mediastinitis/peritonitis if micro-perforation is suspected.

### **Complications of Corrosive Poisoning** 🚨

The complications of corrosive ingestion are divided into immediate and delayed phases:

#### **I. Immediate / Acute Complications**

- **Asphyxia:** Fatal airway obstruction due to edema of the glottis or immediate laryngeal spasm.
- **Hemorrhagic Shock:** Massive, immediate upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage due to erosion of major blood vessels.
- **Perforation & Chemical Peritonitis:** Catastrophic rupture of the esophagus (mediastinitis) or stomach (chemical peritonitis), presenting as a rigid, board-like abdomen.

#### **II. Delayed / Chronic Complications**

- **Esophageal Strictures:** Occurs weeks after ingestion due to dense, fibrous scar tissue contractures.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction:** Develops **3 to 4 weeks** after acid exposure due to stenosis and scarring of the pyloric antrum.
- **Delayed Gastric Bleeding:** Occurs **3 to 4 days** post-ingestion as the necrotic mucosal eschar/scab sloughs off.
- **Delayed Perforation:** Occurs up to **4 days** after exposure due to progressive tissue necrosis.
- **Malnutrition & Marasmus:** Profound wasting due to permanent esophageal strictures preventing normal food intake.

### **Medico-Legal & Forensic Aspects** ⚖️

#### **I. Relevant Law & Provisions (Latest 2023 Forensic Framework)**

- ★ **Section 124(1) BNS, 2023 (formerly Sec 326-A IPC):** Deals with voluntarily causing **grievous hurt by use of acid** (Vitriolage) [335, Image "THE NEW INDIAN CRIMINAL LAWS"].
    - _Punishment:_ Imprisonment for a minimum of **10 years to life imprisonment**, along with a fine paid directly to the victim to cover medical expenses.
- ★ **Section 124(2) BNS, 2023 (formerly Sec 326-B IPC):** Punishes voluntarily **throwing or attempting to throw acid** [335, Image "THE NEW INDIAN CRIMINAL LAWS"].
    - _Punishment:_ Imprisonment for **5 to 7 years** and a fine.
- ★ **Section 200 BNS, 2023:** Declares the **failure or refusal of a hospital/doctor to treat an acid attack victim immediately** as a punishable criminal offense [Image "24. CORROSIVES POISONS"].
- ★ **Section 397 BNSS, 2023:** Mandates that **all acid attack victims must receive immediate, free medical treatment** at any government or private hospital [Image "24. CORROSIVES POISONS"].
- **Section 33 BNSS, 2023:** Lays down the **mandatory legal duty of every medical practitioner** to immediately report all cases of suspected poisoning to the nearest police officer [Image "22. GENERAL TOXICOLOGY"].
- **Section 123 BNS, 2023:** Covers voluntarily causing **hurt by means of poison** with criminal intent [Image "22. GENERAL TOXICOLOGY"].
- **Section 286 BNS, 2023:** Punishes **negligent conduct** with respect to handling or storing poisonous substances [Image "22. GENERAL TOXICOLOGY"].
- **Section 26 BSA, 2023:** Governs **Dying Declarations**. If a corrosive ingestion victim is in critical condition, the treating doctor must immediately document their statement [Image "THE NEW INDIAN CRIMINAL LAWS"].

### **Autopsy Findings in Corrosive Deaths** 🔍🩺

When conducting a postmortem examination on a suspected corrosive poisoning case, the pathologist must look for:

#### **I. External Findings**

- Corrosive burns and **trickle marks** running down the angles of the mouth, chin, cheeks, or neck, mapping the flow of the liquid.
- The color of skin burns reflects the specific toxic agent (e.g., black/charred for sulphuric acid, yellow for nitric acid).

#### **II. Internal Findings**

- ★ **Important Exam Point: Magenstrasse (Street of the Stomach):** Liquid corrosive acids slide rapidly along the lesser curvature of the stomach (forming a path known as _Magenstrasse_) and pool in the pyloric antrum, causing severe localized burns and perforation. The esophagus may be relatively spared, and the duodenum is protected by the alkaline environment and reactive pylorospasm.
- ★ **Autopsy Dissection Rule:** To preserve and demonstrate this pathognomonic pathology, the stomach must be opened **along the greater curvature**.
- **Double Ligature Method:** The stomach is removed intact by applying two ligatures at the cardiac end of the esophagus and two below the pyloric end of the stomach, cutting between them to prevent loss of contents.

```
                     [AUTOPSY ENCOUNTER: SUSPECTED ACID INGESTION]
                                           │

                                           │

                       (Check for chemical or unusual odors)

                  (Exception: For Carbolic Acid, use Saturated Salt!)
```

#### **III. Viscera Preservation Guidelines**

★ **Important Exam Point: Preservative Selection**

- In deaths due to acid or alkali poisoning (except Carbolic acid), the viscera and skin must be preserved in **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit**.
- ★ **The PAPPA Contraindication:** Rectified spirit is strictly contraindicated for **Carbolic Acid (Phenol)** because phenol is highly soluble in alcohol, which ruins the sample. Use **Saturated solution of common salt** instead.

### **Clinical Correlation**

👩‍⚕️ **Hospital Scenario:** A 24-year-old female is rushed to the emergency department in a state of deep shock. Her family states she drank toilet bowl cleaner (\(H_2SO_4\)) following a marital dispute. The patient has severe oral burns, **chalky white teeth**, is drooling excessively, and is vomiting **blackish, coffee-ground fluid**.

- **Doctor's Action:** The doctor immediately assesses the airway. Recognizing the ingestion of a strong mineral acid (\(H_2SO_4\)), the doctor **avoids gastric lavage and emetics** to prevent esophageal perforation. The doctor administers a glass of milk to dilute the acid and olive oil as a demulcent. IV fluids are started to combat shock, and morphine is given for pain. Because of suspected homicidal or suicidal poisoning, the doctor notifies the police under **Section 33 BNSS, 2023** and preserves the first vomitus sample [Image "22. GENERAL TOXICOLOGY"].

### **High-Yield Exam Points** ★

- ★ **Most Common Acid Used in Vitriolage:** Sulphuric Acid (\(H_2SO_4\)).
- ★ **The Xanthoproteic Reaction:** Nitric acid turns the skin and tissues **yellow** by reacting with aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine).
- ★ **Stomach vs. Esophagus Injury:** Acid ingestion primarily injures the **Stomach** (due to esophageal squamous cell protection), while alkali ingestion primarily damages the **Esophagus**.
- ★ **Leathery Stomach:** Pathognomonic of **Carbolic Acid (Phenol)** poisoning.
- ★ **Carboluria:** The excretion of **dark smoky-green urine** in phenol poisoning due to the oxidation of hydroquinone and pyrocatechol metabolites in air.
- ★ **The Stricture Timeline:** Esophageal strictures develop as a chronic complication, while **Gastric Outlet Obstruction** manifests **3 to 4 weeks** post-acid exposure.

### **Viva Questions & Model Answers** 💬

**Q1: Why is gastric lavage absolutely contraindicated in Sulphuric Acid poisoning but indicated in Carbolic Acid poisoning?**  
**Answer:** In sulphuric acid ingestion, the esophageal and gastric walls are severely softened and weakened by coagulative necrosis, making the insertion of a lavage tube highly likely to cause immediate rupture. Carbolic acid (Phenol), however, causes coagulation and precipitation of proteins that actually hardens and thickens the stomach wall (**Leathery Stomach**), providing structural resistance that allows a cautious gastric wash.

**Q2: What is the "Magenstrasse" and why is it forensically significant?**  
**Answer:** "Magenstrasse" (street of the stomach) refers to the path along the lesser curvature of the stomach. Ingested liquid corrosive acids naturally slide rapidly along this path under gravity and pool at the pyloric antrum, causing severe localized necrosis and perforation. To preserve and document this pathognomonic finding, the autopsy surgeon must open the stomach along the **greater curvature**.

**Q3: Why should we never use Sodium Bicarbonate as a neutralizing agent in acid ingestion?**  
**Answer:** Sodium Bicarbonate reacts chemically with the acid to produce Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\)) gas. The rapid accumulation of gas distends the already chemically weakened stomach wall, risking sudden, fatal gastric rupture. It also creates an exothermic reaction that worsens tissue burns.

### **Common Mistakes Students Make** ❌

- **Writing "Perform Gastric Lavage" for all Acid Poisoning:** Students routinely forget that lavage is **contraindicated** in mineral acids. Always emphasize the **Phenol exception**.
- **Confusing Section 124(1) and 124(2) BNS:** Students write Section 124 BNS generally. Remember: **124(1) BNS** is for the completed act of acid throwing, while **124(2) BNS** is for the attempt.
- **Using Saturated Salt for All Viscera:** Students often write "preserve viscera in saturated salt" as a default. For corrosive acids and alkalis, **Absolute Alcohol or Rectified Spirit** is the correct preservative of choice (except carbolic acid).
