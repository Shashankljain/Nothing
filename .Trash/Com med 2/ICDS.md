**Organizational Structure**

The delivery of ICDS is a collaborative effort between the ==Ministry of Women and Child== ==Development== and the ==Ministry of Health and Family Welfare==:

- **National Level:** Ministry of Women and Child Development.
- **District Level:** District Programme Officer (DPO).
- **Block Level:** Child Development Project Officer (CDPO).
- **Sector Level:** Supervisors (Mukhya Sevika).
- **Village Level:** **Anganwadi Centre (AWC)** manned by an Anganwadi Worker (AWW) and an Anganwadi Helper (AWH)

## Delivery of Services under ICDS — 
7. Supplementary Nutrition

Beneficiaries

- Children below 6 years
- Pregnant women
- Lactating/nursing mothers
- Special nutritional support is provided to severely malnourished children

Aim

To supplement the nutritional intake of beneficiaries.

Nutritional norms given in the text

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|Beneficiary|Calories/day|Protein/day|
|Child 6–72 months|500 kcal|12–15 g|
|Severely malnourished child 6–72 months|800 kcal|20–25 g|
|Pregnant & lactating women|600 kcal|18–20 g|

Revised nutritional and feeding norms

- State Governments/UTs are mandated to provide more than one meal to children attending AWCs.
- This includes:

- Morning snack → milk/banana/egg/seasonal fruit/micronutrient-fortified food
- Followed by a hot cooked meal

- For:  
    

- Children below 3 years
- Pregnant women
- Lactating mothers  
    → Take-home ration (THR) is provided.

  

Important points

- ICDS services are universal.
- Below-poverty-line status is NOT a criterion for registration as a beneficiary.
- Supplementary nutrition is provided for 300 days in a year.
- Children are weighed every month.
- Nutrition education and health education are provided to mothers.

  

Malnutrition

- Children suffering from 1st-degree malnutrition → supplementary nutrition.
- Children with 2nd and 3rd-degree malnutrition → therapeutic supplementary food.
- Children suffering from 4th-degree malnutrition → hospitalization is recommended.

  

2. Nutrition and Health Education

Target group

- Women in the age group 15–45 years
- Special priority to:

- Nursing mothers
- Expectant/pregnant mothers

Mode of delivery

- Education is provided through specially organized courses in villages.
- Anganwadi workers play an important role.
- It is also provided during home visits.

Main purpose

To improve:

- Knowledge regarding nutrition
- Maternal health
- Child health
- Infant and young child feeding
- Prevention of malnutrition

  

3. Immunization

Children

Immunization of children against 9 vaccine-preventable diseases is provided under the programme.

Pregnant women

- Tetanus immunization is recommended.

Exam point: Immunization is one of the integrated services delivered through ICDS in collaboration with the health system.

  

4. Health Check-up

Health check-up includes:

A. Antenatal care

For expectant/pregnant mothers.

B. Postnatal care

For:

- Nursing mothers
- Newborn infants

C. Health examination of children

- Children under 6 years are examined.

D. Micronutrient supplementation

- Expectant mothers are given:

- Iron
- Folic acid
- Protein supplements

E. Physical examination

- A minimum of 3 physical examinations are done.
- High-risk mothers are referred to appropriate institutions for special care.

  

Health care of children under 6 years

It consists of:

1. Recording weight and height

- At periodic intervals.

3. Monitoring developmental milestones
4. Immunization
5. General health check-up

- Every 3–6 months
- To detect:

- Disease
- Malnutrition
- etc.

7. Treatment of common illnesses

- Diarrhoea
- Dysentery
- Respiratory tract infections
- Other common/prevalent diseases

9. Deworming
10. Prophylaxis

- Against vitamin A deficiency
- Against anaemia

12. Referral

- Serious cases are referred to hospitals/appropriate health facilities.

  

5. Health Records

Health records are maintained for:

- Children
- Antenatal care
- Delivery
- Other relevant health information

Important record

A health record card containing the child’s health information is maintained.

- The card is given to the mother.
- It helps in maintaining continuity of care.

  

6. Non-formal Pre-school Education

Target group

Children aged 3–6 years.

Place

- Anganwadi Centre
- Generally established in villages/communities.

Objective

To provide opportunities for development of:

- Desirable attitudes
- Values
- Behaviour patterns
- Social development
- Cognitive development
- School-readiness

Methods

- Locally produced inexpensive toys
- Play materials
- Creative activities
- Play-based learning

Key point

It is non-formal pre-school education, not formal school education.

  

7. Referral Services

Referral is an important component of ICDS.

Who requires referral?

- Children with serious illness
- Severely ill/malnourished children
- High-risk pregnant women
- Other beneficiaries requiring specialized care

Where are they referred?

- Appropriate health institutions
- Hospitals
- Higher-level health facilities

Purpose

To ensure that conditions which cannot be managed at the Anganwadi level receive appropriate medical/specialized care.

  

8. Schemes for Adolescent Girls

The text mentions two schemes for adolescent girls:

A. Kishori Shakti Yojana

Implemented using the infrastructure of ICDS.

Target

Adolescent girls aged 11–18 years.

Major areas addressed

- Self-development
- Nutrition
- Health status
- Literacy
- Numerical skills
- Vocational skills

  

B. Nutrition Programme for Adolescent Girls

- Approved in 2009–10 on a pilot basis.
- Initially implemented in selected districts.
- Targeted undernourished adolescent girls.
- Nutritional supplementation was provided to beneficiaries.
