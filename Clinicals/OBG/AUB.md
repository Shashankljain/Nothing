# Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB) — Comprehensive Clinical Case & Viva Analysis

---

## 1. Patient Case Summary & Clinical Demographics

- **Patient Name & Age**: Mrs. R, 32 years old.
- **Residence & Occupation**: Resident of Yelahanka, Bengaluru; housewife.
- **Husband's Details**: Mr. P, 38 years old, auto driver / worker, monthly income ≈4000 INR (Socioeconomic Class IV / Lower Middle Class).
- **Obstetric Status**: Gravida 2, Para 2, Living 2 (G₂P₂L₂). Both full-term institutional vaginal deliveries (last child born 17 years ago, tubal sterilization done).
- **Dates**: Last Menstrual Period (LMP) = 24/05/2021; Date of Examination = 07/06/2021.
- **Chief Presentation**: Heavy menstrual bleeding for 9 months and severe dysmenorrhea during menstruation for 9 months.
- **Present Illness Course**:
    - Onset was gradual over the last 9 months, presenting as prolonged and heavy menstrual flow.
    - Menstrual duration increased from a baseline of 3–4 days to 7–8 days per cycle.
    - Flow increased from 2–3 pads/day to 7–8 fully soaked pads/day.
    - Associated with passage of large blood clots and night-time pad changes.
    - Associated with severe dysmenorrhea (crampy lower abdominal pain starting 1–2 days before menses and persisting throughout bleeding).
    - Associated with systemic features of anemia: easy fatigability, generalized weakness, exertional dyspnea, and dizziness/giddiness.
    - Negative History: No intermenstrual bleeding, postcoital bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, burning micturition, fever, weight loss, or loss of appetite. No epistaxis, gum bleeding, petechiae, purpura, hematuria, or melena. No symptoms of thyroid dysfunction (cold/heat intolerance, voice changes, constipation/diarrhea). No history of anticoagulant or hormonal pill intake. No family history of bleeding disorders or gynecological malignancies.

## 2. Chronological Viva Exchange: Mentor Questions, Student Responses, and Mentor Corrections

### Phase 1: Case Presentation Rules & Chief Complaints Framing

- **Mentor Question**: How should chief complaints and history of present illness be framed in a clinical case presentation?
- **Student Presentation**: Stated that the patient was admitted 2 days ago with complaints of heavy bleeding.
- **Mentor Correction & Clinical Pearl**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Never write or state "patient was admitted 2 days ago" within chief complaints.
    - Chief complaints must strictly be presented as **Symptom + Duration** in chronological order (e.g., "Heavy menstrual bleeding for 9 months, Dysmenorrhea for 9 months").

---

### Phase 2: Definition & Parameters of Normal vs. Abnormal Menstruation

- **Mentor Question**: Define normal menstrual parameters (frequency, duration, volume, regularity) and explain how Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB) is defined.
- **Student Response**: Defined normal duration as 3–5 days, frequency as 28–30 days, and flow as 2–3 pads/day.
- **Mentor Correction & Standard Diagnostic Criteria**:
    - **Normal Menstrual Cycle Parameters (FIGO Criteria)**:
        - **Frequency**: 24 to 38 days (median 28 days).
            - Frequent: <24 days.
            - Infrequent / Oligomenorrhea: >38 days.
        - **Duration**: 3 to 8 days.
            - Prolonged: >8 days.
        - **Blood Loss Volume**: 5 to 80 mL per cycle.
            - Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (HMB): >80 mL per cycle.
        - **Regularity**: Cycle-to-cycle variation ≤7 to 9 days between the shortest and longest cycle.
    - **Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB) Definition**: Any alteration in normal menstrual frequency, duration, regularity, or blood loss volume in non-pregnant women of reproductive age.

---

### Phase 3: Clinical Assessment & Quantifying Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (HMB)

- **Mentor Question**: How do you clinically assess and quantify menstrual blood loss when subjective pad counts vary?
- **Student Response**: Relied on pad count per day.
- **Mentor Teaching Point**:
    - While exact volumetric blood measurement (e.g., alkaline hematin method) is impractical clinically, HMB is reliably diagnosed by evaluating the following clinical markers:
        1. **Number of fully soaked pads per day**: Increase from baseline (e.g., 7–8 pads/day vs 2–3 baseline).
        2. **Passage of blood clots**: Indicates rapid, heavy bleeding exceeding local endometrial fibrinolytic capacity.
        3. **Night-time pad changes**: Requirement to wake up at night to change pads or bedsheets.
        4. **Staining of clothes/bedding**: Flooding through protection.
        5. **Systemic symptoms of anemia**: Fatigue, dizziness, exertional dyspnea, pallor.
        6. **Quality of Life impact**: Interference with physical, social, or occupational activities.

### Phase 4: PALM-COEIN Etiological Classification System

- **Mentor Question**: Explain the FIGO PALM-COEIN classification system for Abnormal Uterine Bleeding.
- **Student Response**: Named fibroid, adenomyosis, polyps, and hormonal imbalance.
- **Mentor Explanation & Classification Matrix**:

|Category|Component|Structural vs. Non-structural|Key Clinical Features & Presentation|
|---|---|---|---|
|**P**|Polyp|Structural|Endometrial or cervical polyps; presents with intermenstrual, postcoital, or heavy menstrual bleeding.|
|**A**|Adenomyosis|Structural|Ectopic endometrial glands/stroma in myometrium; classic triad of severe dysmenorrhea, HMB, and diffusely enlarged tender uterus (≤12 weeks).|
|**L**|Leiomyoma (Fibroid)|Structural|Submucosal (FIGO 0–2: causes HMB/intermenstrual bleeding), Intramural (FIGO 3–5: causes HMB/dysmenorrhea), Subserosal (FIGO 6–7: causes pressure symptoms).|
|**M**|Malignancy & Hyperplasia|Structural|Endometrial hyperplasia (simple/complex, with/without atypia), Endometrial carcinoma, Cervical carcinoma.|
|**C**|Coagulopathy|Non-structural|von Willebrand disease, immune thrombocytopenia, liver disease, anticoagulant use; HMB since menarche.|
|**O**|Ovulatory Dysfunction|Non-structural|Anovulatory bleeding (PCOS, hypothyroidism, hyperprolactinemia, perimenopause); irregular, unpredictable, heavy breakdown bleeding.|
|**E**|Endometrial Primary|Non-structural|Local endometrial hemostatic defect (endothelin-1 / prostaglandin imbalance); regular, heavy cycles with normal anatomy.|
|**I**|Iatrogenic|Non-structural|Copper-T IUCD, hormonal contraceptives, progestins, psychotropics (hyperprolactinemia), anticoagulants.|
|**N**|Not yet classified|Non-structural|Chronic endometritis, uterine arteriovenous malformations (AVMs).|

### Phase 5: Differential Diagnosis of AUB by Age Group

- **Mentor Question**: How do differential diagnoses for AUB shift across different female age groups?
- **Student & Mentor Combined Analysis**:
    - **Adolescents / Young Females (<20 years / Pubertal AUB)**:
        - Anovulatory cycles (hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis immaturity).
        - Bleeding disorders / Coagulopathies (von Willebrand disease).
        - Pelvic Infection / PID.
        - Pregnancy-related complications.
    - **Reproductive Age Group (20–40 years)**:
        - Leiomyoma (Fibroid uterus).
        - Adenomyosis.
        - Endometrial / Cervical Polyps.
        - Pregnancy-related complications (abortion, ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy).
        - Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID).
        - Ovulatory dysfunction (PCOS).
    - **Perimenopausal Age Group (40–50 years)**:
        - Anovulatory dysfunctional uterine bleeding.
        - Adenomyosis.
        - Leiomyoma.
        - Endometrial hyperplasia.
        - Malignancy (Endometrial or Cervical carcinoma).
    - **Postmenopausal Age Group (>50 years)**:
        - Atrophic endometritis / vaginitis (most common cause, ~60–80%).
        - Endometrial carcinoma.
        - Endometrial hyperplasia / polyps.
        - Estrogen replacement therapy / HRT.
        - Cervical carcinoma.

### Phase 7: Diagnostic Workup & Investigation Protocols

- **Mentor Question**: What investigations should be ordered for this 32-year-old patient with a 16-week uterine mass and HMB?
- **Laboratory Testing**:
    1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Hemoglobin level (evaluate severity of anemia), Hematocrit, Platelet count, TLC/DLC.
    2. **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**: Confirm microcytic hypochromic anemia.
    3. **Blood Grouping & Rh Typing**: Essential for potential cross-matching and blood transfusion.
    4. **Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) & Prolactin**: Screen for thyroid dysfunction and hyperprolactinemia causing ovulatory dysfunction.
    5. **Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT)**: Mandatory in all reproductive-age females to rule out pregnancy complications.
    6. **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, APTT)**: Indicated if coagulopathy is clinically suspected.
- **Imaging Modalities**:
    - **Pelvic Ultrasonography (Transvaginal TVS / Transabdominal TAS)**:
        - Gold standard initial imaging tool.
        - Evaluates uterine dimensions, endometrial thickness (ET), number, size, and location (FIGO 0–8) of fibroids.
        - **Sonographic Differentiation: Fibroid vs. Adenomyosis**:

|Sonographic Feature|Leiomyoma (Fibroid)|Adenomyosis|
|---|---|---|
|Uterine Shape|Irregularly or focal enlargement|Diffuse, globular, symmetric enlargement|
|Echotexture|Well-defined, hypoechoic mass with pseudocapsule|Heterogeneous myometrium, asymmetric thickening|
|Acoustic Shadowing|Edge shadowing / whorled pattern|Fan-shaped acoustic shadowing / striations|
|Myometrial Cysts|Absent|Subendometrial myometrial cysts present|
|Junctional Zone|Normal|Thickened Junctional Zone (≥12 mm)|

- **Endometrial Sampling / Biopsy Indications**:
    - Indications: Age >40–45 years with AUB, or age <40 years with risk factors for endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma (PCOS, obesity, persistent AUB, failed medical management, or thickened ET >4 mm in postmenopausal / abnormal ET in premenopausal).
    - Modalities: Pipelle endometrial sampling, Dilatation and Curettage (D&C), or Hysteroscopy-guided biopsy (gold standard for focal intra-cavitary lesions).

### Phase 8: Medical Management Options for AUB & Fibroids

- **Mentor Question**: Detail the pharmacological treatment options for AUB and Leiomyoma.
- **Non-Hormonal / Hemostatic Agents**:
    1. **Tranexamic Acid (Antifibrinolytic)**:
        - Dose: 1 g (or 500 mg to 1000 mg) orally 3–4 times daily during heavy bleeding days.
        - Mechanism: Inhibits plasminogen activation, reducing clot dissolution. Reduces menstrual blood loss by 40–50%.
    2. **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)**:
        - Agents: Mefenamic Acid (500 mg TID), Naproxen, Ibuprofen.
        - Mechanism: Inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX), altering endometrial prostaglandin synthesis (PGF2α / PGE2 ratio). Reduces blood loss by 20–30% and relieves dysmenorrhea.
- **Hormonal Agents**:
    1. **Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCPs)**: Suppresses ovulation and causes endometrial atrophy, reducing blood loss and cycle irregularity.
    2. **Oral Progestins**:
        - Agents: Norethisterone acetate (5 mg BID/TID), Medroxyprogesterone acetate (10 mg daily).
        - Schedule: Cyclical (Days 5–26 or 15–25) or continuous high-dose therapy.
    3. **Levonorgestrel-releasing Intrauterine System (LNG-IUS / Mirena)**:
        - Local intrauterine release of 20 μg/day LNG.
        - Causes profound endometrial atrophy and local hemostasis.
        - First-line medical treatment for AUB-E, AUB-A, and AUB-L (without cavitary distortion). Reduces menstrual blood loss by up to 90% at 12 months.
    4. **Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) Agonists / Antagonists**:
        - Agents: Leuprolide, Goserelin.
        - Mechanism: Downregulates pituitary GnRH receptors, inducing medical menopause and hypoestrogenism.
        - Effect: Shrinks fibroid volume by 40–50% within 3 months; used pre-operatively or short-term (3–6 months) with add-back therapy.
    5. **Selective Progesterone Receptor Modulators (SPRMs)**:
        - Agent: Ulipristal acetate (5 mg daily). Suppresses fibroid growth and induces amenorrhea (subject to hepatic safety monitoring).

---

### Phase 9: Surgical Management Protocol

- **Mentor Question**: What surgical options exist for a 32-year-old patient with a 16-week fibroid uterus?
- **Uterine-Sparing / Fertility-Preserving Options**:
    - **Myomectomy**:
        - Indicated if the patient desires future fertility or uterine preservation.
        - Approaches: Hysteroscopic myomectomy (for submucosal FIGO 0, 1, 2 fibroids), Laparoscopic myomectomy, or Open Abdominal Myomectomy (for large intramural/subserosal fibroids).
    - **Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE)**: Interventional radiological catheterization and embolization of uterine arteries to induce fibroid ischemia and infarction.
- **Definitive Surgical Management**:
    - **Hysterectomy**:
        - Indicated when family completion is achieved, in large symptomatic fibroids (e.g., 16 weeks size), failed medical management, or severe anemia/pain.
        - Surgical Routes: Total Abdominal Hysterectomy (TAH), Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH), Laparoscopic-Assisted Vaginal Hysterectomy (LAVH), or Vaginal Hysterectomy (VH).
        - Preservation of Ovaries: Ovaries are preserved in premenopausal women (<45–50 years) without ovarian pathology to prevent surgical menopause and cardiovascular/bone risks.

### II. Summary of All Important Clinical Facts Mentioned

- Chief complaints must never state "patient admitted 2 days ago"; present as symptom + duration.
- Normal menstrual parameters: Frequency 24–38 days, Duration 3–8 days, Volume 5–80 mL.
- Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (HMB) is defined as blood loss >80 mL per cycle.
- Key clinical indicators of HMB: >7–8 fully soaked pads/day, passage of blood clots, night-time pad changes, staining of clothes, and systemic anemia.
- PALM represents structural causes (Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma, Malignancy/Hyperplasia); COEIN represents non-structural causes (Coagulopathy, Ovulatory dysfunction, Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not yet classified).
- Atrophic endometritis/vaginitis is the single most common cause of postmenopausal bleeding (~60–80%).
- Adenomyosis presents with the classic triad of severe dysmenorrhea, HMB, and a diffusely enlarged tender uterus (≤12 weeks size).
- Intramural and submucosal fibroids are the primary structural causes of HMB among leiomyomas.
- Levonorgestrel-releasing Intrauterine System (LNG-IUS / Mirena) reduces menstrual blood loss by up to 90% at 12 months.
- Tranexamic acid reduces blood loss by 40–50%; NSAIDs reduce blood loss by 20–30%.
- GnRH agonists shrink fibroid volume by 40–50% within 3 months but are limited to short-term use due to bone density loss.
- Hysterectomy is definitive management for women who have completed their family or failed medical therapy.

### IV. All Important Numbers, Cut-off Values, and Diagnostic Criteria

- **Menstrual Cycle Frequency**: 24 to 38 days (Frequent <24 days, Infrequent >38 days).
- **Menstrual Duration**: 3 to 8 days (Prolonged >8 days).
- **Menstrual Blood Loss**: 5 to 80 mL (HMB >80 mL).
- **Cycle Regularity Variation**: ≤7 to 9 days variation between shortest and longest cycles.
- **Fibroid Mass Size in Index Case**: 16 weeks gravid uterine size.
- **Postmenopausal Endometrial Thickness Cut-off**: >4 mm on TVS warrants endometrial biopsy.
- **Thickened Junctional Zone in Adenomyosis**: ≥12 mm on MRI/TVS.
- **Tranexamic Acid Dose**: 1 g (or 500–1000 mg) TID during menses.
- **Mefenamic Acid Dose**: 500 mg TID during menses.
- **LNG-IUS Release Rate**: 20 μg/day of levonorgestrel.
- **Leuprolide Dose**: 3.75 mg IM monthly.

### VI. Points Explicitly Emphasized by the Speaker (IMPORTANT)

- **IMPORTANT**: Never write or state "patient was admitted 2 days ago" in chief complaints; state symptom + duration.
- **IMPORTANT**: Always rule out pregnancy via UPT in every woman of reproductive age presenting with AUB.
- **IMPORTANT**: Differentiate between fibroid (well-defined, non-tender, focal/asymmetric) and adenomyosis (diffusely enlarged, tender, globular, severe dysmenorrhea).
- **IMPORTANT**: Endometrial biopsy is mandatory in women >40–45 years with AUB or in younger women with risk factors for endometrial malignancy.
- **IMPORTANT**: LNG-IUS is the most effective non-surgical medical treatment for AUB without cavitary-distorting lesions.
- **IMPORTANT**: Preserve ovaries in premenopausal women undergoing hysterectomy unless specific ovarian pathology exists.
