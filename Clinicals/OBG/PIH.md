# Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (HDP) — Comprehensive Viva Analysis & Case Masterclass

This detailed clinical case and viva analysis is based strictly on the transcript of the clinical case presentation **"PREGNANCY INDUCED HYPERTENSION - Clinical Case Presentation"**.

## Chronological Viva Exchange: Mentor Questions, Student Responses, and Mentor Corrections

### Phase 1: Terminology & Paradigm Shift (PIH vs. Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy)

- **Mentor Opening Instruction**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: The term **PIH (Pregnancy Induced Hypertension)** is obsolete and must **NO LONGER** be used in clinical presentations.
    - Historical progression of terms: _Preeclamptic Toxemia / Toxemias of Pregnancy_ → _Pregnancy Induced Hypertension (PIH)_ → **Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (HDP)**.
    - **Clinical Examination Rationale**: In undergraduate and postgraduate clinical examinations, students are not provided with laboratory investigation reports upfront. Without investigation reports (e.g., urine protein, LFT, RFT), it is impossible to specify the exact entity within the spectrum at initial presentation.
    - **Correct Provisional Diagnosis Format**: _"A case of Hypertensive Disorder of Pregnancy for evaluation."_
    - **Spectrum of Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy**:
        1. Gestational Hypertension
        2. Preeclampsia / Eclampsia Spectrum
        3. Chronic Hypertension
        4. Chronic Hypertension Superadded with Preeclampsia

### Phase 3: Chief Complaints Framing & Obstetric History Rules

- **Student Presentation**: Stated under "Chief Complaint": _"The patient came for a regular antenatal checkup and was found to have high blood pressure levels on evaluation."_
- **Mentor Question**: How should an obstetric case presentation begin, and is an asymptomatic finding a "chief complaint"?
- **Mentor Correction & Clinical Rules**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: More than 50% of pregnant women attending antenatal clinics are experiencing normal physiological pregnancies and are completely asymptomatic. Do **NOT** call them "patients" straight away.
    - Do **NOT** use the heading "Chief Complaints" if the woman is entirely asymptomatic and was picked up incidentally during screening.
    - **Standard Opening Format**: Always begin with parity/gravidity and duration of amenorrhea:
        
        > _"Here is a primigravida with 8 months of amenorrhea who came for a routine antenatal checkup and during examination was incidentally found to have high blood pressure."_
        
    - **Obstetric Nomenclature Rules**:
        - Avoid abbreviated jargon like "G1 P0 A0" or "G1".
        - Explicitly state: _"Gravida 1"_ or _"Primigravida"_. Writing "Primigravida with G1" is redundant because a primigravida is inherently Gravida 1.
        - For multigravidae, state fully: _"Gravida 2, Para 1, Living 1"_.

### Phase 4: First Trimester History, Ultrasound Indications, & Time Management

- **Student Presentation**: Reported spontaneous conception, UPT positive at 1.5 months, consumed folic acid, dating scan normal, no nausea/vomiting, no urinary frequency, no fatigue, no pain abdomen, no bleeding PV, no fever with rash, no radiation or drug exposure.
- **Mentor Teaching Points**:
    - **Spontaneous Conception**: Relevant when a patient has been married for ≥2 years to rule out infertility treatments. If married for <1 year, explicitly stating "spontaneous" is unnecessary.
    - **Exam Time Management**: Candidates get only 5 to 8 minutes for history presentation in exams. Summarize as: _"First trimester was uneventful."_
    - **Ultrasound Scanning Guidelines**:
        - Routine ultrasound is **NOT** mandatory according to Government of India guidelines due to the non-availability of sonologists in many public settings.
        - An early scan (7–8 weeks) confirms intrauterine location, viability, chorionicity, and dating. A single ideal scan is timed at 18–20 weeks.

### Phase 5: Second Trimester History, Immunization Protocols (TT, Td, Tdap), & Phraseology

- **Student Presentation**: Quickening felt in 5th month (18–20 weeks). Received 2 doses of TD vaccine. Took iron and calcium. Level II Anomaly scan at 5th month was told to be normal. No pedal edema, headache, blurring of vision, epigastric pain, pain abdomen, leaking/bleeding PV, fatigue, palpitations, or dysuria.
- **Mentor Discussion on Immunization (TT vs. Td vs. Tdap)**:
    - **National Immunization Schedule**: Recommends **Tetanus Toxoid (TT)** — 2 doses given 4 to 6 weeks apart in a primigravida (free of cost in government setups).
    - **Td (Tetanus-Diphtheria)**: Administered in select hospital settings.
    - **Tdap (Tetanus, Diphtheria, Acellular Pertussis)**: Recommended at 27 to 34 weeks to protect the newborn against pertussis. Costs ~1200 INR. Because >70% of the population is below the poverty line, Tdap is not universally mandatory in public health setups.
- **Phraseology Adjustment**: When presenting without direct access to written reports, state: _"Patient was told that her routine investigations and anomaly scan were normal."_

### Phase 6: Third Trimester History & The 3 Pillars (Labor, Imminent Eclampsia, Fetal Well-being)

- **Mentor Question**: What key events must be evaluated in the third trimester history of a pregnant woman?
- **Mentor Teaching — The 3 Pillars of Third Trimester History**:
    1. **Events Related to Onset of Labor**: Preterm or term labor pains, leaking PV (rupture of membranes), or bleeding PV (show/abruption).
    2. **Signs of Imminent / Impending Eclampsia**:
        - Severe headache
        - Blurring of vision / visual disturbances
        - Epigastric pain / right upper quadrant pain
        - Persistent vomiting
        - Decreased urine output (oliguria)
        - _Clinical Significance_: Indicates end-organ dysfunction and warns that the patient may throw a convulsion (eclampsia) shortly.
    3. **Fetal Well-being**: Maternal perception of daily fetal movements (DFMC).

### Phase 8: History Summary, Booked Case Criteria, & Diagnostic Fallacies

- **Student Summary**: _"A 23-year-old primigravida with 8 months amenorrhea, previously normotensive, came for regular ANC checkup and was found to have high BP... suggestive of HDP, probably gestational hypertension."_
- **Mentor Corrections & Clinical Errors**:
    - **"Previously Normotensive"**: Do not claim the patient was previously normotensive unless previous antenatal records are explicitly verified.
    - **Prior Anti-hypertensive Therapy**: Always determine if a referred patient was already started on anti-hypertensive medication prior to referral. If a referred patient presents with BP 130/80 mmHg while taking Labetalol, missing the drug history invalidates the diagnosis.
    - **Booked Case Definition**:
        - Minimum **4 antenatal visits** (WHO now advocates an **8-visit model**).
        - Minimum **2 doses of TT / Td** immunization.
        - Minimum **100 iron and folic acid (IFA) tablets** consumed.
        - _(Note: Ultrasound scanning is NOT a mandatory criterion for defining a booked case)_.
    - **Gestational Hypertension vs. Preeclampsia Fallacy**:
        - _Imminent Eclampsia vs. Preeclampsia_: There are signs of imminent eclampsia, but no such entity as "impending preeclampsia".
        - _Gestational Hypertension_: BP ≥140/90 mmHg detected after 20 weeks gestation **WITHOUT**proteinuria or decreased end-organ perfusion.
        - _Preeclampsia_: BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks **WITH** proteinuria (≥0.3 g/24h or ≥1+ dipstick) **OR**features of decreased end-organ perfusion.
        - _Diagnostic Fallacy_: At the initial summary stage (before urine protein testing), one **CANNOT**differentiate gestational hypertension from preeclampsia.

### Phase 9: General Physical Examination (GPE), Vital Signs, Weight Gain Dynamics, & Maternal Gait

- **Vital Signs Presentation**:
    - Conscious, cooperative, oriented, moderately built and nourished.
    - Pulse: 90 bpm, regular, good volume, no delays.
    - RR: 17 breaths/min; Temp: Afebrile (98.4 °F).
    - BP: 144/94 mmHg.
    - **Mentor Advice**: State simply _"BP was 144/94 mmHg"_. Avoid adding unnecessary suffixes like "measured in right arm in sitting position" unless explicitly questioned by the examiner.
- **Anthropometry & BMI Rules**:
    - Height: 158 cm; Present Weight: 65 kg; Pre-pregnancy BMI: 21.6 kg/m²; Present BMI: 25.3 kg/m².
    - **Mentor Rule**: Do **NOT** calculate or monitor BMI at every antenatal visit.
    - Focus on **Total Weight Gain**: Target weight gain during normal pregnancy is **10 to 12 kg** (minimal/loss in 1st trimester, ~5 kg in 2nd, ~5 kg in 3rd). At 34 weeks, an expected weight gain of ~8 to 9 kg is normal.
- **Maternal Gait Evaluation**:
    - Observe the patient walking to the weighing scale.
    - **Waddling Gait**: Normal physiological gait of late pregnancy. The anterior abdominal distension shifts the center of gravity anteriorly from the vertebral column; the patient widens her base of support (feet apart) to maintain balance.
    - **Limping Gait**: Indicates asymmetrical or crooked pelvis, predicting labor complications.
- **Breast, Spine, Thyroid Examination**: Always examine breasts with a female chaperone present before documenting "breasts normal".

### Phase 10: Abdominal Examination Inspection, Scar Rules, & Palpation Errors

- **Inspection**: Globular shape, longitudinally distended, umbilicus central and everted, linea nigra and striae gravidarum present, hernial orifices intact, no scar marks, no dilated veins.
- **Scar Reporting Rule**: Write about scar marks ONLY if they are present. Writing "no scar marks" in a primigravida with no history of surgery is redundant.
- **Palpation Errors**:
    - **Removal of Superfluous Terms**: Student wrote "no local rise of temperature or tenderness".
    - **Mentor Correction**: **Remove "no local rise of temperature"** — abdominal skin does not show localized temperature variation without a focal superficial skin lesion.
    - **Significance of Abdominal Tenderness**:
        - Localized tenderness over a mass = Red degeneration of fibroid.
        - Tense, rigid, tender uterus = **Abruptio Placentae** (associated with HDP in 25–30% of cases).
    - **Fundal Height**: 32 weeks; **SFH**: 34 cm; **Abdominal Girth**: 96 cm at umbilicus.
    - **Analysis of Elevated Abdominal Girth (96 cm at 34 weeks)**:
        1. Polyhydramnios
        2. Multifetal gestation (Twins)
        3. Maternal obesity / Abdominal wall edema
        4. Fetal Macrosomia
        5. Discrepancy in gestational age (wrong dates)

### Phase 11: Leopold Maneuvers (Grips 1–4) & Guideline Timings

- **Leopold Maneuvers Findings**:
    - Fundal Grip (Leopold 1): Soft, broad, irregular mass = Breech.
    - Lateral Grip (Leopold 2): Right side smooth, convex = Spine; Left side irregular, nodular = Limbs.
    - First Pelvic / Pawlik's Grip (Leopold 3): Smooth, hard, globular mass = Fetal head.
    - Second Pelvic Grip (Leopold 4): Fingertips converge below head = Head is **UNENGAGED**.
- **Royal College & Guideline Rules on Leopold Maneuvers**:
    - Leopold maneuvers are **NOT** routinely necessary before **34 to 36 weeks** because fetal presentation changes frequently (15–20% instability) prior to 36 weeks.
    - **Exceptions where Leopold maneuvers MUST be performed regardless of gestational age**:
        1. Patient presenting with labor pains or leaking PV (suspected preterm labor).
        2. Decision made to deliver / terminate pregnancy due to severe maternal/fetal compromise.

### Phase 12: Omitted Abdominal Examination Parameters (Uterine Activity, Clinical Liquor, EFW)

- **Mentor Question**: What critical abdominal examination findings were omitted?
- **1. Uterine Activity**: State whether the uterus is **relaxed** or **acting** (contractions).
    - Document contraction frequency (number per 10 minutes), duration (seconds), and intensity.
    - **Intensity Test**: Indent the uterine fundus with an index finger at the peak of a contraction. If the uterine wall can be indented, intensity is inadequate; if non-indentable, intensity is adequate.
- **2. Clinical Assessment of Amniotic Fluid (Liquor Volume)**:
    - In HDP, uteroplacental insufficiency leads to **Oligohydramnios**.
    - Co-existence of HDP with **Polyhydramnios** is extremely rare and suggests underlying maternal diabetes mellitus, congenital fetal anomalies, or fetal macrosomia.
    - _Clinical Clue for Adequate Liquor_: Easy ballottement of the fetal head during the First Pelvic Grip.
- **3. Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW)**: Apply Johnson's formula for term vertex presentations.

### Phase 13: Diagnostic Investigations & Re-checking BP Protocols

- **Rule for Re-checking Blood Pressure**:
    - Diagnosis of hypertension requires **two separate BP readings ≥140/90 mmHg recorded at least 4 to 6 hours apart** (to rule out white-coat hypertension).
    - Exception: Severe hypertension (≥170/110 mmHg) requires immediate confirmation within minutes.
- **Investigation Profile Wording**: Avoid the term "PIH Profile". Explicitly state the individual tests.

### Phase 14: Systemic & End-Organ Investigations (Hemogram, Urine Analysis, LFT, RFT, Fundoscopy)

1. **Complete Hemogram & Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**:
    - Hematocrit: Evaluates hemoconcentration.
    - Platelet Count: Detects thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL in HELLP syndrome).
    - Peripheral Blood Smear: Identifies schistocytes, helmet cells, and immature RBCs indicating microangiopathic hemolytic anemia.
2. **Urine Analysis**:
    - Bedside tests: Heat coagulation test, Nitric acid test, Sulfosalicylic acid test, Dipstick (≥1+ indicates significant proteinuria).
    - Quantitative tests: **24-hour Urine Protein** (gold standard, >300 mg/24h) or **Spot Urine Protein-to-Creatinine Ratio (PCR)** (≥0.3 corresponds to >300 mg/24h).
    - Urine Sugar: Screen for co-existing Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (present in 20–25% of preeclampsia cases).
    - Urine Microscopy: Check for pus cells (asymptomatic bacteriuria) and casts (hyaline/granular/RBC casts indicating chronic renal disease).
3. **Liver Function Tests (LFT)**:
    - Serum AST / ALT: Elevation >2 times upper limit of normal (>70–80 IU/L).
    - Serum LDH (Lactate Dehydrogenase): Marker of systemic hemolysis.
4. **Renal Function Tests (RFT)**:
    - Serum Creatinine: Value >1.1 mg/dL indicates severe preeclampsia.
    - Serum Uric Acid: Elevated levels correlate strongly with adverse perinatal outcomes.
    - Blood Urea.
5. **Fundoscopy**:
    - Evaluates retinal arteriolar narrowing, AV nicking, papilledema, and retinal detachment.
    - Long-standing AV changes suggest **Chronic Hypertension** rather than acute gestational hypertension.

---

### Phase 15: Antepartum Fetal Surveillance (mBPP & Umbilical Doppler)

- **Components of Fetal Assessment**:
    - **Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC)**.
    - **Ultrasound Biometry & Growth**: Confirms gestational age, evaluates abdominal circumference (AC), estimated fetal weight, and asymmetric FGR.
    - **Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI)**: Assesses chronic fetal hypoxia.
    - **Non-Stress Test (NST)**: Assesses acute fetal hypoxia (reactive vs. non-reactive FHR trace).
    - **Modified Biophysical Profile (mBPP)**: Combines **NST (acute hypoxia)** + **AFI (chronic hypoxia)**.
    - **Umbilical Artery Doppler**: Evaluates placental vascular resistance (increased S/D ratio, Absent End-Diastolic Flow / AEDF, or Reversed End-Diastolic Flow / REDF).

---

### Phase 16: Antihypertensive Therapy & Pharmacological Management

- **Indication to Start Antihypertensive Therapy**:
    - Initiated when sustained blood pressure is **≥140/90 mmHg**.
- **Pharmacological Agents**:
    1. **First-Line Oral Agent**: **Oral Labetalol** (100 mg TID, titrated up to 600–1200 mg/day; usually 600 mg max clinically).
    2. **Second-Line Oral Agent**: **Oral Nifedipine** (sustained-release 10–20 mg BID).
        - _Warning_: **NEVER** administer sublingual nifedipine (risks precipitating uncontrolled hypotension and fetal collapse).
    3. **Emergency Hypertensive Crisis**: IV Labetalol.
- **Prophylaxis Against Convulsions**:
    - **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4)**: Drug of choice for prevention and treatment of eclampsia (Pritchard IM or Zuspan IV regimen).

---

### Phase 17: Indications for Immediate Delivery / Termination of Pregnancy

- **Absolute Indications to Deliver (Irrespective of Gestational Age)**:
    1. **Eclampsia** (onset of convulsions) — delivery is the definitive cure.
    2. **Term Gestation** (≥37–38 weeks).
    3. **Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD)**.
    4. **Abruptio Placentae**.
    5. **Acute Pulmonary Edema**.
    6. **Uncontrolled Hypertension** despite maximal doses of two antihypertensive drugs.
    7. **HELLP Syndrome**.
    8. **Rapidly Worsening Renal/Liver Function** (e.g., serum creatinine rapidly rising >1.1–1.5 mg/dL).
    9. **Severe Fetal Compromise** (Reversed end-diastolic flow on umbilical Doppler or persistent non-reactive NST).

### Phase 18: Systemic Complications of Severe Preeclampsia (Anatomical Breakdown)

- **Systemic Complications Framework**:

```
Central Nervous System (Brain)
  → Cerebral Edema
  → Cerebrovascular Accident (CVA / Hemorrhage / Infarction)
  → Eclampsia (Convulsions)

Ophthalmic (Eyes)
  → Retinal Arteriolar Spasm
  → Retinal Detachment
  → Cortical Blindness

Upper Airway & Respiratory
  → Aspiration Pneumonia (post-convulsion)
  → Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
  → Acute Pulmonary Edema
  → Acute Left Ventricular Failure (LVF) / Cardiomyopathy

Hepatic (Liver)
  → HELLP Syndrome (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver Enzymes, Low Platelets)
  → Subcapsular Liver Hematoma
  → Rupture of Liver Capsule & Intraperitoneal Hemorrhage

Renal (Kidneys)
  → Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) / Acute Renal Failure
  → Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN — most common cause of oliguria)
  → Acute Cortical Necrosis (irreversible, leading to Chronic Kidney Disease)

Obstetric & Fetal
  → Abruptio Placentae
  → Preterm Labor
  → Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR)
  → Intrauterine Fetal Demise (IUFD)
  → Intrapartum Fetal Distress & Asphyxia

Vascular & Hematological
  → Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC)
  → Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) / Deep Vein Thrombosis
```

### Phase 19: Delivery Timing, Labor Management, & Postpartum Care Protocols

- **Decision Tree at Term (38 Weeks Gestation)**:
    1. **Step 1: Admission**: Admit the patient at 38 weeks.
    2. **Step 2: Decision to Deliver**: Proceed with delivery.
    3. **Step 3: Route Selection**: Assess Pelvis, Fetal Presentation, Lie, Position, and CPD using **Müller-Munro-Kerr method**. Vaginal delivery is preferred unless obstetric contraindications exist.
    4. **Step 4: Induction of Labor**:
        - Assess **Bishop Score**:
            - _Bishop Score >6_: Favorable cervix → Induce labor. Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM) during labor promotes progress and causes a physiological drop in BP.
            - _Bishop Score <6_: Unfavorable cervix → Priming/ripening with Prostaglandin E2 (Dinoprostone gel) or PGE1 (Misoprostol).
- **Intrapartum Care**:
    - **Partogram Monitoring**:
        - _Maternal Monitoring_: BP every 30 minutes, pulse, temperature, urine output, impending eclampsia signs.
        - _Fetal Monitoring_: Continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring (EFM) or 1:1 intermittent auscultation.
        - _Labor Monitoring_: Contractions, cervical dilation, descent, ARM.
- **Second Stage Management**:
    - **Cut short the second stage of labor** using **Outlet Forceps** or Vacuum extraction.
    - _Rationale_: Prevents maternal straining and Valsalva maneuver, which surge intracranial pressure (>100 mmHg) and risk CVA or pulmonary edema.
- **Third Stage Management (AMTSL)**:
    - Administer **Oxytocin** (10 IU IM/IV).
    - **CONTRAINDICATION**: **NEVER administer IV Methergine (Methylergometrine)** in hypertensive patients (causes catastrophic vasoconstriction and hypertensive crisis).
- **Postpartum Care & Hospital Stay**:
    - Monitor in the labor room for **24 to 48 hours** (eclampsia can occur up to 48 hours postpartum).
    - Discharge timeline: Non-severe GDM/HTN = 4–5 days; Severe Preeclampsia / Eclampsia = 5–7 days.

---

### Phase 20: Screening & Prevention (Aspirin & Calcium Protocol)

- **Screening Tools**: 1st and 2nd trimester **Uterine Artery Doppler** (presence of persistent diastolic notch indicates failed trophoblastic invasion and high resistance).
- **Pharmacological Prevention**:
    - **Low-Dose Aspirin (75 to 150 mg daily)**:
        - Initiated **BEFORE 16 weeks of gestation** (ideally 12–16 weeks) and continued until **36 weeks**.
        - Initiated based on high-risk factors: Chronic HTN, pre-existing DM, chronic renal disease, prior preeclampsia, obesity, multifetal pregnancy, APLA syndrome, elderly primigravida.
    - **High-Dose Calcium Supplementation (1.5 to 2.0 g/day)**: Reduces preeclampsia risk in populations with low dietary calcium intake.

### IV. All Important Numbers, Values, Cut-offs, and Criteria

- **Hypertension Diagnostic Cut-off**: BP ≥140/90 mmHg on two occasions at least 4 to 6 hours apart.
- **Severe Hypertension Cut-off**: BP ≥170/110 mmHg.
- **Gestational Age Milestones**: Early scan (7–8 weeks), Anomaly scan (18–20 weeks), Leopold maneuvers (≥34–36 weeks), Delivery at term (37–38 weeks).
- **Familial Preeclampsia Risk**: 20% to 40% (25–30% average) if mother/sister affected; 3% eclampsia risk.
- **Expected Total Pregnancy Weight Gain**: 10 to 12 kg (8–9 kg by 34 weeks).
- **Significant Proteinuria Cut-offs**: ≥0.3 g/24h, Spot Urine PCR ≥0.3, or Dipstick ≥1+.
- **Severe Preeclampsia Laboratory Cut-offs**: Serum Creatinine >1.1 mg/dL, AST/ALT >2x normal (>70–80 IU/L), Platelets <100,000/μL.
- **Tdap Vaccination Timing & Cost**: 27–34 weeks gestation; ~1200 INR.
- **Aspirin Prophylaxis Timing & Dose**: 75 to 150 mg daily started **before 16 weeks** until 36 weeks.
- **High-Dose Calcium Dose**: 1.5 to 2.0 g/day.

### V. All Mnemonics or Memory Aids

- **HDP**: Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy.
- **HELLP**: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver Enzymes, Low Platelets.
- **mBPP**: Modified Biophysical Profile (NST + AFI).
- **AMTSL**: Active Management of Third Stage of Labor.
- **AEDF / REDF**: Absent End-Diastolic Flow / Reversed End-Diastolic Flow (on Umbilical Doppler).

### VI. Points Explicitly Emphasized by the Speaker (IMPORTANT)

- **IMPORTANT**: Stop using the obsolete term "PIH"; state "Hypertensive Disorder of Pregnancy for evaluation".
- **IMPORTANT**: Do NOT state "Chief Complaint" for an asymptomatic woman detected during routine screening.
- **IMPORTANT**: Always check if a referred hypertensive patient is already taking antihypertensive medication.
- **IMPORTANT**: Symptoms of imminent eclampsia (headache, epigastric pain, visual blurring, vomiting) warn of impending fits.
- **IMPORTANT**: Do NOT calculate or monitor BMI at every visit; track total weight gain instead.
- **IMPORTANT**: Remove "no local rise of temperature" from abdominal palpation notes.
- **IMPORTANT**: Leopold maneuvers are not required before 34–36 weeks unless labor is suspected or delivery is planned.
- **IMPORTANT**: Sublingual nifedipine is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe acute hypotension and fetal collapse.
- **IMPORTANT**: Eclampsia is an absolute indication to terminate pregnancy regardless of gestational age.
- **IMPORTANT**: NEVER administer IV Methergine in hypertensive patients during the third stage of labor.
- **IMPORTANT**: Low-dose Aspirin must be started **before 16 weeks** of gestation to be effective in preventing preeclampsia.
