## 2. Chronological Viva Exchange: Mentor Questions, Student Responses, and Mentor Corrections

### Phase 1: Chief Complaints Framing & Terminology Correction ("Amenorrhea" vs. "Gestation")

- **Student Presentation**: Stated chief complaints as "amenorrhea for 8 months", easy fatigability, breathlessness on exertion, and palpitations for 2 months, and lower limb swelling for 1 week.
- **Mentor Question & Correction**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Avoid stating "amenorrhea for 8 months" when the patient is already diagnosed as pregnant and attending routine antenatal care.
    - **Correct Phraseology**: State **"8 months of gestation"** (or 32/36 weeks of gestation).
    - **Clinical Rationale**: Stating "amenorrhea" implies evaluating an unknown cause of missed periods, whereas "gestation" confirms an established pregnancy under active antenatal care.

---

### Phase 2: Physiological vs. Pathological Edema & Mechanical IVC Compression

- **Student Presentation**: Reported swelling over lower limbs for 1 week that decreases on rest.
- **Mentor Question**: Why is it clinically vital to ask whether lower limb swelling decreases on rest?
- **Student Response**: To differentiate physiological edema of pregnancy from pathological causes.
- **Mentor Question**: What causes physiological edema in pregnancy?
- **Student Response**: Mentioned hypoproteinemia, hemodilution, and pooling of fluid (more on the right side).
- **Mentor Correction & Clinical Pearl**:
    - **Mechanism of Physiological Edema**: Primary cause is **mechanical compression of the Inferior Vena Cava (IVC)** by the fetus/gravid uterus, which impairs venous return from the lower extremities to the heart.
    - **Effect of Rest / Posture**: When the patient rests in the **left lateral position**, mechanical IVC compression is relieved, venous return improves, and the edema decreases.
    - **Clinical Instruction**: Instruct pregnant patients to lie down in the left lateral position and rise via a lateral turn to prevent sudden IVC compression and sudden fluctuations in cardiac venous return.

### Phase 3: Dyspnea Grading, History of Present Illness (HPI), & Language Rules

- **Student Presentation**: Patient reported lethargy and tiredness increasing progressively in intensity, leading to inability to perform normal work activities, followed by exertional breathlessness and palpitations.
- **Mentor Question**: How do you grade dyspnea in pregnancy?
- **Student Response**: Applied the NICHE / NYHA classification (Grades 1 to 4):
    - _Grade 1_: Slightly more breathless than normal.
    - _Grade 2_: Breathless on heavy / extra exertion.
    - _Grade 3_: Breathless on mild exertion / unable to carry out daily routine work activities.
    - _Grade 4_: Dyspnea present at rest.
    - Student classified patient as **Grade 3**.
- **Negative History in HPI**:
    - No syncope, chest pain, or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND) (rules out primary cardiac failure).
    - No generalized swelling, headache, visual blurring, or epigastric pain (rules out preeclampsia warning signs).
    - No cough with expectoration, hemoptysis, or evening rise of temperature (rules out tuberculosis).
    - No bleeding per vaginum (PV) or leaking PV.
    - No history of worm infestation.
    - No bleeding per rectum (PR), melena, or hematemesis.
    - No fever with chills, dysuria, or skin discoloration.
- **Mentor Correction on Wording**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Avoid using medical jargon (e.g. "melena", "hemoptysis", "hematemesis") in the History of Present Illness when describing patient reports.
    - Use patient-understandable / colloquial phrasing (e.g. "no history of passing black tarry stools", "no blood in cough", "no blood in vomit/stools").

### Phase 4: First Trimester History, Dating Scan, & Hyperemesis

- **Student Presentation**: Spontaneous conception, non-consanguineous marriage, regular 28–30 day cycles (3–5 days flow). Diagnosed at 1.5 months amenorrhea by Urine Pregnancy Test (UPT). Booked at government hospital at 2 months gestation; routine tests and dating scan normal. Prescribed folic acid ("yellow tablet"), taken irregularly. - **Mentor Teaching Points**:
    - **Dating Scan Importance**: Establishes accurate Expected Date of Delivery (EDD) and enables accurate fetal growth tracking.
    - **Non-Consanguineous Marriage**: Rules out inherited hemoglobinopathies (e.g. Thalassemia).
    - **Hyperemesis Gravidarum**: Severe early vomiting causes weight loss and acute nutritional depletion, triggering early-onset anemia.

### Phase 5: Second Trimester History & Hemoglobin Screening Protocol

- **Student Presentation**: Quickening felt at 3–4 months; fetal movements well perceived. Received 2 doses of Td vaccine (and COVID vaccine). Level II Anomaly scan at 5th month (18–20 weeks) normal. Irregular intake of prescribed iron, folic acid, and calcium tablets ("yellow, red, and white tablets"). No warning signs of preeclampsia.
- **Mentor Question**: What is the recommended schedule for checking hemoglobin levels during pregnancy?
- **Student Response**: Recommended checking in 1st trimester, at 24 weeks, 32 weeks, and as required.
- **Mentor Refinement & Protocol Schedule**:
    - **1st Antenatal Visit (1st Trimester)**: Establishes baseline Hb and identifies pre-existing iron deficiency or hemoglobinopathies.
    - **24 to 28 Weeks**: Second screening (detects peak physiological hemodilution-induced anemia).
    - **30 to 32 Weeks**: Third screening (evaluates third-trimester drop and allows time for iron therapy before delivery).
    - **36 Weeks**: Final pre-delivery screening.
- **Quickening**: Serves as a surrogate marker for gestational age if early first-trimester dating scans were missed.

### Phase 6: Third Trimester History, Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR / IUGR), & Inter-Pregnancy Interval

- **Student Presentation**: Good fetal movements; no bleeding PV, leaking PV, or abdominal pain. Ultrasound at admission showed fetal growth lagging behind gestational age (Fetal Growth Restriction / IUGR).
- **Mentor Discussion on Inter-Pregnancy Interval**:
    - **Biological Significance**: Allows replenishment of maternal iron, folate, and nutritional stores, and allows uterine scar healing (in prior C-sections).
    - **Ideal Inter-Pregnancy Interval**: **18 months to 2 years** (or 3 to 4 years). A short interval (<18 months) prevents iron store recovery, directly predisposing to recurrent anemia and preterm birth.

- **Personal History**: Mixed diet, middle/lower class, no addictions.
- **Dietary History (24-Hour Recall Method)**:
    - Consumes 1300 to 1500 kcal/day with 33 g protein/day (severely deficient).
    - Pregnant daily calorie requirement = **~2600 kcal/day**.
    - Pregnant protein requirement = **1.5 to 2.0 g/kg/day**.
- **Daily Iron Requirements Breakdown**:
    - Non-pregnant non-menstruating baseline: **1 mg/day** (compensates for basal cutaneous and GI epithelial desquamation).
    - Menstruating female: Requires an additional ~0.8 mg/day (total **1.8 mg/day** / dietary allowance ~18 mg/day).
    - Pregnant female (by trimester):
        - _1st Trimester_: **1 to 2 mg/day**.
        - _2nd Trimester_: **4 to 5 mg/day** (increases up to 10 mg/day in Indian guidelines due to iron-deficient diet).
        - _3rd Trimester_: **6 to 8 mg/day** (up to 10 mg/day in Indian guidelines).
- **Total Extra Iron Cost of Entire Pregnancy**:
    - Total extra elemental iron required = **1000 mg** (1000 to 1100 mg):
        - Maternal RBC mass expansion: **500 mg**.
        - Fetal and placental tissue development: **300 to 350 mg**.
        - Delivery blood loss compensation: **250 mg**.
        - Less amenorrhea savings: **-150 to -200 mg**.
        - **Net extra elemental iron required = ~1000 mg**.

### Phase 8: Prophylactic & Therapeutic Iron Dosage Guidelines (MoHFW vs. WHO)

- **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW / Anemia Mukt Bharat)**:
    - Prophylactic dose: **100 mg elemental iron + 500 μg folic acid daily** for 100 days (started after 14–16 weeks) and continued for 3 months postpartum.
    - _Anemia Mukt Bharat Recommendation_: **60 mg elemental iron + 500 μg folic acid**.
    - _Absorption Logic_: Giving 100 mg oral iron daily for 100 days = 10,000 mg total oral iron. At 10% GI absorption rate, exactly 1000 mg elemental iron is absorbed to meet the pregnancy requirement.
    - Therapeutic dose for diagnosed anemia: Double the prophylactic dose (**200 mg elemental iron daily** = 20 mg absorbed/day).
- **WHO Guidelines**:
    - Prophylactic dose: **60 mg elemental iron + 400 μg folic acid daily**.
    - Therapeutic dose: **120 mg elemental iron daily**.

### Phase 9: General Physical Examination (GPE), Pre-Pregnancy Weight, & Ganzoni's Formula

- **GPE Findings**: Conscious, oriented, sitting comfortably, poor build, malnourished, general condition poor. Height: 152 cm, Current weight: 53 kg, Pre-pregnancy weight: 44 kg (Total weight gain: 9 kg), BMI: ~16 kg/m² (underweight).
- **Vital Signs**: Pulse: 84 bpm (right radial, regular, good volume, no radio-radial delay), BP: 122/80 mmHg (sitting, right arm, confirmed after 15–20 mins rest), RR: 18 breaths/min, Afebrile.
- **Pre-Pregnancy Weight & Ganzoni's Formula**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Pre-pregnancy weight (NOT current weight) is required to calculate the intravenous iron requirement using Ganzoni's formula.
    - **Ganzoni's Formula**: \[\text{Total Iron Deficit (mg)} = 2.4 \times (\text{Target Hb} - \text{Actual Hb}) \times \text{Pre-pregnancy Weight (kg)} + 1000\text{ mg}\] _(Where 2.4 = \(0.008 \times 3000\text{ mL blood volume} \times 100\); Target Hb = 11 g/dL; 1000 mg = depot replenishment store according to FOGSI/Indian guidelines; some use 500 mg)_.
- **Edema Testing & Pitting vs. Non-Pitting Rules**:
    - Student incorrectly described pedal edema as "non-pitting".
    - **Testing Technique**: Press thumb firmly 2 cm above the medial malleolus for 5 to 10 seconds (or up to 30 seconds) and observe after release.
    - **Pitting Edema Causes**: Hypervolemia, decreased venous return, congestive cardiac failure, chronic kidney disease, severe anemia.
    - **Non-Pitting Edema Causes**: Protein accumulation / lymphatic obstruction (e.g. filariasis).
    - **Clinical Rule**: Anemia and cardiac failure present with **pitting edema**, NOT non-pitting edema.
- **Cutaneous & Systemic Signs**: Pallor present, skin pale, nail ridges present, no clubbing or cyanosis, no palpable lymph nodes, spine normal, breasts normal. Respiratory system: Bilateral equal air entry, no added sounds. Cardiovascular system: Apex beat in 4th intercostal space in anterior axillary line, S₁ and S₂ normal, no murmurs, no sensory or motor deficit.

### Phase 11: Case Summary, Provisional Diagnosis, & Reasoning (Anemia vs. Cardiac Disease)

- **Summary**: 22-year-old female, G₄P₃L₂A₁ at 32 weeks + 5 days gestation with singleton live fetus in longitudinal lie cephalic presentation, not in labor, with pallor, pedal edema, fatigue, and exertional dyspnea.
- **Provisional Diagnosis**: 32 weeks + 5 days gestation with live singleton fetus in cephalic presentation, with features suggestive of **Anemia**.

### Phase 12: Diagnostic Workup, WHO Guidelines, & Red Cell Indices (MCV, Ferritin)

- **Laboratory Investigation Protocol**:
    1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Hemoglobin, Hematocrit, Red Cell Indices (MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW), Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS).
- **WHO Diagnostic Criteria for Anemia in Pregnancy**:
    - Global / WHO cut-off: Hb <11.0 g/dL (Indian / WHO context: 1st trimester <11.0 g/dL, 2nd trimester <10.5 g/dL, 3rd trimester <10.5 g/dL).
    - **Grading of Anemia (ICMR / WHO)**:
        - **Mild Anemia**: 10.0 to 10.9 g/dL.
        - **Moderate Anemia**: 7.0 to 9.9 g/dL.
        - **Severe Anemia**: <7.0 g/dL.
        - **Very Severe Anemia (ICMR)**: <4.0 g/dL.
- **Red Cell Indices & Diagnostic Flow**:
    - **Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)**: Most critical initial red cell index.
        - _Microcytic_: MCV <80 fL (Iron deficiency, Thalassemia trait, Lead poisoning, Sideroblastic anemia, Anemia of chronic disease).
        - _Normocytic_: MCV 80 to 100 fL.
        - _Macrocytic_: MCV >100 fL (Vitamin B12 deficiency, Folic acid deficiency).
    - **Differentiating Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA) from Thalassemia Trait**:
        - _Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW)_: Elevated in IDA (anisocytosis), normal in Thalassemia trait.
        - _Serum Ferritin_: Single best diagnostic test for iron deficiency. Do NOT order costly complete iron panels (iron, TIBC, transferrin saturation) for all patients.
- **WHO 2022 Serum Ferritin Guidelines & Cut-off Values**:
    - Normal range: 15 to 200 μg/L (or ng/mL).
    - **Ferritin <15 μg/L**: Clear-cut Iron Deficiency Anemia (depleted stores & deficient circulating iron).
    - **Ferritin <30 μg/L**: Depleted Iron Stores (pre-anemic state).
    - **Ferritin <70 μg/L in presence of Infection / Inflammation**: Indicates iron deficiency (because ferritin is an acute-phase reactant elevated by inflammation).
    - **Ferritin >150 to 200 μg/L**: Iron Overload.

### Phase 13: Management of Anemia in Pregnancy (Oral Iron, Parenteral Iron, & Blood Transfusion)

- **Prevention & Pre-conception Care**: Nutritional counseling, pre-conception Hb normalization, family planning spacing (≥18–24 months).
- **Management Decision Tree Based on Gestational Age & Severity**:
    1. **Oral Iron Therapy**:
        - Indications: Mild anemia (Hb 10.0 to 10.9 g/dL) at any gestational age, or moderate anemia in early pregnancy (<30–34 weeks) if well tolerated.
        - Therapeutic Dose: **200 mg elemental iron daily** (20 mg absorbed/day).
        - Oral Salts & Elemental Iron Content:
            - _Ferrous Sulfate_: 200 mg tablet contains 60 to 65 mg elemental iron (30–33%). Cheapest, but highest GI side effects (nausea, constipation, epigastric distress).
            - _Ferrous Fumarate_: 33% elemental iron (100 mg elemental iron per 300 mg).
            - _Ferrous Gluconate_: 12% elemental iron (35 to 50 mg elemental iron).
            - _Ferrous Ascorbate_: 20% elemental iron. Better oral tolerability and GI safety, but more expensive.
    2. **Parenteral (IV / IM) Iron Therapy**:
        - Indications: Moderate anemia (Hb 7.0 to 9.9 g/dL) between 30 and 34 weeks gestation; oral iron intolerance / severe GI side effects; malabsorption (post-bariatric/bypass surgery); non-compliance with oral pills.
        - Contraindications: 1st trimester of pregnancy; prior history of anaphylaxis to iron.
        - **IMPORTANT Rule**: Stop oral iron **24 hours before** starting parenteral iron to prevent saturation of transferrin and minimize anaphylactic reactions.
        - Parenteral Iron Formulations:
            - _Iron Dextran_: Obsolete / contraindicated due to high risk of severe anaphylaxis; requires test dose; IM injections are painful.
            - _Iron Sucrose_: Minimal risk of anaphylaxis (test dose generally not mandatory, but advised if allergic history exists). Administered as 100–200 mg IV infusions.
            - _Ferric Carboxymaltose (FCM)_: High-dose single infusion (up to 1000 mg elemental iron in 15 minutes). No anaphylaxis risk. Approved for postpartum anemia (and increasingly used antepartum in guidelines).
        - Timeline for Response: Takes **2 to 3 weeks** for reticulocyte response and **4 weeks** to raise Hb by 1.0 g/dL (for both oral and parenteral iron).
    3. **Blood Transfusion**:
        - Indications:
            - Gestational age **>34 weeks** with severe anemia (Hb <7.0 g/dL), regardless of cardiac symptoms (no time for oral/IV iron to build Hb before labor).
            - Gestational age **<34 weeks** with Hb <5.0 g/dL (even if asymptomatic) OR Hb 5.0 to 7.0 g/dL with impending cardiac failure.
            - Anemia presenting during active labor with severe distress.

---

### Phase 14: Intrapartum & Postpartum Management Protocol

- **Intrapartum Care in Anemic Patients**:
    - Administer oxygen; keep patient in propped-up position (reduces venous return and cardiac burden).
    - Keep cross-matched blood ready.
    - **Shorten the Second Stage of Labor**: Perform prophylactic **Forceps / Vacuum delivery** to prevent maternal exhaustion, straining, and sudden cardiac volume overload.
    - Post-delivery Venous Return Surge: Following placental delivery, uterine contraction squeezes blood into systemic circulation, increasing venous return by ~80%, risking acute cardiac failure.
- **Postpartum Management & Contraception**:
    - Check Hb at **2 weeks postpartum**.
    - Continue prophylactic/therapeutic oral iron for **at least 3 months postpartum**.
    - Contraceptive Counseling: Advise inter-pregnancy spacing of **at least 18 months to 2 years**.
