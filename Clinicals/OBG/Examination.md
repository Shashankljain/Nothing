# Clinical Examination Demonstration in Obstetrics — Detailed Viva Analysis & Procedural Guide

---

## 1. General Physical Examination (GPE) & Vital Signs Protocol

### General Physical Examination Guidelines

- **Patient Demographics & Description**:
    - State the approximate age of the woman (e.g. "young lady"). Pregnant women are typically young patients.
    - State built and nourishment (e.g. "young lady, moderately built and nourished").
    - **Build**: Refers to the skeletal structure and height of the patient.
    - **Nourishment**: Refers to muscle mass and subcutaneous nutritional status.
    - Document sensorium: State that the patient is "conscious and oriented".
- **Anthropometric Measurements & BMI Calculation**:
    - Measure and record height in centimeters and weight in kilograms.
    - Comment on total weight gain during pregnancy; most patients recall their first-trimester registration weight.
    - **Body Mass Index (BMI) Rule**: BMI must be calculated using the **pre-pregnancy weight**.
    - **Clinical Fallback**: If pre-pregnancy weight is unrecorded, the first-trimester registration weight serves as the pre-pregnancy weight, as first-trimester weight gain is minimal (0.5 kg to 1 kg).
- **Head-to-Toe Examination (PENCIL / PICKLE Acronym)**:
    - Systematic check using standard acronyms: **PICKLE** or **PENCIL**:
        - **P**: Pallor
        - **I / E**: Icterus / Edema (pedal edema)
        - **C**: Cyanosis / Clubbing
        - **K / C**: Koilonychia
        - **L**: Lymphadenopathy
        - **E / B**: Edema / Breast examination
        - **S**: Spine evaluation
        - **T**: Thyroid examination
    - **Additional Systemic / Head-to-Toe Findings**:
        - Nutritional anemia features: Hair changes, nail changes, angular cheilitis / stomatitis, glossitis, or dental caries in the oral cavity.
        - Lower extremity inspection: Check for varicose veins while inspecting for pedal edema.

---

### Vital Signs Protocol & Technique

- **Terminology Requirement**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Avoid using the informal term "vitals"; explicitly state **"Vital Signs"**.
- **Parameters Recorded**: Temperature, Pulse rate, Blood Pressure (BP), and Respiratory rate.
- **Standardized Blood Pressure Technique**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Blood pressure in a pregnant patient must **ALWAYS** be measured in the **sitting position**.
    - **Reason**: Measuring BP in the supine position must be strictly avoided to prevent **supine hypotensive syndrome** (caused by compression of the inferior vena cava by the gravid uterus).
    - Ensure all pre-requisites (15–20 minutes rest, arm supported at heart level) are met and documented.

## 2. Systemic Examination Sequence

- **Obstetric vs. General Medicine Sequence**:
    - In general medical cases, the primary abnormal system is presented first.
    - **In Obstetrics**: The general systemic examination is documented **FIRST**, prior to the abdominal/obstetric examination.
- **Systems Evaluated**:
    - Cardiovascular System (CVS).
    - Respiratory System (RS).
    - Central Nervous System (CNS) — usually documented as normal unless specific neurological pathology exists.
    - Obstetric / Abdominal Examination follows systemic documentation.

---

## 3. Abdominal Examination: Prerequisites & Patient Positioning

### Essential Prerequisites Before Examination

1. **Verbal Consent**: Explain all procedural steps to the patient and obtain informed verbal consent.
2. **Bladder Evacuation**: Ensure the patient has completely emptied her bladder prior to examination.
3. **Privacy**: Maintain complete patient privacy using screen curtains.
4. **Female Chaperone**:

- **IMPORTANT**: Male students or male clinicians must **ALWAYS** be accompanied by a female staff nurse, female Group D attendant, or female student.

5. **Draping**: Keep the patient fully draped, exposing only the abdomen for examination.
6. **Examiner Preparation**: Ensure examiner's hands are warm and nails are trimmed short to prevent discomfort or reflex abdominal guarding.

---

### Patient Positioning Rules

- **Standard Position for Palpation & Inspection**:
    - Patient lies supine with **thighs semiflexed at hips and knees**.
    - **Rationale**: Semiflexion relaxes the anterior abdominal wall musculature, facilitating deep palpation.
- **Exception Rule for Symphysio-Fundal Height (SFH)**:
    - Legs are **extended straight** ONLY during Symphysio-Fundal Height measurement.

---

## 4. Inspection of the Gravid Abdomen

### Inspection Checklist & Systemic Parameters

1. **Size and Shape of Abdomen**:
    - Normal finding: "Abdomen is uniformly distended".
    - Fullness in Flanks:
        - In term pregnancies, the presenting part enters the pelvic brim, causing the fetal shoulders to occupy the flanks; document "uniformly distended with fullness in the flanks".
        - In mid-trimester, flanks are not consistently full due to active fetal movements.
        - In early pregnancy (5th to 6th month), lower abdomen alone appears distended.
    - Asymmetrical enlargement: Suggests uterine anomalies (e.g. bicornuate uterus) or a transverse lie.
2. **Umbilicus**:
    - Normal finding: "Central and normal" (or slightly stretched).
    - Everted Umbilicus: Indicates severe abdominal distension (e.g. polyhydramnios, multifetal gestation) or an umbilical hernia.
3. **Skin Changes**:
    - **Linea Nigra**: Hyperpigmented cutaneous line along the abdominal midline.
    - **Striae Gravidarum**: Pink/purplish stretch marks indicative of current pregnancy.
    - **Striae Albicans**: Silver-white glistening scars from past pregnancies.
4. **Surgical Scars**:
    - Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS) scars: Pfannenstiel (suprapubic transverse), infraumbilical midline, or infraumbilical paramedian.
    - Other surgical scars: Supraumbilical scars (e.g. prior cholecystectomy).
5. **Hernial Sites & Orifices**:
    - Inspect incisional, umbilical, and inguinal regions.
    - Standard finding: "Hernial orifices are free".
6. **Respiratory Movements**:
    - Abdominal respiration is difficult to appreciate in late pregnancy and is omitted from routine inspection reporting.

## 5. Palpation Phase I: Fundal Height, Symphysio-Fundal Height (SFH) & Abdominal Girth

### Step-by-Step Fundal Height & Gestational Age Estimation

- **Uterine Anatomical Milestones**:
    - **12 weeks**: Uterus emerges above pubic symphysis into the abdomen.
    - **16 weeks**: Midway between pubic symphysis and umbilicus.
    - **20 weeks**: Lower border / 2/3rd distance to umbilicus.
    - **24 weeks**: At the level of the umbilicus.
    - **28 weeks**: Lower 1/3rd between umbilicus and xiphisternum.
    - **32 weeks**: Upper 2/3rd between umbilicus and xiphisternum.
    - **36 weeks**: At the level of the xiphisternum.
    - **38–40 weeks**: "Lightening" or downward fall of uterine height back to 32-week level due to engagement of fetal head into pelvis, with increased flank fullness.

---

### Procedure 1: Clinical Fundal Height Estimation

- **Step 1**: Examiner stands on the patient's right side facing the patient.
- **Step 2**: Use the right hand to correct **dextrorotation** of the uterus (dextrorotation occurs naturally due to presence of sigmoid colon on the left). Apply gentle lateral pressure using palmar or dorsal surface.
- **Step 3**: Use the left hand's ulnar border, starting high at the xiphisternum, and move downwards until firm resistance of the uterine fundus is encountered.
- **Step 4**: Correlate fundal height with gestational age by Period of Amenorrhea (POA / LMP). Document as "corresponding to POA", "more than POA", or "less than POA".

---

### Procedure 2: Symphysio-Fundal Height (SFH) Measurement

- **Step 1**: **Ask the patient to extend her legs fully**.
- **Step 2**: Mark the upper border of the pubic symphysis and the top of the uterine fundus with a pen.
- **Step 3**: Place the zero mark of a non-stretchable measuring tape at the upper border of the pubic symphysis.
- **Step 4**: Run the tape along the longitudinal axis of the uterus to the fundal mark with the **inches scale facing the examiner** (prevents observer measurement bias).
- **Step 5**: Turn the tape over and record the measurement in **centimeters**.

---

### Procedure 3: Abdominal Girth Measurement

- **Step 1**: **Ask the patient to semiflex her legs again** at hips and knees.
- **Step 2**: Pass measuring tape around the maternal abdomen at the level of the umbilicus.
- **Step 3**: Record the abdominal girth in centimeters.

## 6. Palpation Phase II: Leopold Maneuvers (Obstetric Grips)

### General Rules for Leopold Maneuvers

- **Gestational Age Cut-off**:
    - **IMPORTANT**: Leopold maneuvers are performed **ONLY from 28 weeks onwards** (7th month and above).
    - Before 28 weeks (e.g. 20–24 weeks), presentation does not alter management; record fundal height, feel fetal parts, check external ballottement, and listen to FHR.
- **Examiner Orientation**:
    - **Leopold 1, 2, and 3**: Examiner stands facing the **patient's face**.
    - **Leopold 4 (Second Pelvic Grip)**: Examiner turns and stands facing the **patient's feet**.

---

### Step 1: Leopold 1 — Fundal Grip

- **Purpose**: Determine which fetal pole occupies the uterine fundus.
- **Technique**:
    - Palpate fundus using palmar surfaces of fingers of both hands.
    - Examine epigastrium and sweep into right and left hypochondria.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
    - **Cephalic Presentation (Breech at Fundus)**: Broad, soft, irregular, non-ballottable mass.
    - **Breech Presentation (Head at Fundus)**: Hard, smooth, round, ballottable mass.

---

### Step 2: Leopold 2 — Umbilical / Lateral Grip

- **Purpose**: Locate fetal back (spine) and small parts (limbs).
- **Technique**:
    - Steady one side of uterus with palmar surface of one hand.
    - Palpate opposite side from hypochondrium through lumbar area down to iliac fossa with palmar surface of other hand.
    - Reverse hands and repeat for opposite side.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
    - **Fetal Back (Spine)**: Smooth, continuous, firm, curved resistance.
    - **Fetal Limbs**: Irregular, small, knob-like nodular structures (limb buds).

---

### Step 3: Leopold 3 — First Pelvic Grip (Pawlik's Grip)

- **Purpose**: Identify presenting part in lower uterine pole, its ballottability, and engagement status.
- **Technique**:
    - Rest ulnar border of right hand on upper border of pubic symphysis.
    - Grasp lower pole of uterus between spread thumb and fingers.
    - Attempt gentle side-to-side movement.
    - Place left hand on fundus to feel for transmitted ballottement.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
    - **Cephalic Presenting Part**: Hard, smooth, globular mass.
    - **Unengaged (Floating) Head**: Head moves freely side-to-side between fingers (ballottable).
    - **Engaged Head**: Head is fixed within pelvis; side-to-side movement is restricted or absent.

---

### Step 4: Leopold 4 — Second Pelvic Grip

- **Purpose**: Confirm presentation, assess engagement, determine attitude (flexion/deflexion), and evaluate skull maturity.
- **Technique**:
    - Turn to face patient's feet.
    - Place palmar surfaces of fingers of both hands on lower abdominal quadrants (lumbar to iliac areas) on either side of midline.
    - Press fingers downward toward pelvic brim, attempting to converge fingertips below presentation.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
    - **Engaged Head**: Examiner's hands diverge / cannot approximate fingers below presentation.
    - **Unengaged Head**: Hands converge / fingertips approximate below presentation.
    - **Flexion vs. Deflexion (Cephalic Prominence)**:
        - Occiput is felt on same side as fetal back; Sinciput is felt higher on side of limbs.
        - Well-flexed head: Sinciput is felt higher than occiput.
    - **Skull Maturity**: Pliability and hardness of cranial bones indicate preterm vs. term fetus.

## 7. Palpation Phase III: Amniotic Fluid, Fetal Weight Estimation & Special Cases

### Amniotic Fluid (Liquor) Clinical Assessment

- **Normal**: "Clinically liquor feels adequate".
- **Polyhydramnios**: Uterine height > POA, abdominal skin stretched/shiny, fetal parts difficult to outline, presence of fluid thrill.
- **Oligohydramnios**: Uterine height < POA, fetal parts easily and distinctly felt.

---

### Fetal Weight Estimation (Johnson's Formula)

- **Formula**: \[\text{Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW in grams)} = (\text{SFH in cm} - n) \times 155\]
- **Station / Engagement Constant (\(n\))**:
    - \(n = 12\) if fetal head is **unengaged** (above station 0).
    - \(n = 11\) if fetal head is **engaged** (at or below station 0).

---

### Special Clinical Situations During Palpation

- **Uterine Contractions**:
    - Comment on contractions **ONLY if patient presented with labor pain**.
    - **Braxton Hicks Contractions**: Uterus becomes hard and well-defined without maternal pain.
    - **True Labor Contractions**: Uterus becomes firm, non-indentable, accompanied by painful sensations.
- **Previous Cesarean Section**: Check for scar tenderness over lower uterine segment.
- **Multifetal Gestation**: Palpate 3 fetal poles (e.g. 2 heads + 1 breech or 2 breeches + 1 head).

---

## 8. Auscultation of Fetal Heart Rate (FHR)

### Auscultation Modalities & Timelines

- **Handheld Obstetric Doppler**: Detects FHR as early as **12 weeks** gestation.
- **Pinard Fetoscope / Stethoscope**: Detects FHR from **18–20 weeks** onwards.

---

### Auscultation Landmarks

- **Site of Maximum Intensity**: Located over the fetal back (spine side).
- **Cephalic Presentation**: FHR heard best in **infraumbilical region** on side of fetal back.
- **Breech Presentation**: FHR heard best in **supraumbilical region** or upper quadrants.
- **Transverse Lie / Malpresentation**: FHR heard near umbilicus or in maternal flanks.
- **Multifetal Gestation**: Locate two distinct FHR maximum intensity points separated by a silent zone.

---

## 9. Synthesis: Case Summary & Final Obstetric Diagnosis Formulation

### Formulating the Case Summary

- **Structure**: Present maternal age, gestational age by LMP, booking and immunization status, chief complaints, and **ONLY positive findings** from history and physical examination.
- **Rule**: Exclude negative findings from summary.

---

### Standard Order of Final Obstetric Diagnosis

1. **Age**: e.g. "26-year-old..."
2. **Obstetric Index / Parity**: e.g. "...primigravida..."
3. **Gestational Age**: Calculated strictly **by LMP** (NOT by examination unless LMP is unknown/unreliable).
4. **Presentation & Lie**: Stated **ONLY for pregnancies ≥28 weeks**.
5. **Labor Status**: State "in labor" or "not in labor" **ONLY if term pregnancy (≥37 weeks) or presenting with abdominal pain**.
6. **Obstetric Risk Factors**: e.g. Previous LSCS, GDM (stated before medical factors).
7. **Medical Risk Factors**: e.g. Rheumatic heart disease, Anemia (state presence/absence of failure).

- **Master Diagnosis Example**:
    
    > _"26-year-old primigravida at 36 weeks of pregnancy in cephalic presentation with mild anemia not in failure, not in labor."_

### II. Summary of All Important Clinical Facts Mentioned

- Calculate BMI using pre-pregnancy weight (or 1st trimester weight if pre-pregnancy weight is unknown).
- Always refer to "Vital Signs" instead of "vitals".
- Blood pressure must be checked in the sitting position to prevent supine hypotensive syndrome.
- Male doctors/students must always have a female chaperone during examination.
- Patient's legs are semiflexed for all palpation steps EXCEPT Symphysio-Fundal Height (SFH) measurement, where legs are extended.
- Uterus becomes an abdominal organ at 12 weeks.
- Fundal height falls back to the 32-week level after 36 weeks due to head engagement ("lightening").
- Always correct dextrorotation with the right hand before estimating fundal height.
- Turn measuring tape with inches facing examiner during SFH measurement to avoid observer bias.
- Leopold maneuvers are performed ONLY at ≥28 weeks gestation.
- Leopold 1, 2, and 3 face the patient's face; Leopold 4 faces the patient's feet.
- FHR is audible at 11–12 weeks by Doppler and 18–20 weeks by Pinard fetoscope / stethoscope.
- Final diagnosis must state gestational age by LMP (not by examination).

### III. All Clinical Cases & Examples Discussed

- **Standard Antenatal Patient**: Pregnant female undergoing routine physical examination.
- **Term Pregnancy Case**: Female at 36+ weeks with engaged head and fullness in flanks.
- **Mid-Trimester Pregnancy Case**: Female at 20–24 weeks (no Leopold maneuvers recorded).
- **Previous Cesarean Section Case**: Patient evaluated for scar tenderness over lower uterine segment.
- **Multifetal Gestation / Polyhydramnios Case**: Patient presenting with everted umbilicus, fluid thrill, or multiple fetal poles.
- **Master Diagnosis Case Example**: 26-year-old primigravida at 36 weeks in cephalic presentation with mild anemia not in failure, not in labor.

### IV. All Important Numbers, Cut-off Values, and Diagnostic Criteria

- **β-hCG Thresholds**: >25 mIU/mL (UPT positive); >1500 mIU/mL (TVS gestational sac at 5 weeks).
- **1st Trimester Weight Gain**: 0.5 kg to 1 kg.
- **Resting Period Before Vital Signs**: 15–20 minutes.
- **Gestational Fundal Landmarks**: 12 weeks (pubic symphysis), 16 weeks (midway), 24 weeks (umbilicus), 36 weeks (xiphisternum).
- **Leopold Maneuver Cut-off**: ≥28 weeks gestation.
- **Fetal Heart Rate Detection**: 11–12 weeks (Doppler), 18–20 weeks (Fetoscope/Stethoscope).
- **Johnson's Formula Constant**: \(n = 12\) (unengaged head), \(n = 11\) (engaged head).
- **Term Gestation Cut-off**: 37 completed weeks.
