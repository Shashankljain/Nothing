## 2. Chronological Viva Discussion: Mentor Questions, Student Answers & Mentor Corrections

### **A. First Trimester History & Ultrasound Surveillance**

#### **1. Routine Ultrasound Scans in First Trimester**

- **Mentor Question:** What do you see in the first-trimester scan? Is it mandatory for all patients?
- **Student Answer:** Performs an NT (Nuchal Translucency) scan, as well as a dating scan, viability scan at 5–6 weeks, and a check scan at 5–6 weeks.
- **Mentor Correction & Explanation:**
    - **Over-exposure warning:** Stated that there is no need to expose the patient to multiple early scans (such as separate "check" and "viability" scans). A single early pregnancy ultrasound around 7 to 8 weeks is sufficient for an uneventful pregnancy.
    - **Indications for Early Ultrasound (<7–8 weeks):** Strictly indicated only if the patient has vaginal bleeding, severe pain, prior ectopic pregnancy, prior molar pregnancy, or recurrent miscarriages.
    - **Aims of First-Trimester Scan:**
        1. Confirm intrauterine pregnancy (gestational sac inside uterus; rule out ectopic pregnancy).
        2. Confirm live gestation by visualizing the fetal pole and cardiac activity.
        3. Differentiate single vs. twin/multiple gestation.
        4. Evaluate choriodecidual reaction and rule out subchorionic hemorrhage.
        5. _(Note on Molar Pregnancy)_: Molar pregnancy cannot be reliably diagnosed at 5–6 weeks; it becomes clearer as pregnancy advances.
    - **Ultrasound & Beta-hCG Timeline Milestones:**
        - **\(\mathbf{\beta}\)-hCG Cutoff:** When \(\beta\)-hCG exceeds **1,500 mIU/mL**, a gestational sac **must**be visible on Transvaginal Sonography (TVS).
        - **5 Weeks:** Gestational sac visible on TVS.
        - **5 Weeks + 5 Days:** Yolk sac appears.
        - **6 Weeks:** Fetal pole appears.
        - **6 Weeks + 2 Days:** Cardiac activity appears.
        - **7 Weeks:** Amniotic sac forms.
        - **8 Weeks:** Ideal timing for early dating scan in uneventful pregnancy; fetal pole, cardiac activity (measured via M-mode), and accurate pregnancy dating are established.

#### **2. Screening for Down Syndrome & Aneuploidies**

- **Mentor Question:** When do you perform the NT scan, and what are you ruling out?
- **Student Answer:** Done between **11 to 13 weeks + 6 days** to rule out Down syndrome and anencephaly.
- **Mentor Correction & Explanation:**
    - **NT Scan Limitations:** NT scan alone screens for Down syndrome but cannot confirm diagnosis and lacks high sensitivity when used in isolation.
    - **Combined Screening Protocols:**
        - **First Trimester:** Combine NT scan with **Double Marker** serum testing.
        - **Second Trimester:** Perform **Quadruple Marker** testing.
        - **Targeted Trisomies:** Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), Trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome), and Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome).

### **B. Second Trimester History, Physical Examination & Clinical Protocols**

#### **1. Role of Quickening**

- **Mentor Question:** What is the clinical significance of quickening?
- **Student Answer:** Indicates fetal development and viability.
- **Mentor Correction & Explanation:**
    - **IMPORTANT:** Quickening is a key **surrogate parameter to assess/confirm gestational age**, especially where early dating scans are unavailable.
    - **Timing of Quickening:**
        - **Primigravida:** Typically felt at **18 weeks**.
        - **Multigravida:** Typically felt 2 weeks earlier, around **16 weeks**.

#### **2. Second Trimester Anomaly Scan (Level II / TIFFA)**

- **Mentor Question:** Which ultrasound scan is ordered in the second trimester, and what is the exact timing?
- **Student Answer:** Targeted Anomaly Scan (Level II) performed between **18 to 20 weeks**.
- **Mentor Question:** Why not before 18 weeks, and why not after 20 weeks?
- **Student Answer:** Student was unsure of the cut-off reasons.
- **Mentor Explanation:**
    - **Why not before 18 weeks:** Although organogenesis finishes by 12 weeks, organs must grow adequately in size for structural anomalies to be visualized clearly on ultrasound.
    - **Why not after 20 weeks:** Legally under the MTP (Medical Termination of Pregnancy) Act (as quoted in the session), legal termination for severe congenital anomalies was capped at 20 weeks, making 18–20 weeks the optimal therapeutic window.

#### **3. Tetanus Immunization (TT / Td)**

- **Mentor Question:** What is the schedule for Tetanus Toxoid (TT) immunization in pregnancy?
- **Student Answer:**
    - If the patient received TT in a previous pregnancy within the past 3 years: Give **1 booster dose**.
    - If primigravida (first pregnancy): Give **2 doses, 4 weeks apart**.
- **Mentor Evaluation:** Student answered correctly.

#### **4. Blood Pressure & Weight Gain Evaluation**

- **Mentor Question:** How should blood pressure (BP) be checked, and what are the weight gain guidelines?
- **Student Answer:** Patient BP was 130/70 mmHg, pulse rate 110 bpm, pre-pregnancy weight 68 kg, BMI 24.98 kg/m².
- **Mentor Correction & Clinical Pearls:**
    - **Terminology:** Always present as **"Vital Signs"**, never shorten to "vitals" in exams.
    - **BP Measurement Protocol:** Blood pressure normally falls in the second trimester. Measure after **15–20 minutes of rest**, with the patient sitting, **back supported**, and **feet flat on the floor** (avoid supine position to prevent supine hypotension syndrome).
    - **Weight Gain Guidelines:**
        - **First Trimester:** Minimal to no weight gain expected (0.8 kg is normal).
        - **Total Ideal Weight Gain:** **11 to 12.5 kg** across pregnancy.
        - **Clinical Risk:** Excessive weight gain starting in the second trimester points toward risk of **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)** or **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (PIH)**. Pre-pregnancy weight also helps calculate IV sucrose/iron requirements.

#### **5. Abdominal Examination & Auscultation**

- **Mentor Question:** What are the Obstetric Grips (Leopold's Maneuvers), and when can fetal heart sounds (FHS) be heard?
- **Student Answer:** Listed grips: Fundal grip, Lateral grip, First Pelvic grip, Second Pelvic grip. Did not measure fundal height in the written presentation and did not auscultate FHS with a stethoscope.
- **Mentor Correction:**

    - **Auscultation Timing:**

### **C. Core Disease Discussion: Definitions, Overt vs. Gestational Diabetes**

#### **1. Definitions & The Definition Fallacy**

- **Mentor Question:** Define Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM).
- **Student Answer:** Carbohydrate intolerance of variable severity with onset or first recognition during the present pregnancy.
- **Mentor Correction & Explanation:**
    - **Definition Fallacy:** By current formal definition, any diabetes recognized for the first time during pregnancy is classified as GDM. However, this creates a fallacy because an **overt diabetic** who missed pre-conceptional screening will be mislabeled as GDM if diagnosed in the first trimester.
    - **Pathophysiologic Distinction:** True GDM arises in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters due to placental anti-insulin hormones. GDM is analogous to **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT)** in non-pregnant individuals, whereas Overt Diabetes is equivalent to frank Diabetes Mellitus.

#### **2. Diagnostic Cut-offs: Overt Diabetes vs. GDM**

|Parameter|Overt / Pre-gestational Diabetes|Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)**|\(\ge 126\text{ mg/dL}\)|\(92\text{ to }125\text{ mg/dL}\) (IADPSG) / \(\ge 140\text{ mg/dL}\) (DIPSI non-fasting)|
|**Random Blood Sugar (RBS)**|\(\ge 200\text{ mg/dL}\) (with symptoms)|\(< 200\text{ mg/dL}\)|
|**HbA1c**|\(\ge 6.5%\)|\(< 6.5%\)|
|**Pathophysiology**|Pre-existing insulin deficiency/resistance prior to conception|Pregnancy-induced insulin resistance appearing in 2nd/3rd trimester|

#### **3. Clinical Consequences & Congenital Anomalies**

- **Mentor Question:** Why is it critical to differentiate Overt Diabetes from GDM?
- **Student Answer:**
    - **Management:** Overt DM requires immediate insulin; GDM starts with a 2-week trial of Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT).
    - **Congenital Anomalies:** Congenital malformations occur predominantly in Overt Diabetes because hyperglycemia and free radical formation exist during early organogenesis (first 8–12 weeks). In true GDM, organogenesis is complete before hyperglycemia develops.
- **Congenital Anomaly Profile in Overt DM:**
    - **Most Common Organ System Involved:** Cardiovascular System (CVS).
    - **Most Common Cardiac Anomalies:** Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Atrial Septal Defect (ASD).
    - **MOST SPECIFIC Cardiac Anomaly:** **Transposition of Great Vessels (TGV)**.
    - **MOST SPECIFIC Overall Anomaly:** **Caudal Regression Syndrome** (sacral agenesis).
    - **Other Defects:** Neural Tube Defects (NTD), anencephaly.
- **Pre-conception Counseling & HbA1c Thresholds:**
    - Target Pre-conception HbA1c: **\(< 5.7%\)** (ideal) or \(< 6.5%\).
    - **CRITICAL PEARL:** If a pregnant diabetic presents with **HbA1c \(> 10.5%\)** in the first trimester, the risk of severe congenital malformations is extremely high; the patient must be counseled and offered the option of Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP).

#### **4. Target Organ Monitoring in Overt DM**

- Pregnancy is a diabetogenic state that **accelerates pre-existing microvascular complications**.
- **Monitoring Protocols for Overt DM (Every Trimester):**
    1. **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Fundoscopic / Endoscopic eye examination in every trimester. Severe, active retinopathy in the 1st trimester is an indication for MTP.
    2. **Diabetic Nephropathy:** Monitor renal parameters each trimester via **Serum Creatinine** and **Urine Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio (UACR)**.
    3. **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** Higher risk during pregnancy; monitor if blood sugars are severely elevated.

### **E. Comprehensive Management of GDM**

```
                         [ GDM Diagnosed ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                     Trial of MNT & Exercise
                      (Duration: 2 Weeks)
                                 │
               ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
               ▼                                   ▼
   Targets Achieved                   Targets NOT Achieved
 (FBS <95, 2-hr PP <120)            (FBS ≥95, 2-hr PP ≥120)
               │                                   │
               ▼                                   ▼
      Continue MNT Alone                Initiate Pharmacotherapy
                                          (Insulin Preferred)
```

#### **1. Step 1: Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) & Exercise**

- **MNT Trial Duration:** **2 weeks**.
- **Diet Pattern:** Small, split, frequent meals.
- **Exercise Prescription:** **150 minutes per week** (30 minutes/day for 5 days/week) of brisk walking **after meals**(walking before meals risks hypoglycemia).
- **Target Blood Sugar Goals on MNT:**
    - **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS):** \(< 95\text{ mg/dL}\).
    - **1-Hour Postprandial (PPBS):** \(< 140\text{ mg/dL}\).
    - **2-Hour Postprandial (PPBS):** \(< 120\text{ mg/dL}\).

#### **2. Step 2: Pharmacotherapy (Oral Hypoglycemics vs. Insulin)**

- **Role of Oral Hypoglycemic Agents (OHAs - Metformin / Glibenclamide):**
    - _Guidelines Status:_ Metformin and Glibenclamide are safe in short-term studies, but **ACOG does not recommend them as first-line therapy**.
    - **Reason:** OHAs **cross the placenta**. Long-term offspring safety data (over 7–12 years) is lacking.
- **Drug of Choice:** **INSULIN**. Insulin does **not** cross the placenta, eliminating direct fetal risk.

#### **3. Insulin Regimens & Titration Protocols**

- **Mixtard (30/70) Drawback:** Mixtard contains a fixed combination of intermediate and short-acting insulin. It prevents independent dose titration for individual mealtime spikes.
- **Preferred Insulin Regimen (Basal-Bolus / Split-Mixed):**
    - **Short-acting / Regular Human Insulin (Actrapid):** Administered 15 minutes before each meal (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner) to control postprandial sugars. Starting dose: **2 to 4 units** per meal.
    - **Intermediate/Long-acting Insulin (NPH / Glargine):** Administered at **bedtime** to control fasting hyperglycemia the following morning.
- **Blood Glucose Monitoring Frequency:**
    - **During Titration:** 4 times daily (Fasting + 2-hr post-breakfast, post-lunch, post-dinner).
    - **Once Controlled:** Fasting + 1 rotated postprandial reading daily, with weekly/biweekly clinical review.

### **F. Fetal & Maternal Surveillance**

#### **1. Fetal Complications in GDM**

- **Antenatal / Intrapartum:** Fetal Macrosomia (\(>4000\text{ g}\) or \(>4500\text{ g}\)), Shoulder Dystocia, Erb's Palsy, Unexplained Stillbirth / Sudden IUD.
- **Neonatal:**
    - **Neonatal Hypoglycemia** (due to intrauterine fetal hyperinsulinemia responding to maternal hyperglycemia — **Pederson Hypothesis**).
    - **Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS):** Hyperinsulinemia delays surfactant synthesis (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine) in fetal lungs.
    - **Polycythemia & Hyperbilirubinemia**.

#### **2. Prevention of Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)**

- Administer antenatal corticosteroids (**Betamethasone / Dexamethasone**) after **28 weeks** if preterm delivery is anticipated.
- **IMPORTANT:** Corticosteroids significantly elevate maternal blood sugars. Insulin dosage must be actively titrated upward under strict glucose monitoring during steroid coverage.

#### **3. Antepartum Fetal Surveillance Schedule**

1. **First Trimester:** Aneuploidy screening (NT + Double marker).
2. **18–20 Weeks:** Targeted Anomaly Scan (Level II).
3. **20–24 Weeks:** **Fetal Echocardiography** (mandatory to rule out cardiac anomalies).
4. **32 Weeks Onward (Core Surveillance):**
    - **Daily Fetal Movement Count (DFMC / Kick Chart):** Universal recommendation.
    - **Non-Stress Test (NST):** Weekly/biweekly starting at 32 weeks.
    - **Growth Ultrasound:** Every 4 weeks to monitor fetal growth velocity and detect macrosomia or IUGR.

### **G. Timing & Mode of Delivery**

#### **ACOG Delivery Timing Guidelines**

|Clinical Scenario|Recommended Timing of Delivery|
|:--|:--|
|**GDM on MNT with Good Glycemic Control**|**39+0 to 41+0 weeks** (Manage like normal pregnancy)|
|**GDM on Insulin with Good Glycemic Control**|**39+0 to 40+0 weeks**|
|**GDM with Poor Glycemic Control**|**Early Term (37+0 to 38+6 weeks)**|
|**GDM with Co-morbidities** (Preeclampsia, prior stillbirth, vascular disease)|**Late Preterm (34+0 to 36+6 weeks)**|

- **Indication for Primary Elective Cesarean Section:** Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW) **\(\ge 4,500\text{ grams}\)** (or \(\ge 4,000\text{ g}\) with clinical pelvic narrowing) to prevent shoulder dystocia and brachial plexus injury.

#### **Intrapartum Glucose Management Protocol**

- Omit the morning short-acting insulin dose on the day of induction/labor.
- Usual bedtime intermediate/long-acting insulin dose is taken the night before.
- Maintain continuous hourly intrapartum capillary blood glucose monitoring (\(80\text{--}110\text{ mg/dL}\)) using a regular insulin and dextrose infusion protocol.
- Monitor urine for ketone bodies and serum electrolytes.

### **H. Postpartum & Neonatal Care**

#### **1. Postpartum Maternal Management**

- **Immediate Postpartum:** Discontinue MNT and insulin immediately after delivery of the placenta (placental expulsion removes Human Placental Lactogen and progesterone, reversing insulin resistance).
- **Day 3 Postpartum:** Check Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS) and Postprandial Blood Sugar (PPBS).
- **6 Weeks Postpartum:** Perform a **75g 2-hour OGTT** to reclassify glycemic status (Normal, Impaired Glucose Tolerance, or Type 2 Diabetes).
- **Long-term Follow-up:** Annual Fasting Blood Sugar, and repeat **75g OGTT every 3 years**.

#### **2. Neonatal Management Protocol**

- **Initiate Breastfeeding:** Within **30 minutes of birth** to prevent neonatal hypoglycemia.
- **Cord Clamping:** Perform **early cord clamping** in macrosomic neonates to prevent polycythemia and subsequent hyperbilirubinemia.
- **Vitamin K:** Administer **1 mg Vitamin K IM** universally.
- **Monitoring:** Continuous blood glucose monitoring for early detection of asymptomatic hypoglycemia.

---

### **I. Pathophysiology Note: Why Pregnancy is a Diabetogenic State**

- **Progesterone:** Directly increases maternal tissue insulin resistance.
- **Human Placental Lactogen (HPL):** Secreted by the placenta; acts as a potent anti-insulin hormone, diverting glucose to the developing fetus.
