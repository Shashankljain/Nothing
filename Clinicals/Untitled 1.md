# HYDROCELE – CLINICAL CASE PRESENTATION

> [!info] Case at a glance
> **Patient:** Ramlal, 40-year-old male  
> **Occupation:** Handyman  
> **Chief complaint:** Right-sided scrotal swelling  
> **Duration:** 2 years  
> **Final diagnosis:** Uncomplicated primary right-sided hydrocele

---

# 1. HISTORY

## 1.1 History of Presenting Illness

A 40-year-old male presented with swelling over the right side of the scrotum for 2 years.

- Patient was apparently normal 2 years ago.
- Initially noticed a small swelling accidentally on the right side of the scrotum.
- Swelling gradually increased in size.
- It subsequently attained its present size and remained unchanged.

### Important points in the history

| Feature | History |
|---|---|
| Onset | Gradual |
| Duration | 2 years |
| Progression | Gradually increased, then became stable |
| Increase on straining | Absent |
| Reduction on lying down | Absent |
| Reduction on compression | Absent |
| Rapid increase in size | Absent |

> [!important] Clinical significance
> A long-standing, gradually progressive, painless scrotal swelling that does not reduce on lying down or compression suggests hydrocele.

## 1.2 Differential Diagnosis Based on Change in Size

**Question: Which scrotal swellings decrease in size in the supine position?**

| Condition | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Inguinal hernia | Rapid reduction |
| Varicocele | Reduces on lying down |
| Lymphovarix | May reduce on lying down or compression |
| Congenital hydrocele | Slowly reduces over minutes or hours |
| Testicular tumour | Does not reduce |

Other swellings that may decrease on lying down:
- Umbilical hernia
- Ventral hernia
- Incisional/postoperative hernia
- Varicose veins of the lower limbs

## 1.3 History of Associated Symptoms and Complications

No history of:
- Pain
- Fever
- Vesicles or discharge from the scrotal skin
- Ulcers or sinuses/fistulae
- Difficulty in urination
- Difficulty during sexual intercourse

No history of trauma.

> [!tip] Why ask about trauma?
> Trauma may cause a hematocele. It may also simply draw the patient's attention to a pre-existing swelling.

## 1.4 History to Rule Out Filariasis

No history of fever with chills associated with pain and swelling.

No history of swelling of the feet.

### How does filariasis cause scrotal swelling?

Three important mechanisms:

1. **Hydrocele and chylocele:** Accumulation of fluid in the tunica vaginalis.
2. **Lymphovarix and lymphoedema:** Dilatation of lymphatic vessels and lymphatic obstruction.
3. **Elephantiasis:** Late-stage thickening and enlargement of scrotal skin.

> [!important] Remember
> Filariasis can produce:
> - Hydrocele
> - Chylocele
> - Lymphovarix
> - Scrotal lymphoedema
> - Scrotal elephantiasis

## 1.5 History to Rule Out Sexually Transmitted Infections

No history of:
- Exposure to sexually transmitted diseases
- Burning during urination
- White urethral discharge

### Differential diagnosis

**Gonococcal epididymo-orchitis**
- Usually associated with a history suggestive of sexually transmitted infection.
- May present with pain and inflammatory symptoms.
- Less likely in this patient because the swelling is painless and long-standing.

**Tertiary syphilis**
- Testicular gumma may cause testicular/scrotal swelling.
- This is a rare presentation.

## 1.6 History to Rule Out Tuberculosis

The discussion covers both renal and pulmonary tuberculosis.

### A. Renal tuberculosis

Ask for:
- Increased frequency of urination
- Pyuria
- Haematuria
- Dysuria
- Joint pain

### B. Pulmonary tuberculosis

Ask for:
- Cough with expectoration
- Haemoptysis
- Evening rise of temperature
- Sweating
- Loss of appetite
- Weight loss
- Contact with a person suffering from tuberculosis

### Why ask about pulmonary tuberculosis in a case of scrotal swelling?

> [!important] Viva point
> Genitourinary tuberculosis is usually secondary to a primary focus elsewhere in the body, commonly the lungs.

### How does tuberculosis cause scrotal swelling?

- Nodular enlargement of the epididymis
- Secondary hydrocele
- Thickening and induration of the spermatic cord

## 1.7 History to Rule Out Malignancy

No history of:
- Rapid weight loss
- Upper abdominal swelling or mass

These questions are asked to consider the possibility of a malignant testicular tumour.

### Possible testicular tumours discussed

| Tumour | Age group mentioned |
|---|---|
| Seminoma | Around 40 years |
| Teratoma | Younger age group, approximately 20–30 years |

The mentor discusses seminoma as a likely possibility in a patient around 40 years of age.

## 1.8 Past, Personal and Family History

- No history of previous aspiration or puncture of the swelling.
- No history of surgery related to the swelling.
- Personal history: No additional relevant information.
- Family history: No additional relevant information.

# 2. LOCAL EXAMINATION

## 2.1 Position of the Patient

Examine the patient in the **standing position**.

### Why examine in the standing position?

1. To observe whether the swelling increases in size.
2. To detect an associated hernia.
3. To detect varicocele, which may be missed in the supine position.
4. To assess the full size of a hernia.

> [!tip] Clinical pearl
> Always examine a scrotal swelling in both standing and supine positions when appropriate.

## 2.2 Inspection

### A. Number and Site

- Single swelling
- Present on the right side of the scrotum

### B. Size

**10 × 8 × 5 cm**

> [!important] Examination point
> Always mention the swelling in three dimensions whenever possible.

### C. Shape and Surface

- Well-defined
- Spherical
- Smooth surface
- Slight constriction in the middle

### D. Nodule on the Surface

**Question: What would a nodule on the surface suggest?**

| Consistency of nodule | Possibility discussed |
|---|---|
| Hard | Malignant testicular tumour |
| Soft and cystic | Hydrocele hernia |

### E. Water-sac Hernia

The discussion describes a condition occurring in prolonged and severe hydrocele.

- Atrophy of supporting tissues may occur.
- The vaginal sac may protrude due to defects involving the cremasteric muscle and coverings.
- The sac may become covered mainly by skin.

This is referred to in the discussion as **water-sac hernia**.

## 2.3 Inspection of Scrotal Skin

The skin over the swelling showed reduced wrinkling.

No:
- Skin thickening
- Redness
- Oedema
- Vesicles
- Ulcers
- Sinuses or fistulae
- Scars of previous surgery or infection

### Clinical significance of skin changes

| Finding | Association discussed |
|---|---|
| Reduced or absent wrinkling | Tuberculous epididymitis |
| Increased wrinkling | Filarial scrotal oedema |
| Scrotal oedema | Filariasis, anasarca, urine extravasation, infection |

### Causes of scrotal oedema

1. Filarial elephantiasis
2. Generalised anasarca
3. Extravasation of urine
4. Oedema around an infective focus

### Position of sinus and ulcer

> [!important] High-yield clinical point
> - **Posterior sinus:** Classically associated with tuberculous epididymitis.
> - **Anterior ulcer:** May occur following rupture of a testicular gumma.

**Can tuberculosis produce an anterior sinus?**

Yes.

- In approximately 7–8% of men, the testis may be displaced anteriorly.
- The epididymis then lies anteriorly.
- A tuberculous sinus arising from the epididymis may therefore open on the anterior surface.
- This can be assessed by palpating the opposite testis.

### Causes of skin excoriation in large hydrocele

1. **Buried penis:** The penis sinks into the enlarged scrotum. The patient cannot direct the urinary stream, causing urine to flow over the scrotal skin.
2. **Friction:** Scrotal skin rubs against the medial aspect of the thighs while walking.
3. **Irritating applications:** Application of irritating herbal remedies may damage the skin.

## 2.4 Cough Impulse

- No visible expansile impulse over the swelling.
- No expansile impulse over the inguinal canal.

This helps assess an associated inguinal hernia.

# 3. PALPATION

> [!warning] Examination sequence
> Always assess **local temperature and tenderness first** during palpation.

## 3.1 Local Temperature and Tenderness

- No local rise in temperature.
- No tenderness.

## 3.2 Upper Border of the Swelling

**Finding:** Upper border can be reached.

### How is this examined?

- Place the thumb anteriorly.
- Place the remaining fingers posteriorly.
- Palpate the upper limit of the swelling.

Only the spermatic cord structures are felt below the external inguinal ring.

### Structures of the spermatic cord

| Structure | Feel |
|---|---|
| Vas deferens | Firm, whip-like structure |
| Cremasteric muscle fibres | Thread-like structures |

> [!important] What does reaching the upper border indicate?
> The swelling is confined to the scrotum and is not extending into the inguinal canal.

## 3.3 Surface and Consistency

- Well-defined
- Smooth surface
- Cystic consistency

### Differential diagnosis of cystic scrotal swellings

1. Hydrocele
2. Chylocele
3. Recent hematocele
4. Spermatocele
5. Epididymal cyst
6. Hydatid cyst of Morgagni
7. Cystic areas within a teratoma

## 3.4 Reducibility vs Compressibility

**Question: What is the difference between reducibility and compressibility?**

| Feature | Reducibility | Compressibility |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Swelling decreases in size on pressure | Swelling decreases in size on pressure |
| After pressure is released | Does not spontaneously regain its size | Spontaneously regains its size |
| Mechanism discussed | Displacement of contents/viscera | Emptying and refilling of fluid or blood |

### Swellings showing compressibility

- Cavernous haemangioma
- Lymphangioma
- Narrow-necked meningocele
- Capillary haemangioma

> [!tip] Remember
> Reducibility generally involves displacement of contents, whereas compressibility involves emptying of fluid or blood from the swelling.

## 3.5 Fluctuation Test

### Definition

Fluctuation is the presence of a transmitted impulse in **two planes at right angles to each other**.

**What does a positive fluctuation test indicate?**

It indicates that the swelling contains fluid.

### Method

1. First, fix the swelling.
2. Stabilise it using the ring and middle fingers of both hands.
3. Place the thumbs and index fingers at the upper and lower poles.
4. Press one finger at a time.
5. Feel for the transmitted impulse with the diagonally opposite finger.

### Why should the swelling be fixed?

The scrotum is freely movable.

If it is not stabilised, movement of the entire swelling may produce a false impression of fluctuation.

> [!important] Key point
> Fix the swelling before performing fluctuation to avoid a false-positive result due to movement of the scrotum.

# 4. TRANSILLUMINATION / DIAPHANOSCOPY

## 4.1 Method

1. Pull the scrotal skin over the swelling with one hand.
2. Use a flashlight with the other hand.
3. First place the flashlight against the normal side to assess normal skin translucency.
4. Then place it against the lateral surface of the swelling.
5. Observe the transmitted glow.

**Finding in this case:** A red glow was seen throughout the swelling.

This indicates that the swelling is translucent.

## 4.2 Why Examine the Normal Side First?

Normal skin translucency varies according to:
- Skin thickness
- Power of the flashlight
- Lighting in the examination room

Thin skin or a powerful flashlight may produce a false impression of positive transillumination.

## 4.3 What if the Glow Is Doubtful?

- Perform the test in a completely dark room.
- Use rolled black paper to observe the glow.

The mentor specifically mentions using black paper during an examination demonstration.

## 4.4 Interpretation

A positive transillumination test suggests:
- Clear fluid within the swelling.
- No significant thickening of the sac wall.

In this case, the finding supports hydrocele.

## 4.5 False-negative Transillumination

A hydrocele may fail to transilluminate when:

1. The fluid is cloudy.
2. The sac wall is thickened due to infection.
3. The sac is calcified.
4. Scrotal elephantiasis is present.
5. The examination is performed in a brightly lit room.
6. The flashlight is placed behind the swelling, allowing the testis to block the light.

## 4.6 False-positive Transillumination

May occur in:

- Congenital hernia in infants due to thin-walled intestinal contents and thin skin.
- Individuals with fair, thin skin, where normal skin translucency may be mistaken for a positive result.

## 4.7 Transillumination in Epididymal Cysts

| Appearance | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Single cyst | Brightly translucent |
| Multiple small cysts | Mosaic appearance |

The mosaic appearance is called the **Chinese lantern appearance**.

## 4.8 Scrotal Swellings That Transilluminate

- Hydrocele
- Epididymal cyst
- Hydatid of Morgagni
- Sometimes spermatocele

### Other swellings in the body that may transilluminate

- Thin-walled hydrocele
- Epididymal cyst
- Cystic hygroma of the neck
- Ranula / cystic swelling in the floor of the mouth
- Thin-walled meningocele

> [!important] Exam highlight
> A positive transillumination test supports a fluid-filled swelling, but it is not exclusive to hydrocele.

## Why is this diagnosed as hydrocele?

The diagnosis is supported by:

1. Long-standing swelling.
2. Painless nature of the swelling.
3. Swelling surrounding the testis.
4. Cystic consistency.
5. Positive fluctuation.
6. Positive transillumination.

> [!important] One-line viva answer
> A persistent, painless scrotal swelling surrounding the testis, which is cystic, fluctuant and translucent, is suggestive of hydrocele.

# 9. HYDROCELE – DEFINITION AND CONTENTS

## 9.1 Definition

A hydrocele is an abnormal accumulation of fluid in a part of the processus vaginalis, usually within the tunica vaginalis.

When fluid accumulates in the tunica vaginalis, it is called a **vaginal hydrocele**.

## 9.2 Contents of Hydrocele Fluid

According to the discussion, the fluid contains:

- Water and inorganic salts
- Proteins, including approximately 6% albumin and fibrinogen
- Cholesterol crystals
- Sometimes tyrosine crystals

## 9.3 Characteristics of Hydrocele Fluid

| Characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| Colour | Clear, pale yellow or amber |
| Appearance | Shimmering |
| Reason for shimmer | Cholesterol crystals |
| Coagulation | Coagulates when a drop of blood is added |
| Reason for coagulation | Fibrinogen content |

# 11. TREATMENT

## 11.1 Why Operate?

Surgery is suggested because:
- The swelling causes discomfort.
- An untreated hydrocele may develop complications.

## 11.2 Complications of Hydrocele

1. Massive enlargement of the swelling.
2. Concealment of the penis.
3. Difficulty in urination.
4. Difficulty during sexual intercourse.
5. Recurrent balanoposthitis.
6. Skin excoriation.
7. Haemorrhage.
8. Haematoma formation.
9. Calcification of the sac wall.
10. Rupture.
11. Rarely, testicular atrophy.

> [!danger] Clinical highlight
> A very large hydrocele may bury the penis within the scrotum, resulting in urinary difficulty, sexual difficulty and recurrent skin complications.

## 11.3 Surgical Procedures for Hydrocele

The procedure depends on the size and thickness of the sac.

| Type of sac | Procedure mentioned |
|---|---|
| Thin and small sac | Simple eversion |
| Thin and small sac | Lord's plication |
| Large sac | Subdartos pocket procedure (name in transcript needs verification) |
| Large sac | Jaboulay's operation – partial excision and eversion |
| Very thick sac | Subtotal excision with suturing of edges for haemostasis |

### A. Simple Eversion

- Eversion of the sac.

### B. Lord's Plication

- Plication of the sac.

### C. Jaboulay's Operation

- Partial excision of the sac.
- Eversion of the remaining sac.

### D. Very Thick Sac

- Subtotal excision.
- Suturing of the edges to achieve haemostasis.

> [!warning] Term to verify from the original audio
> The transcript renders one procedure for a large sac as “Sharma and Zero” / “subdartos pocket”. The exact eponym should be checked against the original audio or textbook before memorising it.

## 11.4 Recurrence After Surgery

**Question: What will you do if hydrocele recurs several years after surgery?**

The student suggests suspecting another underlying condition because a vaginal hydrocele is not expected to recur after successful eversion of the sac.

The mentor agrees with this response.
