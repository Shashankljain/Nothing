### Integrated Viva: History Summary Rules & Anatomical Localization of Respiratory Lesions

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Mayor & Dr. Deepthi]**
> 
> How should functional dyspnea be reported during history taking, and how do you clinically localize a respiratory disease to the airways versus parenchyma versus interstitium based on history?

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Neha]**
> 
> - **Reporting Dyspnea**: I reported the specific physical activities impaired (climbing 1 floor, hurrying on level ground, walking 100 m) rather than stating a grade.
> - **Anatomical Localization**: Parenchymal disease (like pneumonia) presents with larger volumes of purulent sputum and predominant cough over breathlessness. Interstitial disease presents with dry cough and progressive breathlessness.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Teaching Points [Dr. Mayor & Dr. Deepthi]**
> 
> - **Functional Activity Reporting**: Always describe the exact physical activity impaired during history taking. Do NOT mention mMRC or NYHA grades in HPI because you do not yet know if the cause is cardiac or respiratory.
> - **Airway vs. Parenchymal vs. Interstitial vs. Vascular Localization**:
>     - _Airway Disease_: Associated with wheezing or stridor, rhonchi, and fixed/reversible airflow obstruction.
>     - _Parenchymal Disease_: Large volume productive sputum, consolidation, or focal lung signs.
>     - _Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)_: Predominantly dry cough, progressive exertional breathlessness, and fine late-inspiratory cellophane crepitations.
>     - _Occupational / Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis (HP)_: Tailors and cleaners exposed to organic textile dusts can develop **Byssinosis** or Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis, presenting with exertional dyspnea and cough.
> - **Summary Rule**: Do NOT jump to a definitive diagnosis of COPD at the end of history if the onset sequence is atypical (e.g., dyspnea for 1 year, but cough for only 3 months). Keep your mind open to differentials such as ILD, Tuberculosis, Lung Malignancy, and Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis.

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Deepthi]**
> 
> What pulse and blood pressure abnormalities occur in respiratory failure and COPD?

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Neha]**
> 
> - **Type 2 Respiratory Failure (CO₂ Retention)**: Causes a **high-volume pulse**, wide pulse pressure, and fine/flapping tremors (asterixis) due to CO₂-induced peripheral vasodilation.
> - **Irregular Pulse**: Indicates **Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT)** (common in severe COPD) or Atrial Fibrillation.
> - **Blood Pressure**: Type 1 respiratory failure can cause hypotension.
> - **Pulsus Paradoxus**: Exaggerated fall in systolic BP (`>10 mmHg`) during inspiration, seen in **Acute Exacerbation of COPD** and **Status Asthmaticus**.

## 8. Diagnostic Workup & Spirometry Protocols

### Diagnostic Workup Protocol

```
                        DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP FOR COPD
                                     │
     ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                               ▼                               ▼
1. Spirometry (Gold Standard)   2. High-Resolution CT (HRCT)     3. Sputum Culture & X-Ray
   • Baseline FEV1/FVC <70%        • Emphysema vs. ILD overlap      • >25 PMNs, <10 epithelial cells
   • Post-bronchodilator test      • Bulla visualization            • Expiratory CXR (Pneumothorax)
```

1. **Spirometry with Bronchodilator Reversibility Testing (Gold Standard)**:
    - **Diagnostic Criterion**: **Post-bronchodilator FEV1 / FVC ratio <70% (<0.70)** confirms fixed, non-fully reversible airflow obstruction.
    - **Reversibility Testing**: Differentiates Asthma (significant reversibility: FEV1 increases by `>12%` AND `>200 mL` post-salbutamol) from COPD (fixed or partially reversible obstruction).
2. **GOLD Spirometric Severity Grading (Based on Post-Bronchodilator FEV1 % Predicted)**:
    - **GOLD 1 (Mild)**: FEV1 `≥80%` predicted.
    - **GOLD 2 (Moderate)**: `50% ≤ FEV1 <80%` predicted.
    - **GOLD 3 (Severe)**: `30% ≤ FEV1 <50%` predicted.
    - **GOLD 4 (Very Severe)**: FEV1 `<30%` predicted.
3. **Sputum Microscopy, Culture & Sensitivity**:
    - **Sputum Adequacy Criteria**: To confirm a valid lower respiratory tract sample (not saliva), the specimen MUST contain: \[\mathbf{>25 \text{ Neutrophils (PMNs)}} \quad \text{AND} \quad \mathbf{<10 \text{ Epithelial Cells per low-power field (LPF)}}\].
4. **Chest X-Ray Protocols (PA, Expiratory & Lateral Views)**:
    - **PA View Findings**: Hyperinflated lung fields, `>6` anterior ribs or `>10` posterior ribs visible above diaphragm, flattened hemidiaphragms, tubular vertical heart shadow, widened intercostal spaces, increased bronchovascular markings, and bulla shadow [34–36].
    - **Rib Counting Rule**: In a normal inspiratory CXR, the **6th anterior rib** intersects the midpoint of the right hemidiaphragm. If the **7th or 8th anterior rib** intersects it, hyperinflation is present.
    - **Objective Measurement of Diaphragmatic Flattening**: Draw a straight line connecting the costophrenic angle to the cardiophrenic angle. Drop a perpendicular line from the midline to the highest apex of the diaphragm. A perpendicular height **<1.5 cm** confirms flattening of the diaphragm.
    - **Expiratory Chest X-Ray**: **MANDATORY view to detect a small Pneumothorax**, which can be completely missed on an inspiratory film.
    - **Lateral View**: Visualizes bullae located behind the cardiac silhouette or diaphragm.
5. **High-Resolution Computed Tomography (HRCT Chest)**: Gold standard to differentiate centrilobular/panlobular emphysema, evaluate bullae, detect ILD overlap, and rule out underlying malignancy.
6. **Diffusing Capacity of the Lung for Carbon Monoxide (DLCO)**:
    - **Decreased DLCO**: Seen in **Emphysema** (destruction of alveolar-capillary beds) and **ILD**.
    - **Normal DLCO**: Seen in Chronic Bronchitis and Asthma.
7. **Specialized Biomarkers & Serology**:
    - **FeNO (Fractional Excretion of Nitric Oxide)**: Evaluates eosinophilic allergic airway inflammation (elevated in Asthma).
    - **Helium Dilution Technique**: Replaces outdated body plethysmography to measure Total Lung Capacity (TLC) and Residual Volume (RV).

### I. Smoking Cessation Protocols

- **Single Most Effective Intervention**: Smoking cessation is the ONLY intervention (alongside Long-Term Oxygen Therapy) that slows the accelerated decline of FEV1 in COPD.
- **Pharmacotherapy for Smoking Cessation**:
    1. **Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT)** (Gums, patches, inhalers).
    2. **Bupropion** (Atypical antidepressant).
    3. **Varenicline** (Partial nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist).

---

### II. GOLD ABCD / ABE Pharmacotherapy Guidelines

- **Group A (Low Symptoms, Low Risk)**: Single Bronchodilator (Short- or Long-acting LAMA / LABA).
- **Group B (High Symptoms, Low Risk)**: LAMA + LABA combination.
- **Group E / C / D (High Risk of Exacerbations, ≥2 exacerbations or 1 hospitalization)**:
    - **LAMA + LABA** combination.
    - **Add Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS)** (Triple Therapy: LAMA + LABA + ICS) if blood eosinophils are **≥300 cells/μL** or in patients with co-existing asthma features.
- **Vaccination**: Annual **Influenza Vaccine** and **Pneumococcal Vaccines** (PCV13 and PPSV23).

---

### III. Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT) Protocols

- **Indications for LTOT**:
    1. Resting **PaO₂ ≤55 mmHg** or **SaO₂ ≤88%** (on room air).
    2. Resting **PaO₂ 56 to 59 mmHg** or **SaO₂ 89%** with evidence of **Cor Pulmonale**, pulmonary hypertension (P2 loud), or secondary polycythemia (hematocrit `>55%`).
- **Administration Duration**: Must be administered for **>15 hours per day** (optimal clinical benefit achieved at **>20 hours per day**).
- **Target Oxygen Saturation**: Maintain SpO₂ between **88% and 92%** to avoid abolishing the hypoxic respiratory drive in chronic hypercapnic patients.

---

### IV. Surgical & Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV) Interventions

- **Surgical Interventions**:
    - **Bullectomy**: Surgical excision of a large bulla occupying `>33%` of a hemithorax compressing adjacent healthy lung tissue.
    - **Lung Volume Reduction Surgery (LVRS)**: Resection of hyperinflated emphysematous lung zones to improve diaphragmatic mechanics.
- **Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP) Protocol**:
    - **Indications**: Acute exacerbation with hypercapnic respiratory failure (PaCO₂ `>45–55 mmHg`, pH `<7.35`).
    - **CRITICAL CONTRAINDICATION WARNING**: Exercise extreme caution when initiating positive-pressure invasive or non-invasive ventilation in patients with **large pulmonary bullae**, as high airway pressures can rupture the bulla, causing a **tension pneumothorax**.

### II. Summary of All Important Clinical Facts Mentioned

- Low-grade fever relieved by medication should be reported as "present on and off", not "continuous".
- Harrison's cough classification defines acute as `<3 weeks`, subacute as `3 to 8 weeks`, and chronic as `>8 weeks`.

- Functional activity impairment must be described in HPI rather than stating mMRC/NYHA grades.
- Smoking Index = Cigarettes/day × Years; values `>400` indicate high risk for COPD and carcinoma.
- Digital clubbing is NOT a feature of uncomplicated COPD; its presence indicates ILD, Lung Carcinoma, or Bronchiectasis.
- Smoking-associated ILDs include RB-ILD, DIP, and IPF.
- Type 2 respiratory failure (CO₂ retention) causes high-volume pulse, wide pulse pressure, and asterixis (flapping tremors).
- Multifocal Atrial Tachycardia (MAT) is a common cause of an irregular pulse in COPD.
- Pulsus paradoxus is an exaggerated fall in systolic BP (`>10 mmHg`) during inspiration seen in acute exacerbation of COPD.
- Oral thrush in patients not on long-term inhaled steroids warrants evaluation for immunocompromised states (HIV).
- Tactile Vocal Fremitus must be reported as "equal bilaterally, but reduced when compared to normal".
- Normal chest expansion is `4 to 6 cm`; normal AP to Transverse ratio is `0.70` (approaches `1.0` in barrel chest).
- Cricosternal distance `<2 fingerbreadths` confirms a hyperinflated chest.
- COPD causes Class 3 Pulmonary Hypertension secondary to chronic hypoxia.
- Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC `<70%` establishes fixed airflow obstruction on spirometry.
- Adequate lower respiratory sputum samples must contain `>25 PMNs` and `<10 epithelial cells` per LPF.
- An expiratory chest X-ray is essential to detect a small pneumothorax.
- Diaphragmatic flattening on CXR is confirmed when the perpendicular height from the costocardiophrenic line to the apex is `<1.5 cm`.
- DLCO is decreased in emphysema and ILD, but normal in chronic bronchitis and asthma.
- LTOT is indicated when PaO₂ `≤55 mmHg` or SaO₂ `≤88%`, administered for `>15 to 20 hours/day`.
- Positive pressure ventilation in patients with bullous emphysema carries a high risk of bulla rupture and tension pneumothorax.
