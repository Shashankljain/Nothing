> - **Occupational Pathophysiology**: Prolonged standing causes blood to pool in the lower limb deep and superficial veins due to gravity, leading to **Chronic Venous Hypertension**.

### Integrated Viva: Symptoms, Pain Mechanics & HPI Structure

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What are the common chief complaints of varicose veins, and what causes pain in these patients?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Common Symptoms**: Dilated tortuous veins, leg swelling/edema, dull/dragging pain, hyperpigmentation, itching, or non-healing ulceration. Early cases are mostly asymptomatic.
> - **Causes of Pain**:
>     1. _Chronic Venous Hypertension_: Stretching of the vein wall, ==hypoxia== of the ==tunica== ==media==, and compression of adjacent somatic nerves.
>     2. _Secondary Infection_: Thrombophlebitis or infected venous ulcer.
>     3. _Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)_: Causes severe ==bursting== pain and ==night== cramps.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> How does edema progress over the day, and what does the anatomical extent of edema tell you about its underlying etiology?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> Edema increases over the day and becomes **more marked in the ==evening==** after whole-day standing/working due to elevated hydrostatic pressure.
> 
> - **Localized Edema (around ankle)**: Indicates venous ==pooling==/congestion or localized ==arteriovenous== (AV) malformations.
> - **Generalized Edema (entire lower limb)**: Indicates that the patient has developed **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)**.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Where do venous ulcers commonly occur based on the specific vein involved?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Great Saphenous Vein (GSV) Involvement**: Ulcer occurs in the **Gaiter Zone**, primarily around the **medial malleolus**.
> - **Short Saphenous Vein (SSV) Involvement**: Ulcer occurs on the **lateral aspect** of the lower leg.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What is the correct logical order for presenting HPI screening questions in a varicose vein case?

> [!abstract] **Mentor Rule on HPI Presentation Structure**
> 
> **IMPORTANT**: Never write HPI screening points haphazardly. Follow this strict sequence:
> 
> 1. **Primary Venous Symptoms**: Ask about dilated veins, leg heaviness, night cramps, and bursting pain.
> 2. **Systemic Vascular & Neural Screening**: Rule out arterial claudication pain (pain on walking) and neuropathic paresthesias.
> 3. **Complications of Varicose Veins**: Ask about hyperpigmentation, itching, edema, secondary ulcers, and ==pulmonary embolism symptoms== (fever, breathlessness, cough, hemoptysis secondary to DVT).
> 4. **Etiology & Secondary Causes**: Ask about abdominal/pelvic pain, constipation, abdominal distension (abdominal/pelvic malignancy compressing veins), prolonged immobilization, or long sitting.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Can prolonged sitting cause varicose veins? What is "E-Thrombosis"?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> ==Prolonged sitting== leads to blood stasis.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Explanation**
> 
> **E-Thrombosis**: Individuals working at computers or sitting for long hours continuously for days develop stasis of blood in the deep veins, predisposing to DVT. Obstruction of the deep venous system secondary to DVT leads to **secondary varicose veins**.

## 4. Definitions, Classifications & Anatomical Sites

### Complete Definition of Varicose Veins

> **Varicose veins are ==permanently== dilated, elongated, and tortuous veins with a diameter >==3== mm, associated with pathological reflux of blood due to incompetent venous valves.**

---

### Classification of Vein Sizes

|Vein Category|Diameter Cut-off|Clinical Appearance|
|---|---|---|
|**Spider Veins / Telangiectasia**|**<1 mm**|Fine intradermal thready veins|
|**Reticular Veins**|**1 mm to 3 mm**|Subdermal non-palpable bluish veins|
|**Varicose Veins**|**>3 mm**|Permanently dilated, tortuous subcutaneous veins|

---

### Integrated Viva: Anatomical Sites & Etiological Classification

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Where else in the body can varicose veins occur besides the lower limbs?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> 1. **Lower Abdominal Wall**: Caput Medusae (Portal Hypertension).
> 2. **Esophagus**: Esophageal Varices.
> 3. **Anal Canal / Rectum**: Hemorrhoids / Piles.
> 4. **Bare Area of Liver**.
> 5. **Scrotum / Testis**: Testicular Varicocele.
> 6. **Retroperitoneum & Vulval Vagina**: Vulval varicosities.
> 
> - **Lower Limbs**: Most common site in humans due to our **erect posture**.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Classify the etiological causes of varicose veins.

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> 1. **Congenital**: Klippel-Trenaunay syndrome, Parkes-Weber syndrome, FoxC2 gene mutation, connective tissue diseases, ==congenital absence of valves==.
> 2. **Primary**: Intrinsic muscular or valvular weakness of vein walls combined with prolonged standing.
> 3. **Secondary**: ==Obstruction== or ==destruction== of veins:
>     - _Pelvic / Abdominal Masses_: Uterine fibroids, ovarian tumors, pelvic lymphadenopathy, pregnancy.
>     - _Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)_: Iliac or femoral vein thrombosis leading to valvular destruction.
>     - _Occupational_: Prolonged standing or sitting (e-thrombosis).

### Integrated Viva: Skin Changes, Lipodermatosclerosis & Phlegmasia

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What are the theories explaining skin hyperpigmentation, eczema, and ulceration in chronic venous hypertension?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> Hemosiderin deposition from extravasated red blood cells causes hyperpigmentation.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Pathophysiological Breakdown**
> 
> Three major theories explain chronic venous ulceration:
> 
> 1. **Fibrin Cuff Theory**: Venous hypertension distends pericapillary pores, allowing fibrinogen leakage into interstitial space, forming an pericapillary fibrin cuff that blocks oxygen diffusion.
> 2. **White Cell Trapping Theory**: Decreased capillary blood flow traps activated leukocytes in microcirculation, releasing proteolytic enzymes and free radicals that cause skin necrosis.
> 3. **Ambulatory Venous Hypertension Theory**: Valvular incompetence prevents venous pressure from dropping during calf muscle contraction, sustaining high capillary pressure.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Define Lipodermatosclerosis, Atrophie Blanche, and Corona Phlebectatica.

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Lipodermatosclerosis**: Subcutaneous ==fibrosis== and ==scarring== resulting in hardening and ==thickening== of skin in the gaiter area ("inverted champagne bottle" appearance).
> - **Atrophie Blanche**: Smooth, ivory-white avascular atrophic skin patches surrounded by hyperpigmented halos in the gaiter zone.
> - **Corona Phlebectatica**: Fan-shaped intradermal venular flare around the medial malleolus indicating severe venous hypertension.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Differentiate between Phlegmasia Alba Dolens and Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens. How do you distinguish them on physical examination?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Phlegmasia Alba Dolens ("White Leg")**: Massive deep vein thrombosis of ==iliofemoral veins== combined with ==perivascular lymphatic inflammation==, causing severe pale, milky white leg edema. **Distal arterial pulses are PALPABLE**.
> - **Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens ("Blue Leg")**: ==Total occlusion== of primary and collateral venous outflow (including iliac/femoral veins), leading to intense venous gangrene, severe ==pain==, and a dark blue/cyanotic leg. **Distal arterial pulses are ==NON-PALPABLE==** due to secondary arterial collapse.

---

### Category I: Tests for Saphenofemoral / Saphenopopliteal Junction Incompetence

#### 1. Brodie-Trendelenburg Test Part 1 (Trendelenburg I)

- **Technique**: Patient supine. Elevate leg to 45° to empty superficial veins. Apply thumb/tourniquet pressure over Saphenofemoral Junction (3.5 cm below and lateral to pubic tubercle). Ask patient to stand up quickly while releasing SFJ pressure.
- **Interpretation**: Rapid filling of veins from **above downwards** immediately upon release = **POSITIVE (SFJ Incompetence)**. (_Negative in this patient_).

#### 2. Morris's Expansile Cough Impulse Test

- **Technique**: Patient supine. Empty veins, place fingers over SFJ, ask patient to cough forcibly.
- **Interpretation**: Feeling an **expansile fluid impulse** striking fingers = **POSITIVE (SFJ Incompetence)**. (_Negative in this patient_).

---

### Category II: Tests for Perforator Incompetence

#### 1. Brodie-Trendelenburg Test Part 2 (Trendelenburg II)

- **Technique**: Patient supine. Empty veins, apply SFJ tourniquet. Ask patient to stand up **WITHOUT releasing SFJ pressure** and observe for 30–60 seconds.
- **Interpretation**: Gradual filling of veins from **below upwards** within 30 seconds despite SFJ occlusion = **POSITIVE (Perforator Incompetence)**. (_POSITIVE in this patient_).

#### 2. Multiple Tourniquet Test

- **Technique**: Patient supine. Empty veins. Apply 3 to 4 tourniquets at different levels:
    1. Below SFJ (upper thigh).
    2. Above knee (lower thigh).
    3. Below knee (upper leg).
    4. Above ankle (lower leg).
- Ask patient to stand up.
- **Interpretation**: Venous segment filling between two tourniquets indicates an **incompetent perforator** at that specific anatomical level. (_Positive for above-knee, below-knee, and above-ankle perforators in this patient_).

#### 3. Fegan's Test (Method of Palpation)

- **Technique**: Patient standing. Mark all visible dilated venous blowouts with ink. Patient lying supine; elevate limb to empty veins. Palpate marked blowout sites with index finger.
- **Interpretation**: Palpating distinct **circular fascial defects / depressions** ("buttonhole" defects) in the deep fascia = **POSITIVE (Perforator Incompetence)**.

#### 4. Pratt's Test

- **Technique**: Patient supine. Empty veins. Apply an ==Esmarch elastic bandage== from toes to mid-thigh, and apply an SFJ tourniquet. Patient stands. Unroll Esmarch bandage from top down; sudden localized bulging of a vein indicates an incompetent perforator.

---

### Category III: Tests for Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) & Deep Venous Patency

#### 1. Perthes' Test

- **Technique**: Apply a tourniquet around upper thigh to occlude superficial veins. Ask patient to walk briskly for 5 minutes.
- **Interpretation**:
    - _Normal / Patent Deep Veins_: Superficial veins collapse due to calf muscle pump suctioning blood into deep veins.
    - _Deep Vein Occlusion (DVT)_: Superficial veins become **more distended/turgid** and severe leg pain develops.

#### 2. Modified Perthes' Test

- **Technique**: Performed **==WITHOUT emptying superficial veins==** before applying thigh tourniquet. Patient walks for 5 minutes while examiner observes superficial vein caliber and assesses pain.
- **Advantage over Classic Perthes**: Provides **BOTH subjective (pain) and objective (vein caliber reduction)**evaluation.

#### 3. Homans' & Moses' Signs for DVT

- **Homans' Sign**: Passive dorsiflexion of the foot with knee extended produces severe calf muscle pain.
- **Moses' Sign**: Squeezing calf muscles anteroposteriorly against the tibia produces tenderness.

---

### Category IV: Percussion & Auscultation Tests

#### 1. Schwartz Test (Percussion Test)

- **Technique**: Place fingers of one hand over SFJ and tap lower dilated varicose veins with fingers of opposite hand.
- **Interpretation**: Feeling an **impulse/wave transmitted upward** to SFJ = **POSITIVE** (indicates an incompetent continuous blood column without functional valves). (_POSITIVE in this patient_).

#### 2. Auscultation for Arteriovenous (AV) Malformations

- Auscultate over dilated veins with stethoscope.
- _Bruits_: Hearing a continuous **==Machinery Murmur==** indicates an **==Arteriovenous Fistula / AV Malformation==**; hearing a soft **==Venous Hum==** indicates rapid venous flow. (_No bruit heard in this patient_).

## 9. Diagnostic Workup & Management Protocols

### Diagnostic Workup Protocol

```
                      DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP FOR VARICOSE VEINS
                                       │
     ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
1. Duplex Scan (Gold Standard)  2. Abdominal/Pelvic USG          3. Blood & Pre-Op Fitness
   • B-mode USG (Anatomy)       • Rule out secondary mass         • RBS, HbA1c, ECG, CXR
   • Color Doppler (Reflux)     • Assess iliac veins              • D-Dimer (if DVT suspected)
```

1. **Venous Duplex Scan (Gold Standard)**: Combines ==B-mode ultrasonography== (visualizes venous anatomy) with ==Color Doppler== (measures flow velocity and reflux duration `>0.5 seconds`). Maps exact perforator blowout locations on the skin.
2. **Abdominal & Pelvic Ultrasound**: Mandatory in elderly patients to rule out ==secondary== pelvic/abdominal tumors or retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy.
3. **Chest X-Ray & ECG**: Evaluates fitness for surgery in chronic smokers.
4. **Glycemic Profile**: RBS and HbA1c screening for co-existing diabetes.
5. **D-Dimer Test**: Fibrin degradation products test to rule out active ==DVT==.

---

### Management Protocols

#### I. Conservative Medical Management & Bisgaard's Regimen

```
                           BISGAARD'S ULCER REGIMEN
                                       │
     ┌───────────────────┬─────────────┴─────────────┬───────────────────┐
     ▼                   ▼                           ▼                   ▼
1. Wound Care       2. Limb Elevation           3. Compression       4. Active/Passive
   • Debridement       • Above heart level         • 40 mmHg firm        Ankle Exercises
   • Dressing          • Gravity drainage            bandaging
```

- **Bisgaard's Regimen for Venous Ulcers**:
    1. Local wound debridement and sterile saline dressings.
    2. **Limb Elevation**: Elevate leg above heart level when resting.
    3. **Firm Compression Bandaging**: Apply **==40 mmHg compression==** using multi-layer elastic bandages to overcome superficial venous hypertension.
    4. **Active/Passive Ankle Exercises**: Activates calf muscle pump.
- **Phlebotonic Medications**: Micronized Purified Flavonoid Fraction (MPFF / Flavonoids) to reduce venous endothelial inflammation.
- **Compression Stockings**: Class II (20–30 mmHg) or Class III (30–40 mmHg) stockings used for prophylaxis and asymptomatic C1–C2 varicose veins.

---

#### II. Surgical Management Protocols & Procedures

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What are the surgical options for a patient with isolated perforator incompetence versus saphenofemoral junction incompetence?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Isolated Perforator Incompetence**:
>     1. **SEPS (Subfascial Endoscopic Perforator Surgery)**: Endoscopic interruption of perforating veins beneath the deep fascia.
>     2. **Stab Avulsion / Ligation**: Small skin incisions over Doppler-marked perforator sites to ligate perforator veins.
> - **Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) Incompetence**:
>     1. **Trendelenburg Operation (Flush Ligation + Stripping)**.

---

> [!abstract] **Step-by-Step Surgical Breakdown: Trendelenburg Operation**
> 
> 1. **Groin Incision & Tributary Ligation**: Transverse groin incision 1 cm below and parallel to the inguinal ligament. Ligate all **5 named tributaries** of the GSV at the SFJ:
>     - Superficial Circumflex Iliac Vein
>     - Superficial Epigastric Vein
>     - Superficial External Pudendal Vein
>     - Deep External Pudendal Vein
>     - Accessory Saphenous Vein.
> 2. **Flush Ligation**: Perform high transfixion and ligation of the GSV flush at its junction with the femoral vein (leaving no residual stump to prevent recurrence).
> 3. **Vein Stripping**: Insert a **Myers / Keshner stripper** into the GSV and strip the vein downward **ONLY up to just below the knee**.
> 
> - **Why Stop Below Knee?**: Stripping the GSV below the knee risks injuring the **==Saphenous Nerve==**, leading to severe persistent **Saphenous Neuralgia**.
> - **Short Saphenous Vein (SSV) Surgery**: Flush ligation at the Saphenopopliteal Junction (SPJ). Stripping risks injuring the **==Sural== Nerve**.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What are the minimally invasive thermal ablation options and indications for Sclerotherapy?

> [!note] **Student**
> 
> - **Endovenous Thermal Ablation**:

### II. Summary of All Important Clinical Facts Mentioned

- Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle in venous walls, predisposing females to varicose veins.
- Edema localized around the ankle indicates venous congestion/pooling; edema involving the entire lower limb indicates Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT).
- Great Saphenous Vein (GSV) ulcers occur over the medial malleolus gaiter zone; Short Saphenous Vein (SSV) ulcers occur over the lateral leg aspect.
- Prolonged sitting causes "E-Thrombosis" leading to secondary varicose veins.
- Varicose veins are defined as permanently dilated, elongated, and tortuous veins >3 mm in diameter with pathological reflux.
- Spider veins are <1 mm; Reticular veins are 1 to 3 mm; Varicose veins are >3 mm.
- Localized vein dilation indicates perforator incompetence; continuous vein dilation along the entire limb indicates SFJ incompetence.
- Fibrin cuff theory and White cell trapping theory explain pericapillary tissue hypoxia and skin necrosis in venous ulcers.
- Phlegmasia Alba Dolens has palpable distal arterial pulses; Phlegmasia Cerulea Dolens has non-palpable pulses and leads to venous gangrene.
- Tenderness over a vein indicates superficial thrombophlebitis; tenderness over bone indicates periostitis/osteomyelitis.
- Brodie-Trendelenburg I tests SFJ competence; Brodie-Trendelenburg II tests perforator competence.
- Perthes' test assesses deep vein patency; vein collapse during walking confirms patent deep veins.
- Diagnosis MUST be written under 6 mandatory headings including CEAP classification.
- Venous Duplex scanning combines B-mode anatomy with Color Doppler velocity/reflux measurement.
- Bisgaard's regimen requires 40 mmHg compression bandaging combined with limb elevation and ankle exercises.
- GSV stripping MUST stop just below the knee to avoid injuring the Saphenous Nerve.
- Tessari foam sclerotherapy is indicated for spider veins, reticular veins, and small local recurrences, but contraindicated in main trunk GSV incompetence.
