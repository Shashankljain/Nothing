### Integrated Viva: Definitions, Measurements & Presentation Strategy

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> How do you define a Wound, and how does a Wound differ from an Ulcer?

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh]**
> 
> A wound is any breach in the continuity of a covering membrane like skin or mucous membrane, which may or may not lead to molecular death of tissue.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Correction & Definition [Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> A ==break in the continuity of **ANY tissue==** (including skin, mucous membrane, or bone) is called a **Wound**. A discontinuity in the lining epithelium or mucous membrane **accompanied by ==molecular death== of tissue** is specifically called an **Ulcer**. Thus, an ulcer is a specialized type of wound.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Synthesis: Leg Ulcer Pain Profiles [Dr. Nishant & Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> - **Traumatic Ulcer**: Painful; uncomplicated traumatic ulcers heal rapidly and ==do not persist for 3 months.==
> - **Arterial Ulcer**: **==Severely== painful** (Rest Pain); localized to toe tips and weight-bearing end-artery areas.
> - **Venous Ulcer**: Mildly painful or painless; localized near the **gaiter area** (around the medial malleolus).
> - **Diabetic / Neuropathic Ulcer**: Completely **==PAINLESS==** due to sensory nerve destruction, despite extensive tissue necrosis.

### Integrated Viva: Pathophysiology of Diabetic Foot Triad

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> Explain the complete 3-part pathophysiology of the Diabetic Foot Triad (Neuropathy + Ischemia + Infection) [59–62].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Lecture [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> 1. **Neuropathy**:
>     - _Sensory Neuropathy_: ==Unnoticed minor trauma== (nail prick, tight shoe) acts as a persistent foreign body.
>     - _Motor Neuropathy_: Paralyzes intrinsic foot muscles, collapsing normal foot arches (==flat foot==); shifts weight-bearing load from metatarsal heads and heel to the center of the sole, causing **Trophic Ulcers**.
>     - _Autonomic Neuropathy_: Loss of sweating reflex produces dry, brittle skin prone to cracking and ulceration.
> 2. **Ischemia**: Microangiopathy (micro-thrombosis) + infection-induced thrombi + smoking-induced PAD reduce blood flow and cause chronic tissue hypoxia.
> 3. **Infection**: Elevated glucose and tissue hypoxia impair antibiotic penetration and white blood cell function, allowing infection to spread along deep fascial planes and tendon sheaths.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> Why did you specifically ask for a history of claudication and altered sensations, and what other conditions cause nerve damage leading to neuropathic ulcers?

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh]**
> 
> Claudication rules out ==Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD)==. Asking for ==altered sensations== (numbness, tingling, burning, pins-and-needles) evaluates sensory nerve injury/neuropathy.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Breakdown [Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> Other conditions causing nerve damage and neuropathic ulcers that must be ruled out include: 
> - spinal cord injuries, 
> - spinal nerve root compression, 
> - **Syphilis (Tabes Dorsalis)**, and 
> - **==Peripheral Neuritis==**.

### Integrated Viva: Night Rest Pain Mechanics vs. Claudication

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Nishant & Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> Why is adequate sleep important, and why do patients with Peripheral Arterial Disease experience severe rest pain during the night? [24–26]

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh]**
> 
> Resting pain disrupts sleep. Patients sit with ==legs hanging over the bedside== to increase perfusion.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Breakdown: 3 Mechanisms of Night Rest Pain [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> 1. **Loss of Gravity Assistance**: In the supine sleeping position, gravity no longer assists arterial blood flow to the distal extremity.
> 2. **Cold-Induced Vasoconstriction**: Ambient nighttime temperatures drop, inducing cutaneous arteriolar vasoconstriction and worsening tissue ischemia.
> 3. **Absence of Sensory ==Distraction==**: In night quietness, lack of mental distraction heightens the patient's conscious perception of ischemic pain.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Kishan]**
> 
> Define Intermittent Claudication. Can a patient have claudication pain while sleeping in bed?

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh / Himu]**
> 
> Claudication is ==cramping muscular pain== occurring during exercise/walking a specific distance, relieved by rest, and ==strictly **reproducible==** upon walking the same distance again. It **NEVER** occurs during sleep (pain during sleep is Rest Pain).

## 8. Diagnostic Workup, Perioperative Glycemic Management & Surgical Rules

### Workup Protocol Matrix

```
                     DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP FOR DIABETIC FOOT ULCER
                                        │
     ┌──────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                  ▼                                  ▼
1. Hematological & Glycemic      2. Local Imaging & Vascular  3. Microbiological                                                                  & Histopathology
   • CBC, RBS, HbA1c        • Plain X-Ray Foot (AP/Lat)       • Wound Culture &                                                                  Sensitivity
   • Renal & Liver Function • Arterial Doppler Ultrasonography • Wedge Biopsy of                                                                 Ulcer Margin
```

1. **Glycemic & Routine Blood Profile**: CBC, RBS, KFT/LFT, and **HbA1c**.
2. **Imaging & Vascular Studies**:
    - **Plain X-Ray Left Foot (AP & Lateral)**: Evaluates cortical erosion, periosteal reaction, soft tissue gas, or **Osteomyelitis**.
    - **Lower Limb Arterial Doppler Ultrasonography**: Evaluates ==macrovascular== vs. ==microvascular== flow.
3. **Microbiology & Histopathology**:
    - **Wound Culture & Sensitivity**: Swab or deep tissue aspirate taken **==BEFORE==** initiating new antibiotics.
    - **Wedge Biopsy of Ulcer Margin**: Biopsy taken from the junction of normal skin and ulcer edge to rule out **Marjolin's Ulcer (Squamous Cell Carcinoma)**.

### Integrated Viva: Perioperative Glycemic Control, Biopsy & Amputations

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> A diabetic patient presents with severe leg ulcer sepsis, RBS 420 mg/dL, and HbA1c 12.5%. Do you delay emergency debridement/surgery until HbA1c normalizes?

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh]**
> 
> No, HbA1c reflects 3-month average glucose control and will not drop in a few days. We optimize perioperative blood sugar to `<250 mg/dL` using short-acting insulin and proceed with surgery.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Emergency Rule [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> **Never wait for HbA1c to drop before performing required surgery!** HbA1c takes 3 months to change. As long as periprocedural blood glucose is ==brought below **250 mg/dL==** with short-acting insulin, proceed with surgical debridement because active sepsis drives stress hyperglycemia.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> Why perform a Wedge Biopsy, where should it be taken, and how do diabetic amputations differ from PAD amputations? [65–68, 70–73]

> [!note] **Student [Rakesh]**
> 
> Biopsy rules out malignancy (Marjolin's Ulcer). It must be taken from the edge of the ulcer.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Surgical Breakdown [Dr. Nishant]**
> 
> - **Wedge Biopsy**: Take tissue as a wedge incorporating **==both normal skin and abnormal ulcer edge==**. Biopsy from the central floor yields only ==necrotic== slough and false reports; cancer arises at the proliferating edge.
> - **Diabetic vs. PAD Amputations**:
>     - _Diabetic Foot_: Perform **==Conservative== Amputation** (as distal as possible, e.g. ==Ray amputation==).
>     - _Peripheral Arterial Disease_: Amputate at the level of adequate vascular perfusion (e.g. Below-Knee Amputation).
> - **Preservation of Metatarsal Heads**: In toe amputations, preserve the metatarsal heads whenever possible because they maintain weight-bearing arches and assist foot lift-off during gate propulsion.
> - **Offloading Footwear**: Post-amputation, provide **==Offloading Boots==** with custom internal arch supports to redistribute weight away from flat-foot areas and prevent secondary trophic ulceration.
