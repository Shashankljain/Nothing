### Viva Discussion: Reasons for Presentation in Varicose Veins

Patients with varicose veins typically present to a surgeon for one of three primary reasons:

1. **Cosmetic Concerns**: Prominent, unsightly dilated veins causing cosmetic embarrassment (especially in young females).
2. **Secondary Effects & Complications**:
    - Dragging pain in the leg.
    - Edema of the ankle and foot.
    - Itching, scaling, or stasis eczema of the skin.
    - Oozing or ulceration in the lower third of the leg.
3. **Medical Fitness Clearance**: Young males desiring to join the army or police force requiring medical fitness certification.

### Viva Discussion: Superficial Thrombophlebitis & Clinical Rule

- **Question**: Why did you specifically ask for a history of recent red, painful lumps with fever along the vein?
- **Answer**: To rule out recent attacks of **superficial thrombophlebitis**.
- **IMPORTANT**: If a patient has suffered a recent attack of superficial thrombophlebitis, **==injection sclerotherapy== or surgical ==intervention== MUST BE ==POSTPONED== for at least ==*3 months*==** after the attack has completely subsided.

### Differential Diagnosis: Causes of Unilateral Lower Limb Edema

1. **Lymphatic Obstruction**:
    - Filariasis (_Wuchereria bancrofti_).
    - Milroy's disease (congenital lymphedema).
    - Malignant lymph node deposits in the groin.
    - Destruction of lymphatics following groin/pelvic radiotherapy or block dissection of groin nodes.
2. **Deep Venous Obstruction**:
    - Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT).
    - Compression of iliac or deep veins by an intra-abdominal, pelvic, or retroperitoneal tumor mass.
3. **Venous Valvular Incompetence**:
    - Primary or secondary Varicose Veins.
4. **Post-Traumatic**:
    - Persistent post-traumatic edema following severe ==sprains== or ==bone fractures==.

## 4. Etiology, Pathophysiology, and Secondary Causes

### Occupational Mechanics & Pathophysiology

- **Question**: How does prolonged standing cause varicose veins?
- **Answer**: Continuous standing for prolonged periods causes the continuous **hydrostatic pressure of the vertical blood column** (from the heart down to the lower limb) to act on the lower limb veins. This continuous pressure produces progressive dilation of the thin vein walls, increasing the venous circumference, stretching the valve rings, and causing valvular incompetence and pathological **regurgitation of blood** from deep to superficial systems.

### Causes of Secondary Varicose Veins

1. **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)**: Recanalization of thrombosed deep veins destroys deep valves, forcing high-pressure blood through perforators into superficial veins.
2. **Extraluminal Compression**: Pelvic or retroperitoneal tumor masses, chronic colonic stasis/constipation, or an enlarged pregnant uterus compressing the IVC or iliac veins.
3. **Hormonal & Pregnancy Factors**:
    - **Progesterone & Relaxin**: Cause smooth muscle relaxation in venous walls, increasing distensibility.
    - **Gravid Uterus**: Mechanical compression of iliac veins in the 3rd trimester.
    - _Clinical Fact_: Varicosities arising purely due to pregnancy usually decrease or regress significantly post-delivery.
4. **Co-existing Pathologies Screened**:
    - Polycythemia vera or severe anemia.
    - History of similar swelling in opposite leg (ruled out).
    - History of bleeding per rectum (rules out hemorrhoids/piles).
    - History of scrotal swelling or dragging pain in scrotum on standing (rules out varicocele).

### Significance of Family History

- A positive family history indicates an **inherited congenital weakness of the elastic tissue and smooth muscle in vein walls or valves**, causing saphenofemoral valve failure early in life under normal physiological pressures (Primary Varicose Veins).

## 5. Physical Examination — Inspection

### Inspection Findings (Patient Standing)

- **Venous Dilatation**: Tortuous, dilated, non-pulsatile vessel extending from in front of the medial malleolus, running along the medial aspect of the leg and thigh up to the saphenous opening (Long Saphenous Vein system). Dilatation of the dorsal venous arch is also visible.
- **Pulsatile Varicose Veins**: Varicose veins can be pulsatile **ONLY** when secondary to an **Arteriovenous (AV) Fistula**.

### Eponyms & Inspection Signs

1. **Ankle Flare**:
    - _Definition_: A diffuse, fan-shaped plexus of small, dilated subcutaneous veins situated between the medial malleolus and the heel.
    - _Significance_: Indicates **incompetence of the lower ankle perforators** (Cockett's perforators). Classically seen in secondary varicose veins due to DVT.
2. **Blow-out (Blowout)**:
    - _Definition_: A visible, localized, saccular dilatation of a superficial vein situated directly over an incompetent perforator.
    - _Mechanism_: High-pressure blood forced outward from deep veins through an incompetent perforator at right angles to the superficial vein balloons out the weak superficial vein wall. Typically located over constant medial leg perforators.
    - _Saphena Varix_: A blow-out situated specifically at the saphenous opening.
3. **Collateral Veins on Lower Abdomen / Hypogastrium**:
    - If dilated collateral veins are present on the lower abdomen and collapse when the patient lies down or elevates the leg, it confirms **thrombosis or obstruction of the Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) or iliac veins**.
4. **Skin Changes**:
    - Hyperpigmentation, stasis eczema, scaling, oozing, ulceration, edema.
    - **Mechanism of Hyperpigmentation**: Chronic ambulatory venous hypertension leads to micro-extravasation of Red Blood Cells (RBCs) and high molecular weight proteins into the dermis. RBC breakdown releases **hemosiderin**. Ingestion of hemosiderin by dermal macrophages combined with a local melanocyte reaction produces characteristic **dark brown hyperpigmentation** in the lower third of the leg.
5. **Stasis Eczema**:
    - Non-contagious inflammatory skin condition in response to exogenous/endogenous stimuli (scratching, trauma, herbal applications). Skin is dry, scaly, and oozing.

---

### Complications of Varicose Veins

1. Superficial thrombophlebitis.
2. Pigmentation.
3. Stasis eczema.
4. Lipodermatosclerosis.
5. Varicose ulceration.
6. Bleeding from ruptured varicose veins.
7. **Talipes Equinus Deformity**.

### Mechanism of Talipes Equinus Deformity

- **Definition**: A horse-like plantarflexion deformity where the patient walks on their toes.
- **Pathophysiology**: In patients with long-standing, painful varicose ulcers over the medial malleolus, the patient adopts a habit of walking on their toes and the lateral border of the foot to relax the soft tissues over the painful medial ankle. Over time, the **Tendo Achillis undergoes permanent contracture**, fixing the foot in equinus deformity.

## 6. Physical Examination — Palpation & Clinical Demonstration Tests

### Palpation Findings

- Local temperature normal; no tenderness.
- Veins are soft, collapsible, and non-cord-like (rules out active superficial thrombophlebitis).
- **Fascial Defect Test (Fegan's Test)**:
    - Elevate the leg to empty veins; palpate the deep fascia along the line of the saphenous vein. A distinct circular gap/defect in the deep fascia can be felt at the site of an incompetent perforator.
    - Confirmation: On standing, an isolated segment of the vein over this gap fills up rapidly.

---

### Special Diagnostic Tests Matrix for Valvular Incompetence

#### 1. Cough Impulse Test at Saphenous Opening

- **Method**: Patient stands. Place the dip of the middle finger lightly just below the saphenous opening.
- **Anatomical Location of Saphenous Opening**: **3.8 cm (1.5 inches) below and lateral to the pubic tubercle**.
- **Interpretation**: Ask patient to cough. An expansile fluid thrill felt under the finger indicates **Saphenofemoral (SFJ) Valve Incompetence**.

#### 2. Cruveilhier's Sign & Saphena Varix vs. Femoral Hernia

- **Saphena Varix**: A single varicosity/blow-out situated at the saphenous opening.
- **Cruveilhier's Sign**: Palpating fingers placed over a saphena varix while the patient coughs feel a fluid thrill resembling a **"cat's purr"** or jet of water. Indicates saphenofemoral incompetence.
- **Differentiating Saphena Varix from Femoral Hernia**:

|Feature|Saphena Varix|Femoral Hernia|
|---|---|---|
|**Consistency**|Extremely soft, easily collapsible|Firm, doughy, or tympanitic|
|**Skin Color**|Bluish tinge visible through thin skin|Normal skin color|
|**Cough Impulse**|Positive **Cruveilhier's Sign** (fluid thrill like cat's purr)|Expansile cough impulse (no fluid thrill)|
|**Reducibility**|Disappears immediately on supine posture|Requires manual reduction or irreducible|

#### 3. Schwartz Percussion Test (Impulse Test)

- **Method**: Percuss/tap the most prominent varicosity on the lower leg while placing receiving fingers over the upper part of the long saphenous vein / saphenous opening.
- **Interpretation**: An impulse transmitted upward along the fluid column and felt by receiving fingers confirms continuity of the blood column and incompetence of intervening venous valves. Also confirms saphena varix.

#### 4. Morrissey's Test

- **Method**: Patient supine, leg elevated 30° to 45° to drain veins. Pressure applied over the saphenous opening with thumb. Patient stands up.
- **Interpretation**: If the saphenofemoral valve is incompetent, retrograde venous pressure causes blood to rise slowly in the vein or fall back rapidly when pressure is released.

#### 5. Trendelenburg Test (Trendelenburg I & II)

- **Procedure**: Patient lies supine, leg elevated to 30° to empty superficial veins. Apply thumb pressure over the saphenous opening (3.8 cm below and lateral to pubic tubercle). Patient stands up quickly.

|Trendelenburg Result|Behavior WITH Pressure (30 seconds)|Behavior AFTER Release of Pressure|Anatomical Interpretation|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Trendelenburg Positive**|Veins remain empty|Veins fill **rapidly from ABOVE DOWNWARD**|**Isolated Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) Incompetence**|
|**Trendelenburg Double Positive**|Veins fill **rapidly from BELOW UPWARD** within 30 sec|Veins fill **EVEN MORE RAPIDLY from above**|**Combined SFJ & Perforator Incompetence**|
|**Trendelenburg Nil**|Veins fill rapidly from below upward|No additional rapid filling from above|**Isolated Perforator Incompetence** (SFJ Competent)|
|**Trendelenburg Negative**|Veins fill slowly from below (>30 sec)|No rapid filling from above|**Normal (Competent SFJ and Perforators)**|

- **Fallacies of Trendelenburg Test**: Pressure applied at wrong site, pressure insufficient or excessive, or presence of an un-occluded high thigh perforator below the saphenous opening.

---

### Eponymous Clarification: Trendelenburg in Surgery

1. **Trendelenburg Position**: Head-low tilted operative posture.
2. **Trendelenburg Sign**: Pelvic tilt test for gluteus medius muscle weakness / congenital dislocation of hip (CDH).
3. **Trendelenburg Test**: Diagnostic tourniquet test for varicose veins.
4. **Trendelenburg Operation (Surgery)**: High ligation of LSV at SFJ.
5. **Trendelenburg Operation (Vascular)**: Pulmonary artery embolectomy.

---

#### 6. Multiple Tourniquet Test (Pratt's / Comparative Tourniquet Test)

- **Purpose**: Isolates the exact anatomical levels of incompetent perforating veins.
- **Method**: Patient lies supine, leg elevated to empty veins. Apply tourniquets at 3–4 levels:
    1. Just below saphenous opening (tests **Hunterian / Mid-thigh perforator**).
    2. Upper thigh / Above knee (tests **Dodd's / Above-knee perforator**).
    3. Just below knee (tests **Boyd's / Below-knee perforator**).
    4. Lower leg (tests **Cockett's / Ankle perforators**).
- **Interpretation**: Patient stands up; rapid filling of a vein segment between two specific tourniquets isolates the incompetent perforator at that level.

## 7. Deep Vein Examination & Perthes Test

### Clinical Signs for Active DVT

1. **Homan's Sign**: Passive dorsiflexion of the foot with the knee extended produces severe pain in the calf muscle.
2. **Ochsner's / Neuhof's Sign**: Direct anteroposterior compression/squeezing of the relaxed calf muscle against the tibia elicits deep tenderness.
3. **Posterior Tibial Vein Palpation**: Patient supine, knee flexed, foot flat on couch; direct pressure applied over posterior tibial vein against bone to elicit tenderness.

---

### Perthes Test (Deep Vein Patency Test)

- **Purpose**: Assesses the **patency of deep veins** and competence of perforating valves.
- **Method**: Apply a tourniquet around the upper thigh (just below SFJ) to occlude superficial veins. Ask the patient to walk briskly for 5 minutes (or perform 10 to 12 forceful heel raises).
- **Interpretation**:
    - **Normal / Negative Perthes**: Superficial varicose veins **collapse and empty completely** because the active calf muscle pump propels blood into patent deep veins through competent perforators.
    - **Positive Perthes Test**: Superficial veins become **MORE engorged, tense, and painful** because blood cannot pass through blocked deep veins and is forced outward into superficial veins.

---

### CRITICAL VIVA WARNING: Perthes Test Decision

- **IMPORTANT**: A **Positive Perthes Test is an ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION TO SURGERY OR SCLEROTHERAPY**.
- **Reason**: Deep veins are occluded; superficial varicose veins are acting as the sole collateral pathway for venous return. Removing or ligating superficial veins destroys this essential collateral network, resulting in catastrophic **venous gangrene**.

## 8. Diagnosis, Definitions, and Management Protocols

### Provisional Diagnosis

> **"Primary uncomplicated varicose veins of the left long saphenous venous system with incompetence of the saphenofemoral valve and technical / below-knee perforators, with hyperpigmentation of the lower leg skin."**

### Definition of Varicose Veins

- Dilated, elongated, and tortuous veins of the superficial venous system.
- **Common Sites of Varicosities**:
    1. Long Saphenous System (lower limb).
    2. Short Saphenous System (lower limb).
    3. Lower Esophageal Veins (Esophageal varices in portal hypertension).
    4. Pampiniform Plexus (Varicocele in scrotum).
    5. Superior Hemorrhoidal Plexus (Piles / Hemorrhoids).

---

### Diagnostic Workup Protocol

1. **Routine Preoperative Tests**: CBC, Urine Routine, Blood Sugar, Chest X-ray, ECG.
2. **Specific Imaging Modalities**:
    - **Color Doppler Ultrasonography**: **Gold standard, non-invasive imaging modality**. Precisely maps incompetent SFJ, SPJ, and perforating veins, and verifies deep vein patency.
    - **Ascending / Descending Venography (Phlebography)**: Radiopaque contrast injected into superficial foot veins with an ankle tourniquet. Used if Doppler results are inconclusive or deep venous anomaly is suspected.
    - **I-131 Fibrinogen Test & Serum D-Dimer**: Performed if acute DVT is clinically suspected.

---

### Surgical & Interventional Management Options

#### 1. Standard Open Surgical Repair

- **Trendelenburg Operation (Flush Ligation)**: High ligation of the Long Saphenous Vein flush at the Saphenofemoral Junction. All 5 anatomical tributaries must be identified and divided to prevent recurrence:
    1. Superficial Epigastric vein.
    2. Superficial Circumflex Iliac vein.
    3. Superficial External Pudendal vein.
    4. Deep External Pudendal vein.
    5. Accessory Saphenous vein.
- **Stripping of Long Saphenous Vein**: Performed using a flexible **Myers Stripper** from the groin down to just below the knee (avoids saphenous nerve injury below the knee).
- **Subfascial Perforator Ligation**:
    - _Open_: Cockett & Linton subfascial perforator ligation.
    - _Endoscopic_: **SEPS (Subfascial Endoscopic Perforator Surgery)** — subfascial visualization and clipping of perforators through small endoscopic ports.

#### 2. Endovenous Thermal Ablation (EVLT / RFA)

- **EVLT (Endovenous Laser Therapy)** or **RFA (Radiofrequency Ablation)**: Catheter inserted under ultrasound guidance; thermal energy ablates the endothelial lining, causing vein occlusion. Superior cosmetic and functional recovery.

#### 3. Injection Sclerotherapy (Compression Sclerotherapy)

- **Indications**: Small, isolated residual or spider varicosities **<3 mm in diameter**.
- **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**: Incompetent Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) or main trunk varicosities. Sclerotherapy without SFJ ligation leads to rapid recurrence.
- **Sclerosant Agents**: Sodium Tetradecyl Sulfate (STSD) or Polidocanol.
- **Mechanism**: Sclerosant injected into an empty vein causes chemical endothelial destruction, followed by firm compression bandaging for 3 to 6 weeks to induce fibrotic occlusion.

---

### Management Protocol for Varicose Ulcer

- **Golden Rule**: _"All varicose ulcers will heal with rest and leg elevation."_ The main clinical challenge is preventing recurrence.
- **Conservative Protocol**:
    1. Complete bed rest with leg elevation above heart level.
    2. Regular saline dressings and gentle massage of leg tissues surrounding the ulcer towards the heart.
    3. Active movements of toes, foot, and ankle to promote calf muscle pump action.
    4. Compression bandaging (**Unna's Boot**) to reduce chronic edema.
    5. Broad-spectrum antibiotics based on wound swab culture and sensitivity.
- **Definitive Surgery**: Once the ulcer has completely healed, proceed with **Trendelenburg operation and ligation of all incompetent perforators** to prevent ulcer recurrence.

---

### Contraindications to Varicose Vein Surgery

1. Active Deep Vein Thrombosis (Positive Perthes test).
2. Pregnancy (relative contraindication).
3. Recent attack of superficial thrombophlebitis (<3 months).
4. Severe peripheral arterial occlusive disease.
5. Unfit, bedridden, or elderly patients with limited life expectancy.
