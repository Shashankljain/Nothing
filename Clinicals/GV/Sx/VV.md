    - Dragging-type pain in the left leg on walking approximately 100 meters for the past 2 months.
    - Never shooting or radiating in nature.

    - No history of intermittent claudication (cramping calf pain on walking relieved by rest).

- **Negative History for Complications**:
    - No history of skin blackening (hyperpigmentation), scaling, itching, oozing, or ulceration in the lower third of the leg.
    - No history of swelling over the ankle or foot (edema).
    - No history of bleeding from the dilated veins.
    - No history of attacks of red, painful, tender lumps over the swelling accompanied by fever (rules out superficial thrombophlebitis).
- **Negative History for Associated Venous & Systemic Pathologies**:
    - No history of similar swellings in the right lower limb.
    - No history of bleeding per rectum (rules out hemorrhoids/piles).
    - No history of scrotal swelling or dragging pain in the scrotum on prolonged standing (rules out varicocele).

    - Works as a factory worker standing continuously for 8 hours per day for the last 3 years without wearing elastic compression stockings.
- **Negative History for Secondary Causes of Varicose Veins**:
    - No history of fever with severe calf pain and swelling, long-term bed recumbency, recent bone fractures, or major surgical operations (rules out Deep Vein Thrombosis / DVT).
    - No history of abdominal or pelvic swelling, bladder disturbances, or bowel disturbances (rules out pelvic/retroperitoneal tumor mass compressing deep veins).

- **Family & Personal History**:
    - No prior medical or surgical treatment taken for the swelling.
    - Personal history non-contributory.

## 2. Chronological Viva Exchange: Mentor Questions, Student Responses, and Mentor Corrections

### Phase 1: Clinical Presentation Reasons & Unilateral Lower Limb Edema

- **Mentor Question**: What are the common reasons for which a patient with varicose veins presents to a surgeon?
    
- **Student Response & Clinical Spectrum**:
    
    1. **Cosmetic Concerns**: Prominent dilated veins causing distress (especially in young females).
    2. **Secondary Effects / Symptoms**:
        - Dragging pain in the leg.
        - Ankle and foot edema.
        - Itching, scaling, or eczema of the skin.
        - Oozing or ulceration in the lower third of the leg.
    3. **Medical Fitness Requirement**: Young male candidates seeking recruitment into the army or police force requiring medical fitness clearance.
- **Mentor Question**: What are the causes of unilateral leg edema (edema of the left lower limb)?
    
- **Student & Mentor Classification of Unilateral Leg Edema**:
    
    1. **Lymphatic Obstruction**:
        - Filariasis (_Wuchereria bancrofti_).
        - Milroy's disease (congenital lymphedema).
        - Malignant deposits in inguinal lymph nodes.
        - Destruction of lymphatics following pelvic/groin radiotherapy or block dissection of groin nodes.
    2. **Venous Obstruction**:
        - Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT).
        - Compression of iliac/deep veins by a pelvic, retroperitoneal, or prostatic tumor mass.
    3. **Venous Valvular Incompetence**:
        - Primary or secondary Varicose Veins.
    4. **Post-Traumatic**:
        - Post-traumatic edema following severe sprains or bone fractures.

### Phase 2: Etiology, Pathophysiology, and Superficial Thrombophlebitis Rules

- **Mentor Question**: Why did you specifically ask for a history of recent red, painful lumps with fever along the vein?
    
- **Student Response**: To rule out recent attacks of **superficial thrombophlebitis**.
    
- **Mentor Teaching & Clinical Rule**:
    
    - **IMPORTANT**: If a patient has suffered a recent attack of superficial thrombophlebitis, **injection sclerotherapy or surgical intervention MUST BE POSTPONED for at least 3 months** after the attack has completely subsided.
- **Mentor Question**: What is the mechanism by which prolonged standing causes varicose veins?
    
- **Student Response & Pathophysiology**:
    
    - Continuous standing for prolonged periods causes the continuous **hydrostatic pressure of the vertical blood column** (from the heart down to the lower limb) to act on the lower limb veins.
    - This persistent pressure produces progressive dilatation of the thin vein walls, leading to stretching of the valve rings, failure of valve cusps to meet (valvular incompetence), and **pathological regurgitation of blood**from deep to superficial veins.
- **Mentor Question**: What are the main causes of secondary varicose veins?
    
- **Student & Mentor Synthesis**:
    
    1. **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)**: Recanalization of thrombosed deep veins destroys deep vein valves, shunting high-pressure blood through perforators into superficial veins.
    2. **Extraluminal Compression**: Pelvic or retroperitoneal tumor masses, enlarged pregnant uterus, or chronic constipation compressing the IVC or iliac veins.
    3. **Hormonal & Pregnancy Factors**:
        - **Progesterone & Relaxin**: Cause smooth muscle relaxation in venous walls, increasing distensibility.
        - **Gravid Uterus**: Mechanical compression of iliac veins in the 3rd trimester.
        - _Clinical Fact_: Varicose veins arising purely due to pregnancy usually regress significantly post-delivery.
- **Mentor Question**: What is the significance of a positive family history in varicose veins?
    
- **Student Response**: Indicates an **inherited congenital weakness of the elastic tissue and muscle in vein walls or valves**, causing saphenofemoral valve failure early in life (primary varicose veins).

### Phase 3: Physical Examination — Inspection Findings & Clinical Eponyms

#### Inspection Findings (Patient Standing)

- **Venous Dilatation**: Tortuous, dilated, non-pulsatile vessel extending from in front of the medial malleolus, running along the medial aspect of the leg and thigh up to the saphenous opening (Long Saphenous Vein system). Dilatation of the dorsal venous arch is also visible.
    
- **Mentor Question**: Can varicose veins ever be pulsatile?
    
- **Student Response**: **Yes**. Secondary varicose veins associated with an **Arteriovenous (AV) Fistula** exhibit visible and palpable pulsations.
    
- **Mentor Question**: Define Ankle Flare and state its clinical significance.
    
- **Student & Mentor Definition**:
    
    - **Ankle Flare**: A diffuse, fan-shaped hemangiomatous-like plexus of small, dilated subcutaneous veins situated between the medial malleolus and the heel (over the malleolar flare region).
    - **Significance**: Indicates **incompetence of the lower ankle perforators** (Cockett's perforators). Classically seen in secondary varicose veins following DVT.
- **Mentor Question**: What is a "Blow-out" (Blowout), and where is it typically located?
    
- **Student & Mentor Definition**:
    
    - **Blow-out**: A localized, saccular dilatation of a superficial vein situated directly over an incompetent perforating vein.
    - **Mechanism**: High-pressure blood forced outward from deep veins during calf muscle contraction passes at right angles through the incompetent perforator, ballooning out the weak superficial vein wall.
    - **Location**: Typically located over constant medial leg perforator sites.
- **Mentor Question**: What is the significance of dilated collateral veins on the lower abdomen/hypogastrium?
    
- **Student Response & Test**:
    
    - Indicates **thrombosis or obstruction of the Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) or iliac veins**.
    - _Test_: If abdominal veins collapse when the patient rests or elevates the leg, it confirms IVC/iliac vein obstruction.
- **Mentor Question**: Explain the exact mechanism of skin hyperpigmentation in varicose veins.
    
- **Student Response & Pathophysiology**:
    
    1. Chronic ambulatory venous hypertension leads to capillary damage and micro-extravasation of Red Blood Cells (RBCs) and high molecular weight proteins into the dermis/subcutis.
    2. RBC breakdown releases **hemosiderin** into surrounding tissues.
    3. Ingestion of hemosiderin by dermal macrophages combined with a local melanocyte reaction produces characteristic **dark brown hyperpigmentation** in the lower third of the leg.
- **Mentor Question**: Enumerate the complications of varicose veins mentioned in the presentation.
    
- **List of Complications**:
    
    1. Superficial thrombophlebitis.
    2. Skin hyperpigmentation.
    3. Stasis eczema (varicose eczema).
    4. Lipodermatosclerosis.
    5. Varicose ulceration.
    6. Bleeding from a ruptured varicose vein.
    7. **Talipes Equinus Deformity**.
- **Mentor Question**: Explain the mechanism of Talipes Equinus deformity in varicose veins.
    
- **Student Response & Pathophysiology**:
    
    - **Talipes Equinus Deformity**: A horse-like plantarflexion deformity where the patient walks on their toes.
    - **Mechanism**: In patients with long-standing, painful varicose ulcers over the medial malleolus, the patient adopts a habit of walking on their toes and the lateral border of the foot to relax the soft tissues over the painful medial ankle.
    - Over time, the **Tendo Achillis undergoes permanent contracture**, fixing the foot in equinus deformity.

### Phase 4: Physical Examination — Palpation & Special Clinical Tests

#### Palpation Findings

- Local temperature normal; no tenderness.
- Veins are soft, collapsible, and non-cord-like (rules out active superficial thrombophlebitis).
- **Fascial Defect Test (Fegan's Test)**:
    - Palpate the deep fascia along the line of the saphenous vein while the leg is elevated.
    - A distinct, circular gap or defect in the deep fascia can be felt at the site of an incompetent perforator.
    - When the patient stands, an isolated segment of the vein over this gap fills rapidly.

#### Detailed Description of Clinical Tests:

1. **Cough Impulse Test at Saphenous Opening**:
    
    - _Method_: Patient stands. Lightly place the dip of the middle finger just below the saphenous opening (3.8 cm / 1.5 inches below and lateral to the pubic tubercle). Ask the patient to cough.
    - _Positive Result_: An expansile fluid thrill / impulse is felt under the finger, indicating **Saphenofemoral Valve Incompetence**.
2. **Cruveilhier's Sign & Saphena Varix Differentiating Criteria**:
    
    - **Saphena Varix**: A saccular dilatation of the top of the long saphenous vein at the saphenous opening.
    - **Cruveilhier's Sign**: Tapping or coughing produces a fluid thrill over the saphena varix resembling a "cat's purr" or jet of water.
    - **Differentiating Saphena Varix from Femoral Hernia**:

|Feature|Saphena Varix|Femoral Hernia|
|---|---|---|
|**Consistency**|Extremely soft, easily collapsible|Firm, doughy, or tympanitic|
|**Skin Color**|Bluish tinge visible through thin skin|Normal skin color|
|**Cough Impulse**|Positive **Cruveilhier's Sign** (fluid thrill)|Expansile cough impulse (no fluid thrill)|
|**Reducibility**|Disappears immediately on supine posture|Requires manual reduction or irreducible|

3. **Schwartz Percussion Test**:
    
    - _Method_: Patient stands. Percuss/tap the most prominent part of the varicosity on the lower leg while placing receiving fingers over the upper part of the long saphenous vein / saphenous opening.
    - _Positive Result_: An impulse is transmitted upward and felt by the upper receiving finger.
    - _Significance_: Confirms continuity of a fluid blood column and incompetence of intervening venous valves between the two points. Also used to confirm saphena varix.
4. **Morrissey's Test**:
    
    - _Method_: Patient supine, leg elevated 30° to 45° to drain veins. Pressure applied over the saphenous opening with thumb. Patient stands up.
    - _Positive Result_: If the saphenofemoral valve is incompetent, blood falls back slowly into the vein under hand pressure or rapidly when pressure is released.
5. **Trendelenburg Test (Trendelenburg I & II)**:
    
    - _Procedure_: Patient lies supine, leg elevated to 30°–45° to completely empty superficial veins. Apply thumb pressure or a tourniquet over the saphenous opening (3.8 cm below and lateral to pubic tubercle). Patient stands up quickly.

|Trendelenburg Result|Behavior WITH Pressure (30 seconds)|Behavior AFTER Release of Pressure|Anatomical Interpretation|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Trendelenburg Positive**|Veins remain empty|Veins fill **rapidly from ABOVE DOWNWARD**|**Isolated Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) Incompetence**|
|**Trendelenburg Double Positive**|Veins fill **rapidly from BELOW UPWARD** within 30 sec|Veins fill **EVEN MORE RAPIDLY from above**|**Combined SFJ & Perforator Incompetence**|
|**Trendelenburg Nil**|Veins fill rapidly from below upward|No additional rapid filling from above|**Isolated Perforator Incompetence** (SFJ Competent)|
|**Trendelenburg Negative**|Veins fill slowly from below (>30 sec)|No rapid filling from above|**Normal (Competent SFJ and Perforators)**|

- **Eponymous Clarification — Trendelenburg in Surgery**:
    1. _Trendelenburg Position_: Head-low tilted operative posture.
    2. _Trendelenburg Sign_: Pelvic tilt test for gluteus medius weakness / congenital dislocation of hip (CDH).
    3. _Trendelenburg Test_: Diagnostic tourniquet test for varicose veins.
    4. _Trendelenburg Operation_: High ligation and division of LSV at SFJ (also refers to pulmonary artery embolectomy).

6. **Multiple Tourniquet Test (Pratt's / Comparative Tourniquet Test)**:
    
    - _Purpose_: Accurately isolates the anatomical level of incompetent perforating veins.
    - _Method_: Patient lies supine, leg elevated to empty veins. Apply 3 tourniquets:
        1. Tourniquet 1: Just below saphenous opening (tests **Hunterian / Mid-thigh perforator**).
        2. Tourniquet 2: Just above the knee (tests **Dodd's / Above-knee perforator**).
        3. Tourniquet 3: Just below the knee (tests **Boyd's / Below-knee perforator**).
    - _Result_: Patient stands up; rapid filling of a vein segment between two specific tourniquets isolates the incompetent perforator at that level.
7. **Perthes Test (Deep Vein Patency Test)**:
    
    - _Purpose_: Assesses the **patency of deep veins** and competence of perforating valves.
    - _Method_: Apply a tourniquet around the upper thigh (just below SFJ) to occlude superficial veins. Ask the patient to walk briskly for 5 minutes (or perform 10 to 12 forceful heel raises).
    - _Interpretation_:
        - **Normal / Negative Perthes**: Superficial varicose veins **collapse and empty completely** because the active calf muscle pump propels blood into patent deep veins through competent perforators.
        - **Positive Perthes Test**: Superficial veins become **MORE engorged, tense, and painful** because blood cannot pass through blocked deep veins and is forced outward into superficial veins.

```
                     PERTHES TEST CLINICAL DECISION
                                   │
     ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                                           ▼
Negative Perthes (Deep Veins Patent)               Positive Perthes (Deep Veins Blocked)
• Superficial veins collapse on walking.           • Superficial veins become engorged & painful.
• Deep vein outflow tract is clear.               • Superficial veins act as CRITICAL COLLATERALS.
• SAFE TO PROCEED WITH SURGERY.                   • SURGERY / SCLEROTHERAPY STAGE CONTRAINDICATED!
                                                    (Risk of catastrophic venous gangrene!)
```

- **CRITICAL VIVA WARNING**: A **Positive Perthes Test is an ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION TO SURGERY OR SCLEROTHERAPY**. Removing or ligating superficial varicose veins when deep veins are occluded destroys the sole collateral pathway for venous return, resulting in catastrophic **venous gangrene**.

8. **Clinical Tests for Active DVT (Homan's & Ochsner's / Neuhof's / Pratt's Signs)**:
    - **Homan's Sign**: Passive dorsiflexion of the foot with the knee extended produces severe pain in the calf muscle.
    - **Ochsner's / Neuhof's / Compression Sign**: Direct anteroposterior compression of the relaxed calf muscle against the tibia elicits localized deep tenderness.

### Phase 5: Definitive Diagnosis, Investigations, and Management Protocol

#### Final Clinical Diagnosis

> **"Primary uncomplicated varicose veins of the left long saphenous venous system with incompetence of the saphenofemoral valve and ankle/below-knee perforating veins, accompanied by hyperpigmentation of the lower leg skin."**

---

#### Diagnostic Workup Protocol

1. **Routine Preoperative Investigations**: Complete Blood Count (CBC), Urine Routine, Blood Sugar, Chest X-ray, ECG.
2. **Specific Vascular & Imaging Modalities**:
    - **Color Doppler Ultrasonography (Duplex Scan)**:
        - **Gold standard, non-invasive imaging modality**.
        - Precisely maps the anatomical locations of incompetent SFJ, SPJ, and perforating veins, and assesses deep vein patency.
    - **Ascending / Descending Venography (Phlebography)**:
        - Radiopaque contrast injected into superficial foot veins with an ankle tourniquet.
        - Used when Doppler results are inconclusive or deep venous anomaly/recanalization is suspected.
    - **D-Dimer Assay & I-131 Fibrinogen Test**: Performed if acute deep vein thrombosis is clinically suspected.

---

#### Operative & Interventional Management Options

```
                         VARICOSE VEINS MANAGEMENT
                                     │
     ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                               ▼                               ▼
Standard Surgical Repair       Endovenous Ablation             Injection Sclerotherapy
• Trendelenburg Operation      • Endovenous Laser Therapy      • Ideal ONLY for small
  (Flush Ligation at SFJ)        (EVLT)                          varicosities (<3 mm)
• Stripping of LSV             • Radiofrequency Ablation       • CONTRAINDICATED if
  (using Myers Stripper)         (RFA)                           SFJ is incompetent
• Subfascial Perforator        • Better cosmetic &
  Ligation (SEPS)                functional outcomes
```

1. **Standard Open Surgical Repair**:
    
    - **Trendelenburg Operation (Flush Ligation)**: High ligation of the Long Saphenous Vein flush at the Saphenofemoral Junction. All 5 anatomical tributaries must be identified and divided to prevent recurrence:
        1. Superficial Epigastric vein.
        2. Superficial Circumflex Iliac vein.
        3. Superficial External Pudendal vein.
        4. Deep External Pudendal vein.
        5. Accessory Saphenous vein.
    - **Stripping of the Long Saphenous Vein**: Performed using a flexible **Myers Stripper** from the groin down to just below the knee (avoids injuring the saphenous nerve below the knee).
    - **Subfascial Ligation of Incompetent Perforators**:
        - _Open Method_: Cockett & Linton subfascial perforator ligation.
        - _Endoscopic Method_: **SEPS (Subfascial Endoscopic Perforator Surgery)** — subfascial visualization and clipping of perforators through small endoscopic ports.
2. **Endovenous Thermal Ablation (EVLT / RFA)**:
    
    - **EVLT (Endovenous Laser Therapy)** or **RFA (Radiofrequency Ablation)**: Catheter inserted into the vein under ultrasound guidance; thermal energy ablates the endothelial lining, causing vein occlusion. Offers superior cosmetic and functional recovery.
3. **Injection Sclerotherapy (Compression Sclerotherapy)**:
    
    - **Indications**: Small, isolated residual or recurrent varicosities **<3 mm in diameter**.
    - **ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION**: Incompetent Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) or main trunk varicosities. Sclerotherapy without SFJ ligation leads to rapid recurrence.
    - **Sclerosant Agents**: Sodium Tetradecyl Sulfate (STSD) or Polidocanol.
    - **Mechanism**: Sclerosant injected into an empty vein produces localized chemical endothelial destruction, followed by firm compression bandaging for 3 to 6 weeks to induce fibrotic occlusion.
4. **Management Protocol for Varicose Ulcer**:
    
    - **Bismaal / Conservative Rule**: _"All varicose ulcers will heal with rest and leg elevation."_
    - **Protocol**:
        1. Complete bed rest with leg elevation above heart level.
        2. Regular saline dressings and gentle massage.
        3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics based on wound swab culture and sensitivity.
        4. Firm compression bandaging (**Unna's Boot**) to reduce chronic venous edema.
        5. Active ankle and toe movements to promote calf pump function.
    - **Definitive Surgery**: Once the ulcer has completely healed, proceed with **Trendelenburg operation and perforator ligation** to eliminate high pressure and prevent ulcer recurrence.
5. **Contraindications to Varicose Vein Surgery**:
    
    - Active Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) / Positive Perthes Test.
    - Pregnancy (relative contraindication).
    - Recent attack of superficial thrombophlebitis (<3 months).
    - Severe peripheral arterial occlusive disease.
    - Unfit, bedridden, or elderly patients with limited life expectancy.
