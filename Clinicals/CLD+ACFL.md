### Integrated Viva 1: Known Case Status, Disease Duration & ACLD Diagnostic Framing

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> The student introduced the history stating the patient was "apparently normal / asymptomatic 1 month back." Is this presentation accurate when a patient had a paracentesis 6 months ago and has been on continuous diuretics since then? [3–8]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> The patient told me he was okay in between, so I recorded the presenting complaints as 1 month duration [4–5].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Correction & Explanation [IMPORTANT]**
> 
> **IMPORTANT**: The patient is a **known case of Chronic Liver Disease (CLD)** diagnosed 6 months ago and has been on active diuretic therapy ever since [5–7]. He was NOT normal or asymptomatic 1 month ago.
> 
> - **Duration Rule**: A disease duration of 6 months or more establishes that the condition is **CHRONIC**. Presenting it as a 1-month illness misleads the examiner into thinking it is an acute or subacute liver failure, which carries a drastically different, rapidly downhill prognosis [6–7].
> - **Correct Diagnostic Framing**: Right from the first sentence of the HPI, state clearly: _"The patient is a known case of Chronic Liver Disease diagnosed 6 months ago, now presenting with acute worsening of abdominal distension over 1 month..."_ [6–7].
> - **Clinical Entity**: This presentation represents **Acute-on-Chronic Liver Disease (ACLD) / Acute Decompensation of CLD**, NOT a fresh acute liver disease [7–8].

## 3. Integrated Viva 2: Abdominal Distension, Fluid Features, Ascites Precox & Edema Mechanics

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What are the primary causes of abdominal distension, and how do you differentiate fluid from fat or abdominal mass in history? [9–12]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Causes include fluid, fat, flatus, fetus, fatal mass, and full bladder. Fluid causes generalized distension [10–11].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Differentiating Breakdown**
> 
> - **Generalized vs. Localized Distension**: **Fluid and Fat** produce generalized abdominal distension, whereas a fatal mass, fetus, or full bladder produces localized distension [10–11].
> - **Postural Variation**: In fluid distension (ascites), the shape of the abdomen changes with posture (in supine position, fluid bulges into the flanks; when turning to one side, fluid shifts to the dependent side) [10–11].
> - **Paracentesis History**: A documented history of abdominal fluid tapping (paracentesis) is the most definitive history point confirming intra-abdominal fluid.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What conditions cause **rapidly forming ascites** and **painful ascites**? What does the development of pain in a previously painless ascites indicate? [12–15]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Painful ascites can occur in peritonitis or acute disease. Development of pain indicates a complication [13–15].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Clinical Breakdown**
> 
> - **Rapidly Forming Ascites Etiologies**: Acute Budd-Chiari Syndrome (hepatic vein thrombosis), Acute Pancreatitis, Acute Bacterial Peritonitis, and Traumatic Hemoperitoneum [12–14].
> - **Painful Ascites Etiologies**: Acute Budd-Chiari Syndrome, Acute Pancreatitis, and Acute Peritonitis. (Note: Uncomplicated acute hepatitis does NOT cause ascites).
> - **Development of Pain in Chronic Ascites**: If a patient with long-standing painless ascites develops new-onset abdominal pain, suspect **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)**.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Explain the mechanism of orthopnea in ascites, and differentiate the differential diagnoses of orthopnea combined with ascites and pedal edema [16–18].

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Ascites pushes the diaphragm upward, reducing lung vital capacity. Other causes include cardiac failure and renal failure [16–18].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Pathophysiological Breakdown**
> 
> - **Ascites Volume Cut-off**: Moderate ascites does NOT cause orthopnea. Orthopnea occurs ONLY in **Gross / Massive Ascites** due to severe mechanical elevation of the diaphragm.
> - **Differential Diagnoses of Ascites + Orthopnea + Pedal Edema**:
>     1. **Chronic Liver Disease (CLD)** with massive ascites elevating the diaphragm.
>     2. **Biventricular Heart Failure / Right Heart Failure** (secondary to left heart failure): Right heart failure causes venous stasis, producing ascites and pedal edema, while left heart failure produces pulmonary edema and orthopnea [16–17].
>     3. **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) / Nephrotic Syndrome**: Volume overload produces systemic edema, ascites, and orthopnea.

---

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Explain the systemic mechanism of fluid retention in liver disease. What is **Ascites Precox**, and in which specific conditions does it occur? [19–25]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> In liver disease, splanchnic vasodilation activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, causing secondary hyperaldosteronism.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Lecture: Ascites Precox & Organ Edema Patterns [IMPORTANT]**
> 
> - **Systemic Fluid Mechanism**: Splanchnic arterial vasodilation → reduced effective arterial blood volume → activation of RAAS → **Secondary Hyperaldosteronism**. Because secondary hyperaldosteronism is a systemic process, ascites and pedal edema in typical liver failure start **parallelly / together**.
> - **Definition of Ascites Precox**: Ascites that occurs **predominantly BEFORE pedal edema**, or ascites without pedal edema, or ascites disproportionate to pedal edema ("coxa" = leg/hip) [21–23].
> - **Etiologies of Ascites Precox**:
>     1. **Constrictive Pericarditis** (Classic cardiac cause: thickened pericardium impairs cardiac filling → back pressure falls directly on hepatic veins/liver → massive ascites precox without classic cardiac leg edema) [22–23].
>     2. **Peritoneal Tuberculosis (TB Peritonitis)** (primary peritoneal pathology).
>     3. **Peritoneal Malignancy / Carcinomatosis**.
>     4. **Acute Budd-Chiari Syndrome**.
>     5. **Pancreatic Ascites**.
> - **Comparison of Edema Distribution by Organ Failure**:
>     - _Heart Failure_: Dependent edema (sacral in recumbent patients, lower legs in ambulant patients) due to lack of mobility.
>     - _Renal Failure_: Periorbital edema early morning (patients are mobile and able to lie flat).
>     - _Liver Failure_: Ascites + pedal edema occurring together secondary to systemic hyperaldosteronism [20, 24–25].

## 4. Integrated Viva 3: Localization to Liver & Negative History Rationale

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Ascites and pedal edema can occur in heart failure, kidney disease, and hypoproteinemia. Which specific history points localize the pathology to the LIVER? [25–28]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Ascites followed by pedal edema.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Correction & Specific Localization Markers [IMPORTANT]**
> 
> **IMPORTANT**: Ascites followed by pedal edema is NON-SPECIFIC and does NOT prove liver disease. To localize a case to the liver in history, you MUST elicit:
> 
> 1. **Past History of Jaundice**: An episode of jaundice (yellow discoloration of eyes/urine) points to hepatic parenchymal injury [27–28]. (Note: Jaundice can occur with a normal liver in hemolytic disorders).
> 2. **Right Upper Quadrant Abdominal Pain**: Points to capsular stretching of an enlarged liver.
> 3. **Documented Diagnosis**: Direct history of the patient being diagnosed with liver disease or instructed to stop alcohol by a physician.

---

### Exhaustive Rationale for Negative History Screening in HPI

|Negative History Parameter|Clinical Purpose & Target Complication / Differential|
|---|---|
|**No Abdominal Pain**|Rules out **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)**, Acute Pancreatitis (common in alcoholics, causes pancreatic ascites), and Acute Hepatitis [14–15, 29].|
|**No Fever, Vomiting, Diarrhea**|Rules out infectious SBP; rules out **dehydration** (diarrhea precipitates hepatic encephalopathy; constipation also precipitates it) [29–30].|
|**No Breathlessness / Wheezing**|Rules out **COPD** (in a chronic smoker) leading to **Cor Pulmonale** (right heart failure causing secondary ascites and pedal edema).|
|**No Hematemesis / Melena**|Rules out **Variceal Bleeding** secondary to **Portal Hypertension** (or peptic ulcer disease/gastritis).|
|**No Jaundice / Yellow Urine**|Evaluates acute hepatic decompensation, superadded acute viral hepatitis, or development of **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)** [32–33]. (Note: Uncomplicated cirrhosis with regenerative nodules may present without prominent jaundice).|
|**No Irrelevant Talk / Jerky Movements**|Rules out **Hepatic Encephalopathy**. (Early subtle symptoms: reversal of sleep-wake cycle, excessive daytime somnolence, nighttime awakenings) [33–34].|
|**No Bleeding Tendencies**|Rules out **clotting factor deficiencies** (impaired hepatic synthesis), **thrombocytopenia**(hypersplenism), or dysfibrinogenemia.|
|**No Decreased Urine Output**|Rules out **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)** or acute kidney injury [34–35].|

### Integrated Viva: Natural History of CLD, Endoscopy & Prescribed Medications

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What is the natural history of decompensated CLD with portal hypertension, and why is history of Upper GI Endoscopy mandatory? [36–40]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Prognosis leads to death. Paracentesis was done 6 months ago.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Pathophysiological Breakdown**
> 
> - **Natural History & Prognosis**: Once a patient with cirrhosis develops portal hypertension and decompensates (ascites/varices), the natural history is a steep downhill course. Without intervention, most patients succumb within **2 years**, primarily due to massive variceal GI hemorrhage.
> - **Mandatory Upper GI Endoscopy (OGD) History**: Every patient with decompensated CLD MUST be asked whether an upper GI endoscopy was performed. Endoscopy identifies esophageal/gastric varices and allows prophylactic **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL / banding)** to prevent fatal bleeding.
> - **Standard Medical Regimen in CLD**:
>     1. _Diuretics_: **Spironolactone** (aldosterone antagonist) + **Furosemide** (loop diuretic).
>     2. _Variceal Prophylaxis_: Non-selective Beta-blockers (**Propranolol** or Nadolol).
>     3. _Vitamin Supplements_: Vitamin B-complex (**Thiamine / B1**, Pyridoxine / B6) for alcoholic deficiency.
>     4. _SBP Prophylaxis_: Oral antibiotics (**Norfloxacin** or Co-trimoxazole) in high-risk ascites.
>     5. _Hepatic Encephalopathy Prophylaxis_: **Lactulose** syrup to prevent constipation and reduce ammonia absorption.
>     6. _Intravenous Albumin_: Infused during large-volume paracentesis (>5 liters).

### Integrated Viva: Alcohol Quantification, Safe Limits & CAGE Score

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> What is the threshold for significant alcohol consumption, how do you calculate units, and when can you label a patient as an "Alcoholic"? [42–45]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Significant consumption is 80 g of alcohol daily for >10 years. Safe limit is 2 to 3 standard drinks per day for males.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Categorical Breakdown [IMPORTANT]**
> 
> - **Significant Alcohol Consumption Threshold**: **>80 g/day of ethanol for >10 years** in males (threshold is lower in females: **>20–40 g/day**).
> - **Unit Calculation**: 45 mL of 40% spirits contains ~10 g of alcohol (1 unit). Alcohol consumption MUST be calculated and presented in **Units** [42–43].
> - **Safe Alcohol Limits**: Maximum **2 units/day** (14 units/week) for males; maximum **1 unit/day** (7 units/week) for females [42–43].
> - **Labeling "Alcoholic" vs. "Alcohol Consumer" [IMPORTANT]**: **Do NOT label every alcohol consumer as an "alcoholic"** [43–44]. A patient is diagnosed as alcohol dependent ("alcoholic") ONLY if they fulfill the **CAGE Questionnaire** criteria:
>     - **C**: Have you ever felt you should **Cut down** on your drinking?
>     - **A**: Have people **Annoyed** you by criticizing your drinking?
>     - **G**: Have you ever felt **Guilty** about your drinking?
>     - **E**: Have you ever had a drink first thing in the morning (**Eye-opener**) to steady your nerves or get rid of a hangover?
> - **Smoking Terminology**: Do NOT use terms like "chronic smoker" [44–45]. Record as "Smoker" or "Reformed Smoker" with pack-years [44–45]. Smoking directly accelerates fatty liver disease (MASLD/MASH) progression and increases Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) risk.
> - **Tattooing / IV Drug History**: Elicited specifically to rule out parenterally transmitted **Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C**.

### Integrated Viva: Hypotension in CLD, BMI Correction & Physical Stigmata

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Analyze a Blood Pressure of 100/80 mmHg in a CLD patient. How do you calculate BMI in ascites, and what are the causes of gynecomastia and Dupuytren's contracture? [51–56]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> 100/80 mmHg is low (hypotension) due to infection, sepsis, or hypovolemia from bleeding [51–52].

> [!abstract] **Mentor Clinical Breakdown**
> 
> - **Blood Pressure 100/80 mmHg (Hypotension)**: In a CLD patient, hypotension is alarming. Primary causes:
>     1. **Ongoing Gastrointestinal Bleeding / Hemorrhage** (check for compensatory tachycardia) [51–52].
>     2. **Sepsis / Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis**.
>     3. **Over-diuresis / Intravascular Volume Depletion** [51–52].
>     4. Baseline splanchnic vasodilation of cirrhosis.
> - **BMI Correction in Ascites**: BMI cannot be accurately calculated in massive ascites due to fluid weight [52–53]. Standard weight subtraction rules:
>     - _Mild Ascites_: Subtract **5%** of measured body weight.
>     - _Moderate Ascites_: Subtract **5% to 10%** of body weight.
>     - _Massive / Tense Ascites_: Subtract **20%** of body weight. _(Best clinical practice: Record height and weight as is, and assess mid-arm muscle circumference and temporal wasting for malnutrition)_.
> - **Temperature Reporting**: Never record temperature simply as "afebrile" in vital signs. Record the exact numerical value (e.g., **98.4 °F**); "afebrile" is an interpretation.
> - **Gynecomastia Causes**:
>     - _Drugs_: **Spironolactone** (aldosterone antagonist), Cimetidine, Digoxin, Ketoconazole, Estrogens.
>     - _Disease States_: Chronic Liver Disease (impaired hepatic degradation of estrogen → hyperestrogenism), Klinefelter Syndrome, Testicular Tumors.
> - **Dupuytren's Contracture**: Palmar fibromatosis causing flexion contracture of fingers; classic physical sign of **chronic alcoholism** [54–55].
> - **Testicular Atrophy**: Palpable reduction in testicular volume with **loss of testicular pain sensation** on squeeze; sign of hyperestrogenism in CLD.
> - **Bilateral Parotid Enlargement**: Non-tender parotid sialadenosis due to fatty infiltration in chronic alcoholics.

### Integrated Viva: Ascites Without Shifting Dullness & Cruveilhier-Baumgarten Sign

> [!question] **Tutor / Mentor**
> 
> Why is **Shifting Dullness ABSENT** in a patient who clearly has ascites and a positive Fluid Thrill? [59–60]

> [!note] **Student [Dr. Urai]**
> 
> Tense ascites.

> [!abstract] **Mentor Pathophysiological Breakdown**
> 
> - **Causes of Ascites with ABSENT Shifting Dullness**:
>     1. **Massive / Tense Ascites**: The peritoneal cavity is completely distended with fluid under pressure. Air-filled bowel loops are compressed and cannot shift to the surface during posture changes, rendering shifting dullness negative while **Fluid Thrill is strongly positive**.
>     2. **Minimal Ascites**: Fluid volume `<500 mL` is insufficient to elicit shifting dullness.
>     3. **Loculated / Encysted Ascites**: Fibrotic adhesions (seen in Peritoneal TB or past hemoperitoneum) lock fluid into compartments, preventing fluid shift.
> - **Cruveilhier-Baumgarten Sign / Murmur**: A continuous vascular **Venous Hum** with a palpable thrill audible in the epigastrium over recanalized umbilical/paraumbilical veins in severe portal hypertension.

> 
> - **Hepatic Hydrothorax**: Transudative pleural effusion (typically **RIGHT-SIDED**) occurring in CLD due to direct transdiaphragmatic fluid movement through microscopic diaphragmatic defects (peritoneal-pleural blebs) under negative intrathoracic pressure.
> - **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS)**: Triad of Liver Disease + Intrapulmonary Vascular Dilations (IPVD) + Arterial Deoxygenation [64–65].
>     - **Pathognomonic Symptom**: **Platypnea** (shortness of breath worsening in the upright/sitting position and relieving in the supine position; exact opposite of orthopnea) [64–65].
>     - **Pathognomonic Sign**: **Orthodeoxia** (drop in arterial oxygen saturation `>5%` or PaO₂ `>4 mmHg`when moving from supine to upright position) [64–65].

## 13. Diagnostic Workup Protocol (Blood, USG Cut-offs, Endoscopy, SAAG)

### Diagnostic Workup Protocol

```
                        DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP FOR CHRONIC LIVER DISEASE
                                             │
     ┌───────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                       ▼                                       ▼
1. Complete Blood & LFT Profile       2. Abdominal Ultrasonography            3. Diagnostic Paracentesis
   • Serum Albumin (Chronicity)         • Shrunken Liver (<10 cm)                • SAAG Calculation
   • Prothrombin Time / INR             • Portal Vein Diameter (>12 mm)            (≥1.1 g/dL = Portal HTN)
   • Viral Markers (HBsAg, HCV)         • Spleen Length (>13 cm)                • PMN Count (<250 = No SBP)
   • Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP)
```

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Evaluates anemia, leukopenia, and **thrombocytopenia** (platelet count drop secondary to hypersplenism) or pancytopenia.
2. **Liver Function Tests (LFT)**:
    - Total, Direct, and Indirect Bilirubin; AST, ALT, Alkaline Phosphatase.
    - **Serum Albumin**: **Marker of Chronicity**. Low serum albumin (`<3.5 g/dL`) indicates chronic impaired hepatic synthetic function.
    - **Prothrombin Time / INR**: Marker of hepatic synthetic failure.
3. **Renal Function Tests & Electrolytes**: Serum Creatinine and Urea (screen for HRS), Serum Sodium and Potassium (baseline prior to diuretic therapy).
4. **Viral Serology**: HBsAg and Anti-HCV to rule out viral hepatitis.
5. **Tumor Marker**: **Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP)** to screen for Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC). (Note: Liver biopsy is hazardous in cirrhosis due to coagulopathy and ascites).
6. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) Cut-off Metrics**:
    - **Liver Size**: Shrunken, nodular liver (`<10 cm` bipolar length).
    - **Portal Vein Diameter**: **>12 mm** (indicates Portal Hypertension).
    - **Hepatic Vein Diameter**: **<11 mm**.
    - **Spleen Length**: **>13 cm** (confirms Splenomegaly).
7. **Upper GI Endoscopy (OGD)**: Mandatory to grade esophageal/gastric varices and perform EVL banding.
8. **Diagnostic Paracentesis & SAAG Calculation**:
    - **SAAG (Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient)**: \[\text{SAAG} = \text{Serum Albumin} - \text{Ascitic Fluid Albumin}\]
    - **High SAAG (≥1.1 g/dL)**: Indicates **Portal Hypertension** (Cirrhosis, Alcoholic Hepatitis, Heart Failure, Budd-Chiari Syndrome).
    - **Low SAAG (<1.1 g/dL)**: Non-portal etiology (Peritoneal TB, Peritoneal Carcinomatosis, Pancreatic Ascites, Nephrotic Syndrome).
    - **Neutrophil (PMN) Count**: **≥250 cells/mm³** diagnoses **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** [71–72].

## 14. Comprehensive Management Protocols (Dietary Sodium, Diuretic Ratios, SBP, Transplant)

### Comprehensive Management Protocol

```
                        MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS FOR CLD ASCITES
                                         │
     ┌───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                                   ▼                                   ▼
1. Dietary Sodium Restriction       2. Diuretic Combination Therapy     3. Definite Curative Care
   • <2 g/day of Sodium                 • Spironolactone + Furosemide       • Liver Transplantation
   • Home cooked, no added salt           (100 mg : 40 mg ratio)              (Curative treatment of choice)
```

#### I. Dietary & Lifestyle Interventions

- **Absolute Alcohol Cessation**.
- **Strict Sodium Restriction**: **<2 g/day of Sodium** (<1 teaspoon of salt/day). All meals cooked at home without added salt; patient adds a single pinch of salt per meal at table. Avoid all outside/processed foods.
- **Fluid Restriction**: Necessary ONLY if serum sodium drops below **120 to 125 mEq/L**.

#### II. Diuretic Combination & Step-Up Protocol

- **First-Line Diuretic Regimen**: **Spironolactone** (Aldosterone antagonist) + **Furosemide** (Loop diuretic) in a **100 mg : 40 mg ratio** (e.g., 100 mg Spironolactone + 40 mg Furosemide daily) to maintain potassium balance [74, 75–76].
- **Maximal Diuretic Doses**: Step up every 3–5 days to a maximum of **Spironolactone 400 mg/day** and **Furosemide 160 mg/day**.
- **Target Weight Loss**: Maximum **0.5 kg/day** in patients without peripheral edema; maximum **1.0 kg/day** in patients with peripheral edema.
- **Indications to Hold / Stop Diuretics [IMPORTANT]**:
    1. Severe Hyponatremia (`<120 mEq/L`).
    2. Progressive Renal Failure / Rising Serum Creatinine.
    3. Severe Hyperkalemia (on Spironolactone).
    4. Development of active SBP, GI Bleeding, or overt Hepatic Encephalopathy [76–77].

#### III. Prophylaxis & Complication Protocols

- **SBP Treatment & Secondary Prophylaxis**: 7-day course of IV 3rd gen Cephalosporin (Ceftriaxone) + IV Albumin (`1.5 g/kg` day 1, `1.0 g/kg` day 3); secondary prophylaxis with **Norfloxacin** (400 mg/day).
- **Hepatic Encephalopathy Management**: **Lactulose** syrup (titrated to 2–3 soft stools/day) + Rifaximin (400 mg TDS) [74–75].
- **Definitive Treatment of Choice**: **Liver Transplantation**.

### II. Summary of All Important Clinical Facts Mentioned

- A disease duration of `≥6 months` defines Chronic Liver Disease; presenting a worsening 6-month illness as a 1-month history is incorrect.
- Ascites and pedal edema in CLD start parallelly due to systemic **secondary hyperaldosteronism**.
- **Ascites Precox** (ascites preceding pedal edema) occurs in Constrictive Pericarditis, Peritoneal TB, Peritoneal Malignancy, and Acute Budd-Chiari [21–23].
- Jaundice, RUQ pain, and past medical records are mandatory to localize ascites to the liver in history [27–28].
- Reversal of sleep-wake cycle (daytime somnolence, nighttime insomnia) is the earliest history marker of Hepatic Encephalopathy [33–34].
- Diarrhea and constipation both precipitate hepatic encephalopathy.
- Significant alcohol intake threshold is **>80 g/day for >10 years** in males.
- Alcohol consumers must NOT be labeled "alcoholic" unless they meet **CAGE Questionnaire** criteria.
- Non-compliance with diuretics is the single most common cause of acute decompensation in CLD.
- Blood pressure of 100/80 mmHg in CLD indicates hypotension caused by variceal hemorrhage, sepsis/SBP, or over-diuresis [51–52].
- Subtract **20% of body weight** for BMI calculation in massive ascites.
- **Shifting dullness is ABSENT in massive/tense ascites** because compressed fluid-filled abdomen prevents bowel loop shifting; fluid thrill remains positive.
- **Platypnea** (dyspnea on sitting) and **Orthodeoxia** (SpO₂ drop on sitting) are pathognomonic for **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome** [64–65].
- Serum Albumin is the primary marker of chronicity in liver function tests.
- Portal vein diameter `>12 mm` on USG confirms portal hypertension.
- **SAAG ≥1.1 g/dL** indicates portal hypertension ascites; **Ascitic PMN ≥250 cells/mm³** confirms SBP [71–72].
- Diuretics MUST be given in a **100 mg Spironolactone : 40 mg Furosemide** ratio [75–76].
- Stop diuretics if serum sodium `<120 mEq/L`, serum creatinine rises, or active GI bleed/SBP/encephalopathy develops [76–77].
