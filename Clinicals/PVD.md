# 2. HISTORY OF PRESENTING ILLNESS

## A. History of Intermittent Claudication

The patient was apparently normal 2 years ago, when he started experiencing pain in the lower limbs after walking approximately 200 metres.

### Characteristics of pain

- Pain occurs after walking a particular distance.
- Pain is severe and forces the patient to stop walking.
- Pain subsides completely after taking rest for approximately 3–5 minutes.
- After relief, the patient can walk a similar distance again before pain reappears.

This is suggestive of **intermittent claudication**.

Over the last 2 years:
- Claudication distance gradually decreased.
- Initially, the patient could walk approximately 200 metres.
- At presentation, he could walk only 25–30 metres.
- Similar pain was also present in the thighs and buttocks.

### Viva: What is intermittent claudication?

Intermittent claudication is pain in the muscles of the lower limb occurring during exercise or walking due to inadequate arterial blood supply, which is relieved by rest.

### Viva: What is the significance of intermittent claudication?

It indicates impairment of blood supply to the muscles, where the available blood supply is insufficient to meet the increased metabolic demands during exercise.

### Viva: What are the sites of intermittent claudication?

The site of pain depends on the level of arterial obstruction.

| Level of arterial obstruction | Site of pain |
|---|---|
| Aortoiliac disease | Buttocks and thighs |
| Femoral artery disease | Thigh |
| Popliteal artery disease | Calf |
| Distal arterial disease | Foot |

### Viva: What is claudication distance?

Claudication distance is the distance a patient can walk on a plain surface at normal walking speed before the appearance of pain.

### Viva: What is the significance of claudication distance?

- It is a good index of major arterial obstruction.
- It differs depending on the side and extent of arterial obstruction.
- It depends on the state of collateral circulation.
- It is also influenced by general health, temperature, walking speed and physical ability.

### Viva: How is intermittent claudication graded?

The transcript discusses grading according to the severity of pain and its effect on walking.

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| Grade I | Pain occurs after considerable walking |
| Grade II | Patient can continue walking despite pain |
| Grade III | Pain is severe and forces the patient to stop walking |

### Viva: What is the mechanism of intermittent claudication?

1. During exercise, the oxygen demand of muscles increases.
2. Diseased arteries cannot supply sufficient blood.
3. Local ischaemia develops.
4. Metabolic products accumulate.
5. This produces pain during walking.

### Viva: How do you differentiate vascular claudication from spinal claudication?

| Feature | Vascular claudication | Spinal claudication |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Reduced arterial blood supply | Compression of nerve roots |
| Pain | Ischaemic muscular pain | Neurological or radicular pain |
| Relief | Rest | Often relieved by sitting or changing posture |
| Relation to walking | Related to a relatively reproducible walking distance | Related to spinal posture and walking |

The discussion specifically mentions nerve root compression and prolapsed intervertebral disc as differential diagnoses.

### Viva: What advice is given to a patient with intermittent claudication?

- Advise the patient to walk at a slower pace.
- Avoid steep hills.
- Wear comfortable shoes.
- Encourage regular walking within tolerable limits.
- Stop smoking completely.
- Improve general health.
- Consider a job involving less walking if necessary.

The transcript also mentions long-term medical treatment, including pentoxifylline and vitamin E, and discusses lumbar sympathectomy and exercise-related treatment. These are retained as topics mentioned in the discussion.

## B. History of Rest Pain

Since the last 1 month, the patient has developed continuous pain in the right foot.

### Characteristics

- Continuous pain in the right foot.
- Interferes with sleep.
- Severe in nature.
- Partially relieved by analgesics.
- Relieved by hanging the foot down.

This is suggestive of **ischaemic rest pain**.

### Viva: What is the significance of rest pain?

Rest pain indicates severe reduction in arterial blood supply.

It is due to ischaemia of peripheral nerves and tissues and indicates progression towards the gangrenous stage.

### Viva: Differentiate intermittent claudication from rest pain.

| Feature | Intermittent claudication | Rest pain |
|---|---|---|
| Occurrence | During exercise or walking | Even at rest |
| Cause | Inadequate blood supply during exercise | Severe reduction in blood supply |
| Site | Muscles supplied by the affected artery | Usually distal part of the limb |
| Relief | Rest | Often relieved by dependency |
| Significance | Arterial insufficiency | Advanced arterial insufficiency |

> **Important:** Rest pain is a significant clinical indicator of severe peripheral arterial disease.

## C. History Suggestive of Raynaud's Phenomenon

### Viva: What is Raynaud's phenomenon?

Raynaud's phenomenon is episodic vasospasm of small arteries, usually affecting the fingers and toes, precipitated by cold exposure or emotional stress.

### Clinical features

- Exposure to cold causes an attack.
- Initial vasospasm produces pallor.
- This is followed by cyanosis due to reduced blood flow.
- Congestion and redness may occur during recovery.
- Repeated attacks may lead to trophic changes.

### Viva: What are the precipitating factors?

1. Cold temperature.
2. Emotional stress.
3. Immersion of the hand in ice-cold water.
4. Air conditioning or minor temperature changes in advanced cases.

Initially, attacks may occur only with severe cold exposure. Later, even minor temperature changes may precipitate attacks.

### Viva: What are the causes of Raynaud's phenomenon?

The discussion mentions:

- Primary Raynaud's phenomenon.
- Buerger's disease.
- Cervical rib and thoracic outlet syndrome.
- Cervical spondylosis.
- Atherosclerosis.
- Scleroderma.
- Trauma.
- Chilblains.
- Other conditions associated with vascular or trophic changes.

## D. History Suggestive of Buerger's Disease

### Viva: What are the main features of Buerger's disease?

- It commonly affects young male smokers.
- It involves small and medium-sized arteries.
- Superficial veins may also be involved.
- It can present with intermittent claudication and gangrene.
- Tobacco consumption has a strong association.

The mentor also discusses differentiating Buerger's disease from atherosclerotic peripheral vascular disease.

# 4. IMPORTANCE OF MARITAL STATUS AND POTENCY

The patient is married and has children.

### Viva: Why should marital status and potency be assessed before lumbar sympathectomy?

Because lumbar sympathectomy may produce sexual dysfunction.

### Viva: How can lumbar sympathectomy cause impotence?

Bilateral lumbar sympathectomy may interfere with sympathetic nerve supply involved in ejaculation.

Possible complications include:

- Impotence.
- Retrograde ejaculation.

The discussion specifically mentions:
- Removal of lumbar sympathetic ganglia.
- Loss of sympathetic control of the internal sphincter during ejaculation.
- Retrograde ejaculation into the urinary bladder.

> **Important:** Sexual function and fertility-related history should be assessed before performing lumbar sympathectomy.
