# External Cephalic Version (ECV) in Breech Presentation [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **External Cephalic Version (ECV)**: An obstetric manipulative procedure performed entirely through the maternal abdominal wall designed to alter a fetal malpresentation (breech presentation or transverse lie) into a favorable **cephalic (vertex) presentation** at or near term [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 540].

---

### 2. Role of ECV in the Management of Breech Presentation [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical Purpose & Benefits

- **Reduction in Breech Delivery Rate**: Successfully converts **60% to 65%** of breech presentations into vertex presentations at term (success rate is ~60% in multigravidae and ~40% in primigravidae) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360; Ch 37, p. 541].
- **Reduction in Cesarean Section Rate**: Decreases the overall rate of Cesarean deliveries by approximately **50%** for breech indication [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
- **Minimization of Perinatal Morbidity & Mortality**: Avoids fetal risks inherent to vaginal breech delivery (such as cord prolapse, head entrapment, and brachial plexus trauma) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358; Ch 37, p. 541].

#### B. Optimal Timing of Version [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primigravida**: Performed at **36 completed weeks** of gestation (spontaneous version is unlikely after 36 weeks due to lower liquor ratio) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360; Ch 37, p. 541].
- **Multigravida**: Performed at **37 completed weeks** of gestation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360; Ch 37, p. 541].
- _Rationale_: Performing ECV at term minimizes spontaneous reversion back to breech, and should an acute intrapartum complication occur during the procedure, an immediate emergency Cesarean section can be safely performed on a mature fetus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

#### C. Prerequisites & Predictors of Success [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Confirmed Fetal Wellbeing**: Normal, reactive Non-Stress Test (NST) prior to procedure [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
2. **Adequate Amniotic Fluid**: Amniotic Fluid Index (AFI) **>10 cm** (adequate liquor allows smooth fetal rotation) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
3. **Unengaged Podalic Pole**: Breech must be mobile above the pelvic brim [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
4. **Flexed Fetal Head**: Head must not be hyperextended ("stargazing posture") [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
5. **Relaxed Uterine Wall & Adequate Pelvis**: Absence of uterine irritability or structural abnormalities [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

#### D. Contraindications to ECV [⭐ High-Yield]

##### Absolute Contraindications

- Antepartum Hemorrhage (Placenta Previa or Abruption) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Multiple Gestation (Twins/Triplets) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Ruptured Membranes (PROM) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Non-Reassuring Fetal Heart Rate Pattern / Fetal Distress [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Prior History of **>1 Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS)** or Classical Scar [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Severe Congenital Fetal Anomalies or Hydrocephalus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

##### Relative Contraindications

- Maternal Obesity (weight >65 kg) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Severe Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR) or Oligohydramnios (AFI <5 cm) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Frank Breech deeply engaged in the pelvis with extended legs acting as splints [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Severe Pre-eclampsia or Hypertension [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

### 4. Stepwise Technique of External Cephalic Version [⭐ High-Yield]

The procedure must be carried out in a **Labor Delivery Complex** equipped with operating theater facilities for immediate emergency Cesarean section if needed [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

```
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Step 1: Mobilization    │               │ Step 2: Forward Roll    │               │ Step 3: Completion      │

│   pelvic brim into      │               │   pressure on head &    │               │   crossing; push head   │
│   iliac fossa           │               │   breech in opposite    │               │   into pelvic inlet     │
│ • Pawlik grip on head   │               │   directions            │               │ • Monitor FHR           │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Pre-Procedure Preparations

1. **Informed Consent**: Obtain detailed written consent explaining benefits and risks [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
2. **Patient Positioning**: Patient lies supine with a **15° left lateral tilt** (using a wedge under the right hip to prevent aortocaval compression); hips and knees are slightly flexed to relax the abdominal wall [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
3. **Bladder**: Patient must empty her bladder prior to the maneuver [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
4. **Tocolysis Administration**: Administer **Inj. Terbutaline 0.25 mg SC** 15 to 30 minutes prior to the procedure to relax the myometrium and increase success rates [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
5. **Real-Time Ultrasound**: Perform USG immediately before starting to re-confirm presentation, placental site, and liquor volume [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

#### B. Actual Manipulative Steps ("Forward Roll" Maneuver) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Step 1: Mobilization of the Breech**: Both hands are placed over the lower abdomen; the podalic pole (breech) is dislodged from the pelvic brim and pushed laterally into one iliac fossa toward the side of the fetal back [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
- **Step 2: Dual-Pole Rotation ("Forward Roll")**:
    - The operator grasps the cephalic pole with one hand and the podalic pole with the other hand [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
    - Intermittent, firm (but never violent) pressure is applied in opposite directions to rotate the fetus, attempting to guide the head downward toward the pelvic inlet and the breech upward toward the fundus via a **forward somersault movement** (maintaining fetal head flexion throughout) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
    - _Pause & Monitor_: Check fetal heart rate intermittently during the rotation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
- **Step 3: Guiding Head into Pelvis**: Once the lie becomes transverse, the operator changes hand placement (without crossing wrists) and guides the cephalic pole down directly into the lower uterine segment above the pelvic brim [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

#### C. Post-Procedure Protocol

1. **Fetal Surveillance**: Continuous Cardiotocography (CTG) / FHR monitoring for **1 hour post-ECV** to verify fetal wellbeing [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
2. **Immunoprophylaxis**: Administer **Anti-D Immunoglobulin (100 μg / 500 IU IM)** to all non-sensitized Rh-negative mothers to prevent isoimmunization secondary to micro-fetomaternal hemorrhage [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
3. **Discharge Advice**: Instruct patient to report immediately if she experiences vaginal bleeding, liquor leakage, uterine contractions, or reduced fetal movements [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

### 5. Complications & Causes of Failure [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Complications of ECV

1. **Transient Fetal Bradycardia**: Most common finding (~10%), usually resolves spontaneously within 3 minutes; if bradycardia persists >6 minutes, perform emergency Category 1 CS [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
2. **Placental Abruption & Uterine Bleeding**: Caused by excessive shear forces during version [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
3. **Premature Rupture of Membranes (PROM)** and labor initiation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
4. **Umbilical Cord Entanglement or True Knot Formation** leading to acute fetal hypoxia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].
5. **Fetomaternal Transfusion**: Can trigger Rh-isoimmunization if Anti-D is omitted [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 541].

#### B. Causes of ECV Failure

- Breech with extended legs (Frank breech splinting the fetal trunk) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Scanty liquor volume (Oligohydramnios) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Irritable uterus or rigid abdominal wall in obese patients [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
- Short umbilical cord (absolute or relative) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The "Forward Roll" Flexion Rule**: Always attempt rotation in the direction that maintains fetal flexion (forward somersault, pushing head toward abdomen). Never force backward extension of the fetal spine, as hyperextending the head causes impaction and cervical spinal cord trauma!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **External Cephalic Version (ECV)** with **Internal Podalic Version (IPV)**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **ECV** is performed **externally through the abdominal wall** at **36–37 weeks** to turn a breech into a **cephalic**presentation.
    - **IPV** is an intra-uterine manipulation performed **inside the uterus** during labor to turn a transverse lie into a **breech** (indicated almost exclusively for delivery of the **second twin**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 37, p. 542].

### 9. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: V-E-R-S-I-O-N (ECV Checklist)

- **V**: **Valid consent & Tocolysis** (Inj. Terbutaline 0.25 mg SC).
- **E**: **Exclude contraindications** (Placenta previa, twins, prior >1 LSCS, PROM).
- **R**: **Reactive NST & USG pre-check** (confirm liquor & fetal heart rate).
- **S**: **Success rate ~65%** (higher in multiparae & complete breech).
- **I**: **Intermittent gentle forward-roll pressure** (maintain fetal head flexion).
- **O**: **One-hour post-ECV CTG monitoring**.
- **N**: **Non-sensitized Rh-negative mothers need Anti-D Ig (100 μg IM)**.
