# Cord Prolapse: Definition, Etiology, Diagnosis, and Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition

- **Cord Prolapse**: The abnormal descent of the umbilical cord alongside or ahead of the fetal presenting part, resulting in cord compression between the presenting part and the maternal bony pelvis or cervix.

#### B. Clinical Classification (Three Clinical Varieties) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Occult Prolapse**: The umbilical cord lies alongside the fetal presenting part but is **not palpable on digital vaginal examination**; membranes may be intact or ruptured.
2. **Cord Presentation (Funic Presentation)**: The umbilical cord lies below the fetal presenting part within the **INTACT bag of membranes**.
3. **Overt Cord Prolapse**: The umbilical cord lies inside the vagina or protrudes outside the vulval introitus following **RUPTURE of membranes**.

#### C. Incidence

- **Overall Incidence**: **0.5%** in vertex presentations.
- **Incidence in Malpresentations**: **3%** in flexed breech, **10–15%** in footling breech, **5–10%** in transverse lie (up to **20%**), and **10%** in compound presentations; predominantly affects multiparous women.

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Any factor that prevents the fetal presenting part from tightly fitting into and adapting to the lower uterine segment predisposes to cord prolapse:

```

                               │     Etiology of Cord Prolapse          │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Malpresentations     │               │ 2. Fetal & Pelvic       │               │ 3. Iatrogenic Factors   │
│ • Transverse lie (5–10%)│               │ • Contracted pelvis     │               │ • High ARM / RAM        │
│ • Breech (3–15%)        │               │ • Unengaged head        │               │ • Manual rotation       │
│ • Compound (10%)        │               │ • Polyhydramnios        │               │ • ECV / IPV             │
│                         │               │ • Prematurity / Twins   │               │ • Stabilizing induction │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Malpresentations (>50% of cases)**: Transverse lie (5–10%), breech presentation (3% overall; 10–15% in footling breech), and compound presentation (10%).
2. **Fetal & Pelvic Factors**: Contracted pelvis, unengaged presenting part at onset of labor, prematurity (small fetus), and multiple gestation (twins).
3. **Liquor & Placental Factors**: Polyhydramnios (sudden gush of fluid carries the cord down upon rupture), minor degree placenta previa, marginal cord insertion, and an abnormally long cord (>70 cm).
4. **Iatrogenic / Procedure-Related Factors**:
    - Artificial Rupture of Membranes (ARM) when the presenting part is unengaged or high.
    - Manual rotation of the fetal head.
    - External Cephalic Version (ECV) or Internal Podalic Version (IPV).
    - Application of scalp electrode or placement of intrauterine pressure catheter.

### 4. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Diagnostic Features by Clinical Variety [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Occult Prolapse**:
    - _Detection_: Difficult to diagnose clinically on digital exam.
    - _EFM Pattern_: Suspected when continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring shows **persistent variable decelerations** or persistent fetal bradycardia (<110 bpm).
    - _Imaging_: Diagnosed by Intrapartum Color Doppler Ultrasonography (IUSG).
2. **Cord Presentation**:
    - _Digital PV Exam_: Soft, smooth, sinuous, **pulsating tubular structure felt through the intact bag of membranes**.
3. **Overt Cord Prolapse**:
    - _Digital PV Exam_: Soft, coiled loop of cord directly palpated in the vagina or visible protruding outside the vulval introitus after rupture of membranes.
    - _Assessment of Fetal Viability_: **Pulsation in the cord** indicates a living fetus; absence of pulsation requires Doppler USG or FHS auscultation to confirm fetal demise before planning intervention.

### 6. Treatment & Management Plan [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Management of Dead Fetus

- Confirm fetal demise using bedside ultrasonography or auscultation.
- Avoid emergency operative delivery; allow labor to proceed awaiting **spontaneous vaginal delivery**.

#### B. Management of Living Fetus [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Immediate Safe Vaginal Delivery (Only under specific prerequisites)

- **Prerequisites**: Cervix **FULL Y dilated (10 cm)**, head engaged at low station (+2 or lower), and no pelvic contraction.
- **Procedure**:
    - _Vertex Presentation_: Immediate delivery via **Forceps** or **Ventouse**.
    - _Breech Presentation_: Immediate **Breech Extraction** by an experienced operator.

##### 2. Emergency Cesarean Section (Category 1 CS — Treatment of Choice in >90% of cases)

- **Indication**: Cervix incompletely dilated OR presenting part high/unengaged.

##### 3. First-Aid Resuscitation & Decompression Measures (While preparing for CS) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Manual Elevation of Presenting Part**: Pass two gloved fingers into the vagina to push the presenting part upward away from the prolapsed cord until the baby is delivered at surgery.
2. **Bladder Filling (Vantoch's Technique)**: Instill **400 to 750 mL of warm sterile normal saline** into the urinary bladder via a Foley catheter, inflate the balloon, and clamp the catheter. The distended bladder lifts the presenting part off the cord. _(Crucial: Empty the bladder immediately prior to making the uterine incision!)_.
3. **Maternal Postural Elevation**: Place patient in **exaggerated Sims position** (with a pillow under the hip), **Trendelenburg position**, or **Knee-chest position** to allow gravity to pull the fetus away from the pelvic inlet.
4. **Gentle Replacement of Cord into Vagina**: Tuck the prolapsed cord loop gently back into the vagina to protect it from cold room air and prevent vasospasm. _(Never attempt to push the cord back into the uterine cavity!)_.
5. **Medical Resuscitation & Tocolysis**:
    - Administer **Oxygen at 8–10 L/min** via face mask and IV crystalloid fluids.
    - **Discontinue Oxytocin** immediately if an infusion is running.
    - Administer **Inj. Terbutaline 0.25 mg SC** as an acute tocolytic to suppress uterine contractions and relieve cord pinching.

### 7. Complications & Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Fetal Complications

- Acute fetal hypoxia, brain damage, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), cerebral palsy, or intrapartum death secondary to cord occlusion.
- Perinatal mortality ranges from **15% to 50%** in neglected cases.

#### B. Maternal Complications

- Morbidity associated with emergency operative delivery (anesthesia risks, PPH, surgical lacerations, sepsis).

#### C. Prognosis

- If definitive delivery is accomplished within the **golden window of 10 to 30 minutes**, perinatal mortality is dramatically reduced to **5% to 10%**.

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Dual Decompression Protocol**: In acute cord prolapse with an incompletely dilated cervix, combining **manual vaginal finger elevation of the presenting part** with **instillation of 500 mL saline into the bladder (Vantoch's technique)** provides reliable, continuous cord decompression during transfer to the operating theater.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Attempting to manually push/reduce the prolapsed cord back inside the uterine cavity, OR handling the cord excessively.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **NEVER attempt to replace the cord into the uterine cavity**—it triggers fatal vasospasm, causes membrane rupture in cord presentation, and wastes critical time.
    - **Avoid unnecessary handling or pulling on the cord**, as cold exposure and mechanical irritation induce severe umbilical artery vasospasm.

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-R-O-L-A-P-S-E (Cord Prolapse Management)

- **P**: **Position maternal hips high** (Exaggerated Sims / Trendelenburg / Knee-chest).
- **R**: **Replace cord into vagina gently** (avoids cold-induced vasospasm; do not reduce into uterus).
- **O**: **Oxygen administration (8–10 L/min)** + Stop Oxytocin.
- **L**: **Lift presenting part manually** (two fingers pushing head upward).
- **A**: **Avoid unnecessary handling of cord**.
- **P**: **Perform Bladder Filling (400–750 mL normal saline)** via Foley catheter.
- **S**: **Subcutaneous Terbutaline (0.25 mg)** for acute tocolysis.
- **E**: **Emergency Category 1 Cesarean Section** within 10–30 minutes.

### Quick Revision

- **Classification**: Occult (beside head, unpalpable), Cord Presentation (below head, intact membranes), Overt Prolapse (in vagina/vulva, ruptured membranes).
- **Highest Risk**: Transverse lie (5–10%), footling breech (10–15%), compound presentation (10%), and high ARM.
- **Key Diagnostic Sign**: Pulsating cord in vagina on PV (overt); persistent variable decelerations on CTG (occult).
- **First-Aid Decompression**: Manual finger elevation of head + Bladder filling (500 mL saline) + Exaggerated Sims position + SC Terbutaline.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Immediate Category 1 Cesarean Section (or immediate forceps/breech extraction if cervix is fully 10 cm dilated).

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Cord prolapse is an obstetric emergency characterized by the descent of the umbilical cord ahead of the presenting part following membrane rupture, leading to severe fetal hypoxia from cord compression and vasospasm, which demands immediate first-aid decompression (manual head elevation, bladder filling, and tocolysis) and definitive delivery via emergency Cesarean section within 10 to 30 minutes.**.
