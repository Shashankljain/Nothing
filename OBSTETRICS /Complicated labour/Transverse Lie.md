# Transverse Lie: Etiology [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transverse Lie**: A major malposition in which the long axis of the fetus lies perpendicularly (or at an acute angle) to the long axis of the centralized uterus or maternal spine, causing the fetal shoulder (acromion process) to present at the pelvic inlet (shoulder presentation; denominator = dorsum/acromion; incidence **~1 in 200 births / 0.5%**).

---

### Etiology / Causes (4–5 High-Yield Bullets) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Multiparity & Lax Abdominal Wall**: The **most common maternal cause** (5 times more frequent in multiparae than primigravidae) resulting from loss of uterine tone and laxity of abdominal muscles, allowing the fetus to fall sideways.
2. **Prematurity & Intrauterine Fetal Death**: High prevalence in preterm gestations because the smaller fetal volume relative to liquor volume permits undue mobility; macerated or dead fetuses lose postural tone, predisposing to transverse orientation.
3. **Placental & Amniotic Fluid Abnormalities**: **Placenta previa** occupying the lower segment prevents the head/breech from engaging in the pelvis; **polyhydramnios** provides excessive room for active fetal rotation.
4. **Pelvic & Uterine Structural Defects**: Contracted pelvis (obstructing head engagement), pelvic tumors (fibroids occupying the lower segment), and congenital uterine malformations (**septate, bicornuate, or arcuate uterus**).
5. **Multiple Gestation**: Very common in twin pregnancies, particularly affecting the **second twin** (~40% incidence) after delivery of the first baby.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Fetal long axis lies perpendicular to the maternal spine (incidence ≈ 1 in 200 births).
- **Most Common Cause**: **Multiparity** with a lax abdominal and uterine wall (5× higher risk).
- **Core Etiological Spectrum**: Prematurity, polyhydramnios, placenta previa, contracted pelvis, uterine anomalies (bicornuate/septate), and twin pregnancy (especially 2nd twin).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Transverse lie is an unstable obstetric malpresentation occurring in 0.5% of pregnancies where the fetal axis lies perpendicular to the maternal spine, primarily caused by multiparity with a lax abdominal wall, prematurity, polyhydramnios, placenta previa, contracted pelvis, or uterine structural malformations.**.
