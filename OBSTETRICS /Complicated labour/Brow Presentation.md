# Brow Presentation: Definition, Etiology, Diagnosis, and Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Brow Presentation**: The rarest cephalic malpresentation in which the fetal head occupies an attitude **midway between full flexion (vertex) and full extension (face)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Presenting Part**: The brow (forehead), bounded superiorly by the anterior fontanelle and coronal sutures, and inferiorly by the supraorbital ridges and root of the nose [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 125; Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Denominator**: **Frontum (Fr) / Forehead** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Incidence**: Extremely rare, occurring in approximately **1 in 1,000 births (≈0.1%)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Clinical Nature**: Highly **unstable and transitory**; in about **10%** of cases early in labor, it spontaneously converts either into a vertex presentation (via flexion) or a face presentation (via extension) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

The causes of persistent brow presentation are identical to those of face presentation, resulting from factors that prevent complete flexion or complete extension of the fetal head [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374]:

1. **Pelvic Architectural Factors (Primary Cause)**: **Flat pelvis (Platypelloid pelvis)** is the most common predisposing factor; the wide biparietal diameter (9.5 cm) gets held in the narrow sacrocotyloid diameter, arresting the head in partial extension [DC Dutta 1, Ch 24, p. 343; Ch 26, p. 374].
2. **Fetal Structural Anomalies**: Congenital fetal goiter, cystic hygroma of the neck, or **anencephaly** (absence of cranial vault prevents normal flexion) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371, 374].
3. **Maternal & Soft Tissue Factors**: High parity with a lax abdominal wall (pendulous abdomen) and lateral obliquity of the uterus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370, 374].
4. **Fetal & Cord Factors**: Prematurity (small head), polyhydramnios (excessive fluid mobility), multiple loops of umbilical cord wrapped tightly around the fetal neck, or a dolichocephalic skull shape [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371, 374].

### 4. Mechanism of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Engaging Anteroposterior Diameter**: **Mentovertical diameter = 14 cm (5.5 inches)**, which extends from the midpoint of the mentum (chin) to the highest point on the sagittal suture [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 132; Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Engaging Transverse Diameter**: Biparietal diameter = **9.5 cm** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

#### Cardinal Rule of Delivery in Brow Presentation [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **No Mechanism in Persistent Brow**: In an average-sized fetus with a normal pelvis, **THERE IS NO MECHANISM OF LABOR FOR A PERSISTENT BROW PRESENTATION** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
    - _Reason_: The **14 cm mentovertical diameter** is significantly larger than any diameter of the maternal pelvic inlet (obstetric conjugate = 10 cm, diagonal conjugate = 12 cm, anatomical transverse = 13 cm) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 128; Ch 26, p. 374].
2. **Rare Exception**: If the fetus is premature/extremely small, the pelvis is unusually spacious, and uterine contractions are strong, an anterior brow position may descend until the root of the nose hinges under the symphysis pubis, delivering by flexion followed by extension (similar to face-to-pubis delivery) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
3. **End Result of Persistent Brow**: If unrecognized or left untreated, persistent brow presentation results in **insuperable OBSTRUCTED LABOR**, Bandl's pathological retraction ring formation, and eventual **uterine rupture** in multiparous women [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374; Ch 27, p. 385].

### 5. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Abdominal Examination

- **Palpation**:
    - Head is high, unengaged, and feels disproportionately large [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
    - **Cephalic Prominence**: The sinciput and occiput are felt at nearly the same horizontal level. The groove between the head and the fetal back is **less prominent** than that seen in face presentation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Auscultation**: Fetal heart sounds (FHR) are heard through the fetal chest on the side of the small parts (limbs) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372, 374].

#### B. Vaginal Examination (PV Findings — Diagnostic Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Palpable Landmarks**:
    - **Frontal suture** lying in the transverse or oblique diameter of the pelvic brim [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
    - **Anterior fontanelle (diamond-shaped)** felt at one end of the frontal suture [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
    - **Supraorbital ridges and root of the nose** felt at the opposite end [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
- **Distinguishing Diagnostic Sign**: **The mouth and chin (mentum) are NOT reachable** by the examining finger [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374]. (If the mouth and chin are palpable, the presentation is face presentation) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372, 374].
- In late labor, marked caput succedaneum formation over the forehead distorts anatomical landmarks [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

#### C. Ultrasonography (Gold Standard Diagnostic Tool)

- **Sonography**: Confirms brow presentation, assesses the precise degree of head extension, measures BPD/estimated fetal weight (EFW), evaluates liquor volume, and rules out fetal structural anomalies (e.g., anencephaly, fetal neck goiter) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

#### A. Management During Pregnancy

- If diagnosed antenatally in an uncomplicated pregnancy, allow the patient to await labor onset, as spontaneous conversion to vertex or face presentation frequently occurs [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

#### B. Management During Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Watchful Expectancy for Spontaneous Conversion**: In early labor with intact membranes, a brief period of observation is permissible. In ≈10% of cases, brow presentation spontaneously converts into vertex (via flexion) or face (via extension) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374]. If conversion occurs, labor is managed according to the new presentation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
2. **Persistent Brow Presentation (Treatment of Choice)**:
    - **Emergency / Elective Cesarean Section (CD)** is the definitive management of choice for all cases of persistent brow presentation at term [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
3. **Contraindicated Maneuvers (Absolute Strict Rules)**:
    - **Manual conversion** of brow to vertex or face presentation during labor is **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** due to high risks of uterine rupture, soft tissue injury, and fetal demise [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
    - **Oxytocin augmentation** and **instrumental extraction (forceps or vacuum)** are **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** in persistent brow presentation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

### 8. Complications & Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

1. **Obstructed Labor & Uterine Rupture**: Persistent brow presentation is a major cause of neglected obstructed labor and uterine rupture, particularly in multiparous women [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374; Ch 27, p. 385].
2. **Severe Genital Tract Trauma**: Extensive vaginal and cervical tears if forced instrumental delivery is attempted [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
3. **Maternal Exhaustion, Intrapartum Sepsis, and Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374; Ch 28, p. 388].

#### B. Fetal Complications

1. **Intrapartum Asphyxia & Fetal Demise**: Caused by prolonged pressure and cord prolapse [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
2. **Severe Caput Succedaneum & Facial Deformity**: Huge caput forms over the forehead, accompanied by extensive facial bruising [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].
3. **Intracranial Hemorrhage & Tentorial Tear**: Caused by extreme molding forces acting on the fetal skull [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374; Ch 33, p. 460].

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 14 cm Mentovertical Rule**: Always remember that the engaging diameter in brow presentation is the **Mentovertical diameter (14 cm)**, which is the **largest anteroposterior diameter of the fetal head**. Because 14 cm exceeds all diameters of a normal pelvic inlet (obstetric conjugate 10 cm, transverse 13 cm), **vaginal delivery is impossible for a term baby in persistent brow presentation**.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Brow presentation** with **Face presentation** on vaginal examination.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - In **Brow presentation**, you can feel the supraorbital ridges and root of the nose at one end, but **the mouth and chin (mentum) are NOT reachable**!
    - In **Face presentation**, you can feel the **mouth with hard alveolar ridges, malar eminences, and the mentum (chin)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372, 374].

    4. **Anterior Fontanelle** — upper boundary of brow area.
