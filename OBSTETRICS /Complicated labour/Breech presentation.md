# Breech Presentation: Definition, Causes, Varieties, Diagnosis, Complications, Mechanism of Labor, Methods of Vaginal Delivery, and Indications for Cesarean Section [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

### 1. Definition & Epidemiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Breech Presentation**: A longitudinal lie in which the podalic pole (buttocks, knees, or feet) of the fetus presents at the pelvic inlet [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
- **Incidence**:
    - At term (**≥37 weeks**): Present in **3% to 4%** of all solitary pregnancies [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
    - At 28 weeks: Present in **20% to 25%** of pregnancies (decreases as gestational age advances due to spontaneous cephalic version) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
- **Clinical Significance**: It is the most common malpresentation in clinical obstetrics, carrying higher perinatal morbidity and mortality compared to cephalic presentation due to risks of cord prolapse, head entrapment, and intracranial birth trauma [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 360].

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Prior to **36 weeks**, the small fetal size relative to the abundant amniotic fluid volume allows the fetus to freely undergo spontaneous version [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355]. Failure of normal cephalic version by 36 weeks is driven by specific maternal, fetal, or placental factors [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355]:

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │     Etiology of Breech Presentation    │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Maternal & Pelvic    │               │ 2. Fetal Factors        │               │ 3. Placental & Liquor   │
│ • Prematurity (m/c)     │               │ • Extended legs (Frank) │               │ • Polyhydramnios        │
│ • Uterine anomalies     │               │ • Fetal hydrocephalus   │               │   (undue mobility)      │
│   (Bicornuate/Septate)  │               │ • Anencephaly / NTD     │               │ • Oligohydramnios       │
│ • Contracted pelvis     │               │ • Chromosomal trisomies │   (prevents turn)       │
│ • Pelvic tumors/fibroids│               │   (Trisomy 13, 18, 21)  │               │ • Placenta previa       │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Maternal & Pelvic Factors

1. **Prematurity**: The **single most common cause** of breech presentation at delivery [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
2. **Uterine Malformations**: Bicornuate, septate, or subseptate uterus restricts normal intra-uterine accommodation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 360].
3. **High Parity & Lax Abdominal Wall**: Multiparae with lax abdominal and uterine muscles allow undue fetal mobility [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
4. **Pelvic Inadequacy / Contracted Pelvis**: Prevents engagement of the larger cephalic pole [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
5. **Pelvic Tumors**: Lower segment fibroids or ovarian cysts occupying the pelvic inlet [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

#### B. Fetal Factors

1. **Fetal Anomalies**: Hydrocephalus (enlarged head occupies the roomier fundus), anencephaly, neural tube defects, and trisomies (13, 18, 21) altering fetal muscular tone [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
2. **Extended Legs (Frank Breech)**: Extended legs act as splints along the fetal body, preventing flexed adaptation and spontaneous version [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356, 360].
3. **Multiple Pregnancy**: Twins or higher-order gestations preventing version due to mutual crowding [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

#### C. Placental & Amniotic Fluid Factors

1. **Hydroamnios (Polyhydramnios)**: Excessive liquor volume leading to undue fetal mobility [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
2. **Oligohydramnios**: Scanty liquor volume preventing active kicking movements needed for version [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356, 360].
3. **Placenta Previa & Cornu-Fundal Implantation**: Lower segment implantation reduces pelvic room, while fundal implantation reduces fundal volume [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
4. **Short Umbilical Cord**: Absolute or relative cord shortening restricting fetal rotation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

#### D. Recurrent / Habitual Breech

- Recurrence of breech in **3 or more consecutive pregnancies** (recurrence rate **8% to 10%**); caused by congenital uterine malformations or repeated cornu-fundal placental implantation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

### 3. Classification & Varieties [⭐ High-Yield]

Breech presentation is divided into **Complete (Flexed)** and **Incomplete** varieties based on the attitude of the fetal lower limbs [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355]:

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │     Varieties of Breech Presentation   │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐                                                         ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Complete (Flexed) Breech│                                                         │ Incomplete Breech       │
│ (~25–30%)               │                                                         │ (~70–75%)               │
│ • Normal flexed posture │                                                         └────────────┬────────────┘
│ • Hips flexed, knees    │                                                                      │
│   flexed                │                     ┌────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ • Feet beside buttocks  │                     ▼                                        ▼                                        ▼
└─────────────────────────┘          ┌─────────────────────┐                  ┌─────────────────────┐                  ┌─────────────────────┐
                                     │ Frank Breech (65–70%)│                  │ Footling Breech     │                  │ Knee Presentation   │
                                     │ • Extended legs     │                  │ • Feet present      │                  │ • Knees present     │
                                     │ • Hips flexed, knees│                  │   below buttocks    │                  │   below buttocks    │
                                     │   extended          │                  │ • Single / Double   │                  │ • Rare variety      │
                                     └─────────────────────┘                  └─────────────────────┘                  └─────────────────────┘
```

1. **Complete (Flexed) Breech (~25–30%)**: Normal fetal attitude maintained; thighs flexed at hips and legs flexed at knees; feet lie adjacent to buttocks [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
2. **Incomplete Breech (~70–75%)**: Deflexion of hips or knees occurs:
    - **Frank Breech (Breech with Extended Legs — 65–70%)**: Most common variety; thighs flexed at hips, knees fully extended along the anterior body trunk; feet lie near the face [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
    - **Footling Presentation**: One (single footling) or both (double footling) feet drop below the level of the buttocks presenting at the os [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
    - **Knee Presentation**: One or both knees present at the os [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].

#### Positions in Breech Presentation (Denominator = Sacrum) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Left Sacroanterior (LSA)**: Sacrum points to left iliopubic eminence (**most common position — ~50%**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **Right Sacroanterior (RSA)**: Sacrum points to right iliopubic eminence [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **Right Sacroposterior (RSP)**: Sacrum points to right sacroiliac joint [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **Left Sacroposterior (LSP)**: Sacrum points to left sacroiliac joint [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

### 5. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Abdominal Examination [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Inspection**: Uterus appears longitudinal; fundal height corresponds to gestational age [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **Palpation**:
    - **Fundal Grip**: A hard, smooth, round, highly ballotable mass (fetal head) is felt in the uterine fundus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
    - **Lateral Grip**: Smooth continuous back felt on one flank; irregular mobile small parts (limbs) felt on the opposite flank [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
    - **Pelvic Grip (First & Second)**: A soft, broad, irregular, non-ballotable mass (breech) is felt occupying the lower uterine segment above the pelvic brim [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **Auscultation**: Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) are heard **at or above the level of the umbilicus** on the side of the fetal back (in engaged frank breech, FHS may be heard lower down) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

#### B. Vaginal Examination (PV) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **During Pregnancy**: High, soft presenting part felt through the fornices [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
- **During Labor**:
    - Smooth, soft buttocks with a linear intergluteal cleft, ischial tuberosities, sacrum, coccyx, and external genitalia are palpable [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
    - Fresh meconium staining on examination finger is common due to abdominal compression [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
    - **Feet Identification**: In complete breech or footling, feet are identified by the **prominence of the heel**, lesser mobility of the great toe, and toes arranged in a straight line (distinguished from hand by lack of opposable thumb and presence of heel) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

#### C. Ultrasonography (Gold Standard Diagnostic Tool) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Diagnostic Role**: Confirms presentation, specifies exact variety (complete, frank, footling), measures BPD/AC for estimated fetal weight (EFW), assesses liquor volume, localizes placenta, and evaluates **fetal head attitude** (flexed vs. hyperextended/"stargazing head") [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

### 7. Mechanism of Labor (Sacroanterior Position) [⭐ High-Yield]

The mechanism of labor involves three distinct, sequential stages for the delivery of the **buttocks**, **shoulders**, and **aftercoming head** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │  Three Stages of Breech Mechanism      │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Delivery of Buttocks │               │ 2. Delivery of Shoulders│               │ 3. Aftercoming Head     │
│ • Bitrochanteric (10 cm)│               │ • Bisacromial (12 cm)   │               │ • Suboccipitobregmatic  │
│   engages in oblique    │               │   engages in oblique    │               │   (9.5 cm) engages      │
│ • Internal rotation 45° │               │ • Internal rotation 45° │               │ • Internal rotation 45° │
│ • Lateral flexion birth │               │ • Lateral flexion birth │ • Delivery by FLEXION   │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Delivery of the Breech / Buttocks [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**: The **bitrochanteric diameter (10 cm)** engages in the oblique pelvic diameter (left oblique in LSA) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
2. **Descent**: Proceeds continuous with uterine contractions [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
3. **Internal Rotation of the Anterior Hip**: The anterior hip touches the pelvic floor first and rotates **1/8th of a circle (45°)** anteriorly to lie behind the symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
4. **Delivery of the Buttocks**: Anterior hip hinges under symphysis pubis; posterior hip sweeps over the perineum by **lateral flexion of the fetal spine** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
5. **Restitution**: Buttocks rotate 1/8th of a circle backward to realign with the original oblique diameter [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].

#### B. Delivery of the Shoulders [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**: The **bisacromial diameter (12 cm)** engages in the same oblique diameter as the buttocks [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
2. **Descent & Internal Rotation**: Shoulders descend; internal rotation brings the anterior shoulder **45°** forward under the symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
3. **Delivery**: Posterior shoulder sweeps over perineum first, followed by anterior shoulder release via lateral flexion of trunk [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357, 360].

#### C. Delivery of the Aftercoming Head [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**: The head enters the pelvic brim in the opposite oblique diameter with the **suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm)** or **suboccipitofrontal (10 cm)** diameter [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
2. **Descent & Flexion**: Head descends maintaining a fully flexed attitude [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
3. **Internal Rotation of Occiput**: The occiput rotates **1/8th of a circle (45°)** anteriorly to lie behind the symphysis pubis (subocciput hinges under pubic arch) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].
4. **Delivery of the Head**: Delivered by **FLEXION**—the chin, mouth, nose, forehead, vertex, and occiput successively emerge over the anterior perineal margin [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 357].

### 8. Methods of Vaginal Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

There are three distinct clinical methods for conducting vaginal breech delivery [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361]:

#### A. Spontaneous Breech Delivery (10%)

- Fetus is expelled completely by maternal efforts without any operative assistance or traction (not recommended as routine practice) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

#### B. Assisted Vaginal Breech Delivery (AVBD — Standard Method of Choice) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Core Principle**: **"NO TOUCH TO THE FETUS"** until the breech delivers spontaneously up to the umbilicus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361–362]. **NEVER PULL FROM BELOW, ALWAYS PUSH FROM ABOVE** via maternal expulsive forces [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

##### Stepwise Conduct of AVBD [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Preparation**: Patient in lithotomy position (15° lateral tilt); empty bladder; perform **liberal mediolateral episiotomy** under pudendal/epidural anesthesia when posterior buttock distends perineum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361–362].
2. **Delivery of Extended Legs (Pinard's Maneuver)**:
    - If legs are extended (Frank breech), press two fingers into the **popliteal fossa** in a direction of **abduction and flexion** of the thigh; the knee flexes spontaneously and the foot slips down to be delivered [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362, 364].
3. **Delivery of Extended Arms (Lovset's Maneuver — Standard Choice)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Indication_: Extended arms or nuchal displacement of arms [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
    - _Principle_: Utilizes pelvic curvature; when anterior shoulder is above symphysis, posterior shoulder is below sacral promontory [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
    - _Procedure_: Hold fetal trunk by femoropelvic grip (thumbs on sacrum); rotate body **180°** keeping back anterior under downward traction; the posterior shoulder rotates to anterior under symphysis and is delivered under the pubic arch. The body is then rotated back **180°** in opposite direction to deliver the other shoulder [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364–365].
4. **Delivery of Aftercoming Head (Three Classical Methods)** [⭐ High-Yield]:

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │  Delivery of Aftercoming Fetal Head    │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                         ▼                                         ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Burns-Marshall       │               │ 2. Modified Mauriceau-  │               │ 3. Piper / Das Forceps  │
│    Method               │               │    Smellie-Veit (MSV)   │               │    Application          │
│ • Baby hangs by weight  │               │ • Baby straddles arm    │               │ • Head in cavity        │
│ • Nape of neck at pubis │               │ • Index/middle fingers  │               │ • Piper forceps applied │
│ • Ankles held; swung in │               │   on malar bones (flexion│               │   from below            │
│   wide arc over abdomen │               │ • Subpubic traction     │               │ • Controlled delivery   │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

- **Burns-Marshall Method**: Baby is allowed to hang by its own weight until the nape of the neck (subocciput) appears under symphysis pubis; operator grips baby's ankles with one finger in between and **swings the trunk in a wide arc upward and forward over the maternal abdomen** while guarding the perineum with the other hand [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–363].
- **Modified Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) Technique**: Fetal body straddles operator's supinated forearm; middle finger placed in fetal mouth (or index and middle fingers over **malar eminences** to maintain head flexion without fracturing mandible); index and ring fingers of other hand placed over shoulders on either side of neck to apply downward and backward traction until subocciput hinges under pubis, then trunk swung upward [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].
- **Forceps to Aftercoming Head (Piper / Standard Forceps)**: **Safest method** for aftercoming head; baby's body held horizontal by assistant; specialized **Piper forceps** (long shank, reverse pelvic curve) applied from below to deliver head slowly over **1 minute** to prevent sudden compression-decompression intracranial injury [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363, 366].

#### C. Breech Extraction (Operative Manual Extraction)

- **Definition**: Operative extraction of the entire fetal body by the obstetrician [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
- **Indications**: (1) Delivery of second twin in transverse lie/breech after Internal Podalic Version (IPV), (2) Sudden cord prolapse in full dilation, (3) Severe acute fetal distress with fully dilated cervix [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

### 9. Indications for Cesarean Section in Breech Presentation [⭐ High-Yield]

Modern obstetrics favors planned elective Cesarean Section (CD) for breech at **≥38 weeks** to reduce perinatal mortality [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360–361]. Overall CS rate for breech ranges from **80% to 100%** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].

```
                               ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                               │   Indications for Cesarean Section     │
                               └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐                                                         ┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Elective CS (at >=38 wks)│                                                         │ Emergency CS in Labor   │
│ • EFW >3.5 kg or <1.5 kg│                                                         │ • Failure of progress   │
│ • Hyperextended head    │                                                         │   (no breech descent   │
│   ("stargazing fetus")  │                                                         │   after 2h full dil.)   │
│ • Footling presentation │                                                         │ • Fetal distress (EFM)  │
│ • Contracted pelvis /   │                                                         │ • Cord prolapse         │
│   Prior CS / Primigravida│                                                        │ • Cervical dystocia     │
└─────────────────────────┘                                                         └─────────────────────────┘
```

#### A. Elective Cesarean Section Indications (at ≥38 Weeks) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Fetal Weight Extremes**: Estimated fetal weight **>3.5 kg** (macrosomia risk) or **<1.5 kg** (very low birth weight / extreme prematurity) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360–361].
2. **Deflexed / Hyperextended Head ("Stargazing Fetus")**: Risk of cervical spinal cord injury, vertebral fracture, and medullary hemorrhage during vaginal birth [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358, 360].
3. **Footling Presentation**: High risk of cord prolapse (**10–15%**) and head entrapment through partially dilated cervix [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360–361].
4. **Pelvic Inadequacy / Contracted Pelvis**: Any degree of inlet, mid-cavity, or outlet contraction [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360].
5. **Associated Obstetric Complications**: Pre-eclampsia, placenta previa, FGR with abnormal Doppler (AEDV/REDV), previous uterine scar/CS, elderly primigravida, or pregnancy following ART [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 360–361].

#### B. Emergency Cesarean Section Indications (During Labor) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Arrest of Labor Progress**: Failure of breech to reach the perineum after **2 hours of full cervical dilatation** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
2. **Cord Prolapse**: Occurring prior to full cervical dilatation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
3. **Non-Reassuring Fetal Heart Rate Pattern**: Persistent fetal distress on electronic fetal monitoring [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

### 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

1. **Genital Tract Trauma**: Extensive cervical tears, 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears, vaginal wall lacerations [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358; Shaw 2, Ch 35, p. 453].
2. **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Uterine atony secondary to prolonged labor or traumatic lacerations [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].
3. **Increased Surgical Morbidity**: Morbidity associated with emergency intrapartum CS or operative vaginal procedures [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].

#### B. Fetal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Intrapartum Perinatal Mortality & Asphyxia**:
    - **Cord Compression**: Compression of umbilical cord between the aftercoming head and pelvic brim during head descent [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358, 360].
    - **Cord Prolapse**: Increased incidence (0.5% in complete, **10–15% in footling**) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356, 360].
2. **Head Entrapment**: Entrapment of the larger aftercoming head through an incompletely dilated cervix (especially in preterm babies where head diameter > body diameter) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358, 364].
3. **Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Tear of **tentorium cerebelli** and subarachnoid hemorrhage secondary to rapid compression-decompression forces on the unmolded aftercoming head [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].
4. **Spinal Cord & Visceral Trauma**: Hyperextended head causing cervical spine fracture/transection; rupture of fetal liver, spleen, or adrenal glands from improper grasping of fetal abdomen [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358, 360].
5. **Fractures & Nerve Palsies**: Fracture of femur, humerus, or clavicle during limb delivery; **Erb-Duchenne palsy (C5–C6)** or Klumpke's palsy from excessive neck traction [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358; Ch 33, p. 460].

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The "No Touch" Policy & Femoropelvic Grip**: During assisted vaginal breech delivery, **NEVER touch or pull the baby** until the breech has delivered up to the umbilicus. Once delivered to the umbilicus, hold the baby strictly by the **femoropelvic grip** (thumbs parallel over sacrum and fingers wrapping around pelvis/thighs). **NEVER grip the soft fetal abdomen**, as aggressive abdominal compression ruptures the fetal liver, spleen, or kidneys!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Pulling on the fetus from below when labor slows down, OR confusing **Lovset's maneuver** with **Pinard's maneuver**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Traction from below causes deflexion of the head and nuchal displacement of arms**, resulting in catastrophic head entrapment! Always push from above (maternal bearing down + suprapubic assistant pressure).
    - **Pinard's maneuver** is used for **extended legs** (popliteal fossa pressure). **Lovset's maneuver** is used for **extended arms** (180° trunk rotation).

### 13. Diagram Guides

### Diagram 1 : Varieties of Breech Presentation

- **Type**: Morphological schematic diagram [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355].
- **Outline shape to draw**: Three fetal ovoid outlines inside uterine cavity.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Complete Breech** — thighs flexed, knees flexed, feet beside buttocks.
    2. **Frank Breech** — thighs flexed, knees extended along abdomen (feet near head).
    3. **Footling Breech** — single or double feet presenting below buttocks.
- **Annotation**: _"Frank breech is the most common variety (65–70%), carrying the lowest risk of cord prolapse (0.5%), whereas footling carries 10–15% risk of cord prolapse."_ [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 360].

### Diagram 2 : Delivery of Aftercoming Head by Burns-Marshall Method

- **Type**: Operative technique diagram [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].
- **Outline shape to draw**: Sagittal pelvic section with baby hanging downward and then swung upward over mother's abdomen.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Hanging Fetal Body** — body allowed to hang until subocciput appears at pubic arch.
    2. **Ankle Grip** — operator holding ankles with index finger between feet.
    3. **Upward Arc Traction** — trunk swung in a wide arc upward over maternal abdomen.
    4. **Perineal Guarding Hand** — left hand protecting perineum as face and vertex emerge.
- **Annotation**: _"Burns-Marshall method delivers the aftercoming head by swinging the fetal body in a wide arc upward over the mother's abdomen once the subocciput hinges under the symphysis pubis."_ [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].

#### Mnemonic: B-R-E-E-C-H (Management & Delivery Checklist)

- **B**: **Burns-Marshall & Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit** (methods for delivering aftercoming head).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Varieties**: Podalic presentation; Frank breech (65–70% m/c; hips flexed, knees extended), Complete breech (25–30%), Footling breech (10–15% cord prolapse risk).
- **Etiology**: Prematurity (most common overall), uterine anomalies, poly/oligohydramnios, hydrocephalus, placenta previa, extended legs.
- **Diagnosis**: Fundal grip = hard ballotable head; FHS heard at or above umbilicus; PV shows soft buttocks/ischial tuberosities/feet; USG is gold standard.
- **Golden Rule of AVBD**: **"No touch"** policy until umbilicus delivers; **never pull from below, push from above**; femoropelvic grip only (never compress abdomen).
- **Delivery Maneuvers**: Pinard's (extended legs), Lovset's (extended arms), Burns-Marshall / Modified MSV / Piper forceps (aftercoming head).
- **Cesarean Indications**: EFW >3.5 kg or <1.5 kg, hyperextended head, footling presentation, contracted pelvis, non-progress after 2h full dilation.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Breech presentation is the most common obstetric malpresentation—occurring in 3% to 4% of term pregnancies—characterized by the podalic pole presenting at the pelvic inlet, requiring systematic diagnosis via abdominal palpation and ultrasound, and managed either by elective Cesarean section or assisted vaginal breech delivery using strict non-traction principles and specialized maneuvers (Pinard's, Lovset's, and Burns-Marshall/Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit).** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 360–363].
