# Assisted Vaginal Breech Delivery (AVBD) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Assisted Vaginal Breech Delivery (AVBD)**: A planned method of vaginal breech delivery in which the expulsion of the fetal body up to the umbilicus occurs spontaneously by natural uterine contractions and maternal expulsive efforts, while manual assistance is provided by the obstetrician for the delivery of the extended limbs, shoulders, and the aftercoming head [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
- **Clinical Role**: It is the **standard method of choice** for all planned vaginal breech deliveries (accounts for ~90% of vaginal breech births) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

---

### 2. Selection Criteria & Prerequisites (Zatuchni-Andros Score ≥4) [⭐ High-Yield]

To ensure safety and minimize perinatal morbidity, specific clinical criteria must be satisfied prior to undertaking AVBD [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361]:

#### A. Favorable Selection Criteria [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Presentation & Attitude**: Frank breech or complete breech with a **well-flexed fetal head**(hyperextension/"stargazing head" ruled out by USG) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
2. **Estimated Fetal Weight (EFW)**: **2.0 kg to 3.5 kg** (2000–3500 g) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
3. **Gestational Age**: **>34 completed weeks** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
4. **Pelvic Adequacy**: Clinical or radiological confirmation of an adequate pelvis without CPD [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
5. **Zatuchni-Andros Prognostic Score**: **Score ≥4** (evaluates parity, gestational age, EFW, prior breech birth, cervical dilatation, and station; score <4 predicts high failure rate) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
6. **Institutional Prerequisites**: Presence of an experienced obstetrician, anesthetist, and neonatologist, with facilities for rapid emergency Cesarean section (<30 minutes) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

---

### 3. Core Principles of Conduct [⭐ High-Yield]

Four cardinal principles govern safe conduct [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361]:

1. **"NO TOUCH TO THE FETUS" Policy**: Never touch or pull the baby until the breech and trunk deliver spontaneously up to the umbilicus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361–362].
2. **"NEVER PULL FROM BELOW, ALWAYS PUSH FROM ABOVE"**: Avoid premature traction from below, which causes deflexion of the head and nuchal displacement of arms; rely on maternal bearing-down efforts combined with assistant suprapubic pressure [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
3. **Keep Back Anterior**: Always maintain the fetal back in an anterior orientation relative to the maternal pelvis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
4. **Patience**: Allow natural uterine forces to accomplish descent and cervical dilatation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

#### A. Preparation & Positioning

- Patient placed in lithotomy position with a **15° left lateral tilt** when the posterior buttock distends the perineum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
- Bladder emptied using an "in-and-out" catheter [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].
- Administer pudendal nerve block / epidural analgesia and perform a **liberal mediolateral episiotomy** under local 1% Lignocaine infiltration [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361].

#### B. Delivery of Extended Legs (Pinard's Maneuver) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Indication_: Extended legs in Frank breech preventing spontaneous foot delivery [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
- _Technique_: Insert two fingers along the fetal thigh to press into the **popliteal fossa** in a direction of **abduction and flexion of the thigh**; the knee flexes spontaneously, allowing the foot to slip down and be caught at the ankle [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].

#### C. Femoropelvic Grip

- Once delivered to the umbilicus, hold the baby strictly by grasping the pelvic girdle with **thumbs parallel over the sacrum** and fingers wrapping around the thighs/hips [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 361–362]. _(NEVER compress the soft fetal abdominal organs!)_.

#### D. Delivery of Extended Arms (Lovset's Maneuver) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Indication_: Extended arms or nuchal displacement of arms [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
- _Principle_: Leverages pelvic inclination—when the anterior shoulder is above the symphysis, the posterior shoulder lies below the sacral promontory [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
- _Technique_:
    - **Step 1**: Hold baby by femoropelvic grip; maintain downward traction and rotate fetal body **180°** keeping the back anterior; the posterior shoulder rotates to anterior under the symphysis pubis and the arm is hooked out [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364–365].
    - **Step 2**: Rotate body back **180°** in the reverse direction to deliver the remaining shoulder under the pubic arch [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364–365].

#### E. Delivery of Aftercoming Head (Three Standard Methods) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Burns-Marshall Method [⭐ High-Yield]

- Fetus is allowed to hang by its own weight while an assistant applies downward suprapubic pressure to maintain head flexion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–363].
- When the nape of the neck (subocciput) appears under the pubic arch, the operator holds the fetal ankles with one finger in between and **swings the trunk in a wide arc upward and forward over the maternal abdomen**, delivering the face and vertex over the perineum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–363].

##### 2. Modified Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) Technique [⭐ High-Yield]

- Fetal trunk straddles the operator's supinated forearm; middle and index fingers placed over **malar eminences**(cheeks) to maintain head flexion without fracturing the mandible [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].
- Index and ring fingers of the other hand placed over the shoulders on either side of the neck to apply downward and backward traction until subocciput hinges under pubis, then trunk swung upward [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].

##### 3. Forceps to Aftercoming Head (Piper / Standard Forceps) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Safest & Most Controlled Method**: Head brought low into cavity; assistant holds baby's body horizontal [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363, 366].
- **Piper forceps** (long shank, reverse pelvic curve) applied from below to deliver the head slowly over **1 minute** to prevent sudden compression-decompression tentorial tears [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363, 366].

### 6. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications

- Extensive cervical lacerations, 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears (OASI), and postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].

#### B. Fetal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Intrapartum Asphyxia & Cord Compression**: Nape-to-mouth delivery time exceeding **5 to 10 minutes** causes severe hypoxia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358, 364].
2. **Head Entrapment**: Entrapment of larger aftercoming head through an incompletely dilated cervix [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].
3. **Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Tentorial membrane tear secondary to rapid head decompression [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].
4. **Fetal Traumatic Injuries**: Femur/clavicle/humerus fractures; visceral rupture (liver/spleen) from abdominal grasping; Erb-Duchenne brachial plexus palsy (C5–C6) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 358].

### 8. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 5-to-10 Minute Golden Window**: Once the fetal umbilicus has emerged from the vulva, the umbilical cord is compressed between the aftercoming head and the pelvic brim. **The delivery of the mouth MUST be completed within 5 to 10 minutes** to prevent irreversible fetal hypoxic encephalopathy or death!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Attempting traction from below when labor slows, OR placing fingers inside the fetal mouth during Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) maneuver.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Traction from below causes head deflexion and nuchal arm displacement**! Always push from above (maternal bearing down + suprapubic pressure).
    - In **Modified MSV**, fingers are placed over **malar eminences** (cheeks), NOT inside the mouth, to avoid mandibular fracture and soft tissue trauma!

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-L-A-N (Key AVBD Maneuvers)

- **P**: **Pinard's Maneuver** (for extended legs in Frank breech).
- **L**: **Lovset's Maneuver** (180° trunk rotation for extended/nuchal arms).
- **A**: **Aftercoming Head Delivery** (Burns-Marshall / Modified MSV / Piper forceps).
- **N**: **"No Touch" Policy** (until buttocks/trunk deliver up to umbilicus).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Spontaneous expulsion of breech to umbilicus, followed by assisted delivery of limbs, shoulders, and aftercoming head [DC Dutta 1, p. 361].
- **Prerequisites**: EFW 2.0–3.5 kg, gestational age >34 wks, flexed head, adequate pelvis, Zatuchni-Andros score ≥4 [DC Dutta 1, p. 361].
- **Golden Rule**: **"No touch"** policy till umbilicus; **push from above, never pull from below**; maintain back anterior [DC Dutta 1, p. 361].
- **Key Maneuvers**: Pinard's (legs), Lovset's (arms), Burns-Marshall / Modified MSV / Piper forceps (head) [DC Dutta 1, p. 362–365].
- **Critical Timing**: Delivery of mouth within **5–10 minutes** of cord emergence to prevent fatal hypoxia [DC Dutta 1, p. 364].
