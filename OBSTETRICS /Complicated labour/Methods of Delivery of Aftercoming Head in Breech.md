# Methods of Delivery of Aftercoming Head in Breech: Common & Best Methods [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Delivery of the Aftercoming Head**: The final and most critical phase of assisted vaginal breech delivery, occurring after the birth of the shoulders, where the flexed fetal skull must navigate the pelvic canal and be delivered within a strict window of **5 to 10 minutes** from umbilical cord emergence to prevent fetal hypoxia and asphyxia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362].

---

### Key Methods of Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Available Clinical Methods**: Include the **Burns-Marshall method**, **Modified Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) technique**, **Obstetric Forceps (Piper / Das forceps)**, and **Prague's maneuver** (for unrotated occipitoposterior head) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–363, 368].
- **Most Common Method — Burns-Marshall Method** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Technique_: The baby is allowed to hang by its own weight while an assistant applies downward/backward suprapubic pressure over the sinciput to maintain head flexion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362].
    - _Expulsion_: Once the subocciput (nape of neck) hinges under the pubic arch, the operator holds the fetal ankles with a finger in between and **swings the trunk in a wide arc upward and forward over the maternal abdomen** to deliver the face and vertex over the perineum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–363].
- **Best & Safest Method — Forceps Application (Piper / Long Curved Forceps)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Why it is Best_: Traction force is applied **directly to the fetal skull** rather than being transmitted through the neck and spinal cord, preventing brachial plexus and spinal injuries [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].
    - _Advantages_: Maintains head flexion, enables slow and controlled delivery over **1 minute** (preventing rapid compression-decompression tentorial tears), and allows effective airway suctioning prior to complete birth [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363, 366].
- **Modified Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) Technique**:
    - The baby straddles the operator's forearm; index and middle fingers are placed over the **malar eminences**(cheeks) to maintain head flexion without fracturing the mandible, while the other hand applies downward shoulder traction until the subocciput hinges under the pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 363].
- **Prague's Maneuver (For Special Circumstance)**:
    - Reserved for the rare **persistent occipitoposterior position of the aftercoming head**, where the fetal body is swung upward toward the maternal abdomen to flex and deliver the head as face-to-pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 368].

---

### Quick Revision

- **Critical Time Window**: Nape-to-mouth delivery must be completed within **5 to 10 minutes** of cord emergence [DC Dutta 1, p. 362].
- **Most Common Method**: **Burns-Marshall method** (hang by weight → suprapubic pressure → 180° upward arc over mother's abdomen) [DC Dutta 1, p. 362].
- **Best / Safest Method**: **Piper Forceps** (direct cranial traction, eliminates neck traction, controlled delivery over 1 min) [DC Dutta 1, p. 363].
- **Modified MSV Technique**: Malar flexion (fingers on cheek bones) + shoulder traction [DC Dutta 1, p. 363].

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Delivery of the aftercoming head in breech presentation must be completed within 5 to 10 minutes of cord emergence, most commonly performed via the Burns-Marshall method, while the application of specialized Piper forceps represents the best and safest method by exerting traction directly on the fetal skull to eliminate neck strain and intracranial decompression trauma.** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 362–366].
