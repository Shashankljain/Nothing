# Face-to-Pubis Delivery: Mechanics, Diagnosis, and Clinical Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Face-to-Pubis Delivery**: A secondary vaginal delivery mechanism of a vertex presentation in persistent occipitoposterior (POP / occipitosacral) position, where the fetal head descends through the vulval outlet with the **root of the nose (nasion) hinging under the symphysis pubis**, delivering the brow, vertex, and occiput over the perineum by **flexion**, followed by the face by **extension** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351–352].
- **Incidence**: Occurs in approximately **5% to 10%** of all occipitoposterior positions when anterior rotation fails [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351].
- **Prerequisites for Spontaneous Birth**: Average or small fetal size, strong uterine contractions, and a spacious pelvis (spacious gynecoid or anthropoid pelvis) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351].

---

### 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Face-to-pubis delivery results from **malrotation (1/8th circle posterior rotation)** of an occipitoposterior position [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351]:

1. **Extreme Deflexion of the Fetal Head (Primary Trigger)**: Severe deflexion causes the sinciput to land lower than the occiput on the pelvic floor; by Hart's rule of pelvic floor recoil, the **sinciput rotates 1/8th of a circle (45°) anteriorly**, forcing the **occiput 1/8th of a circle (45°) posteriorly into the sacral hollow (Occipitosacral position)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351].
2. **Pelvic Architectural Favorability**: An **Anthropoid pelvis** (with its long anteroposterior diameter and narrow transverse diameter) or a spacious gynecoid pelvis accommodates the broad occiput in the sacral hollow [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 343, 351].
3. **Inadequate Expulsive Forces**: Weak contractions fail to promote secondary head flexion required for long anterior rotation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 350].

### 4. Mechanism of Labor (Detailed Stepwise Description) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Stepwise Movements [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Descent & Engagement**: Head descends in the occipitosacral position with persistent deflexion. The engaging diameter is the **occipitofrontal (11.5 cm)** or **suboccipitofrontal (10 cm)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 347, 351].
2. **Hinging at Symphysis Pubis**: Descent continues until the **root of the nose (nasion)** comes to lie directly beneath and hinges under the symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352].
3. **Delivery by FLEXION (First Phase)**:
    - Strong uterine contractions force the head down against perineal resistance.
    - The head undergoes **FLEXION** around the subpubic hinge, causing the **brow, vertex, and occiput** to emerge successively over the anterior margin of the perineum [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352].
4. **Delivery by EXTENSION (Second Phase)**:
    - Once the occiput escapes over the perineum, the head falls backward, and the **face (eyes, nose, mouth, and chin)** is born under the pubic arch by a movement of **EXTENSION** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352].
5. **Restitution**: The head untwists by rotating **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the direction opposite to internal rotation (face turns toward maternal left thigh in ROP, right thigh in LOP) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352].
6. **External Rotation & Expulsion**: The head rotates another **1/8th of a circle (45°)** as the anterior shoulder rotates forward under the symphysis pubis; shoulders and trunk are born by **lateral flexion** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 348, 352].

#### B. Distending Diameters & Perineal Hazard [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vulval Outlet Distention**: The vulval outlet is stretched simultaneously by the **occipitofrontal diameter (11.5 cm)**and the **biparietal diameter (9.5 cm)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352, 355].
- _Perineal Risk_: Because 11.5 cm distends the introitus (instead of the 9.5 cm suboccipitobregmatic diameter seen in normal OA), **face-to-pubis delivery carries an extreme risk of 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears (OASI)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352, 355].

### 5. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Abdominal Examination Findings

- **Inspection**: Lower abdomen is **flattened below the umbilicus** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 344].
- **Palpation**: Fetal limbs easily felt on both sides of midline; fetal back felt far out in the maternal flank [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 344].
- **Auscultation**: Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) heard far out in the flank, or clear in the midline in direct OP [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 344].

#### B. Vaginal Examination (PV Findings — Diagnostic Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Anterior Fontanelle (Bregma)**: Easily felt anteriorly directly behind the symphysis pubis due to head deflexion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 344, 351].
- **Posterior Fontanelle**: Felt posteriorly in the sacral hollow [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 344, 351].
- **Sagittal Suture**: Lies in the anteroposterior diameter of the pelvic outlet [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351].

### 7. Treatment & Management Plan [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Spontaneous Conduct of Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Watchful Expectancy**: If progress is satisfactory and fetal heart rate is reassuring, allow spontaneous descent [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 359].
2. **Liberal Mediolateral Episiotomy (Mandatory Rule)**: Must be performed under local 1% Lignocaine infiltration as the head distends the perineum to prevent 3rd and 4th-degree complete perineal tears [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355, 363; Ch 37, p. 525].

#### B. Management of Occipitosacral Arrest (Second Stage Arrest) [⭐ High-Yield]

- If descent arrests below the ischial spines (station ≥ +2):
    - **Unrotated Face-to-Pubis Forceps Delivery**: Low forceps blades applied equidistant from sinciput and occiput. Traction directed **horizontally** until root of nose hinges under pubis, then **upward and forward** to deliver occiput by flexion, and finally **downward** to release the face [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 366; Ch 37, p. 524].
    - **Ventouse Extraction**: Cup applied over Flexion Point [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 364].
- If head is unengaged or at/above ischial spines: **Emergency Cesarean Section (CD)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 366].

### 8. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Severe Obstetric Anal Sphincter Injuries (OASI)**: 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears secondary to the large 11.5 cm occipitofrontal diameter distention [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352, 355].
2. **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: From uterine atony (prolonged labor) or extensive soft tissue tears [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 355; Ch 28, p. 388].

#### B. Fetal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Tentorial Tear & Intracranial Hemorrhage**: Compression of occipitofrontal diameter causes vertical vault elongation, straining the falx cerebri and tearing the tentorium cerebelli [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].
2. **Severe Caput Succedaneum & Facial Bruising**: Extensive molding over frontal and parietal bones [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 356].

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Reverse Delivery Sequence Rule**: In normal OA delivery, the head is born by **extension first** (subocciput under pubis). In face-to-pubis delivery, the sequence is **REVERSED**: the head is born by **FLEXION first** (brow, vertex, and occiput sweep over perineum while root of nose hinges under pubis), and **THEN extension** (face drops backward out of pubic arch) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 352]!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Face-to-Pubis Delivery** with **Face Presentation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Face-to-Pubis** is a **vertex presentation** (occipitosacral position) where the face faces anteriorly under the pubic bone during birth [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 351].
    - **Face Presentation** is a **hyperextended head presentation** where the **mentum (chin)** is the presenting part/denominator [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370].

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Delivery in persistent OP where root of nose hinges under pubis, delivering head by **flexion first, then extension** [DC Dutta 1, p. 351–352].
- **Hinge Point**: Root of nose (nasion) under symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, p. 352].
- **Distending Diameter**: Occipitofrontal diameter (**11.5 cm**) + Biparietal diameter (9.5 cm) [DC Dutta 1, p. 352, 355].
- **Key PV Sign**: Anterior fontanelle felt anteriorly behind symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, p. 344].
- **Management**: **Liberal mediolateral episiotomy** is mandatory; low forceps / Ventouse for arrest [DC Dutta 1, p. 355, 366].
- **Major Complications**: 3rd/4th-degree perineal tears (OASI) and fetal tentorial tear [DC Dutta 1, p. 352, 356].
