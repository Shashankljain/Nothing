# Complications of Third Stage of Labor & Management of Acute Uterine Inversion [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks**

---

### 1. Definition & Overview of Third Stage [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition

- **Third Stage of Labor**: The period of labor beginning immediately after the complete delivery of the fetus and ending with the complete separation and expulsion of the placenta and fetal membranes.
- **Normal Duration**: Averages **15 minutes** in both primigravidae and multiparae under expectant management, shortened to **5 minutes** under Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL).
- **Pathological Threshold**: Third stage is considered prolonged if not completed within **30 minutes**.

---

### 2. Complications of the Third Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

The third stage is the most critical stage of labor for maternal survival. An uneventful first and second stage can rapidly transform into a life-threatening catastrophe.

#### Major Complications List [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Leading cause of maternal mortality worldwide. Classified into Atonic (80%), Traumatic (10–15%), Retained Tissue (5–10%), and Coagulopathy (1–2%).
2. **Retained Placenta**: Non-expulsion of placenta >30 minutes post-delivery due to uterine atony, constriction ring, or Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS).
3. **Acute Inversion of the Uterus**: Rare (1 in 20,000 deliveries) life-threatening catastrophe where the uterus turns inside out.
4. **Obstetric Shock**: Hemorrhagic shock (acute blood loss in PPH) or Non-hemorrhagic / Neurogenic shock (cervical stretching and peritoneal traction in acute uterine inversion).
5. **Pulmonary Embolism**: Sudden collapse due to Amniotic Fluid Embolism (AFE) or venous air embolism.
6. **Lacerations & Genital Tract Injuries**: Cervical, vaginal, perineal, or lower segment tears.

### 3. Acute Inversion of the Uterus: Definition & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition

- **Acute Uterine Inversion**: An obstetric emergency in which the uterine fundus turns inside out through the cervical os into the vagina or outside the vulva, occurring within **24 hours of delivery**.
- **Incidence**: Approximately **1 in 20,000 deliveries**.

#### B. Classification / Degrees of Inversion [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **First Degree (Incomplete)**: Dimpling or cupping of the uterine fundus; fundus remains above the level of the internal cervical os.
2. **Second Degree (Incomplete)**: Inverted fundus projects through the external cervical os into the vagina.
3. **Third Degree (Complete)**: Entire uterine fundus and endometrium (with or without attached placenta) protrudes outside the vulva.

### 4. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Uterine inversion requires two prerequisite conditions: (1) Fundal myometrial atony/relaxation, and (2) Cervical dilation combined with downward traction or increased intra-abdominal pressure.

#### A. Spontaneous Causes (40%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fundal Attachment of Placenta (75%)**: Implantation of the placenta directly over the fundus.
- Localized fundal atony with sharp rise in intra-abdominal pressure (coughing, sneezing, bearing-down).
- Short umbilical cord (absolute or relative due to coiling) and morbidly adherent placenta.

#### B. Iatrogenic / Induced Causes (60% — Mismanagement of 3rd Stage) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Traction on Umbilical Cord**: Pulling the cord when the uterus is atonic and uncontracted.
- **Fundal Pressure (Credé Maneuver)**: Applying strong downward fundal pressure while the uterus is relaxed.
- Manual separation of a fundally attached placenta.

### 6. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Symptoms

- **Acute Lower Abdominal Pain**: Agonizing pelvic pain with persistent bearing-down sensation.
- **Sudden Postpartum Collapse**: Rapid onset of severe shock.

#### B. Physical Signs

- **Profound Shock Out of Proportion to Blood Loss [⭐ High-Yield]**: Combination of neurogenic shock (peritoneal/pelvic nerve traction) and hemorrhagic shock (open placental sinuses).
- **Abdominal Examination**:
    - **Cupping / Dimpling** felt at the fundal site.
    - Complete **absence of the round uterine fundus** on abdominal palpation.
- **Vaginal / Vulval Examination**:
    - Pear-shaped, reddish-purple, soft, bleeding mass protruding outside the introitus (in 3rd degree complete inversion) or occupying the vagina (in 2nd degree).
    - Placenta may still be partially or completely attached to the inverted mass.

#### STEP 1: Immediate Resuscitation & Safety Rules [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Call for Extra Assistance**: Activate multidisciplinary emergency team (Senior Obstetrician, Anesthetist, Hematologist).
- **Airway & Circulation**: Administer 100% O₂; insert two large-bore IV cannulae (**14G/16G**); rapidly infuse warm Normal Saline / Ringer's Lactate and cross-matched blood.
- **MANDATORY SAFETY RULE [⭐ High-Yield]**: **DO NOT administer oxytocic drugs (Oxytocin / Methergine) BEFORE repositioning the uterus!** Uterotonics cause tetanic contraction of the cervical ring, making manual replacement impossible.
- **Placental Management Rule**: **DO NOT peel off an attached placenta before replacement** unless it is already completely detached or hugely bulky, as peeling off an inverted placenta causes uncontrolled catastrophic hemorrhage.

#### STEP 2: Immediate Manual Replacement (Johnson's Maneuver) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Anesthesia**: Performed under **General Anesthesia (GA)** using halogenated agents (Halothane/Isoflurane) to ensure complete uterine ring relaxation.
- **Technique (Johnson's Method)**:
    1. The entire gloved hand is introduced into the vagina.
    2. The inverted fundus is held in the palm of the hand, with finger tips positioned at the cervicovaginal junction.
    3. Firm, steady upward pressure is exerted along the axis of the pelvic inlet.
    4. **Golden Principle**: **"The part that was inverted last is replaced first"** (cervicovaginal junction replaced first, followed by lower segment, and fundus last).
    5. The external hand provides abdominal counter-support over the pelvic brim.

#### STEP 3: Hydrostatic Replacement Method (O'Sullivan's Method) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Indicated when manual replacement fails or GA is delayed.
- **Technique**:
    1. Inverted uterus is placed back into the vagina.
    2. A douche nozzle connected to a reservoir of **warm sterile normal saline (up to 5 L)** placed 3 feet above patient level is inserted into the posterior vaginal fornix.
    3. The vaginal introitus is blocked tightly around the nozzle using the operator's palms or a silicon ventouse cup.
    4. Fluid distends the vagina; the resulting high intravaginal hydrostatic pressure stretches the vaginal walls and pushes the fundus upward into its normal position.

#### STEP 4: Surgical Replacement Methods (For Refractory Inversion) [⭐ High-Yield]

If tight cervical constriction ring prevents manual/hydrostatic replacement, proceed to surgical intervention:

1. **Abdominal Operations (Laparotomy)**:
    - **Huntington's Operation**: Laparotomy → Serial clamping of the inverted cup with Allis forceps on the round ligaments and upward traction while an assistant pushes the fundus up vaginally.
    - **Haultain's Operation**: Laparotomy → Incise the posterior wall of the cervical constriction ring vertically → releases ring spasm → fundus easily replaced → cervical incision sutured.
2. **Vaginal Operations**:
    - **Spinelli's Operation**: Incision of the anterior cervical ring via vaginal route.
    - **Kustner's Operation**: Incision of the posterior cervical ring via vaginal route.

#### STEP 5: Post-Replacement Care [⭐ High-Yield]

1. Retain the hand inside the uterine cavity until **Inj. Oxytocin 20 IU IV drip** or **Inj. Methergine 0.2 mg IV** induces a firm, tetanic myometrial contraction.
2. Perform **Manual Removal of Placenta (MRP)** only AFTER the uterus is replaced and fully contracted.
3. Administer broad-spectrum parenteral antibiotics (**Ampicillin 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV**).

### 10. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The "Last Out, First In" Rule**: During Johnson's manual replacement for acute uterine inversion, push up the portion of the uterus that inverted last (the cervicovaginal junction) first. Maintain hand position inside the cavity until parenteral oxytocin excites a hard contraction to prevent re-inversion.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Giving Inj. Oxytocin or Methergine immediately upon diagnosing uterine inversion, OR pulling on the attached placenta.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Uterotonics are STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED prior to replacement** because they cause tetanic spasm of the cervical constriction ring, permanently trapping the inverted fundus.
    - **Peeling the placenta off an inverted uterus causes massive blood loss**; replace the uterus with placenta intact, then perform MRP after uterine tone is restored.

### 12. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: I-N-V-E-R-T (Management Steps)

- **I**: **Immediate Resuscitation & Call for Help**.
- **N**: **NO Uterotonics before repositioning** (prevents constriction ring lock).
- **V**: **Voluminous Fluid / O'Sullivan Hydrostatic Method** (5 L warm saline).
- **E**: **Execute Johnson's Manual Replacement under GA** (last out replaced first).
- **R**: **Ring release via Haultain's / Huntington's operation** if manual fails.
- **T**: **Tetanic Contraction via IV Oxytocin AFTER replacement**.

### Quick Revision

- **3rd Stage Complications**: PPH (80% atonic), Retained placenta, Acute uterine inversion, Obstetric shock, Pulmonary embolism.
- **Acute Inversion Definition**: Turn inside out of uterus within 24 hours of birth (1 in 20,000).
- **Etiology**: Cord traction on unseparated placenta + fundal atony + fundal placenta (75%).
- **Shock Mechanism**: Profound **Neurogenic Shock** (peritoneal/pelvic nerve traction) + Hemorrhagic Shock.
- **Management Sequence**: Resuscitate → DO NOT give oxytocics before replacement → Johnson's Manual Replacement under GA ("last out, first in") → O'Sullivan Hydrostatic method → Haultain's abdominal operation if ring tight → Oxytocin IV + MRP AFTER replacement [123–126].
