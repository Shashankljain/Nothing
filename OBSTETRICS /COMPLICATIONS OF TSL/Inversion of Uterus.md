# Inversion of Uterus: Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **3 Marks / Short Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition

- **Acute Uterine Inversion**: An obstetric emergency in which the uterine fundus turns inside out through the cervical os into the vagina or protruding outside the vulva, occurring within **24 hours of delivery** (incidence ≈ 1 in 20,000 deliveries) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].

---

### 2. Etiology & Causes of Uterine Inversion [⭐ High-Yield]

Uterine inversion requires two prerequisite conditions: **fundal myometrial atony/relaxation** and **cervical dilation**combined with downward traction or increased intra-abdominal pressure [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].

#### A. Spontaneous Causes (40% of cases) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fundal Placental Implantation (75%)**: Implantation of the placenta directly over the uterine fundus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- **Sudden Rise in Intra-Abdominal Pressure**: Sharp increase in pressure due to coughing, sneezing, or premature bearing-down efforts while the fundus is relaxed [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- **Placental & Cord Anomalies**: Short umbilical cord (absolute or relative due to neck coiling) and morbidly adherent placenta (Placenta Accreta Spectrum) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- **Localized Fundal Atony**: Weakness of the myometrial wall specifically at the placental attachment site [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].

#### B. Iatrogenic Causes (60% of cases — Mismanagement of 3rd Stage) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Traction on Umbilical Cord**: Traction applied to the cord when the uterus is atonic and uncontracted [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396; Marrow Vol 1, p. 45].
- **Fundal Pressure (Credé Maneuver)**: Applying strong downward fundal pressure while the uterus is relaxed [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- **Faulty Manual Removal Technique**: Improper traction or manual separation of a fundally attached placenta [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].

#### C. Predisposing Risk Factors

- Uterine overdistension (multiple gestation, fetal macrosomia >4 kg) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- Prolonged labor causing myometrial exhaustion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
- Pharmacological uterine relaxation (tocolytics, Magnesium Sulfate [MgSO₄], halogenated general anesthetics) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].

---

### Quick Revision

- **Prerequisite Pathology**: Fundal uterine atony + dilated cervix [DC Dutta 1, p. 396].
- **Most Common Site**: Fundal attachment of placenta (75%) [DC Dutta 1, p. 396].
- **Most Common Iatrogenic Cause**: Umbilical cord traction on an uncontracted, relaxed uterus [DC Dutta 1, p. 396].
- **Primary Spontaneous Trigger**: Sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure (coughing/bearing down) [DC Dutta 1, p. 396].

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Uterine inversion is a life-threatening third-stage obstetric emergency caused primarily by iatrogenic mismanagement—specifically cord traction or fundal pressure applied on an atonic uterus—or spontaneously due to fundal placental attachment (75%) combined with a sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure.** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 28, p. 396].
