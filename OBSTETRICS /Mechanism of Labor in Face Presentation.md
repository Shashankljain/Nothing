# Mechanism of Labor in Face Presentation [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Epidemiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Face Presentation**: A cephalic malpresentation in which the fetal head is **completely extended** so that the occiput comes into direct contact with the fetal back, and the face presents at the pelvic inlet [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370].
- **Denominator**: **Mentum (chin)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 8, p. 125; Ch 26, p. 370].
- **Incidence**: Occurs in approximately **1 in 500 births (0.2%)**; more frequent in multiparae (70%) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370].
- **Types**:
    - _Primary Face Presentation_: Present during pregnancy prior to labor onset (rare) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370].
    - _Secondary Face Presentation_: Develops during labor from a deflexed occipitoposterior (OP) position (most common) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370].

---

- **Right Mentoposterior (RMP)** & **Left Mentoposterior (LMP)**: Account for 20% to 25% of cases [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370–371].

### 3. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Maternal Factors**: Multiparity with lax abdominal wall / pendulous abdomen, lateral obliquity of the uterus, **contracted flat pelvis (associated in 40% of cases)**, and lower segment pelvic tumors [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370–371].
2. **Fetal Factors**:
    - **Anencephaly (15% — most common fetal anomaly)**: Absence of cranial vault allows free extension of the head [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371; Ch 27, p. 385].
    - Congenital fetal goiter or neck hygroma (prevents head flexion) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
    - Multiple loops of umbilical cord wrapped tightly around the neck [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
    - Dolichocephalic fetal head (long AP diameter) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].

### 5. Detailed Mechanism of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Mechanism in Mentoanterior Position (LMA / RMA — 60–80%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**:
    - **Engaging Diameter**: **Submentobregmatic (9.5 cm)** in fully extended head, or **Submentovertical (11.5 cm)**in partially extended head [DC Dutta 1, Ch 9, p. 132; Ch 26, p. 371].
    - **Transverse Diameter**: Biparietal diameter (9.5 cm) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
    - _Delayed Engagement_: Engagement is delayed because the distance between the chin (mentum) and biparietal plane is **7 cm** (compared to only 3 cm in vertex presentation) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371, 373].
2. **Descent & Increasing Extension**: Descent occurs continuously with uterine contractions; resistance from the pelvic wall pushes the occiput upward, causing **increasing extension** of the head until the chin reaches the pelvic floor [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
3. **Internal Rotation of Mentum**:
    - The chin (mentum) touches the pelvic floor first and rotates **1/8th of a circle (45°)** anteriorly to lie behind the symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
    - Further descent occurs until the **submentum hinges under the pubic arch** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
4. **Delivery of the Head by FLEXION**:
    - Unlike vertex presentation (which delivers by extension), **the head in face presentation is born by FLEXION** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
    - As the submentum hinges under the symphysis pubis, strong contractions force the head into flexion, releasing the **chin, mouth, nose, brow, vertex, and lastly the occiput** over the anterior perineal margin [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
5. **Restitution**: The head untwists by rotating **1/8th of a circle (45°)** opposite to the direction of internal rotation [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].
6. **External Rotation & Shoulder Expulsion**: The head rotates another **1/8th of a circle (45°)** in the same direction as restitution as the shoulders rotate into the AP diameter; anterior shoulder emerges under pubis, and the trunk is born by **lateral flexion** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].

#### B. Mechanism in Mentoposterior Position (RMP / LMP — 20–25%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Favorable Outcome (20–30%)**: Long anterior rotation of the mentum through **3/8th of a circle (135°)** occurs, converting it into a mentoanterior position, which then delivers by flexion [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371–372].
- **Unfavorable Outcome (70–80% — Persistent Mentoposterior)**:
    - **NO SPONTANEOUS DELIVERY IS POSSIBLE** in persistent mentoposterior position [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371–372].
    - **Reason**: The fetal neck is too short (~5 cm) to span the total length of the sacral hollow (12 cm). To allow further descent, the fetal chest and thorax must enter the pelvic inlet alongside the head, presenting the massive **Bregmaticosternal diameter (18 cm)** to the pelvic brim [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].
    - Because 18 cm cannot pass through a normal pelvic inlet (11 cm), **persistent mentoposterior results in absolute, insuperable OBSTRUCTED LABOR** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].

### 6. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Abdominal Examination

- **Palpation**:
    - _Lateral Grip_: Fetal back is difficult to palpate (felt far out in flank); chest is thrown anteriorly against abdominal wall and easily mistaken for back [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].
    - _Pelvic Grip_: A **deep groove** is felt between the prominent occiput (cephalic prominence on the same side as fetal back) and the fetal back [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].
- **Auscultation**: Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) are heard distinctly through the **fetal chest on the side of the small parts (limbs)** in mentoanterior positions [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].

#### B. Vaginal Examination (PV Findings — Diagnostic Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Palpation of facial landmarks: **Mouth with hard alveolar ridges, nose, malar eminences, supraorbital ridges, and mentum** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].
- _Caput Distortion_: Marked facial edema and caput succedaneum distort facial features in prolonged labor [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].

1. **Mentoanterior Position**:
    - **Watchful Expectancy**: Spontaneous vaginal delivery occurs in >90% of cases [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].
    - **Conduct of Second Stage**: Perform a **liberal mediolateral episiotomy** under local 1% Lignocaine to accommodate the **11.5 cm submentovertical diameter** distending the introitus [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].
    - Low forceps application is safe if second stage is prolonged [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].
2. **Persistent Mentoposterior Position**:
    - If anterior rotation fails to occur upon full cervical dilatation: **Immediate Emergency Cesarean Section (CD)** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373–374]. _(Manual rotation or podalic version is strictly contraindicated in modern practice d/t uterine rupture risk)_ [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 374].

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Maternal**: Prolonged labor, extensive 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears (OASI), postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), obstructed labor, and uterine rupture in neglected persistent mentoposterior [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].
- **Fetal**: High perinatal mortality (~10–15%) secondary to **cord prolapse** (common d/t ill-fitting face), severe facial edema/bruising, cerebral congestion, and intrapartum asphyxia [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 373].

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Reverse Delivery Mechanism Rule**: In normal vertex (OA) presentation, the head delivers by **EXTENSION**(subocciput hinges under pubis). In mentoanterior face presentation, the delivery mechanism is **REVERSED**: the head delivers by **FLEXION** (submentum hinges under pubis, releasing chin, brow, and occiput over perineum) [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371]!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Expecting spontaneous face-to-pubis delivery in persistent mentoposterior, OR confusing face presentation with breech presentation.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Persistent mentoposterior CANNOT deliver spontaneously** because the bregmaticosternal diameter (18 cm) impacts the pelvis, causing **obstructed labor requiring immediate Cesarean section** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372]. (Unlike persistent occipitoposterior, which can deliver as face-to-pubis).
    - On PV exam, **mouth and malar eminences form a TRIANGLE**, whereas anus and ischial tuberosities in breech form a **STRAIGHT LINE** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 372].

    2. **Submentum** — hinging under lower border of symphysis pubis [DC Dutta 1, p. 371].
    3. **Stretched Perineum** — distended by submentovertical diameter (11.5 cm) [DC Dutta 1, p. 371, 373].
    4. **Direction Arrow of Flexion** — head sweeping into flexion to release chin, face, brow, vertex, and occiput [DC Dutta 1, p. 371].
- **Annotation**: _"In mentoanterior position, the submentum hinges under the symphysis pubis and the head delivers by FLEXION over the perineum."_ [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 371].

### 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: M-E-N-T-U-M (Face Presentation Essentials)

- **M**: **Mentum is the denominator** (LMA is the most common position ~60%).
- **E**: **Extension of head** (engaging diameter: Submentobregmatic 9.5 cm).
- **N**: **No spontaneous birth in persistent mentoposterior** (bregmaticosternal 18 cm causes obstruction).
- **T**: **Triangle formed by mouth & malar eminences** on PV (differentiates from breech straight line).
- **U**: **Under pubic arch, submentum hinges** for delivery by flexion in MA positions.
- **M**: **Mandatory Cesarean section** for persistent mentoposterior positions.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Denominator**: Cephalic malpresentation with completely extended head; denominator is **mentum (chin)**; most common position is LMA [DC Dutta 1, p. 370–371].
- **Engaging Diameters**: Submentobregmatic (**9.5 cm** in full extension) or Submentovertical (**11.5 cm** in partial extension) [DC Dutta 1, p. 132, 371].
- **Delivery Mechanism (Mentoanterior)**: Descent with extension → Internal rotation of mentum (45°) → Submentum hinges under pubis → **Delivery by FLEXION** [DC Dutta 1, p. 371].
- **Persistent Mentoposterior**: Cannot deliver vaginally d/t impaction of **bregmaticosternal diameter (18 cm)** → **Obstructed Labor → Emergency CS** [DC Dutta 1, p. 372].
- **PV Diagnosis**: Facial features (mouth, nose, cheeks) form a triangle; hard alveolar ridges with sucking reflex [DC Dutta 1, p. 372].

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Face presentation is a rare cephalic malpresentation (1 in 500 births) characterized by complete extension of the fetal head with the mentum as denominator, which in mentoanterior positions (LMA 60%) delivers vaginally by flexion once the submentum hinges under the symphysis pubis, but in persistent mentoposterior positions results in insuperable obstructed labor due to impaction of the 18 cm bregmaticosternal diameter, requiring emergency Cesarean section.** [DC Dutta 1, Ch 26, p. 370–374].
