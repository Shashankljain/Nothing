### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Occipitoposterior (OP) Position**: A malposition of the vertex presentation in which the fetal occiput is directed posteriorly over either sacroiliac joint or directly over the sacrum.
    - _Malposition Definition_: Any position of the vertex other than flexed occiput anterior.
- **Varieties**:
    1. **Right Occipitoposterior (ROP)**: Occiput lies over the right sacroiliac joint (traditionally termed the **third position of vertex**); accounts for **80–85%** of all OP positions.
    2. **Left Occipitoposterior (LOP)**: Occiput lies over the left sacroiliac joint (traditionally termed the **fourth position of vertex**).
    3. **Direct Occipitoposterior**: Occiput points directly toward the sacral promontory/hollow.
- **Incidence**: Present in approximately **10%** of all vertex presentations at the onset of labor. **ROP is 5 times more common than LOP** due to dextrorotation of the uterus and the presence of the sigmoid colon occupying the left side of the pelvic cavity.

1. **Pelvic Architectural Factors (>50% of cases)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Anthropoid Pelvis**: Long anteroposterior diameter and narrow transverse diameter favor engagement of the long occipitofrontal diameter in the AP plane or oblique posterior quadrant.
    - **Android Pelvis**: Heart-shaped inlet with a narrow anterior segment forces the wider biparietal diameter into the wider posterior pelvic segment.
2. **Fetal Deflexion Factors**:
    - Primary deflexion of the head prevents the occiput from leading down the pelvic floor gutter.
    - **Anterior Placental Implantation**: Favors a dorsoposterior position of the fetal trunk.
3. **Uterine & Soft Tissue Factors**:
    - Hypotonic or incoordinate uterine contractions lead to persistent deflexion.
    - Relaxed pelvic floor muscles fail to provide the elastic recoil necessary for anterior rotation.

### 5. Mechanism of Labor (Detailed Stepwise Description) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Favorable Mechanism (90% of Cases — Long Anterior Rotation) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Engagement**: Occiput engages in the **right oblique diameter** (in ROP) or **left oblique diameter** (in LOP). Due to deflexion, the engaging AP diameter is the **suboccipitofrontal (10 cm)** or **occipitofrontal (11.5 cm)**, which delays engagement.
2. **Descent & Flexion**: Strong uterine contractions push the head down onto the pelvic floor; increased resistance promotes head flexion, converting the presenting diameter to the smaller **suboccipitobregmatic (9.5 cm)**.
3. **Internal Rotation of the Head (Long Anterior Rotation)**:
    - The well-flexed occiput touches the pelvic floor first and rotates **3/8th of a circle (135°)** anteriorly to lie behind the symphysis pubis.
    - **Torsion of Neck**: The neck can sustain only 2/8th of a circle (90°) of torsion; therefore, the shoulders simultaneously rotate 2/8th of a circle into the corresponding oblique diameter, leaving 1/8th of a circle (45°) torsion in the neck.
4. **Delivery of the Head**: Occiput hinges under the symphysis pubis; head is born by **extension** (occiput, vertex, forehead, face, and chin emerge over the perineum).
5. **Restitution**: The head untwists by rotating **1/8th of a circle** in the direction opposite to internal rotation.
6. **External Rotation**: The anterior shoulder rotates forward under the symphysis pubis, causing the head to rotate another **1/8th of a circle** externally in the same direction as restitution.

#### B. Unfavorable Mechanism (10% of Cases — Malrotation & Face-to-Pubis) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Malrotation (Occipitosacral Position)**:
    - If deflexion persists, the **sinciput touches the pelvic floor first** and rotates **1/8th of a circle anteriorly**, forcing the occiput **1/8th of a circle posteriorly** into the sacral hollow.
2. **Face-to-Pubis Delivery**:
    - Further descent occurs until the **root of the nose (nasion) hinges under the symphysis pubis**.
    - **Flexion**: The brow, vertex, and occiput are born over the anterior margin of the perineum by a movement of **flexion**.
    - **Extension**: Once the occiput is released, the face drops backward and is born by **extension** under the pubic arch.
    - _Perineal Hazard_: Vulval outlet is distended by the large **occipitofrontal diameter (11.5 cm)** and biparietal diameter (9.5 cm), creating an extreme risk of 3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears.

### 6. Clinical Features & Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Abdominal Examination [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Inspection**: The lower abdomen appears **flattened below the umbilicus** because the fetal limbs (and not the smooth back) face anteriorly.
- **Palpation**:
    - _Umbilical Grip_: Small fetal parts (limbs) are easily felt on both sides near the midline. The fetal back is felt far out in the flank and is difficult to outline.
    - _First Pelvic Grip_: Head is unengaged; sinciput and occiput are felt at nearly the same horizontal level due to deflexion. The sincipital prominence is **not as prominent** as in a well-flexed anterior position.
- **Auscultation**: Fetal Heart Sounds (FHS) are heard far out in the flank on the side of the back and are often muffled/difficult to locate (except in direct OP, where FHS is clear in the midline through the fetal chest).

#### B. Vaginal Examination (PV) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Early Labor**:
    - Elongated, sausage-shaped bag of membranes (prone to premature rupture due to poor cervical application of deflexed head).
    - **Sagittal suture lies in an oblique diameter** of the pelvis.
    - **Posterior fontanelle is felt posteriorly near the sacroiliac joint**.
    - **Anterior fontanelle (diamond-shaped) is easily palpable** in the anterior part of the pelvis because of head deflexion.
- **Late Labor**: Caput succedaneum formation obscures sutures; diagnosis is established by feeling the **unfolded pinna of the ear**, which points toward the fetal occiput.

#### C. Imaging & Diagnostic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Bedside Intrapartum Ultrasonography (Gold Standard)**: Diagnostic accuracy **>99%** (compared to 80% on clinical examination).
    - Transabdominal USG shows **fetal orbits facing anteriorly** (toward the maternal abdominal wall) in ROP/LOP positions.

#### A. Management During the First Stage of Labor [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Watchful Expectancy & Supportive Care**:
    - Monitor labor progress using a **WHO Partograph** (cervical dilatation and head descent).
    - Maintain IV access with **Ringer's Lactate solution** to prevent maternal dehydration and ketoacidosis.
    - Administer **epidural analgesia** to relieve severe backache (caused by occiput pressure on sacral nerves) and prevent premature bearing-down efforts before full cervical dilatation.
2. **Management of Inadequate Uterine Action**:
    - Weak contractions, deflexion, and non-rotation form a common triad.
    - **Oxytocin Augmentation**: Administered via IV drip to correct secondary arrest or protracted active phase once cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD) is ruled out.
3. **Indications for Early Cesarean Section in First Stage**:
    - Associated pelvic contraction (android/anthropoid with small dimensions).
    - Severe pre-eclampsia, fetal distress, or cord prolapse.

#### B. Management During the Second Stage of Labor & Arrest Disorders [⭐ High-Yield]

If anterior rotation occurs spontaneously (90%), delivery proceeds normally or with low outlet forceps. If arrest occurs, definitive interventions are selected based on head station and pelvic adequacy:

##### 1. Vacuum Extraction (Ventouse — Preferred First-Line Method) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Deep Transverse Arrest (DTA) or Oblique Posterior Arrest in an adequate pelvis.
- **Technique**:
    - The suction cup is applied over the **Flexion Point (Pivot Point)**, located on the sagittal suture **3 cm in front of the posterior fontanelle** (6 cm behind the anterior fontanelle).
    - Traction along the pelvic axis promotes artificial head flexion; as flexion increases during descent, the occiput **spontaneously rotates anteriorly** on the pelvic floor.

##### 2. Manual Rotation followed by Forceps Delivery [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Arrested ROP/LOP or DTA where vacuum is unavailable or contra-indicated.
- **Prerequisites**: Cervix fully dilated, membranes ruptured, head engaged at or below ischial spines, adequate pelvis, empty bladder, and General Anesthesia (GA) or deep pudendal block.
- **Technique (Whole-Hand Method)**:
    - _Grip_: For ROP, the operator's **left hand** is introduced into the vagina; four fingers are placed over the posterior parietal bone and the thumb over the anterior parietal bone.
    - _Rotation_: By a movement of **pronation**, the head is rotated anteriorly through **135°** to bring the occiput behind the symphysis pubis.
    - _Essential Counter-Maneuver_: Simultaneously, the operator's **external right hand rotates the fetal anterior shoulder abdominally** across the midline toward the opposite side to prevent the head from snapping back into the posterior position.
    - _Forceps Application_: An assistant maintains suprapubic head fixation while forceps blades are applied to deliver the head by extension.

##### 3. Kielland's Rotational Forceps

- Performed exclusively by an expert obstetrician; Kielland forceps lack a classic pelvic curve, permitting safe intra-vaginal head rotation at mid-pelvic station.

##### 4. Spontaneous or Assisted Face-to-Pubis Delivery

- Performed for persistent occipitosacral position when the head is low on the perineal floor. A **liberal mediolateral episiotomy** must be cut to prevent severe obstetric anal sphincter injuries (OASIS).

##### 5. Emergency Cesarean Section

- Indicated when the head is unengaged, severe CPD is present, manual/vacuum rotation fails, or fetal distress supervenes.

### 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Maternal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Prolonged Labor & Uterine Exhaustion**: Increased duration of both 1st and 2nd stages leads to maternal ketoacidosis and dehydration.
2. **Early Rupture of Membranes & Chorioamnionitis**: Poor fit of the deflexed head to the lower uterine segment causes premature liquor loss and ascending infection.
3. **Severe Genital Tract Trauma**: Face-to-pubis delivery and mid-pelvic operative procedures carry a high risk of **3rd and 4th-degree perineal tears**, vaginal lacerations, and cervical damage.
4. **Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)**: Uterine atony secondary to prolonged labor, as well as traumatic lacerations.

#### B. Fetal Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Intrapartum Fetal Asphyxia**: Driven by prolonged labor, uncoordinated contractions, or cord compression.
2. **Birth Trauma**: Cephalhematoma, extensive caput succedaneum formation, intracranial hemorrhage, and facial nerve palsy secondary to instrumental rotation.
3. **Increased Perinatal Mortality**: Elevated to **~10%** in neglected or mismanaged cases.

### 11. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The External Shoulder Counter-Rotation Rule**: During manual rotation of an arrested ROP head, rotating the intra-vaginal head alone is useless because the head will instantly snap back to the posterior position upon release. **The operator's external abdominal hand MUST simultaneously rotate the fetal anterior shoulder across the midline**.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Deep Transverse Arrest (DTA)** with a primary transverse lie, OR attempting manual rotation when the head is unengaged.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **DTA** is an intrapartum arrest disorder where a **cephalic head is deep in the pelvic cavity** at or below the ischial spines with the sagittal suture in the transverse diameter.
    - **Manual or forceps rotation MUST NEVER be attempted on an unengaged head** (above ischial spines); high station arrest requires an immediate **Cesarean Section**.

- **P**: **Persistent deflexion of head** (increases engaging diameter to 11.5 cm).

- **S**: **Sinciput felt easily anteriorly** on abdominal and vaginal exams.
- **T**: **Transverse arrest (DTA)** occurs if incomplete 1/8th rotation occurs.
- **E**: **Elongated bag of membranes** prone to early rupture.
- **R**: **ROP is 5 times more common** than LOP.

- **R**: **Risk of severe 3rd/4th degree perineal tears**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Malposition where fetal occiput lies over sacroiliac joint (ROP 85%, LOP 15%).
- **Diagnostic Signs**: Abdomen flat below umbilicus, limbs felt near midline, anterior fontanelle easily palpable anteriorly on PV exam.
- **Favorable Outcome (90%)**: Long anterior rotation of occiput through **3/8th of a circle (135°)** to deliver as OA.
- **Unfavorable Outcome (10%)**: Oblique posterior arrest, Deep Transverse Arrest (DTA), or Occipitosacral position (delivers as face-to-pubis).
- **Management of Arrest**: Ventouse (cup applied 3 cm in front of posterior fontanelle), Manual Rotation + Forceps under GA, or Cesarean Section if pelvis is inadequate.
