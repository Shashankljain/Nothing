# Gallstone Disease (Cholelithiasis)

### 1. Definition
- **Gallstone disease (Cholelithiasis)** refers to the formation and presence of **calculi** within the **gallbladder** or **biliary tree** resulting from imbalances in bile composition, solubilisation, or stasis.

---

### 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Obesity**: Promotes increased cholesterol synthesis and biliary secretion, posing a major risk for gallstone formation.
- **Ileal Disease or Resection / Short Bowel Syndrome**: Disruption of the **enterohepatic circulation of bile acids** prevents bile salt reabsorption in the terminal ileum, depleting the bile acid pool and causing gallstones in approximately **45%** of patients.
- **Cystic Fibrosis**: Disruption of exocrine pan-biliary secretions leads to gallstones as a systemic gastrointestinal complication in up to **25%** of adult patients.
- **HIV Cholangiopathy**: Predisposes patients to biliary strictures, **papillary stenosis**, and **acalculous cholecystitis**.

---

### 3. Classification
- **Cholelithiasis**: Gallstones located within the lumen of the gallbladder.
- **Choledocholithiasis**: Calculi in the **common bile duct (CBD)**, causing extrahepatic biliary obstruction.
- **Acalculous Cholecystitis**: Inflammatory gallbladder disease occurring in the absence of gallstones, typically associated with conditions such as **HIV cholangiopathy**.

---

### 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Hormonal Stimulation**: Dietary fat intake triggers **cholecystokinin (CCK)** release from S/K cells in the **duodenum** and **upper jejunum**.
- **Biliary Dynamics**: **CCK** induces active gallbladder contraction while simultaneously relaxing the **sphincter of Oddi** to allow bile ejection into the intestine.
- **Solubilisation**: Bile salts and lecithin form **mixed micelles** with dietary lipids, solubilising cholesterol and fat-soluble vitamins.
- **Precipitation**: Impairment of bile salt reabsorption (e.g., terminal ileal resection) disrupts the **enterohepatic circulation**, leading to supersaturation and crystal nucleation into solid calculi.

---

### 5. Clinical Features
- **Symptoms**:
  - **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) / Epigastric Pain**: Episodic, postprandial, or constant biliary pain.
  - **Nausea and Vomiting**: Accompanying biliary colic or acute inflammatory episodes.
  - **Fever & Pleuritic Pain**: Indicates acute inflammatory complications such as **acute cholecystitis** or **ascending cholangitis**.
- **Signs**:
  - **Jaundice**: Develops when calculi obstruct the **common bile duct**.
  - **Right Upper Quadrant Tenderness & Guarding**: Localized abdominal tenderness on physical examination.
  - **Palpable Gallbladder**: Occurs in complete biliary distension or obstruction.

---

### 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Lab Findings**:
  - **Elevated Serum Amylase / Lipase**: Diagnostic for complicating **gallstone pancreatitis**.
  - **Inflammatory Markers**: Raised **C-reactive protein (CRP)** and neutrophil leucocytosis during acute infection.
- **Imaging**:
  - **Abdominal Ultrasound (USG)**: Initial diagnostic test of choice; demonstrates **gallstones** with characteristic **posterior acoustic shadowing** and identifies **biliary tract dilatation**.
  - **Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP)**: Replaces diagnostic ERCP for non-invasive, high-resolution visualisation of the **biliary tree**, **common bile duct**, and **pancreatic duct**.
  - **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: High-resolution imaging tool helpful for detecting **common bile duct stones**, **pancreatic cysts**, and **ampullary lesions**.
  - **Plain Abdominal X-Ray**: Detects radiopaque calcified gallstones (limited sensitivity).
  - **Computed Tomography (CT)**: Used for staging, evaluating complex abdominal masses, or detecting collections.
- **Endoscopic Procedures**:
  - **Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)**: Principal therapeutic intervention for **common bile duct calculi removal**, **sphincterotomy**, **basket retrieval**, and **biliary stent placement**.

---

### 7. Differential Diagnosis

| Feature | Biliary Colic / Uncomplicated Cholelithiasis | Acute Cholecystitis | Choledocholithiasis / Ascending Cholangitis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anatomical Site** | Gallbladder lumen / Cystic duct transiently | Persistent Cystic Duct impaction | Common Bile Duct (CBD) |
| **Fever & Systemic Disturbance** | Absent | Present (fever, pleuritic RUQ pain) | Present with high fever and rigors |
| **Jaundice** | Absent | Absent or minimal | Present (Obstructive Jaundice) |
| **Primary Diagnostic Tool** | Abdominal **Ultrasound (USG)** | Abdominal **USG** | **MRCP** (Diagnostic) / **ERCP** (Therapeutic) |
| **Definitive Intervention** | Elective **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** | **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** + Supportive care | **ERCP** stone clearance followed by **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** |

---

### 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]
- **Non-Pharmacological**:
  - Low-fat dietary intake to reduce **CCK**-mediated gallbladder stimulation.
- **Pharmacological**:
  - Supportive hydration and symptom control during acute colic.
- **Surgical & Interventional**:
  - **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**: Surgical procedure of choice in fit individuals requiring gallbladder removal.
  - **Therapeutic ERCP**: Indicated for **common bile duct calculi removal**, **sphincterotomy**, **basket retrieval**, and **stent insertion**.

---

### 9. Complications
- **Acute Cholecystitis**
- **Ascending Cholangitis**
- **Acute Gallstone Pancreatitis**
- **Obstructive Jaundice**
- **Acalculous Cholecystitis**

---

### 10. Prognosis
- Excellent long-term prognosis following successful **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** and complete ductal clearance via **ERCP**.

---

### 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace
- **Mnemonic: GALLSTONES**
  - **G** — **Gallbladder**: Primary anatomical origin site.
  - **A** — **Abdominal Ultrasound (USG)**: First-line imaging choice.
  - **L** — **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**: Gold-standard surgical cure.
  - **L** — **Loss of Bile Acids**: Short bowel / ileal resection causing gallstones in **45%** of cases.
  - **S** — **Sphincter of Oddi**: Relaxed by **CCK** during gallbladder contraction.
  - **T** — **Therapeutic ERCP**: For extraction of **common bile duct calculi**.
  - **O** — **Obesity**: Major metabolic predisposing factor.
  - **N** — **Non-invasive MRCP**: Diagnostic replacement for ERCP.
  - **E** — **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: For evaluation of choledocholithiasis.
  - **S** — **Severe Complications**: **Cholecystitis**, **Cholangitis**, and **Pancreatitis**.

---

### Clinical Pearl
- Always utilize **MRCP** as the non-invasive diagnostic test of choice for evaluating the biliary tree, reserving **ERCP** strictly for therapeutic procedures (**sphincterotomy**, **stone extraction**, or **stenting**).

---

### Exam Trap
- **The Mix-up**: Reversing the clinical roles of **MRCP** and **ERCP**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**: **MRCP** is purely diagnostic and non-invasive, carrying zero risk of pancreatitis. **ERCP** is an invasive therapeutic procedure carrying a **5–10%** complication rate (e.g., post-ERCP pancreatitis **1–3%**) and is reserved for active stone clearance, **sphincterotomy**, or **stenting**.

### Diagram 1 : Biliary Tree and Sites of Gallstone Impaction
- **Type**: Flow / Structural Labeled Diagram
- **Outline shape to draw**: Inverted Y-shaped biliary tree connected to an oval gallbladder and a C-shaped duodenal loop.
- **Labels in order (proximal-to-distal)**:
  1. **Gallbladder & Cystic Duct**: Primary site of cholelithiasis; cystic duct impaction leads to acute cholecystitis.
  2. **Common Hepatic Duct & Common Bile Duct (CBD)**: Conduit for bile; CBD impaction causes obstructive jaundice.
  3. **Pancreatic Duct**: Unites with CBD at the ampulla; ductal reflux triggers acute pancreatitis.
  4. **Ampulla of Vater & Sphincter of Oddi**: Terminal entrance to duodenum; CCK relaxes sphincter for bile drainage.
- **Arrows/connections**: Hepatic Ducts → Common Hepatic Duct + Cystic Duct → Common Bile Duct + Pancreatic Duct → Ampulla of Vater → Duodenum.
- **Annotation for extra marks**: "MRCP visualises the biliary tree non-invasively, whereas therapeutic ERCP achieves stone extraction via sphincterotomy at the Ampulla of Vater".

### Quick Revision
- **Abdominal Ultrasound (USG)** is the primary non-invasive diagnostic investigation of choice for visualising **gallstones** (showing acoustic shadowing) and assessing **biliary tract dilatation**.
- **MRCP** is the diagnostic test of choice for evaluating the biliary tree, while **ERCP** is reserved for therapeutic **sphincterotomy**, **stone removal**, and **stenting**.
- **Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy** is the preferred definitive treatment for fit individuals with symptomatic gallstone disease.
- Impairment of the **enterohepatic circulation of bile acids** (e.g., in short bowel syndrome or ileal resection) leads to gallstone formation in **45%** of cases.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener
- **Gallstone disease (cholelithiasis)** is a common biliary condition diagnosed initially via **abdominal ultrasound** and definitively managed with **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** or therapeutic **ERCP**.
