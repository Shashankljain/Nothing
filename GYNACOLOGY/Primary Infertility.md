[[C16-Infertility and Sterility]]
# Approach to an Infertile Couple Presenting in the OPD (Primary Infertility — 4 Years Duration) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **10 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Infertility**: Defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the failure of a couple to achieve conception after **1 year (12 months)** of regular, unprotected sexual intercourse [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203].
- **Primary Infertility**: Defined when conception has **never occurred** in the female partner despite regular unprotected coitus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203]. _(In this scenario: Primary Infertility of 4 years duration)_.
- **Incidence**: Affects **10% to 15%** of married couples worldwide [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203].
- **Relative Distribution of Etiology (Rule of Thirds)**:
    - **Male Factor Alone**: **30% to 35%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 205].
    - **Female Factor Alone**: **30% to 35%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 212].
    - **Combined / Both Partners & Unexplained**: **30% to 35%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 224].

#### A. Female Factors (35%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Ovulatory Dysfunction (30–40%)**: Anovulation due to Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (PCOS - most common), hyperprolactinemia, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (Kallmann syndrome), primary ovarian insufficiency (POI), or luteal phase defect (LPD) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 212, 215; Marrow Vol 1, p. 185].
2. **Tubal & Pelvic Factors (25–30%)**: Tubal blockage secondary to Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID - _Chlamydia_, _Gonorrhea_), Genital Tuberculosis (tobacco-pouch/beaded tubes), or peritubal adhesions [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 215, 218; Ch 28, p. 351].
3. **Uterine & Cervical Factors (10–15%)**: Submucous fibroids, intrauterine synechiae (Asherman syndrome), septate uterus, cervical stenosis, or purulent cervicitis destroying cervical mucus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 215; Marrow Vol 1, p. 107, 185].
4. **Peritoneal Factors & Endometriosis (5–10%)**: Peritubal/periovarian adhesions, chocolate cysts distorting tubo-ovarian anatomy, and peritoneal cytokines impairing sperm-ovum transport [Shaw 1, Ch 14, p. 180; Ch 16, p. 224].

#### B. Male Factors (35%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Spermatogenic Defects (50%)**: Primary testicular failure, cryptorchidism, varicocele (most common treatable cause), Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY), thermal/radiation exposure [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206].
2. **Ductal Obstruction (30%)**: Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD d/t CFTR mutation), post-infectious vasal/epididymal block, prior hernia repair injury [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
3. **Sperm Function & Motility Defects (15%)**: Asthenospermia, teratospermia, Kartagener syndrome (immotile cilia syndrome) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206].
4. **Coital & Ejaculatory Dysfunction**: Erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation, retrograde ejaculation, hypospadias [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].

#### C. Combined, Immunological & Unexplained Factors (30%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Coital Errors**: Infrequent coitus, incorrect timing relative to ovulation, dyspareunia, vaginismus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 212].
2. **Immunological Infertility**: Anti-sperm antibodies (ASA) in seminal plasma or cervical mucus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 208, 214].
3. **Unexplained Infertility (10–15%)**: All standard investigations normal in both partners [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 224].

### 5. Clinical Features: Detailed Stepwise Clinical Evaluation Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Initial Couple Consultation & History Taking [⭐ High-Yield]

- **General & Social History**: Age of partners (maternal age >35 years reduces fecundity rapidly), duration of marriage (4 years in this case), occupation (exposure to heat, chemicals, radiation), social habits (smoking, alcohol, tobacco chewing, drug abuse) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 206].
- **Coital History**: Coital frequency (optimal is every 1–2 days around ovulation window), timing relative to cycle, coital difficulties, dyspareunia, vaginismus, use of spermicidal lubricants [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206, 212–213].
- **Female Past History**: Menstrual chronology (regularity, cycle length, oligomenorrhea, dysmenorrhea), past history of PID, appendicitis, puerperal sepsis, curettage (Asherman syndrome), tuberculosis (fever, weight loss, oligomenorrhea), thyroid disorders, or galactorrhea [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Ch 28, p. 351].
- **Male Past History**: Mumps orchitis, cryptorchidism, scrotal trauma/surgery, hernia repair, STIs (urethritis, epididymitis), diabetes mellitus (retrograde ejaculation), or medications (spironolactone, cimetidine, CCBs, anabolic steroids) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].

#### B. Physical Examination Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Female Partner**:
    - _General_: BMI (obesity in PCOS; low BMI in hypothalamic amenorrhea), height, secondary sexual characteristics (Tanner staging), hirsutism/acne (Ferriman-Gallwey score in PCOS), thyroid enlargement, galactorrhea [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Marrow Vol 1, p. 185].
    - _Abdominal_: Palpate for masses (fibroids, tubo-ovarian mass, endometrioma).
    - _Pelvic Bimanual Exam_: Inspect vulva/vagina; speculum exam for cervical erosions/discharge; bimanual exam to evaluate uterine size, position (fixed retroverted uterus in pelvic endometriosis/PID), mobility, and adnexal tenderness or nodularity in pouch of Douglas [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
- **Male Partner**:
    - _General_: Body habitus, gynecomastia, distribution of hair [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].
    - _Genital Exam (Standing Posture)_: Testicular volume (Prader orchidometer; normal 15–25 mL; <6 mL indicates severe atrophy/Klinefelter), epididymal thickening/cysts, vas deferens presence/thickening, varicocele detection via Valsalva maneuver, digital rectal examination for prostate/seminal vesicles [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].

#### STEP 1: Male Partner Evaluation — Semen Analysis (WHO 2010 Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Baseline Rule**: Male partner is evaluated FIRST because semen analysis is non-invasive, quick, and cost-effective [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206, 226].
- **Collection Protocol**: 3 to 5 days of sexual abstinence; collected by masturbation in a sterile wide-mouthed container near the lab (examined within 1 hour) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].
- **WHO 2010 Normal Lower Reference Limits**:
    - Volume **≥1.5 mL**, pH **≥7.2**.
    - Sperm Concentration **≥15 million/mL** (Total Count **≥39 million/ejaculate**).
    - Progressive Motility **≥32%** (or total motile ≥40%).
    - Vitality **≥58% live sperms**.
    - Normal Morphology **≥4%** (Kruger strict criteria).
    - Leukocytes **<1.0 million/mL**.
- _Clinical Precaution_: Never diagnose azoospermia or oligospermia based on a single sample; repeat analysis after **4 to 12 weeks** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].

#### STEP 2: Female Partner Evaluation — Ovulation Testing [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Serial Transvaginal Sonography (TVS) Follicular Monitoring (Gold Standard)**:
    - Performed from **Day 10 on alternate days**. Dominant follicle grows at **1 to 2 mm/day** to reach **18–20 mm**before rupture [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 220; Marrow Vol 1, p. 188].
    - _Sonographic Signs of Ovulation_: Sudden collapse/disappearance of dominant follicle, irregular follicular margins, fluid in Pouch of Douglas, and formation of corpus luteum [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 220; Marrow Vol 1, p. 188].
2. **Mid-Luteal Serum Progesterone Assay**:
    - Drawn on **Day 21 of a 28-day cycle** (or 7 days prior to expected menses).
    - **Serum Progesterone ≥3 ng/mL** confirms ovulation (optimal peak level **≥10–15 ng/mL**) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 220; Marrow Vol 1, p. 188].
3. **Endocrine Baseline Assay (Day 2–3)**:
    - Serum **FSH, LH, Prolactin, TSH, and Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH)** (AMH 1–3 ng/mL normal; <1 ng/mL indicates poor ovarian reserve; >3 ng/mL indicates PCOS) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 225; Marrow Vol 1, p. 190].

#### STEP 3: Female Partner Evaluation — Tubal Patency & Uterine Cavity Testing [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hysterosalpingography (HSG)**:
    - _Timing_: Performed in the pre-ovulatory phase (**Day 7 to 10 of cycle**) after cessation of menses to avoid aborting an early conceptus or intravasation [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Marrow Vol 1, p. 32].
    - _Technique_: Water-soluble radio-opaque dye (Urograffin 10–15 mL) injected via Leech-Wilkinson or Foley cannula under fluoroscopic control.
    - _Findings_: Outlines uterine cavity (shows submucous fibroid/polyp filling defects, bicornuate/septate uterus, Asherman synechiae) and demonstrates tubal patency via **free peritoneal spillage of dye** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Marrow Vol 1, p. 32, 191].
    - _Contraindications_: Active pelvic infection/PID, pregnancy, active uterine bleeding, allergy to iodine [Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509; Marrow Vol 1, p. 32].
2. **Sonosalpingography (SSG / SIS)**: Instillation of 200 mL sterile saline with air bubbles into the uterine cavity under TVS control; avoids radiation and contrast allergy [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
3. **Diagnostic Laparoscopy with Chromotubation (Gold Standard for Tubal & Pelvic Pathology)**:
    - _Indications_: Abnormal or ambiguous HSG, clinical suspicion of endometriosis/PID, unexplained infertility >3 years [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217; Ch 41, p. 524; Marrow Vol 1, p. 191].
    - _Technique_: Laparoscopic visualization of pelvic organs while **methylene blue or indigo carmine dye** is injected transcervically. Direct observation of dye spilling from fimbrial ends confirms tubal patency. Detects peritubal adhesions, fimbrial phimosis, endometriotic implants, and peritoneal tubercles [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217; Ch 41, p. 524; Marrow Vol 1, p. 191].
4. **Diagnostic Hysteroscopy**: Visualizes uterine cavity directly to identify submucous myomas, endometrial polyps, septa, or Asherman synechiae [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218; Ch 41, p. 530; Marrow Vol 1, p. 33].

#### A. STEP 1: Management of Ovulatory Infertility (PCOS & Anovulation) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **First-Line Ovulation Induction Agent — Letrozole (Drug of Choice for PCOS)**:
    - _Mechanism_: Aromatase inhibitor; prevents conversion of androgens to estrogens, clearing central feedback and elevating endogenous pituitary FSH [Marrow Vol 1, p. 102, 188].
    - _Dose_: **2.5 mg to 5 mg PO daily for 5 days** starting from **Day 3 to Day 7** (or Day 2 to 6) of the menstrual cycle [Marrow Vol 1, p. 102, 188]. Higher live birth rate and lower multiple gestation rate than Clomiphene.
2. **Clomiphene Citrate (CC)**:
    - _Mechanism_: Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM); blocks hypothalamic estrogen receptors, disinhibiting GnRH and stimulating pituitary FSH/LH release [Shaw 1, Ch 15, p. 193; Marrow Vol 1, p. 102].
    - _Dose_: **50 mg PO daily for 5 days** starting from **Day 2 to Day 6** of cycle (max dose 150 mg/day). Ovulation trigger with **Inj. hCG 5000–10,000 IU IM** given when dominant follicle reaches **18–20 mm** on serial TVS [Shaw 1, Ch 15, p. 193; Marrow Vol 1, p. 103].
3. **Second-Line Medical & Adjunctive Therapy**:
    - _Metformin_: **500 mg PO t.i.d.** for insulin-resistant PCOS [Shaw 1, Ch 15, p. 193; Marrow Vol 1, p. 105].
    - _Gonadotropin Therapy (hMG / Recombinant FSH)_: **75 to 150 IU SC/IM daily** for CC-resistant PCOS or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 223; Marrow Vol 1, p. 105].
    - _Bromocriptine (2.5 mg PO b.i.d.) / Cabergoline (0.5 mg PO weekly)_ for hyperprolactinemia [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 223].
    - _Anti-Tubercular Therapy (ATT Category 1 for 6 months)_ for documented Genital Tuberculosis [Shaw 1, Ch 28, p. 351; Marrow Vol 1, p. 184].

#### B. STEP 2: Surgical Management of Female & Male Infertility [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Operative Hysteroscopy**: Hysteroscopic metroplasty for septate uterus, adhesiolysis for Asherman syndrome (followed by pediatric Foley catheter insertion + high-dose Estrogen × 3 months), and submucous myomectomy [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218; Marrow Vol 1, p. 42, 184].
2. **Operative Laparoscopy**:
    - _Laparoscopic Ovarian Drilling (LOD / Rule of 4)_: Monopolar electrocautery applied at **4 sites per ovary for 4 seconds at 40 Watts** in CC-resistant PCOS [Marrow Vol 1, p. 105; Shaw 2, Ch 24, p. 317].
    - _Adhesiolysis & Endometriotic Ablation_: Excision of chocolate cysts, destruction of peritoneal implants, and fimbrioplasty [Shaw 1, Ch 14, p. 182; Ch 16, p. 219].
3. **Male Surgical Correction**: Microsurgical varicocelectomy, vasovasal or vasoepididymal anastomosis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 208].

#### C. STEP 3: Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)**:
    - _Technique_: Processed, concentrated highly motile sperm suspension (0.5 mL) is transcervically injected into the uterine cavity **36 hours post-hCG trigger** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 209].
    - _Indications_: Unexplained infertility (3–6 cycles), mild male factor (count >10 million/mL), cervical factor, coital dysfunction, hypospadias [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 209; Marrow Vol 1, p. 120].
2. **In Vitro Fertilization and Embryo Transfer (IVF-ET)**:
    - _Technique_: Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) with GnRH agonist/antagonist protocol + gonadotropins → TVS-guided transvaginal oocyte retrieval → co-incubation of mature oocytes with **50,000 processed sperms** per egg → transfer of 1 to 2 embryos into uterine cavity at Day 3 or Day 5 (blastocyst) stage [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 210].
    - _Indications_: **Bilateral irreparable tubal block (Method of Choice)**, severe pelvic endometriosis, failed IUI (3–6 cycles), diminished ovarian reserve [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 210; Marrow Vol 1, p. 120, 192].
3. **Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection (ICSI)**:
    - _Technique_: Micro-injection of a single selected sperm directly into the ooplasm of a mature metaphase-II egg under a micromanipulator [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 211].
    - _Indications_: **Severe male factor infertility (sperm count <5 million/mL)**, severe astheno/teratospermia, surgically retrieved sperms (PESA/TESA/MESA in obstructive/non-obstructive azoospermia), failed standard IVF [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 211; Marrow Vol 1, p. 225].

### 9. Complications of Infertility & ART Treatments [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pathophysiology**: Massive capillary hyperpermeability driven by VEGF release from hyperstimulated ovaries following hCG administration [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 223].
- **Clinical Features**: Massive ovarian enlargement (>12 cm), ascites, pleural effusion, hemoconcentration (hematocrit >55%), oliguria, electrolyte imbalance, thromboembolism, and acute renal failure [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 223].
- **Prevention**: Use of Letrozole, GnRH agonist trigger instead of hCG, freeze-all embryo strategy.

#### B. Multiple Gestation & Obstetric Risks

- High incidence of twins/triplets (20–30% in IVF/hMG) associated with preterm labor, pre-eclampsia, PPH, low birth weight, and fetal loss [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 210, 226].

#### C. Ectopic & Heterotopic Pregnancy

- Risk of ectopic pregnancy is elevated to **2% to 5%** following tubal pathology or ART procedures [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 211].

---

### 10. Prognosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- Prognosis depends primarily on maternal age, duration of infertility, and underlying etiology.
- **Good Prognosis**: Young female age (<35 years), anovulatory PCOS (80% ovulation, 50–60% pregnancy rate with Letrozole/CC), mild male factor, and early tubal adhesions [Shaw 1, Ch 15, p. 193; Ch 16, p. 210].
- **Poor Prognosis**: Female age >40 years, prolonged infertility (>5 years), severe primary testicular failure (high FSH NOA), bilateral dense hydrosalpinx, and severe frozen pelvis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 210, 226].

### 12. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Male-First Diagnostic Rule**: In evaluating any infertile couple presenting to the OPD, **ALWAYS perform semen analysis of the male partner FIRST** before subjecting the female partner to invasive, painful, or expensive procedures (such as HSG or laparoscopy). Semen analysis is simple, non-invasive, inexpensive, and immediately identifies male factor involvement (which accounts for 50% of cases overall).

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing Hysterosalpingography (HSG) in the post-ovulatory / luteal phase of the cycle, OR ordering HSG in a patient with active PID.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **HSG MUST be performed ONLY in the pre-ovulatory phase (Day 7 to 10 of cycle)** after menses has completely ceased. Performing HSG in the luteal phase risks flushing a fertilized ovum into the peritoneal cavity or disrupting an early implantation.
    - **HSG is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in active pelvic infection / PID** because forceful dye injection disseminates bacteria into the peritoneal cavity, precipitating acute pelvic peritonitis or septic shock!

### 14. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: C-O-U-P-L-E-S (Outpatient Infertility Protocol)

- **C**: **Coital History & Counseling** (frequency every 1–2 days around ovulation window).
- **O**: **Ovulation Assessment** (Serial TVS follicular monitoring + Day 21 S. Progesterone ≥3 ng/mL).
- **U**: **Uterine & Cervical Cavity Evaluation** (TVS, HSG on Day 7–10, SIS, Hysteroscopy).
- **P**: **Patency Testing for Fallopian Tubes** (HSG spillage & Laparoscopic Chromotubation).
- **L**: **Letrozole / Clomiphene Induction** (2.5–5 mg Letrozole or 50 mg CC for anovulation).
- **E**: **Evaluation of Male Partner FIRST** (Semen analysis WHO 2010 criteria).
- **S**: **Stepwise ART Selection** (IUI for mild factors/unexplained → IVF for tubal block → ICSI for severe male factor <5M/mL).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Failure to conceive after **1 year (12 months)** of regular unprotected intercourse (Primary = never conceived).
- **Etiological Share**: Male factor 35%, Female factor 35%, Combined/Unexplained 30%.
- **Male First Rule**: Always begin evaluation with **Semen Analysis** (WHO 2010: Volume ≥1.5 mL, Count ≥15 M/mL, Progressive Motility ≥32%, Morphology ≥4%).
- **Ovulation Tests**: Serial TVS (dominant follicle 18–20 mm) and Day 21 Serum Progesterone **≥3 ng/mL**.
- **Tubal Patency Tests**: HSG pre-ovulatory (**Day 7–10**) shows free spill; Diagnostic Laparoscopy + Chromotubation is gold standard.
- **Management**: Letrozole (DOC for PCOS ovulation induction); IUI for 3–6 cycles in unexplained/mild male factor; IVF-ET for bilateral tubal block; ICSI for severe male factor (<5 million/mL).

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **The management of a couple presenting with 4 years of primary infertility requires a structured, non-judgmental dual-partner evaluation, initiating with non-invasive semen analysis of the male partner, followed by systematic assessment of female ovulation (serial TVS and serum progesterone), tubal patency (HSG and laparoscopic chromotubation), and uterine cavity integrity, guiding etiology-specific medical induction, endoscopic surgery, or assisted reproductive technology (IUI, IVF, and ICSI).**
