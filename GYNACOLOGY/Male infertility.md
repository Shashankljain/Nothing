### 1. Definition & Overview [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition of Infertility

- **Infertility**: Defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the failure of a couple to achieve conception after **1 year (12 months) of regular, unprotected sexual intercourse**.
- **Sterility**: Refers to an absolute and permanent inability to conceive due to irreversible biological factors (e.g., gonadal dysgenesis, bilateral orchidectomy).
- **Primary vs. Secondary Infertility**:
    - _Primary Infertility_: Conception has **never occurred** in the female partner despite regular unprotected intercourse.
    - _Secondary Infertility_: Inability to achieve conception after **having previously achieved at least one successful conception** (regardless of outcome).

#### B. Epidemiology & Relative Gender Contribution

- **Incidence**: Affects **10% to 15%** of married couples worldwide.
- **Couple Dynamics (Rule of Thirds)**:
    - **Male Factor Alone**: **30% to 35% (1/3rd of cases)**.
    - **Female Factor Alone**: **30% to 35% (1/3rd of cases)**.
    - **Combined / Both Partners & Unexplained**: **30% to 35% (1/3rd of cases)**.
- _Key Takeaway_: Both male and female partners contribute equally to the infertile state, mandating simultaneous, non-judgmental evaluation of both partners.

### 2. Etiology / Causes of Male Infertility [⭐ High-Yield]

Male infertility is classified based on the anatomical and pathophysiological level of the defect into pre-testicular, testicular, post-testicular, sperm functional, coital/sexual, and lifestyle/environmental factors:

#### A. Pre-Testicular / Endocrine Causes (Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis Dysfunction)

1. **Hypothalamic Disorders**: **Kallmann Syndrome** (isolated GnRH deficiency associated with anosmia/hyposmia).
2. **Pituitary Disorders**: Pituitary adenoma, panhypopituitarism, or isolated gonadotropin (FSH/LH) deficiency.
3. **Hyperprolactinemia**: Prolactin levels >30 ng/mL suppress GnRH secretion, causing loss of libido, impotence, and defective spermatogenesis.
4. **Other Endocrinopathies**: Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, hypothyroidism, and Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH).

#### B. Primary Testicular Disorders (Disorders of Spermatogenesis — 50%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Varicocele**: Dilatation of the pampiniform venous plexus (most common treatable cause of male infertility); increases scrotal temperature and impairs sperm motility and morphology.
2. **Cryptorchidism (Undescended Testis)**: Exposure of germ cells to higher intra-abdominal body temperature causes progressive testicular atrophy and germ cell loss.
3. **Genetic & Chromosomal Defects**: **Klinefelter Syndrome (47,XXY)** (most common genetic cause, resulting in small atrophic testes and azoospermia), Y-chromosome microdeletions (AZF region).
4. **Infections & Inflammatory Atrophy**: Mumps orchitis (post-pubertal bilateral orchitis leads to testicular atrophy), genital tuberculosis, gonococcal or chlamydial infection.
5. **Iatrogenic & Pharmacological Ingestion**: Radiation exposure, chemotherapy (alkylating agents), drugs (antihypertensives, spironolactone, cimetidine, calcium channel blockers, anabolic steroids, nitrofurantoin).
6. **Thermal Exposure**: Stokers, blast furnace workers, prolonged hot baths, wearing tight scrotal supports.

#### C. Post-Testicular Disorders (Ductal Obstruction — 30%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Congenital Obstruction**: Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD) associated with Cystic Fibrosis (CFTR gene mutation), seminal vesicle agenesis.
2. **Acquired Inflammatory Block**: Bilateral epididymal or vasal occlusion secondary to gonorrhea, tuberculosis, or chlamydial infection.
3. **Surgical Trauma**: Accidental vas deferens ligation during pediatric hernia repair, orchidopexy, or prior voluntary vasectomy.
4. **Young Syndrome**: Triad of obstructive azoospermia, chronic sinusitis, and bronchiectasis due to inspissated mucus.

#### D. Sperm Structural & Functional Defects (15%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Asthenospermia**: Decreased progressive sperm motility.
2. **Kartagener Syndrome (Immotile Cilia Syndrome)**: Absence of dynein arms in sperm axial filaments and respiratory cilia, presenting with situs inversus, bronchiectasis, sinusitis, and complete sperm immotility.
3. **Acrosome Defect (Globozoospermia)**: Round-headed sperms lacking acrosomal caps, rendering them incapable of zona pellucida penetration.
4. **Immunological Infertility (5%)**: Anti-sperm antibodies (ASA) in seminal plasma or cervical mucus agglutinate or immobilize spermatozoa.

#### E. Sexual / Coital Dysfunctions

- Erectile dysfunction (impotence), premature ejaculation, painful coitus (hypospadias, chordee), retrograde ejaculation (ejaculation of semen backward into urinary bladder due to bladder neck incompetence, common in diabetic neuropathy or post-TURP).
- Low coital frequency or incorrect coital timing relative to ovulation.

#### F. Lifestyle, Environmental & Psychological Factors

- Heavy cigarette smoking, chronic alcohol consumption, tobacco/gutka chewing, recreational drug abuse.
- Severe obesity (increases peripheral conversion of androgens to estrogens, altering HPT axis).
- Chronic stress, anxiety, and depression.

### 3. Classification of Semen Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

Semen abnormalities are classified based on volume, sperm concentration, motility, and morphology as per WHO 2010 criteria:

|Diagnostic Term|Seminal Feature & Quantitative Threshold|Underlying Mechanism / Clinical Association|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Aspermia**|**Complete absence of ejaculate fluid** (0 mL).|Retrograde ejaculation, anejaculation, or severe neurological injury.|
|**Azoospermia**|**Complete absence of spermatozoa** in semen after centrifugation.|Testicular failure (NOA) or ductal obstruction (OA).|
|**Oligospermia**|**Sperm concentration <15 million/mL** (or total count <39 million/ejaculate).|Varicocele, endocrine defects, cryptorchidism.|
|**Asthenospermia**|**Progressive motility <32%** (or <50% total motile sperms).|Varicocele, infection, anti-sperm antibodies, Kartagener syndrome.|
|**Teratospermia**|**Normal morphology <4%** (by Kruger strict criteria).|Stress, toxins, varicoceles, heat exposure.|
|**Necrospermia**|**Presence of <58% live sperms** (or >50% dead sperms).|Infection, prolonged stagnation, oxidative stress.|
|**Oligoasthenoteratozoospermia (OAT)**|**Combined defect** in sperm count, motility, and morphology.|Idiopathic, severe varicocele, or toxic damage.|
|**Hypospermia**|Ejaculate volume **<1.5 mL**.|Incomplete collection, short abstinence, CBAVD, ejaculatory duct block.|

### 5. Clinical Features & Diagnostic Assessment [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Clinical History

- **Duration of Infertility & Contraceptive History**: Duration of attempt, previous marriage/paternity, previous contraception used.
- **Coital History**: Coital frequency, timing relative to ovulation, coital position, erectile/ejaculatory difficulties.
- **Occupational & Environmental Exposure**: Exposure to heat (stokers, hot baths), radiation, toxic chemicals, heavy metals.
- **Past Medical & Surgical History**: Mumps orchitis, tuberculosis, STIs, pelvic/scrotal/inguinal operations (hernia repair, orchidopexy, varicocelectomy).
- **Substance Abuse**: Smoking, alcohol, tobacco/gutka, recreational drugs.

#### B. Physical Examination

- **General Examination**: Stature, arm span, body hair distribution, gynecomastia (suggestive of Klinefelter syndrome or androgen insensitivity).
- **Local Scrotal Examination** (in standing posture):
    - _Testicular Size & Volume_: Normal testicular volume is **15 to 35 mL (average 18 mL)**. Testicular volume <6 mL with firm/soft consistency indicates severe testicular atrophy/failure (e.g., Klinefelter).
    - _Epididymis & Vas Deferens_: Palpated for thickening, tenderness, cysts, or congenital bilateral absence of vas deferens (CBAVD).
    - _Varicocele Detection_: Palpated in standing position while performing **Valsalva maneuver** (more common on the left side due to perpendicular insertion of left testicular vein into left renal vein).
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Evaluates prostate size and seminal vesicle tenderness/enlargement.

#### C. Laboratory & Special Diagnostic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Semen Analysis (Gold Standard Initial Test)**:
    - **Abstinence**: 3 to 5 days.
    - **Method of Collection**: Masturbation in a clean container near lab (avoid ordinary latex condoms due to spermicidal additives).
    - **WHO 2010 Normal Criteria**: Volume ≥1.5 mL, pH 7.2–7.8, Sperm Concentration **≥15 million/mL**, Total Sperm Count **≥39 million/ejaculate**, Progressive Motility **≥32%**, Vitality **≥58% live**, Normal Morphology **≥4%**.
2. **Hormonal Profile**:
    - **Serum FSH**: Normal level in azoospermia suggests _Obstructive Azoospermia_; elevated FSH (>2x normal) denotes _Primary Testicular Failure (NOA)_.
    - **Serum LH & Testosterone**: Low testosterone with high LH indicates Leydig cell failure; low FSH/LH indicates hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
    - **Serum Prolactin & TSH**: Evaluates hyperprolactinemia (>30 ng/mL) and thyroid dysfunction.
3. **Scrotal Ultrasonography & Color Flow Doppler**: Accurately measures testicular volume, detects subclinical varicoceles, epididymal cysts, and hydrocele.
4. **Testicular Biopsy (TESA / Tru-Cut Biopsy)**:
    - Indicated in azoospermia with normal serum FSH to differentiate between **testicular failure** (Sertoli-cell-only syndrome, maturation arrest) and **ductal obstruction** (normal spermatogenesis).
5. **Post-Ejaculatory Urine Analysis**: Centrifugation of alkaline post-ejaculatory urine; presence of sperms confirms **retrograde ejaculation**.
6. **Karyotyping & Genetic Analysis**: Indicated in severe oligospermia (<5 million/mL) or non-obstructive azoospermia to rule out Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY) or Y-chromosome microdeletions.
7. **Special Tests**: Hypo-osmotic Swelling (HOS) test (evaluates membrane integrity of live immotile sperms), Anti-Sperm Antibody (ASA) assays, Sperm DNA Fragmentation Index (DFI), and Vasogram.

### 6. Differential Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Clinical Differentiation: Obstructive Azoospermia vs. Non-Obstructive Azoospermia

|Clinical Parameter|Obstructive Azoospermia (OA) [⭐ High-Yield]|Non-Obstructive Azoospermia (NOA) [⭐ High-Yield]|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Primary Etiology**|Physical ductal blockage (post-infection, vasectomy, CBAVD).|Primary testicular germ cell failure, Klinefelter, cryptorchidism.|
|**Testicular Size & Volume**|**Normal volume (15–35 mL)** and firm consistency.|**Markedly reduced (<6 mL)**, soft, and atrophic.|
|**Serum FSH Level**|**Normal FSH level**.|**Markedly Elevated Serum FSH (>2x normal)**.|
|**Fructose in Semen**|Absent in CBAVD / seminal vesicle block; present in epididymal block.|Present.|
|**Testicular Biopsy Findings**|**Normal active spermatogenesis** with lumen filled with sperms.|**Sertoli-cell-only syndrome**, hypospermatogenesis, or arrest.|
|**Definitive Treatment**|Surgical Vasovasal / Vasoepididymal Anastomosis OR **PESA/MESA + ICSI**.|**TESE/TESA + ICSI** or Donor Insemination.|


#### A. General, Preventive & Lifestyle Measures

1. **Coital Education & Timing**: Optimizing coital frequency (every 1–2 days around ovulation); avoiding lubricants that are spermicidal.
2. **Reduction of Scrotal Heat**: Avoid hot baths, saunas, steam rooms; wear loose cotton underwear to improve scrotal ventilation; avoid working in excessively hot environments.
3. **Cessation of Substance Abuse**: Strict avoidance of tobacco (smoking/chewing), alcohol, and recreational drugs.
4. **Antioxidant Therapy**: Administration of **Vitamin E (100 mg)**, **Vitamin C (500–1000 mg)**, **Lycopenes (2 mg daily)**, **L-Carnitine (3 g daily)**, **Coenzyme Q10**, and **Pentoxifylline (400 mg t.i.d.)** for 3 to 6 months to reduce oxidative stress and sperm DNA damage.

#### B. Medical & Hormonal Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Gonadotropin Therapy (for Hypogonadal Hypogonadism / Pituitary Failure)**:
    - **Inj. hCG (Human Chorionic Gonadotropin)**: **3000 to 5000 IU IM twice weekly** for 12 weeks.
    - **Inj. hMG (Human Menopausal Gonadotropin) / Recombinant FSH**: **75 to 150 IU SC/IM thrice weekly**added to hCG therapy for 6 to 12 months to induce complete spermatogenesis (achieves ~40% pregnancy rate).
2. **Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulators (SERMs)**:
    - **Clomiphene Citrate**: **25 mg PO daily for 25 days** followed by a 5-day rest period, given cyclically for 3 to 6 months in idiopathic normogonadotropic oligospermia.
    - **Letrozole**: **2.5 mg PO daily** as an alternative aromatase inhibitor.
3. **Management of Hyperprolactinemia**: **Tab. Bromocriptine (2.5 mg daily)** or **Cabergoline (0.5 mg weekly)**restores LH secretion and testosterone production.
4. **Management of Retrograde Ejaculation**: **Tab. Ephedrine (60 mg PO q.i.d.)** or **Phenylephrine (2.5 mg)** for 2 weeks prior to coitus to tighten the bladder neck. Alternatively, centrifuged alkaline post-ejaculatory urine is processed for IUI.
5. **Antibiotic Therapy**: **Tab. Doxycycline (100 mg PO b.i.d. for 6 weeks)** for chlamydial epididymitis, prostatitis, or leukocytospermia.

#### C. Surgical Interventions [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Microsurgical Varicocelectomy**: Gold-standard treatment for clinical varicocele; improves sperm motility and density in 60% to 70% of cases.
2. **Microsurgical Vasovasal / Vasoepididymal Anastomosis**: Re-establishes ductal patency in localized vasal or epididymal obstruction (including vasectomy reversal).
3. **Orchidopexy**: Performed in early childhood for cryptorchidism to preserve germ cell maturation potential.

#### D. Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) for Male Infertility [⭐ High-Yield]

When medical and surgical options fail or are unfeasible, ART procedures offer definitive solutions:

```

                               │    ART Selection for Male Infertility  │

         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┐

│ 1. Intrauterine         │               │ 2. In Vitro             │               │ 3. Intracytoplasmic     │
│    Insemination (IUI)   │               │    Fertilization (IVF)  │               │    Sperm Injection ICSI │
│ • Sperm count >10 M/mL  │               │ • Sperm count 1–5 M/mL  │               │ • Sperm count <5 M/mL   │
│ • Ejaculatory failure   │               │ • Mild asthenospermia   │               │ • Severe OAT / Immotile │
│ • Hypospadias           │               │ • Failed IUI (3-6x)     │               │ • TESA/PESA/MESA sperms │
└─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘               └─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)**:
    - _Technique_: Ejaculated semen is washed and concentrated via "swim-up" or density-gradient centrifugation to select highly motile, debris-free sperms. Approximately **0.5 mL** of washed sperm suspension is injected transcervically into the uterine cavity **36 hours post-hCG injection**.
    - _Indications_: Mild oligospermia (sperm count >10 million/mL), ejaculatory failure, retrograde ejaculation, hypospadias, anti-sperm antibodies.
2. **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF)**:
    - _Technique_: Oocytes retrieved from female partner are co-incubated with **50,000 processed motile sperms** per oocyte in culture media.
    - _Indications_: Moderate oligospermia (sperm count 1 to 5 million/mL), asthenospermia, failed IUI.
3. **Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection (ICSI)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Technique_: Under a micromanipulator microscope, a **single viable sperm** is directly injected into the cytoplasm of a mature metaphase-II oocyte. Achieves **50% to 70% fertilization rates** and **30% to 40% pregnancy rates** per cycle.
    - _Indications_: Severe male factor infertility (sperm count **<5 million/mL**), severe asthenospermia/teratospermia, complete unresponsiveness to zona penetration, failed standard IVF, and sperms retrieved surgically in azoospermia.
4. **Surgical Sperm Retrieval Techniques (for ICSI in Azoospermia)**:
    - **PESA (Percutaneous Epididymal Sperm Aspiration)**: Fine needle aspiration of sperms from the epididymis under local anesthesia.
    - **MESA (Microsurgical Epididymal Sperm Aspiration)**: Direct surgical aspiration of epididymal fluid under microscopic guidance.
    - **TESA / TESE (Testicular Sperm Aspiration / Extraction)**: Testicular biopsy or needle aspiration to retrieve testicular spermatozoa or spermatids directly from seminiferous tubules.
5. **Donor Insemination (Artificial Insemination by Donor — AID)**:
    - Indicated in uncorrectable non-obstructive azoospermia, severe genetic disorders in the husband, or homozygous Rh-positive husband with severe isoimmunization. Donor semen is cryopreserved and quarantined for **6 months** to rule out HIV transmission before use.

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 74-Day Spermatogenic Cycle Rule**: Spermatogenesis requires **74 days** plus ductal transit time (total ~3 months). Therefore, any medical, surgical (e.g., varicocelectomy), or lifestyle intervention for male infertility will take **at least 3 months** to show demonstrable improvements on repeat semen analysis. Always re-evaluate semen parameters **8 to 12 weeks** post-treatment!

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Assuming that a high FSH level in an azoospermic male can be treated with exogenous gonadotropins (hCG/hMG), OR mistaking Obstructive Azoospermia for Primary Testicular Failure.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **Elevated Serum FSH (>2x normal)** indicates **Primary Testicular Failure (NOA)** where germ cells are absent or un-responsive; administering exogenous FSH/hCG is ineffective! Treatment requires **TESE/TESA + ICSI** or donor insemination.
    - **Normal Serum FSH with normal testicular volume** in azoospermia indicates **Obstructive Azoospermia (OA)**, which has an excellent prognosis with surgical reconstruction or PESA/MESA + ICSI.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Inability of a couple to conceive after **1 year of regular, unprotected intercourse** (male factor responsible in ~30–35% alone and 50% overall).
- **Pre-Testicular Causes**: Kallmann syndrome, hypopituitarism, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid/adrenal disorders.
- **Testicular Causes (50%)**: Varicocele (most common treatable cause), cryptorchidism, Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY), mumps orchitis, toxic drugs, thermal exposure.
- **Post-Testicular Causes (30%)**: CBAVD (CFTR mutation), post-infectious vasal/epididymal block, vasectomy.
- **Diagnostic Differentiation**: High FSH = Testicular Failure (NOA); Normal FSH + Normal Testes = Obstructive Azoospermia (OA).
- **ART of Choice for Severe Male Infertility**: Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection (ICSI) with ejaculated sperms or surgically retrieved sperms (TESA/PESA/MESA).
