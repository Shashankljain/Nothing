# Semen Analysis and Its Interpretation [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Definition

- **Semen Analysis (Seminogram)**: The primary, cornerstone laboratory investigation for assessing male fertility potential. It evaluates the physical characteristics of seminal plasma as well as the quantitative and qualitative parameters of spermatozoa.

#### B. Primary Indications

- Initial evaluation of an infertile couple (performed concurrently with female work-up).
- Verification of sterility following **No-Scalpel Vasectomy (NSV)** (performed at 12–16 weeks or after 20 ejaculations).
- Assessment of testicular recovery following varicocele repair, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy.
- Evaluation of medico-legal cases (paternity disputes, sexual assault).

---

### 2. Protocol for Specimen Collection & Handling [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pre-Test Instructions & Collection Protocol

- **Abstinence Period**: A strict sexual abstinence of **3 to 5 days** must be observed prior to collection (shorter abstinence reduces sperm count; longer abstinence >7 days impairs motility and viability).
- **Method of Collection**:
    - **Masturbation**: Preferred method of choice, ideally performed in a private room adjacent to the laboratory to avoid temperature fluctuations and transit delays.
    - **Coitus Interruptus**: Acceptable second choice into a clean, sterile, wide-mouthed glass or plastic container if masturbation is not culturally or personally feasible.
- **Contraindicated Method**: Ordinary latex condoms MUST NOT be used because they contain spermicidal chemicals and lubricants that artifactually immobilize and kill sperms.

#### B. Specimen Handling & Physical Processing

- **Temperature & Transit**: Specimen should be kept at body temperature (37 °C) and examined within **1 to 2 hours**of production (preferably within 30–60 minutes).
- **Coagulation & Liquefaction**:
    - Freshly ejaculated semen coagulates immediately due to fibrin-like proteins secreted by the **seminal vesicles**.
    - Liquefaction occurs within **20 to 30 minutes** at room temperature under the action of proteolytic enzymes (fibrinolysin and prostate-specific antigen [PSA]) secreted by the **prostate gland**.
- **Normal Appearance**: Homogeneous, translucent, greyish-white opalescent fluid with a characteristic seminal odor.

### 3. Normal Semen Parameters (WHO 2010 Reference Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

|Parameter|WHO 2010 Lower Reference Limit|Normal Range / Desirable Value|Clinical Significance & Source|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Ejaculate Volume**|**≥1.5 mL** (1.5–5.0 mL)|2.0 to 5.0 mL|Reflects secretory function of seminal vesicles (60%) and prostate (30%).|
|**pH**|**≥7.2** (7.2 to 7.8)|Alkaline (7.2–7.8)|Protects sperms against acidic vaginal pH (pH 4.5); pH <7.0 with azoospermia suggests vas/seminal vesicle agenesis.|
|**Sperm Concentration**|**≥15 million/mL**|20 to 120 million/mL|Measures density of spermatozoa in seminal fluid.|
|**Total Sperm Count**|**≥39 million/ejaculate**|>40 million/ejaculate|Total fertilizing capacity per single ejaculate.|
|**Progressive Motility (PR)**|**≥32%**|>50% total (PR + NP)|Sperms moving actively in a forward direction (grades a + b).|
|**Morphology (Kruger Strict)**|**≥4% normal forms**|>14% normal forms|Evaluates normal head, neck, midpiece, and tail anatomy.|
|**Vitality (Viability)**|**≥58%** (or >75% live)|>75% live sperms|Eosin-nigrosin dye exclusion test; distinguishes live immotile sperms from dead sperms.|
|**Leukocyte (WBC) Count**|**<1.0 million/mL**|<1.0 million/mL|WBC ≥1.0 million/mL (leukocytospermia) indicates accessory gland infection/prostatitis.|
|**Round Cells**|**<5.0 million/mL**|<1.0 million/mL|Includes immature germ cells and leukocytes.|
|**Fructose Content**|**Present**|Qualitative positive|Secreted by seminal vesicles; absent in bilateral vas deferens / seminal vesicle congenital absence.|

#### A. Volume Abnormalities

- **Aspermia**: Complete absence of ejaculate fluid (occurs in retrograde ejaculation, anejaculation, or severe neurological injury).
- **Hypospermia**: Ejaculate volume **<1.5 mL**. Causes include incomplete collection, short abstinence, ejaculatory duct obstruction, congenital absence of vas deferens/seminal vesicles, or hypogonadism.
- **Hyperspermia**: Ejaculate volume **>5.5 mL** (seen in prolonged abstinence or active seminal vesiculitis).

#### B. Concentration & Count Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Azoospermia**: Complete absence of spermatozoa in the ejaculated semen after centrifugation.
- **Oligospermia**: Sperm concentration **<15 million/mL**:
    - _Mild Oligospermia_: 10 to 15 million/mL.
    - _Moderate Oligospermia_: 5 to 15 million/mL.
    - _Severe Oligospermia_: **<5 million/mL** (strong indication for ICSI).

#### C. Motility, Viability & Morphological Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Asthenospermia**: Reduced progressive sperm motility (**<32% progressive** or **<50% total motile sperms**). Caused by varicocele, infection, anti-sperm antibodies, or dynein arm defect (Kartagener's syndrome).
- **Teratospermia**: Abnormal sperm morphology with **<4% normal forms** by strict criteria.
- **Necrospermia**: Presence of **<58% live sperms** (high percentage of dead sperms in ejaculate).
- **Oligoasthenoteratozoospermia (OAT) Syndrome**: Combined defect in sperm count, motility, and morphology.

### 6. Differential Diagnosis of Azoospermia [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Diagnostic Differentiation: Obstructive vs Non-Obstructive Azoospermia

|Parameter|Obstructive Azoospermia (OA) [⭐ High-Yield]|Non-Obstructive Azoospermia (NOA) [⭐ High-Yield]|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Primary Etiology**|Physical blockage in vas deferens, epididymis, or ejaculatory duct (post-infection, vasectomy, CBAVD).|Primary testicular failure, genetic defects (Klinefelter 47,XXY), cryptorchidism.|
|**Testicular Size**|**Normal volume (15–25 mL)** and normal firm consistency.|**Reduced volume (<6 mL)**, soft and atrophic.|
|**Serum FSH Level**|**Normal**.|**Markedly Elevated (High FSH >2x normal)**.|
|**Fructose in Semen**|Absent in congenital bilateral absence of vas deferens (CBAVD); present in epididymal block.|Present.|
|**Testicular Biopsy**|**Normal active spermatogenesis** with luminal spermatozoa.|**Sertoli-cell-only syndrome**, maturation arrest, or severe hypospermatogenesis.|
|**Definitive Treatment**|Surgical vasovasal / vasoepididymal anastomosis OR **PESA/MESA + ICSI**.|**TESE/TESA + ICSI** or Donor Insemination.|

### 7. Special & Advanced Seminal Tests [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hypo-Osmotic Swelling (HOS) Test**: Tests functional plasma membrane integrity of live immotile sperms by exposing them to hypoosmotic solution (intact membrane sperms swell and coil at tail); selects viable sperms for ICSI.
2. **Computer-Assisted Semen Analysis (CASA)**: Provides objective, computerized assessment of sperm velocity, lateral head displacement, and kinematic trajectory.
3. **Anti-Sperm Antibody (ASA) Assays**: Immunobead or MAR (Mixed Antiglobulin Reaction) test; detects IgG/IgA antibodies bound to sperms (>50% bound indicates immunological infertility).
4. **Sperm DNA Fragmentation Index (DFI)**: Evaluates single/double-strand DNA breaks (DFI >30% indicates poor fertility and higher abortion risk).

### 8. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 3-Month Repeat Rule**: Never diagnose male infertility or proclaim a patient azoospermic based on a **single abnormal semen analysis report**. Spermatogenesis requires **74 days** plus ductal transport (total ~3 months). Always repeat semen analysis after **4 to 12 weeks** following lifestyle modification (cold baths, avoidance of tight underwear, cessation of smoking/alcohol) before establishing a diagnosis.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Assuming an azoospermic male with a low ejaculate volume (<1.5 mL) and acidic pH (<7.0) has testicular failure.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - Acidic semen (pH <7.0) + low volume (<1.5 mL) + absent fructose + azoospermia strongly indicates **Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD)** or ejaculatory duct obstruction (associated with CFTR gene mutations in Cystic Fibrosis). Testicular volume and serum FSH are completely normal!.

### 9. Diagram Guide

### Diagram : Structure of a Normal Spermatozoon

- **Type**: Morphological schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Elongated cellular outline consisting of head, neck, midpiece, and tail.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Acrosomal Cap** — covers anterior 2/3rd of head; contains hyaluronidase and acrosin enzymes.
    2. **Head Nucleus** — condensed haploid DNA (23,X or 23,Y).
    3. **Neck & Centriole** — connecting segment.
    4. **Midpiece** — contains spiral mitochondrial sheath supplying ATP for motility.
    5. **Tail (Principal & End Piece)** — axial filament (9+2 microtubule arrangement) providing whip-like propulsion.
- **Annotation**: _"Normal spermatozoon measures ~50 μm in length with an intact acrosomal cap, condensed nuclear head, mitochondrial midpiece, and motile tail."_.

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: S-E-M-E-N (WHO Criteria Checklist)

- **S**: **Sperm Concentration ≥15 million/mL** (Total count ≥39 million/ejaculate).
- **E**: **Ejaculate Volume ≥1.5 mL** (pH ≥7.2, alkaline).
- **M**: **Motility ≥32% progressive** (or >50% total motile).
- **E**: **Evaluation of Morphology ≥4% normal forms** (Kruger strict criteria).
- **N**: **Normal Vitality ≥58% live** (WBC count <1.0 million/mL).

### Quick Revision

- **Collection**: Masturbation after **3–5 days abstinence**; examine within **1–2 hours**; never use ordinary latex condoms.
- **WHO 2010 Criteria**: Volume ≥1.5 mL, pH ≥7.2, Concentration ≥15 million/mL, Total Count ≥39 million, Progressive Motility ≥32%, Morphology ≥4%.
- **Liquefaction**: Coagulates by seminal vesicle fluid; liquefies in **20–30 min** by prostatic enzymes.
- **Nomenclature**: Azoospermia (0 sperms), Oligospermia (<15 M/mL), Asthenospermia (PR <32%), Teratospermia (<4% normal), Necrospermia (<58% live).
- **Azoospermia Work-Up**: High FSH = Testicular Failure (NOA); Normal FSH + Normal Testes = Obstructive Azoospermia (OA).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Semen analysis is the cornerstone investigation for male factor infertility, performed after 3 to 5 days of abstinence and evaluated against WHO 2010 criteria (volume ≥1.5 mL, concentration ≥15 million/mL, progressive motility ≥32%, and morphology ≥4%) to differentiate obstructive from non-obstructive causes.**.
