# Male Infertility: Causes and Etiological Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Epidemiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Infertility**: Defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the failure of a couple to achieve conception after **1 year (12 months)** of regular, unprotected sexual intercourse [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203].
- **Epidemiology**: Affects **10% to 15%** of married couples worldwide [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203].
- **Relative Male Contribution**: Both partners contribute equally to infertility. The male partner is directly responsible in **one-third (30–35%)** of cases alone, and contributes as a combined factor in another **30–35%**, making male factor involvement present in approximately **50% of overall infertile couples** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 205].

#### A. Major Contribution Breakdown (Faults in the Male) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Disorders of Spermatogenesis**: **50%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
2. **Obstruction of Efferent Ducts (Post-testicular)**: **30%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
3. **Disorders of Sperm Motility**: **15%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
4. **Sexual / Coital Dysfunction**: Variable proportion [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
5. **Unexplained Factors**: **15%** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].

### 3. Comprehensive Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Pre-Testicular / Endocrine Causes (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal Axis Failure) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hypothalamic Disorders**: **Kallmann Syndrome** (isolated GnRH deficiency associated with anosmia, hyposmia, and delayed puberty) [Shaw 1, Ch 12, p. 143; Ch 16, p. 205].
- **Pituitary Disorders**: Pituitary insufficiency, adenoma, craniopharyngioma, or isolated gonadotropin (FSH/LH) deficiency [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
- **Hyperprolactinemia**: Serum prolactin **>30 ng/mL** suppresses GnRH pulsation, leading to diminished libido, erectile failure, and defective spermatogenesis [Shaw 1, Ch 12, p. 149; Ch 16, p. 205].
- **Other Endocrinopathies**: Thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism), Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH), and uncontrolled diabetes mellitus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].

#### B. Primary Testicular Disorders (Defective Spermatogenesis — 50%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Varicocele**: Dilatation of the pampiniform venous plexus (most common on the left side); acts as the **most common treatable cause of male infertility** by elevating scrotal temperature and inducing toxic venous reflux [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 208].
- **Cryptorchidism (Undescended Testis)**: Exposure of germ cells to intra-abdominal body temperature leads to progressive testicular atrophy and loss of germinal epithelium [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].
- **Genetic & Chromosomal Defects**: **Klinefelter Syndrome (47,XXY)** (present in **1% to 2%** of infertile males; characterized by small atrophic testes **<6 mL**, tall stature, gynecomastia, and azoospermia), Y-chromosome microdeletions (AZF region) [Shaw 1, Ch 9, p. 114; Ch 16, p. 205–206].
- **Orchitis & Infections**: Post-pubertal mumps orchitis (causes severe testicular atrophy), genital tuberculosis, gonococcal, or chlamydial infection [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205; Ch 28, p. 351].
- **Iatrogenic & Pharmacological Agents**: Chemotherapy (alkylating agents), radiation therapy, antihypertensives, anticonvulsants, calcium channel blockers, spironolactone, cimetidine, nitrofurantoin, and anabolic steroids [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206].
- **Testicular Trauma & Torsion**: Accidental blow, scrotal hematoma formation followed by atrophy, or surgical injury during hernia/hydrocele repair [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].

#### C. Post-Testicular Disorders (Ductal Obstruction — 30%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Congenital Anomaly**: Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD) associated with Cystic Fibrosis (CFTR gene mutation) or seminal vesicle agenesis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206].
- **Acquired Inflammatory Blockade**: Occlusion of epididymis or vas deferens secondary to gonorrhea, tuberculosis, _Chlamydia trachomatis_, _Escherichia coli_, _Staphylococcus_, or _Mycoplasma genitalis_ [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206; Ch 29, p. 369].
- **Surgical Trauma**: Accidental vas deferens ligation during childhood inguinal hernia repair (herniorrhaphy scar), orchidopexy, or voluntary vasectomy [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 208].
- **Young Syndrome**: Triad of obstructive azoospermia, inspissated mucus, chronic sinusitis, and bronchiectasis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205].

#### D. Sperm Structural & Functional Defects (15%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Immotile Cilia Syndrome (Kartagener Syndrome)**: Absence of dynein arms in sperm axial filaments and respiratory cilia, presenting with situs inversus, bronchiectasis, sinusitis, and complete asthenospermia [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205–206].
- **Acrosomal Defects**: Globozoospermia (round-headed sperms lacking acrosomal caps, preventing zona pellucida penetration) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 204, 206].
- **Immunological Infertility (5%)**: Presence of anti-sperm antibodies (ASA) in seminal plasma or cervical mucus causing shaky movement or sperm agglutination [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 208].

#### E. Sexual & Coital Dysfunctions

- Low coital frequency, incorrect timing relative to ovulation, erectile dysfunction (impotence), hypospadias (preventing proper intravaginal semen deposition), premature ejaculation, and retrograde ejaculation (semen passes backward into bladder d/t bladder neck incompetence in diabetic neuropathy or post-prostatectomy) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206, 208].

#### F. Environmental, Lifestyle & General Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Occupational Thermal Exposure**: Stokers, blast furnace workers, frequent hot baths/saunas, prolonged sitting, and tight scrotal supports/underwear elevate scrotal temperature, impairing spermatogenesis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 208].
- **Substance Abuse**: Heavy cigarette smoking, chronic alcohol consumption, tobacco/gutka chewing, and recreational drug abuse [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 208].
- **Obesity**: Increases peripheral conversion of androgens to estrogens in adipose tissue, altering the HPG axis [Shaw 1, Ch 9, p. 117; Ch 16, p. 206].
- **Fever**: Any febrile illness can suppress spermatogenesis for up to **6 months** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].

### 5. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### A. Semen Analysis (Cornerstone Initial Test — WHO 2010 Reference Values) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pre-test Protocol**: Collected by masturbation after **3 to 5 days of abstinence** in a clean container; examined within **1 to 2 hours** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206].
- **Reference Parameters**:
    - Ejaculate Volume: **≥1.5 mL** (pH 7.2–7.8) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206–207].
    - Sperm Concentration: **≥15 million/mL** (Total count **≥39 million/ejaculate**) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206–207].
    - Progressive Motility: **≥32%** (Total motile ≥40%) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 206–207].
    - Vitality: **≥58% live sperms** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 207].
    - Morphology: **≥4% normal forms** (Kruger strict criteria) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 207].
    - Leukocytes: **<1.0 million/mL** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 207].

#### B. Secondary Investigations

- **Hormonal Profile**: Serum FSH (high in testicular failure; normal in ductal block), LH, Testosterone, Prolactin, TSH [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 208].
- **Scrotal Ultrasound & Color Doppler**: Evaluates testicular volume and confirms subclinical varicocele [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 208].
- **Post-Ejaculatory Urinalysis**: Centrifugation of alkaline urine confirms retrograde ejaculation if sperms are present [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 208].
- **Testicular Biopsy / FNAC**: Differentiates Obstructive Azoospermia from Non-Obstructive Azoospermia (Sertoli-cell-only syndrome) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 207–208].

### 6. Differential Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Clinical Differentiation: Obstructive vs. Non-Obstructive Azoospermia

|Parameter|Obstructive Azoospermia (OA) [⭐ High-Yield]|Non-Obstructive Azoospermia (NOA) [⭐ High-Yield]|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Primary Etiology**|Physical ductal block (CBAVD, post-infectious, vasectomy) [Shaw 1, p. 205].|Primary testicular failure (Klinefelter, cryptorchidism) [Shaw 1, p. 205].|
|**Testicular Volume**|**Normal (15–35 mL)** and firm consistency [Shaw 1, p. 206].|**Reduced (<6 mL)**, soft, and atrophic [Shaw 1, p. 206].|
|**Serum FSH Level**|**Normal FSH level** [Shaw 1, p. 208].|**Elevated Serum FSH (>2x normal)** [Shaw 1, p. 208].|
|**Testicular Biopsy**|**Normal active spermatogenesis** [Shaw 1, p. 207].|**Sertoli-cell-only syndrome** or maturation arrest [Shaw 1, p. 208].|
|**Treatment of Choice**|Surgical vaso-anastomosis OR **PESA/MESA + ICSI**[Shaw 1, p. 208, 211].|**TESE/TESA + ICSI** or Donor Insemination [Shaw 1, p. 211, 226].|

### 7. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **The 3-Month Repeat Rule**: Never proclaim a patient infertile or azoospermic on a **single abnormal semen analysis report**. Because full spermatogenesis takes **74 days** plus ductal transit time (total ~3 months), always repeat semen analysis after **4 to 12 weeks** following lifestyle modification (avoidance of hot baths, loose cotton underwear, cessation of smoking/alcohol) before establishing a final diagnosis [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 204, 207].

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Assuming that an azoospermic patient with a low ejaculate volume (<1.5 mL) has endocrine hypogonadism.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - Low ejaculate volume (<1.5 mL) + acidic pH (<7.0) + absent seminal fructose + azoospermia strongly indicates **Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD)** or ejaculatory duct obstruction [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 205, 207]. Testicular volume and serum FSH levels in CBAVD are completely normal!

### 9. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: V-A-R-I-C-O-C-E-L-E (Etiology & Clinical Features Checklist)

- **V**: **Varicocele** (most common treatable primary testicular cause).
- **A**: **Azoospermia / Asthenospermia** (disorders of count & motility).
- **R**: **Retrograde Ejaculation** (diabetic neuropathy or bladder neck dysfunction).
- **I**: **Infections & Immotile Cilia** (Mumps orchitis, TB, Chlamydia, Kartagener syndrome).
- **C**: **Cryptorchidism & CBAVD** (undescended testis, CFTR gene mutation).
- **O**: **Occupational Heat & Toxins** (stokers, hot baths, smoking, alcohol).
- **C**: **Chromosomal Anomalies** (Klinefelter 47,XXY syndrome).
- **E**: **Endocrine Dysfunction** (Kallmann syndrome, hyperprolactinemia).
- **L**: **Lifestyle & Obesity** (increased peripheral conversion of androgen to estrogen).
- **E**: **Evaluation via Semen Analysis FIRST** (WHO 2010 criteria).

### Quick Revision

- **Epidemiology**: Male factor present in **50% of overall infertile couples** (30–35% male alone) [Shaw 1, p. 203, 205].
- **Major Causes**: Spermatogenic failure (50%), Ductal obstruction (30%), Sperm motility/structural defects (15%), Sexual dysfunction [Shaw 1, p. 205].
- **Most Common Treatable Cause**: Varicocele (elevates scrotal temperature) [Shaw 1, p. 205, 208].
- **Most Common Genetic Cause**: Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY; small testes <6 mL, high FSH, azoospermia) [Shaw 1, p. 114, 205].
- **WHO 2010 Semen Criteria**: Volume ≥1.5 mL, Concentration ≥15 million/mL, Progressive Motility ≥32%, Normal Morphology ≥4% [Shaw 1, p. 206–207].

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Male factor infertility accounts for 50% of overall couple infertility and is caused by pre-testicular endocrine failure, primary testicular germ cell damage (50%; most commonly varicocele or Klinefelter syndrome), post-testicular ductal obstruction (30%), or sperm functional defects, systematically diagnosed via WHO 2010 semen analysis.** [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 203, 205].
