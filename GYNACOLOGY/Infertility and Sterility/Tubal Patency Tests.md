# Tubal Patency Tests: Timing, Procedures, Interpretation, and Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition & Clinical Significance [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Tubal Patency Testing**: A fundamental diagnostic evaluation performed during female infertility workups to assess the anatomical continuity, luminal patency, and functional integrity of the Fallopian tubes [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
- **Clinical Role**: Tubal factors account for **30% to 40%** of female infertility (primarily secondary to pelvic inflammatory disease, genital tuberculosis, and endometriosis) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216]. Evaluation of tubal patency is a basic mandatory investigation in every infertile couple alongside ovulation testing and semen analysis [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191].

---

### 2. Optimal Timing & Prerequisites [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Optimal Time Window**: Performed during the **early proliferative phase (Day 7 to Day 10 of the menstrual cycle)**, immediately following cessation of menstrual bleeding and prior to ovulation [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].

#### Rationale for Timing [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Prevention of Dislodgement of Pregnancy**: Avoids performing the procedure in an unsuspected early luteal-phase pregnancy, preventing radiation exposure, mechanical disruption, or accidental abortion [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
2. **Optimal Endometrial Thickness**: The thin endometrium during the early proliferative phase minimizes false-positive cornual blockage caused by thick, plush secretory endometrium occluding the tubal ostia [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Ch 40, p. 509].
3. **Prevention of Endometriosis**: Avoids forcing shedding endometrial debris backward through the tubes into the peritoneal cavity (reducing risk of iatrogenic pelvic endometriosis) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216; Ch 40, p. 509].
4. **Vascular Safety**: Cervical and uterine vessels are less congested, decreasing the risk of dye intravasation into the uterine venous plexus [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].

#### A. Hysterosalpingography (HSG) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Role**: Primary **investigation of choice (screening test)** for tubal patency and uterine cavity morphology [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
- **Technique**:
    - Performed outpatient without anesthesia in Day 7–10 of cycle [Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 508].
    - Patient in lithotomy position; cervix exposed and cannulated using a **Leech-Wilkinson cannula**, **Rubin's cannula**, or **Foley catheter (No. 14)** [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].
    - **10 to 15 mL** of water-soluble iodinated radiopaque dye (e.g. Urografin) is slowly injected into the uterine cavity under fluoroscopic/X-ray guidance [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].

##### Interpretation of HSG Findings [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Normal Patent Tubes**: Triangular uterine cavity outline, bilateral linear tubal opacification, and **free peritoneal spill** of dye wrapping around bowel loops [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
2. **Proximal / Cornual Block**: Abrupt stoppage of dye at the uterine cornua with no tubal opacification (most commonly caused by **physiologic cornual spasm [25%]**, mucus plugs, or fibrosis) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
3. **Distal / Fimbrial Block (Hydrosalpinx)**: Distended, retort-shaped dilatation of the ampullary-fimbrial tube **without free peritoneal spill** [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216, 217].
4. **Genital Tuberculosis**: Characteristic findings include **pipe-stem/lead-pipe rigid tubes**, **beaded/hazelnut appearance**, cornual block, calcified lymph nodes, and uterine synechiae ("moth-eaten" cavity) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 33, 178, 192; Shaw 1, Ch 28, p. 351, 352].

#### B. Laparoscopic Chromopertubation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Role**: **GOLD STANDARD investigation** for tubal patency and pelvic factor evaluation [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
- **Technique**:
    - Performed under general anesthesia. Laparoscope introduced umbilical cord level.
    - **Methylene blue or Indigo carmine dye** is injected transcervically via a cannula while observing the pelvic organs directly [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].

##### Interpretation & Advantages [⭐ High-Yield]:

1. **Direct Visualization**: Blue dye is seen issuing as a stream from the fimbrial end into the peritoneal cavity [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
2. **Extratubal Pathology Detection**: Directly visualizes external tubal surfaces, peritubal/periovarian adhesions, fimbrial phimosis, and pelvic endometriosis patches [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
3. **Therapeutic Capability**: Allows immediate operative intervention during the same session (adhesiolysis, fimbrioplasty, ovarian drilling) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].

#### C. Sonosalpingography (SSG / Saline Infusion Sonography - SIS) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Technique**: Instillation of **100 to 200 mL of sterile normal saline** (mixed with air bubbles) into the uterine cavity via a Foley catheter under continuous Transvaginal Sonography (TVS) guidance [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
- **Interpretation**: Microbubbles/saline flow is tracked along the tubal lumen, emerging as a "fimbrial shower" with fluid accumulation in the Pouch of Douglas (POD) [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
- **Advantages**: Non-invasive, office-based, radiation-free, avoids iodine allergy, and evaluates submucous fibroids/polyps simultaneously [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].

#### D. Rubin's Gas Insufflation Test (Historical / Obsolete)

- Insufflation of CO₂ gas transcervically; patency determined by hearing a bubbling sound on abdominal auscultation, manometer pressure drop (<120 mmHg), and shoulder tip pain due to diaphragmatic irritation by CO₂ [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 548, 658]. Obsolete due to high false error rates and gas embolism risk [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 548].

1. **Pelvic Infection & Sepsis**: Reactivation or ascending flare-up of acute/subacute Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) or dissemination of latent Genital Tuberculosis [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].
2. **Severe Pelvic Pain & Cornual Spasm**: Peritoneal irritation caused by contrast medium or high injection pressure triggering painful uterine/cornual muscular spasm [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].
3. **Uterine Perforation**: Mechanical tear of the uterine wall during cervical cannulation or sound insertion [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 531; Ch 42, p. 548].
4. **Contrast Allergic Reactions**: Hypersensitivity or anaphylactoid reactions to iodinated contrast media used during HSG [Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].
5. **Dye Intravasation**: Extravasation of radiopaque contrast directly into myometrial veins and lymphatic channels, carrying a theoretical risk of embolization [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
6. **Vasovagal Syncope**: Neurogenic collapse secondary to cervical traction or painful uterine distention [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].

### 8. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **Management of Bilateral Cornual Block on HSG**: A finding of bilateral cornual block on HSG is false-positive in up to **25% of cases due to physiological cornual spasm**! Before declaring a permanent structural tubal block, perform **Hysteroscopic Fallopian Tube Cannulation** under glucagon/sedation or proceed to **Laparoscopic Chromopertubation** under general anesthesia (which provides muscular relaxation and differentiates true organic occlusion from temporary spasm) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Performing HSG in the premenstrual / luteal phase OR performing HSG in active genital infection.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **HSG is strictly CONTRAINDICATED in the premenstrual/luteal phase** because the thick secretory endometrium can be forced into pelvic veins or peritoneal cavity, and an unsuspected early pregnancy may be disrupted [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].
    - **Active PID or Genital TB is an absolute contraindication to HSG**, as forceful dye injection disseminates infection into the peritoneal cavity [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].

### 9. Diagram Guides

### Diagram 1 : Normal Hysterosalpingogram (HSG) vs. Bilateral Hydrosalpinx

- **Type**: Radiographic schematic diagram [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
- **Outline shape to draw**: Inverted triangular uterine cavity with bilateral tubular projections.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. **Triangular Uterine Cavity** — smooth radiopaque filling [Marrow 1, p. 32].
    2. **Cornual Region** — narrow tubal ostium entry [Marrow 1, p. 192].
    3. **Linear Fallopian Tubes** — thin, uniform opacity along isthmus and ampulla [Shaw 1, p. 217].
    4. **Peritoneal Free Spill (Normal)** — diffuse, wavy cloud of contrast wrapping around pelvic bowel loops [Shaw 1, p. 217].
    5. **Hydrosalpinx (Abnormal)** — clubbed, retort-shaped dilated ampullary end without peritoneal spill [Marrow 1, p. 192; Shaw 1, p. 217].
- **Annotation**: _"Free peritoneal spill on HSG confirms bilateral tubal patency, whereas ampullary dilatation without spill indicates hydrosalpinx."_ [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216–217].

### 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Mnemonic: P-A-T-E-N-T (Tubal Evaluation Checklist)

- **P**: **Pre-ovulatory timing (Day 7 to Day 10 of cycle)**.
- **A**: **Atropine premedication (0.6 mg IM)** to prevent cornual spasm.
- **T**: **Tuberculosis & PID active infections are CONTRAINDICATED**.
- **E**: **External adnexal adhesions & endometriosis** diagnosed via Laparoscopy (Gold Standard).
- **N**: **Non-invasive Sonosalpingography (SSG)** uses saline + microbubbles.
- **T**: **Treatment of choice for irreparable bilateral tubal block is In Vitro Fertilization (IVF-ET)**.

### Quick Revision

- **Optimal Timing**: Day 7 to Day 10 of the menstrual cycle (pre-ovulatory phase) [Marrow 1, p. 32; Shaw 1, p. 216].
- **Screening Test**: **Hysterosalpingography (HSG)** using 10–15 mL water-soluble radiopaque dye [Marrow 1, p. 32; Shaw 1, p. 508].
- **Gold Standard Test**: **Laparoscopic Chromopertubation** using Methylene blue / Indigo carmine dye under GA [Marrow 1, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, p. 217].
- **Non-Invasive Alternative**: **Sonosalpingography (SSG)** using TVS + sterile normal saline [Shaw 1, p. 218].
- **False-Positive Block**: **Physiologic cornual spasm (~25%)** on HSG; relieved by Atropine or general anesthesia [Shaw 1, p. 218].
- **Key Contraindications**: Pregnancy, active pelvic infection (PID/TB), and premenstrual/luteal phase [Marrow 1, p. 32; Shaw 1, p. 509].

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Tubal patency testing is a fundamental component of female infertility evaluation performed during the pre-ovulatory phase (Day 7 to Day 10 of the cycle), classically screened via Hysterosalpingography (HSG) and definitively evaluated by Laparoscopic Chromopertubation (the gold standard), to detect luminal obstruction, fimbrial hydrosalpinx, or peritubal adhesions while avoiding hazards of infection flare-up, contrast allergy, and cornual spasm.** [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216–218].
