# Laparoscopic Chromopertubation (Chromotubation) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mark Weightage

- **5 Marks / Long Answer Depth**

---

### 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Laparoscopic Chromopertubation (Chromotubation)**: A minimally invasive endoscopic procedure in which a colored dye solution (dilute Methylene Blue or Indigo Carmine) is injected transcervically into the uterine cavity while directly observing the Fallopian tubes, fimbriae, and peritoneal cavity via a laparoscope to evaluate tubal patency and extratubal pelvic pathology [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217; Ch 41, p. 519].
- **Gold Standard Status**: It is universally recognized as the **GOLD STANDARD investigation** for evaluating tubal factor infertility and pelvic adnexal architecture [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].

#### A. Clinical Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Evaluation of Female Infertility**: Mandatory evaluation of tubal patency in primary or secondary infertility [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
2. **Confirmation of Abnormal HSG Findings**: Confirms and localizes tubal obstruction when Hysterosalpingography (HSG) shows bilateral cornual block, fimbrial block, or equivocal peritoneal spill [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
3. **Detection of Extratubal Pelvic Pathology**: Diagnoses peritubal/periovarian adhesions, fimbrial phimosis, and unsuspected pelvic endometriosis ("powder-burn" spots or chocolate cysts) missed on imaging [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 14, p. 180].
4. **Pre-Operative Assessment for Tuboplasty**: Evaluates mucosal integrity and tubal mobility before undertaking tubal reconstructive surgery or microsurgery [Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218; Ch 41, p. 519].
5. **Pre-IVF Evaluation**: Identifies hydrosalpinx requiring proximal tubal clipping or salpingectomy prior to In Vitro Fertilization (IVF) to prevent embryotoxic fluid reflux [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218, 224].

#### B. Optimal Timing & Prerequisites [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Timing**: Performed during the **early proliferative phase (Day 7 to Day 10 of the menstrual cycle)** after menstrual flow ceases and prior to ovulation [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 216].
- **Prerequisites**:
    - Complete screening to rule out active Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) or active Genital Tuberculosis [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].
    - Pre-procedure fast (6 to 8 hours) for General Anesthesia (GA) with endotracheal intubation [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
    - Patient must empty her bladder (catheterized on table) to prevent trocar cystotomy [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].

---

### 3. Contraindications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Absolute Contraindications**:
    1. Active pelvic infection or acute PID (risk of spreading acute infection into peritoneal cavity) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].
    2. Confirmed or suspected intra-uterine pregnancy [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].
    3. Severe cardiopulmonary disease or decompensated heart failure (precludes Trendelenburg tilt and CO₂ pneumoperitoneum) [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].
- **Relative Contraindications**:
    1. Extreme obesity, large umbilical/ventral hernia, or multiple previous laparotomy scars (increased bowel perforation risk during primary trocar entry) [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].
    2. Known severe hypersensitivity to Methylene Blue dye [Shaw 1, Ch 40, p. 509].

#### Stepwise Procedure Steps [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Patient Positioning & Bimanual Exam**: Semilithotomy position with **15° to 20° Trendelenburg tilt** after general anesthesia induction [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
2. **Cervical Cannulation**: A cervical cannula (Rubin's cannula, Leech-Wilkinson cannula, or Spackman/Hulka uterine manipulator) is attached to the cervix to permit dye injection and uterine manipulation [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 44, p. 548].
3. **Creation of Pneumoperitoneum**: A 1.25 cm subumbilical skin incision is made; a **Veress needle** is inserted into the peritoneal cavity. Correct entry is confirmed by Palmer's saline drop test. The abdomen is inflated with **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)** gas maintaining intra-abdominal pressure at **12 to 15 mmHg** [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
4. **Trocar & Telescope Insertion**: The primary **10 mm trocar and cannula** are inserted through the subumbilical incision, followed by the laparoscopic telescope connected to a cold fiberoptic light source and video display [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526]. Secondary 5 mm accessory trocars are placed in the lower abdomen for manipulators [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
5. **Dye Injection (Chromopertubation)**: **10 to 20 mL of dilute Methylene Blue or Indigo Carmine solution** is slowly injected through the cervical cannula into the uterine cavity [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
6. **Direct Laparoscopic Visual Assessment**: The operator tracks the blue dye sequentially as it passes through the uterine cornua, isthmus, ampulla, and fimbriated ends into the Pouch of Douglas (POD) [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].

1. **Bilateral Patent Fallopian Tubes (Normal)**:
    - Immediate opacification of the entire tubal length.
    - **Direct visualization of a blue stream ("fimbrial shower/spill")** issuing from both fimbrial ostia and pooling in the Pouch of Douglas [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
2. **Proximal / Cornual Block**:
    - The uterine cornua turn dark blue and distend, but no dye passes into the isthmic tubal lumen [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192].
    - _Clinical Note_: General anesthesia relaxes smooth muscle, significantly reducing false-positive cornual spasm seen on HSG [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
3. **Distal / Fimbrial Block (Hydrosalpinx)**:
    - The ampullary and fimbrial segments distend into a dark-blue, retort-shaped structure without any dye leaking into the peritoneal cavity [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].
4. **Peritubal Adhesions & Endometriosis**:
    - Demonstrates restricted tubal mobility, kinked tubes held by filmy or dense adhesions, "powder-burn" dark spots, or periovarian chocolate cysts [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 34; Shaw 1, Ch 14, p. 180].

### 7. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Vascular & Trocar Injuries**: Laceration of inferior epigastric vessels or major retroperitoneal vessels (aorta, inferior vena cava, iliac arteries) during primary trocar insertion [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
2. **Visceral Perforation**: Accidental puncture of bowel loops, urinary bladder, or stomach by the Veress needle or trocar [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
3. **Anesthetic & Pneumoperitoneum Hazards**: CO₂ gas embolism, subcutaneous emphysema, hypercapnic acidosis, cardiac arrhythmias, and vasovagal collapse [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
4. **Uterine Perforation**: Mechanical tear of the uterine wall during cervical cannulation or sound insertion [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 526].
5. **Infection & Sepsis**: Ascending flare-up of latent Pelvic Inflammatory Disease or pelvic peritonitis [Shaw 1, Ch 41, p. 527].

### 9. Clinical Pearl & Exam Trap

### Clinical Pearl

- **"See and Treat" One-Stop Management**: The paramount advantage of Laparoscopic Chromopertubation over HSG is its ability to perform **simultaneous therapeutic intervention** in the same sitting! If peritubal adhesions or fimbrial phimosis are identified, immediate laparoscopic adhesiolysis or fimbrioplasty can restore natural fertility without requiring a secondary laparotomy [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 192; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up**: Confusing **Hysterosalpingography (HSG)** with **Laparoscopic Chromopertubation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact**:
    - **HSG** is a **radiographic screening test** performed without GA using radiopaque contrast under X-ray, which **cannot view external tubal surfaces or peritubal adhesions** and has a ~25% false-positive cornual spasm rate [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 32; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 218].
    - **Laparoscopic Chromopertubation** is an **endoscopic gold standard test** performed under GA using Methylene Blue dye, allowing **direct 3D visualization of the tubal exterior, ovaries, adhesions, and pelvic endometriosis** while eliminating cornual spasm [Marrow 1, Ch 16, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, Ch 16, p. 217].

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Status**: Endoscopic tubal patency test using transcervical Methylene Blue injection under direct laparoscopic view; **Gold Standard** test for tubal factor infertility [Marrow 1, p. 34, 191; Shaw 1, p. 217].
- **Timing**: Pre-ovulatory phase (Day 7 to Day 10 of menstrual cycle) [Marrow 1, p. 32; Shaw 1, p. 216].
- **Normal Result**: Free flow of blue dye from both fimbrial ends into the Pouch of Douglas [Marrow 1, p. 191].
- **Hydrosalpinx Result**: Distended blue ampullary segment without peritoneal spillage [Marrow 1, p. 192].
- **Key Advantage**: Directly visualizes extratubal adhesions and endometriosis, allowing immediate operative intervention [Marrow 1, p. 192; Shaw 1, p. 218].
