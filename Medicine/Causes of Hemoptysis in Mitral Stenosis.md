# Causes of Hemoptysis in Mitral Stenosis

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Mitral Stenosis (MS)** is a chronic valvular heart disorder (most commonly rheumatic in origin) characterized by mechanical obstruction to left ventricular diastolic filling due to narrowing of the mitral valve orifice area (<2.0 cm²).

**Hemoptysis** (the expectoration of blood originating from the respiratory tract) occurs in up to 10% to 15% of patients with symptomatic mitral stenosis. It ranges from minor blood-streaking of sputum to catastrophic, life-threatening pulmonary hemorrhage.

### 1. Pulmonary Apoplexy (Profuse Frank Hemoptysis) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Character:** Sudden, unprovoked coughing up of abundant, dark red, frank blood (often several hundred mL).
- **Timing:** Occurs relatively **early in the disease course** when left atrial pressure (LAP) rises rapidly before protective pulmonary arteriolar vasoconstriction (reactive pulmonary hypertension) develops.

### 2. Pink Frothy Sputum (Acute Alveolar Pulmonary Edema) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Character:** Profuse, liquid, foamy, pink-tinged or blood-streaked sputum.
- **Trigger:** Precipitated by sudden surges in LAP and pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure exceeding plasma oncotic pressure (>25–30 mmHg). Common triggers include physical exertion, emotional stress, pregnancy, fever, or rapid Atrial Fibrillation.

### 3. Blood-Streaked Sputum (Chronic Bronchitis / "Winter Bronchitis")

- **Character:** Mucoid or purulent sputum flecked with bright red blood streaks.
- **Mechanism:** Secondary to chronic, long-standing pulmonary venous hypertension causing continuous engorgement and mucosal edema of the bronchial tree, predisposing to recurrent winter infections.

### 4. Pulmonary Infarction (Thromboembolic Hemoptysis)

- **Character:** Dark, gelatinous blood or blood-stained sputum accompanied by sudden pleuritic chest pain, fever, and pleural rub.
- **Mechanism:** Occurs when pulmonary embolism occludes a segmental pulmonary artery, leading to localized ischemic necrosis and hemorrhagic pulmonary infarction.

### 5. Sputum with "Heart Failure Cells" (Alveolar Microhemorrhage)

- **Character:** Rusty-colored or brown-tinged sputum.
- **Mechanism:** Chronic elevation of capillary pressure causes diapedesis of red blood cells into alveolar spaces. RBCs are degraded and phagocytosed by alveolar macrophages, forming **hemosiderin-laden macrophages ("heart failure cells")**.

## 3. Etiology & Pathogenetic Mechanisms [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Vascular & Anatomical Causes

1. **Rupture of Subepithelial Bronchial Veins:** The bronchial venous system (systemic circulation) drains into the pulmonary venous system. When Left Atrial Pressure rises, blood backs up into the bronchial veins, causing them to dilate, become tortuous, and form thin-walled **bronchopulmonary varices**. Sudden pressure spikes cause these varices to rupture directly into the bronchial lumen.
2. **Breakdown of Alveolocapillary Membrane:** Extreme capillary hydrostatic pressure (>25–30 mmHg) causes physical disruption of endothelial and alveolar epithelial cell junctions, driving transudation of fluid and RBCs into alveoli.
3. **Pulmonary Thromboembolism:** Stasis of blood in right heart chambers, peripheral venous thrombosis from low cardiac output, or Atrial Fibrillation predisposes to pulmonary emboli.
4. **Intercurrent Respiratory Infections:** Chronic pulmonary stasis impairs local mucosal immunity, leading to recurrent bacterial bronchitis or pneumonia.

---

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Mitral Valve Obstruction:** Stenosis of the mitral valve orifice (<2 cm²) impedes left ventricular filling during diastole.
2. **Left Atrial Hypertension:** Left atrial pressure rises to compensate and maintain cardiac output.
3. **Retrograde Venous Transmission:** High LAP is transmitted backward into pulmonary veins, pulmonary capillaries, and the collateral bronchial venous plexus.
4. **Engorgement & Varicosity:** High back-pressure causes severe engorgement and enlargement of subepithelial bronchial veins (bronchopulmonary anastomoses).
5. **Rupture & Hemorrhage:** Sudden surges in cardiac output or heart rate (reducing diastolic filling time) cause acute pressure spikes that rupture these delicate varices, resulting in **Pulmonary Apoplexy**.

## 5. Clinical Features & Associated Signs

### A. Associated Cardiac Symptoms

- **Exertional Dyspnea:** Progressive breathlessness on effort.
- **Orthopnea & Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND):** Breathlessness when supine.
- **Palpitations:** Secondary to Atrial Fibrillation.

### B. Physical Examination Findings

- **Mitral Facies:** Plum-colored malar flush over cheeks with cyanotic lips.
- **Tapping Apex Beat:** Palpable loud first heart sound (S1).
- **Auscultatory Triad:**
    1. **Loud First Heart Sound (S1)**.
    2. **Opening Snap (OS)** following S2.
    3. **Mid-Diastolic Rumbling Murmur (MDM)** with presystolic accentuation (presystolic accentuation is lost in Atrial Fibrillation).
- **Pulmonary Crackles / Crepitations:** Heard at lung bases during pulmonary edema.

## 7. Management Principles [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Immediate Resuscitative Measures

1. **Positioning:** Sit patient upright to reduce venous return and pulmonary capillary congestion.
2. **Oxygenation:** High-flow supplemental oxygen via face mask or CPAP.

### B. Pharmacotherapy (Dosage Rules)

- **Furosemide (Loop Diuretic):**
    - _Dose:_ **20 to 80 mg IV bolus**.
    - _Mechanism:_ Decreases intravascular volume, rapidly lowering left atrial and pulmonary capillary pressure.
- **Metoprolol / Digoxin (Rate Control):**
    - _Dose:_ Metoprolol **25–50 mg PO** or Digoxin **0.25 mg IV**.
    - _Mechanism:_ Slows heart rate, prolonging diastolic filling time and reducing LAP surge.

### C. Definitive Intervention

- **Percutaneous Transvenous Mitral Commisurotomy (PTMC)** or **Mitral Valve Replacement (MVR)** to relieve mechanical obstruction and permanently reduce LA pressure.

---

## 8. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Paradoxically, as Mitral Stenosis becomes more severe, the frequency of **Pulmonary Apoplexy DECREASES**! This occurs because long-standing high LA pressure induces protective secondary hypertrophic and hyperplastic changes in pulmonary arterioles (reactive pulmonary hypertension). This arteriolar constriction acts as a "safety valve" protecting the downstream pulmonary capillaries and bronchial veins from sudden pressure surges, albeit at the cost of developing right ventricular failure.

---

## 9. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Mistaking **Pulmonary Apoplexy in Mitral Stenosis** for **Pulmonary Tuberculosis** in endemic regions.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Both present with hemoptysis in young adults. However, TB presents with fever, night sweats, weight loss, and apical lung infiltrates, whereas MS presents with exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, a tapping apex beat, a loud S1, and an apical mid-diastolic rumbling murmur.
- **Resolution:** Always perform a cardiovascular examination (auscultate the apex with the bell in left lateral position) in any young patient presenting with unexplained hemoptysis.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Expectoration of blood in MS due to severe pulmonary venous and capillary hypertension.
- **Most Severe Type:** Pulmonary Apoplexy (profuse frank bleeding from ruptured subepithelial bronchial varices).
- **Acute Edema Type:** Pink frothy sputum caused by capillary pressure >25–30 mmHg.
- **Thromboembolic Type:** Pulmonary infarction secondary to PE.
- **Paradoxical Feature:** Apoplexy decreases in late severe MS due to protective reactive pulmonary arteriolar vasoconstriction.
