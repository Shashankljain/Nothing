# Jacksonian Epilepsy

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Jacksonian Epilepsy** (also known as a **Jacksonian March** or **Focal Motor Seizure with March**) is a type of simple partial (focal motor) seizure originating from a localized epileptogenic focus in the primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus, Brodmann Area 4) [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558]. It is clinically defined by unilateral clonic muscle jerking that begins in a specific distal body part and characteristically **"marches" or spreads sequentially** along contiguous motor cortical representations of the motor homunculus (e.g., starting in the thumb, spreading to the wrist, arm, face, and leg), while **consciousness remains completely preserved** [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].

---

1. **Intracranial Granulomas & Parasitic Infections [Most Common Cause in Endemic Regions]:**
    - **Neurocysticercosis (NCC):** Active parenchymal larval cysts of _Taenia solium_ located in the motor cortex [Davidson 1 p. 712, Kdt4 p. 1005].
    - **Tubercular Granuloma (Tuberculoma):** Solitary or multiple caseating granulomas in the frontal cortex [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Kdt 1 p. 439].
2. **Primary & Secondary Brain Tumors:** Primary intra-axial gliomas (astrocytoma, glioblastoma), extra-axial meningiomas compressing the motor strip, or solitary cerebral metastases (breast, lung, renal cell carcinoma) [Kdt 1 p. 437, 439, Robbins 5 p. 1293].
3. **Cerebrovascular Lesions:** Arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), cavernous angiomas, cortical venous sinus thrombosis, and old ischemic or hemorrhagic strokes [Kdt 1 p. 439, Robbins 5 p. 1253].
4. **Head Trauma & Post-Traumatic Scars:** Cortical contusions resulting in **plaque jaune** (yellow-brown gliotic-hemosiderin cortical scars acting as permanent epileptogenic foci) [Kdt 1 p. 437, 451, Robbins 5 p. 1249].
5. **Cortical Malformations:** Focal cortical dysplasia, heterotopias, and vascular anomalies [Robbins 5 p. 1242].

1. **Simple Partial Focal Motor Seizure (Pure Jacksonian March):** Localized motor seizure activity with preserved consciousness throughout the episode [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Hypersynchronous Focal Discharge:** A focal structural lesion in the precentral gyrus creates a local population of hyper-excitable neurons that exhibit paroxysmal depolarization shifts (PDS) driven by excess glutamatergic activation (NMDA/AMPA receptors) [Kdt 1 p. 440].
2. **Breakdown of Surround Inhibition:** Normal surrounding GABAergic interneuronal inhibition fails, allowing the localized epileptogenic discharge to escape its primary focus [Kdt 1 p. 440].
3. **Homuncular Cortical March:** The wave of electrical excitation spreads along adjacent, contiguous cortical motor representations in the precentral gyrus (motor homunculus: thumb/hand → forearm → arm → face → leg) [Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].
4. **Preserved Awareness:** Consciousness is maintained because the seizure activity remains restricted to the unilateral motor cortex and does not recruit the reticular activating system (RAS) or bilateral thalamocortical circuits [Kdt 1 p. 437].
5. **Post-Ictal Exhaustion (Todd's Paralysis):** Following seizure cessation, temporary localized depletion of neuronal ATP and transient hyperpolarization of the motor focus cause transient post-ictal weakness (**Todd's Paresis**) in the affected limb [Davidson 1 p. 181].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. The Jacksonian March (Cardinal Sign)

- **Unilateral Clonic Jerking:** Begins abruptly in a body part with a disproportionately large cortical representation on the motor homunculus [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558]:
    1. **Thumb / Index Finger (Most Common Initial Site)** [Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558]
    2. **Corner of the Mouth / Face** [Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558]
    3. **Great Toe**
- **Sequential Spread ("The March"):** Over 30 to 90 seconds, clonic contractions orderly spread to adjacent body regions (e.g., thumb → wrist → elbow → shoulder → ipsilateral face → lower limb) [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].

---

### B. Preserved Consciousness & Sensorium

- The patient remains fully alert, conscious, and aware during the entire episode, watching their own limb twitch involuntarily [Kdt 1 p. 437].
- Duration of attack typically ranges from **30 to 90 seconds** [Kdt 1 p. 437].

---

### C. Post-Ictal Phenomena

- **Todd's Paralysis (Todd's Paresis) [⭐ High-Yield Pathognomonic Feature]:** Transient focal motor weakness or complete flaccid paralysis of the affected limb following seizure termination [Davidson 1 p. 181]. It lasts from a few minutes up to 24 hours before full motor recovery occurs [Davidson 1 p. 181].

1. **Contrast-Enhanced MRI Brain [⭐ Investigation of Choice / Gold Standard]:** High-resolution structural MRI identifies underlying focal cortical lesions (neurocysticercosis cysts, tuberculoma, glioma, meningioma, vascular AVM, or post-traumatic gliosis) [Davidson 1 p. 181, 712, Kdt 1 p. 451].
2. **Electroencephalogram (EEG):** Demonstrates **focal sharp waves, spikes, or localized spike-and-wave discharges** over the contralateral motor cortex (precentral region) during or between attacks [Davidson 1 p. 181, Kdt 1 p. 437]. _(Note: Normal interictal EEG occurs in ~50% of patients and does not rule out epilepsy) [Davidson 1 p. 181]._
3. **Capillary Blood Glucose & Serum Electrolytes:** Performed to rule out acute metabolic seizure triggers (severe hypoglycemia <40 mg/dL, hyponatremia <120 mEq/L, or hypocalcemia <8.4 mg/dL) [Davidson 1 p. 182, 358, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 3 p. 883].
4. **NCCT Head:** Useful in emergency settings to detect calcified cysticercal lesions or acute intracranial hemorrhage [Davidson 1 p. 181, 712].

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Carbamazepine [⭐ First-Line Drug of Choice for Focal Seizures]

- **Name:** Carbamazepine [Kdt 1 p. 442, 449].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 440, 442). Prolongs the **inactivated state of voltage-gated neuronal Na⁺ channels** in motor cortical neurons, suppressing high-frequency repetitive action potential firing without impairing normal low-frequency basal firing [Kdt 1 p. 440, 442].
    
    ```
    Carbamazepine → Prolongs Inactivated State of Voltage-Gated Na+ Channels → Inhibits High-Frequency Repetitive Firing → Suppresses Focal Seizure
    ```
    
- **Target / Ion Channels:** Voltage-gated neuronal Na⁺ channels [Kdt 1 p. 440, 442].
- **Dose:** **200 mg to 400 mg PO thrice daily (TDS)** (Children: 10 to 20 mg/kg/day in divided doses) [Kdt 1 p. 442, 449].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets / Sustained-release formulations.
- **Frequency:** Thrice daily (TDS) or twice daily (BD for CR formulations) [Kdt 1 p. 442].
- **Features:** Drug of choice for partial (focal) seizures; highly effective in preventing generalization [Kdt 1 p. 442, 449].
- **Side Effects:** Sedation, dizziness, ataxia, diplopia, hyponatremia (SIADH-like effect), skin rash, blood dyscrasias (aplastic anemia, leukopenia) [Kdt 1 p. 442].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate monotherapy upon confirming focal seizure disorder; continue for at least **2 to 3 seizure-free years** before considering gradual tapering [Kdt 1 p. 449].

#### 2. Levetiracetam (Alternative First-Line AED)

- **Name:** Levetiracetam [Kdt 1 p. 447, 449].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 447). Binds selectively to **synaptic vesicular protein SV2A**, inhibiting presynaptic exocytosis of excitatory neurotransmitters (glutamate) [Kdt 1 p. 447].
- **Dose:** **500 mg to 1000 mg PO twice daily (BD)** (Maximum 3000 mg/day) [Kdt 1 p. 447].
- **Features:** Excellent safety profile, minimal hepatic drug interactions, rapid titration, highly effective monotherapy for focal motor seizures [Kdt 1 p. 447, 449].

---

### B. Etiological & Surgical Interventions

- **Neurocysticercosis:** **Albendazole (15 mg/kg/day PO for 8–15 days)** combined with **Prednisolone (1 mg/kg/day PO)** to prevent inflammatory seizure spikes during cyst lysis [Davidson 1 p. 712, Kdt4 p. 1005].
- **Refractory Lesional Epilepsy:** Surgical excision of cortical tumors, AVM resection, or focal lesionectomy [Kdt 1 p. 439].

---

## 9. Complications

1. **Secondary Generalization:** Electrical spread across corpus callosum evolving into Generalized Tonic-Clonic Seizures (GTCS) with loss of consciousness and risk of physical trauma [Kdt 1 p. 437].
2. **Epilepsia Partialis Continua:** A severe form of focal status epilepticus characterized by continuous clonic jerking of a limb lasting for hours, days, or weeks without loss of consciousness [Kdt 1 p. 450].
3. **Todd's Paralysis:** Post-ictal motor deficit causing temporary disability [Davidson 1 p. 181].
4. **Progressive Neurological Deficits:** Secondary to expanding underlying structural mass lesions (e.g., growing brain tumors) [Kdt 1 p. 439].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Seizure control is **excellent (>70–80%)** with single first-line AED monotherapy (Carbamazepine or Levetiracetam) [Kdt 1 p. 448, 449].
- Long-term outcome depends on the definitive management of the underlying structural cortical lesion.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always perform a **Contrast-Enhanced MRI Brain** in any adult presenting with a first episode of Jacksonian epilepsy! Jacksonian seizures are virtually always **secondary to a structural cortical lesion** (most commonly neurocysticercosis, tuberculoma, or brain tumor) in the primary motor precentral gyrus [Davidson 1 p. 181, 712, Kdt 1 p. 439]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Confusing **post-ictal Todd's Paralysis** following a Jacksonian seizure with an **acute ischemic stroke (CVA)**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Todd's paralysis is transient motor weakness following a focal seizure that resolves spontaneously within minutes to 24 hours, whereas an ischemic stroke has an abrupt onset without antecedent clonic marching.
- **Resolution:** Re-examine the patient after a brief observation window; **Todd's paralysis will demonstrate progressive motor recovery**, whereas stroke deficits persist and require acute vascular management!

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Simple partial focal motor seizure characterized by clonic jerking marching along the precentral gyrus with preserved consciousness [Kdt 1 p. 437, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].
- **Most Common Anatomical Onset Site:** Thumb / Index finger or corner of the mouth [Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 2 p. 558].
- **Key Post-Ictal Sign:** **Todd's Paralysis** (transient motor weakness lasting <24 hours) [Davidson 1 p. 181].
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test:** **Contrast-Enhanced MRI Brain** (detects underlying focal lesions) [Davidson 1 p. 181, 712].
- **First-Line Drug of Choice:** **Carbamazepine** (200–400 mg PO TDS) or **Levetiracetam** (500–1000 mg PO BD) [Kdt 1 p. 442, 447, 449].
