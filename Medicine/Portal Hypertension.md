# Portal Hypertension

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Portal Hypertension** is defined as a pathological elevation in the pressure gradient within the portal venous system, characterized by a **Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) >5 mmHg** (normal physiological range: 1 to 5 mmHg) [Robbins 3 p. 861].

- **Clinically Significant Portal Hypertension (CSPH):** Defined as an **HVPG ≥10 mmHg**, which represents the threshold for the development of portosystemic collaterals, esophageal varices, and splenomegaly [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Decompensation Threshold:** An **HVPG ≥12 mmHg** is the critical threshold required for acute esophageal variceal rupture/hemorrhage and ascites formation [Robbins 3 p. 861, 862].

### A. Pre-Hepatic Causes

1. **Obstructive Portal Vein Thrombosis (PVT):** Idiopathic or secondary to neonatal umbilical vein catheterization/sepsis, intra-abdominal infections (appendicitis/pylephlebitis), trauma, hypercoagulable states, or invasion by hepatocellular carcinoma [Robbins 3 p. 861].
2. **Splenic Vein Thrombosis:** Secondary to acute/chronic pancreatitis, pancreatic pseudocysts, or pancreatic head adenocarcinoma, leading to localized "left-sided" or "isolated" variceal hypertension.
3. **Structural Anomalies:** Congenital narrowing, hypoplasia, or atresia of the portal vein.
4. **Increased Portal Inflow:** Massive splenomegaly (e.g., in myelofibrosis, CML, visceral leishmaniasis) producing huge splenic vein blood flow [Robbins 3 p. 861].

### B. Intra-Hepatic Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Presinusoidal:**
    - **Schistosomiasis (_Schistosoma mansoni / japonicum_):** Granulomatous reaction to ova trapped in small portal venules (most common cause worldwide) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
    - **Idiopathic Non-Cirrhotic Portal Hypertension (INCPH) / Non-Cirrhotic Portal Fibrosis (NCPF):**Microvascular obliterative portal venopathy common in India and East Asia [Robbins 3 p. 861].
    - **Sarcoidosis, Primary Biliary Cholangitis (early stage), Congenital Hepatic Fibrosis.**
2. **Sinusoidal (Dominant Cause ~90%):**
    - **Cirrhosis of the Liver (All Causes):** Alcoholic cirrhosis, Chronic Hepatitis B/C, Non-Alcoholic Steatohepatitis (NASH/NAFLD), Wilson's disease, Hemochromatosis, Autoimmune Hepatitis [Robbins 3 p. 861].
    - **Massive Fatty Change (NASH), Diffuse Granulomatous Hepatitis, Amyloidosis.**
3. **Postsinusoidal:**
    - **Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome (SOS / Veno-Occlusive Disease):** Obliteration of central veins secondary to high-dose conditioning chemotherapy for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation or ingestion of pyrrolizidine alkaloid "bush teas" [Robbins 3 p. 862, 863].
    - **Peliosis Hepatis:** Blood-filled parenchymal cavities associated with anabolic steroid or tamoxifen use [Robbins 3 p. 864].

### C. Post-Hepatic Causes

1. **Budd-Chiari Syndrome:** Thrombotic or mechanical occlusion of two or more major hepatic veins [Robbins 3 p. 861, 862].
2. **Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) Obstruction:** Congenital IVC web or extrinsic tumor compression.
3. **Cardiac Causes:** Severe right-sided heart failure (tricuspid regurgitation, cor pulmonale) and **Constrictive Pericarditis** [Robbins 3 p. 861].

## 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

Portal hypertension is classified according to the anatomical site of resistance relative to the liver lobule:

|Anatomical Category|Primary Site of Resistance|Exemplar Pathological Condition|HVPG Pattern|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Pre-Hepatic**|Portal vein before entering liver|Portal Vein Thrombosis, Splenic Vein Thrombosis|Normal WHVP, Normal FHVP (HVPG Normal; Direct Portal Pressure ↑)|
|**Intra-Hepatic (Presinusoidal)**|Portal tracts / Presinusoidal venules|Schistosomiasis, Non-Cirrhotic Portal Fibrosis (NCPF)|Normal WHVP, Normal FHVP (Normal HVPG despite high portal pressure)|
|**Intra-Hepatic (Sinusoidal)**|Hepatic Sinusoids|**Cirrhosis of the liver**(Alcoholic, HBV, HCV, NASH)|**Elevated WHVP, Normal FHVP (Elevated HVPG)**|
|**Intra-Hepatic (Postsinusoidal)**|Terminal hepatic venules / Central vein|Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome (VOD)|Elevated WHVP, Normal FHVP (Elevated HVPG)|
|**Post-Hepatic**|Main hepatic veins / IVC / Heart|**Budd-Chiari Syndrome**, Constrictive Pericarditis|**Elevated WHVP, Elevated FHVP**|

## 4. Pathophysiology & Etiopathogenesis (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

The pathogenesis of portal hypertension involves a dual mechanism: **Increased Intrahepatic Vascular Resistance**combined with **Increased Portal Blood Flow (Hyperdynamic Circulation)** [Robbins 3 p. 861, 862].

```
Portal Pressure (P) = Portal Blood Flow (Q) × Vascular Resistance (R)
```

### 1. Increased Intrahepatic Resistance (Ohm's Law Component)

- **Structural Component (~70%):** Disruption of normal lobular architecture by dense collagenous fibrous septa, regenerative parenchymal nodules, and perivenular scarring compressing intrahepatic portal branches and sinusoids [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Functional / Dynamic Component (~30%):** Active contraction of modified Hepatic Stellate Cells (myofibroblasts) and vascular smooth muscle cells surrounding sinusoids. This is driven by:
    - **Decreased Endothelial Nitric Oxide (NO) Production:** Local intrahepatic NO deficiency due to impaired endothelial NO synthase (eNOS) activation [Robbins 3 p. 861].
    - **Increased Local Vasoconstrictors:** Upregulation of Endothelin-1 (ET-1), Angiotensin II, Thromboxane A₂, and alpha-adrenergic tone [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Sinusoidal Capillarization:** Loss of normal endothelial fenestrations and deposition of basement membrane collagen in the Space of Disse, impeding metabolic exchange [Robbins 3 p. 861].

### 2. Splanchnic Vasodilation & Hyperdynamic Circulation

- **Splanchnic Arterial Vasodilation:** Chronic portal hypertension triggers excessive systemic and splanchnic release of potent vasodilators—predominantly **Nitric Oxide (NO)**, prostacyclin, and TNF-alpha [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Increased Inflow:** Splanchnic arteriolar vasodilation decreases systemic vascular resistance and increases splanchnic blood inflow, which drains directly into the portal vein, perpetuating high portal pressure [Robbins 3 p. 861].

### 3. Portosystemic Collateral Formation & Shunting

When portal pressure exceeds system pressure, embryonic vascular channels open at four major anatomical sites where portal and systemic venous circulations meet [Robbins 3 p. 861, 862]:

1. **Esophagogastric Junction:** Left gastric (coronary) vein collateralizes with intercostal/azygos veins → **Esophageal and Gastric Varices** (high risk of lethal rupture) [Robbins 3 p. 862].
2. **Rectum:** Superior hemorrhoidal vein collateralizes with middle/inferior hemorrhoidal veins → **Rectal Hemorrhoids** [Robbins 3 p. 862].
3. **Falciform Ligament / Umbilicus:** Re-opened recanalized umbilical vein collateralizes with epigastric veins → **Caput Medusae** (dilated periumbilical veins) [Robbins 3 p. 862].
4. **Retroperitoneum & Diaphragm:** Veins of Retzius connecting visceral veins to parietal retroperitoneal veins.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Asymptomatic Phase:** Long compensatory period where portal hypertension is silent until major collateral rupture occurs.
- **Massive Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage:** Sudden, painless, profuse **hematemesis** (vomiting of bright red blood or "coffee-ground" material) and **melena** (black tarry stools) due to ruptured esophagogastric varices [Robbins 3 p. 862]. Associated with high mortality (~20% to 30% per episode) [Robbins 3 p. 862].
- **Abdominal Distension:** Rapidly increasing abdominal girth and weight gain secondary to ascites [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Left Upper Quadrant Fullness:** Pain or dragging sensation in the left hypochondrium caused by massive splenomegaly.
- **Neuropsychiatric Symptoms:** Daytime somnolence, nocturnal insomnia, confusion, asterixis, and memory loss due to **Hepatic Encephalopathy** (portosystemic shunting bypassing liver detoxification) [Robbins 3 p. 861].

1. **Caput Medusae:** Dilated, engorged subcutaneous veins radiating outward from the umbilicus across the abdominal wall [Robbins 3 p. 862]. Venous blood flow flows **away from the umbilicus** (inferior veins flow downward, superior veins flow upward), distinguishing it from IVC obstruction (where all collateral blood flows upward).
2. **Ascites:** Demonstrable on abdominal examination as **shifting dullness** (when >500 mL fluid accumulated) and a **fluid thrill** (when massive ascites present) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
3. **Congestive Splenomegaly:** Palpable firm enlargement of the spleen extending below the left costal margin (splenic weight may reach 500 to 1000 g) [Robbins 3 p. 862].
4. **Rectal Hemorrhoids:** Dilated portosystemic collateral veins in the anal canal [Robbins 3 p. 862].
5. **Cruveilhier-Baumgarten Murmur:** Continuous venous hum heard on auscultation over the epigastrium / recanalized umbilical vein.
6. **Signs of Hypersplenism:** Petechiae, ecchymoses, mucosal bleeding, and pallor secondary to splenic sequestration of platelets, RBCs, and WBCs [Robbins 3 p. 862].
7. **Pulmonary Signs:**
    - **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS):** Dyspnea worse when sitting upright (**platypnea**) and arterial deoxgenation worse when upright (**orthodeoxia**), accompanied by digital clubbing and spider angiomas.
    - **Portopulmonary Hypertension (POPH):** Exertional dyspnea, fatigue, loud P2, and right heart failure signs [Robbins 3 p. 862].

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Direct Hemodynamic Measurement (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG):** Performed via transjugular balloon catheterization of the hepatic vein. \[\text{HVPG} = \text{Wedged Hepatic Vein Pressure (WHVP)} - \text{Free Hepatic Vein Pressure (FHVP)}\]
    - **Normal:** 1 to 5 mmHg.
    - **Portal Hypertension:** >5 mmHg.
    - **Clinically Significant Portal HTN (CSPH):** **≥10 mmHg** (varices form).
    - **High Risk of Variceal Rupture & Ascites:** **≥12 mmHg**.

---

### B. Endoscopic & Imaging Evaluation [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (UGIE) [⭐ Gold Standard for Varices]:**
    - Visualizes and grades esophageal varices (Grade I to IV) and gastric varices.
    - Identifies high-risk stigmata of impending hemorrhage: **Red wale marks**, cherry-red spots, hematocystic spots, and diffuse portal hypertensive gastropathy (snake-skin gastric mucosa).
2. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) with Color Doppler:**
    - Portal vein diameter **>13 mm** (measured during quiet respiration).
    - Reduced, absent, or **reversed (hepatofugal) blood flow** in the portal vein.
    - Identifies splenomegaly (splenic index), ascites, and portal vein thrombosis [Robbins 3 p. 861].
3. **Transient Elastography (FibroScan):** Non-invasive measurement of liver stiffness (values **>15 to 20 kPa** strongly correlate with CSPH).
4. **Contrast-Enhanced CT / MR Angiography:** Visualizes extensive portosystemic collateral pathways, retroperitoneal varices, portal vein thrombosis, and structural lesions in Budd-Chiari syndrome [Robbins 3 p. 861].

---

### C. Laboratory & Ascitic Fluid Investigations

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** **Thrombocytopenia (platelets <100,000/μL)** is the single most sensitive peripheral blood marker of portal hypertension and hypersplenism [Robbins 3 p. 862]. Leukopenia and anemia may coexist (pancytopenia).
2. **Paracentesis & Ascitic Fluid Analysis [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Serum-to-Ascites Albumin Gradient (SAAG):** \[\text{SAAG} = \text{Serum Albumin} - \text{Ascitic Fluid Albumin}\]
    - **High SAAG (≥1.1 g/dL):** Indicates **Portal Hypertension** (e.g., Cirrhosis, Alcoholic Hepatitis, Budd-Chiari, Cardiac Ascites) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
    - **Low SAAG (<1.1 g/dL):** Indicates Non-Portal Hypertensive causes (e.g., Peritoneal Tuberculosis, Peritoneal Carcinomatosis, Nephrotic Syndrome).
    - **Ascitic Neutrophil Count:** Polymorphonuclear (PMN) count **≥250 cells/μL** diagnoses **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)**.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Propranolol (Non-Selective Beta-Blocker for Prophylaxis) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Propranolol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 1 Textbook p. 195, Kdt 3 Textbook p. 585, 615). Non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker. Dual mechanism to reduce portal pressure:
    
    - _β₁-Blockade:_ Decreases cardiac output and heart rate.
    - _β₂-Blockade:_ Blocks β₂-mediated splanchnic vasodilation, allowing **unopposed α₁-adrenergic vasoconstriction** of splanchnic arterioles, markedly reducing splanchnic blood inflow into the portal system.
    
    ```
    Propranolol → Dual β1 & β2 Blockade → ↓ CO + Unopposed Splanchnic α1 Vasoconstriction → Reduced Portal Inflow & HVPG <10 mmHg
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** β₁ and β₂ adrenoceptors.
- **Dose:** **20 mg to 40 mg PO twice daily (BD)**, titrated upwards every 3 to 5 days until resting heart rate is reduced by 25% or reaches **55 to 60 beats/min**. (Max dose 160–320 mg/day).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (BD).
- **Features:** Gold-standard drug for primary and secondary prophylaxis of variceal hemorrhage; reduces risk of first bleeding by 45–50%.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, fatigue, hypotension, bronchospasm (in asthmatics), cold extremities, masking hypoglycemia symptoms, erectile dysfunction.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in patients with medium-to-large varices or red wale marks; continue **indefinitely**unless contraindications develop (e.g., severe bradycardia, refractory ascites, SBP).

#### 2. Terlipressin (Vasoactive Agent for Acute Variceal Bleeding) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Terlipressin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 643). Synthetic prodrug of vasopressin (8-lysine vasopressin). Slowly converted in vivo to lysine vasopressin. Acts selectively on **vascular V₁ receptors** on splanchnic vascular smooth muscle, producing potent vasoconstriction of mesenteric arterioles, significantly decreasing portal blood flow and portal pressure while maintaining hemostasis.
    
    ```
    Terlipressin → Cleaved to Vasopressin → Splanchnic V1 Receptor Activation → Splanchnic Arteriolar Vasoconstriction → ↓ Portal Flow & Immediate Hemostasis
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Vasopressin V₁ receptors.
- **Dose:** **2 mg IV bolus every 4 hours** initially for 24 to 48 hours; once bleeding is controlled, reduce dose to **1 mg IV every 4 hours** for up to 3 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous injection.
- **Frequency:** Every 4 hours.
- **Features:** First-line pharmacological treatment for acute esophageal variceal hemorrhage; **only vasoactive drug proven to reduce overall mortality** in acute variceal bleeding.
- **Side Effects:** Abdominal cramps, diarrhea, hyponatremia, peripheral tissue ischemia, hypertension, bradycardia, myocardial ischemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate immediately upon clinical suspicion of acute variceal hemorrhage; stop 24 to 48 hours after complete hemostasis is achieved (maximum duration 5 days).

#### 3. Octreotide (Somatostatin Analogue for Acute Bleeding) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Octreotide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 643, 644). Synthetic octapeptide surrogate of somatostatin. Binds to somatostatin receptors (SSTR2/SSTR5) on splanchnic vessels, inhibiting the release of vasodilatory peptides (such as glucagon and VIP). Induces selective splanchnic vasoconstriction, lowering portal vein pressure without causing severe systemic arterial hypertension.
    
    ```
    Octreotide → Somatostatin SSTR2/SSTR5 Binding → Inhibits Glucagon/VIP Release → Selective Splanchnic Vasoconstriction → ↓ Portal Pressure
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Somatostatin SSTR2 and SSTR5 receptors.
- **Dose:** **50 μg IV rapid bolus**, followed immediately by a **continuous IV infusion of 25 to 50 μg/hour** for 2 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous bolus followed by continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion over 48 to 120 hours.
- **Features:** High efficacy with a superior safety profile compared to natural vasopressin; preferred alternative when terlipressin is unavailable or contraindicated.
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, abdominal cramps, transient hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia, biliary sludge/gallstones (on long-term use).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at onset of acute variceal bleed; stop after 2 to 5 days of sustained hemostasis.

#### 4. Spironolactone (First-Line Diuretic for Ascites) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Spironolactone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 634, 635, 636). Competitive antagonist at intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors (MR) in late distal tubules and collecting duct principal cells. Prevents Aldosterone-Induced Protein (AIP) synthesis, blocking Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ excretion, reversing secondary hyperaldosteronism in portal hypertension.
- **Dose:** **100 mg PO once daily (OD)**, titrated by 100 mg every 3 to 5 days up to **400 mg/day** (frequently combined with Furosemide in a **100 mg : 40 mg ratio**).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (OD).
- **Features:** Drug of choice for cirrhotic ascites; prevents hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis.
- **Side Effects:** Hyperkalemia, painful gynecomastia, erectile dysfunction, menstrual irregularities, metabolic acidosis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in grade 2 ascites; maintain to achieve target weight loss (max 0.5 kg/day in non-edematous ascites, 1.0 kg/day if peripheral edema present).

### B. Emergency Management Protocol for Acute Variceal Bleeding

1. **Resuscitation:** Transfer to ICU; maintain airway protection (endotracheal intubation if comatose); restore blood volume with packed RBCs using a **restrictive transfusion strategy (target Hemoglobin 7 to 8 g/dL)** (over-transfusion increases portal pressure and precipitates rebleeding).
2. **Immediate Vasoactive Therapy:** Administer **Terlipressin (2 mg IV q4h)** or **Octreotide (50 μg IV bolus + 50 μg/h infusion)** prior to endoscopy.
3. **Endoscopic Therapy (Within 12 Hours):** **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL)** (rubber band ligation) is the gold standard procedure. Endoscopic cyanoacrylate tissue adhesive injection is used for gastric varices.
4. **Prophylactic Antibiotics:** Administer **IV Ceftriaxone (1 g daily for 7 days)** to prevent SBP and septicemia (significantly reduces rebleeding and mortality).
5. **Balloon Tamponade (Bridge Therapy):** Sengstaken-Blakemore tube or Minnesota tube insertion for temporary (<24 hours) mechanical compression of refractory bleeding varices.
6. **Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS):** Side-to-side portacaval shunt created percutaneously by placing a expandable stent between the hepatic vein and intrahepatic portal vein branch. Indicated as rescue therapy for uncontrolled variceal hemorrhage or refractory ascites [Robbins 3 p. 861].

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Ruptured Esophagogastric Varices & Massive Upper GI Hemorrhage:** ~30% mortality per bleeding episode [Robbins 3 p. 862].
2. **Refractory Ascites & Hydrothorax:** Serous fluid accumulation in peritoneal and pleural cavities [Robbins 3 p. 861].
3. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP):** Infection of ascitic fluid without an intra-abdominal surgical source (PMN ≥250/μL) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
4. **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Neuropsychiatric syndrome caused by portosystemic shunting of un-detoxified nitrogenous toxins (NH₃) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
5. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Functional oliguric renal failure driven by extreme splanchnic vasodilation and compensatory renal vasoconstriction [Robbins 3 p. 861].
6. **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS) & Portopulmonary Hypertension (POPH):** Pulmonary microvascular dilations causing platypnea-orthodeoxia or secondary pulmonary hypertension [Robbins 3 p. 862].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Strongly dictated by the severity of underlying liver disease (calculated using Child-Pugh Class or MELD score) and HVPG response to therapy.
- Achieving a reduction in HVPG to **<10 mmHg** or by **>20% from baseline** using non-selective beta-blockers virtually eliminates the risk of variceal bleeding and decompensation.

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In patients presenting with acute gastrointestinal hemorrhage, **NEVER over-transfuse blood to achieve a normal hemoglobin level**! Transfusing blood to target Hb levels >9–10 g/dL increases central venous and portal pressures, which disrupts fresh blood clots and directly triggers catastrophic **rebleeding of esophageal varices**. Always maintain a **restrictive transfusion strategy**, targeting a hemoglobin level of **7.0 to 8.0 g/dL**!

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing **Pure Beta-1 Selective Blockers (e.g., Atenolol, Metoprolol)** for the primary prophylaxis of esophageal variceal bleeding.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Beta-1 selective blockers only decrease heart rate and cardiac output. They do NOT block splanchnic β₂ receptors.
- **Resolution:** **Selective β₁ blockers are INEFFECTIVE for portal hypertension**! Primary prophylaxis requires **Non-Selective Beta-Blockers (Propranolol, Nadolol, or Carvedilol)** because β₂-blockade is mandatory to allow unopposed α₁-adrenergic splanchnic vasoconstriction, which decreases splanchnic inflow and lowers portal pressure!

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** HVPG >5 mmHg; Clinically Significant Portal HTN (CSPH) ≥10 mmHg; Variceal Bleed Risk ≥12 mmHg [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Classification:** Pre-hepatic (PVT), Intra-hepatic (Cirrhosis ~90%, Schistosomiasis, NCPF), Post-hepatic (Budd-Chiari syndrome) [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Core Pathology:** Structural scarring + functional intrahepatic vasoconstriction (low NO, high Endothelin-1) + hyperdynamic splanchnic vasodilation [Robbins 3 p. 861].
- **Classic Consequences:** Esophageal varices, Ascites, Congestive Splenomegaly (thrombocytopenia), Caput Medusae, Hepatic Encephalopathy [Robbins 3 p. 861, 862].
- **Prophylaxis Drug of Choice:** **Propranolol** (20–40 mg PO BD, non-selective β-blocker) [Kdt 1 p. 195, Kdt 3 p. 585].
- **Acute Bleeding Drug of Choice:** **Terlipressin** (2 mg IV q4h) OR **Octreotide** (50 μg IV bolus + 50 μg/h infusion) + **EVL** within 12 hours [Kdt 3 p. 643].

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Portal hypertension is a pathological elevation of the hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG >5 mmHg) driven by increased intrahepatic vascular resistance and splanchnic hyperdynamic inflow, manifesting as life-threatening esophagogastric varices, ascites, and congestive splenomegaly.**
