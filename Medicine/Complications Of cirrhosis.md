# Cirrhosis of Liver: Complications & Prevention of Hepatitis B Infection

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Cirrhosis of the Liver** is the end-stage morphological outcome of chronic liver injury, characterized by diffuse irreversible scarring, fibrous septa formation, and conversion of normal hepatic architecture into structurally abnormal regenerative parenchymal nodules [Robbins 3 p. 235, 241, 245].

---

## 2. Complications of Cirrhosis of Liver [⭐ High-Yield]

The clinical complications of cirrhosis stem from two primary pathophysiological mechanisms: **Portal Hypertension**(HVPG >5 mmHg) and **Progressive Hepatocellular Failure** [Robbins 3 p. 242, 246, 256].

### A. Complications Secondary to Portal Hypertension [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Ascites & Hepatic Hydrothorax:** Accumulation of excess serous fluid in the peritoneal cavity (≥500 mL) driven by sinusoidal hypertension, splanchnic vasodilation (mediated by excess Nitric Oxide), and hypoalbuminemia [Robbins 1 p. 200, Robbins 3 p. 246]. Transdiaphragmatic seepage produces right-sided pleural effusions (hydrothorax) [Robbins 3 p. 246].
2. **Esophagogastric Varices & Massive GI Bleeding:** Dilation of submucosal collateral veins at the esophagogastric junction. When Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) reaches ≥12 mmHg, spontaneous variceal rupture occurs, causing explosive, life-threatening **hematemesis and melena** (associated with 15–20% mortality per episode) [Robbins 3 p. 230, 253].
3. **Congestive Splenomegaly & Hypersplenism:** Chronic splenic venous outflow obstruction enlarges the spleen (up to 1000 g). Sequestration of formed blood elements in expanded splenic red pulp causes **thrombocytopenia**, leukopenia, or pancytopenia [Robbins 3 p. 249, 254].
4. **Portosystemic Collateral Formation:** Opening of embryonic collateral veins at anatomical junctions:
    - Re-opened umbilical vein → **Caput Medusae** (dilated periumbilical abdominal wall veins) [Robbins 3 p. 253].
    - Anorectal junctions → **Rectal Hemorrhoids** [Robbins 3 p. 233, 253].
5. **Pulmonary Vascular Complications:**
    - **Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS):** Intrapulmonary capillary dilations causing ventilation-perfusion mismatch, presenting as dyspnea worse when sitting upright (**platypnea**) and arterial deoxygenation (**orthodeoxia**) [Robbins 3 p. 250].
    - **Portopulmonary Hypertension (POPH):** Pulmonary arterial hypertension developing secondary to chronic portal hypertension [Robbins 3 p. 251].

---

### B. Complications Secondary to Hepatocellular Failure [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hepatic Encephalopathy (Coma):** Reversible metabolic neuropsychiatric syndrome (disturbed consciousness, asterixis, fetor hepaticus, coma) caused by hyperammonemia (NH₃) due to impaired hepatic urea cycle detoxification and portosystemic shunting [Robbins 3 p. 237, 240, 256].
2. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Functional acute kidney injury (AKI) characterized by intense renal afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction in structurally normal kidneys, triggered by extreme splanchnic arterial vasodilation [Robbins 3 p. 238, 257].
3. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP):** Monomicrobial infection of ascitic fluid (**Ascitic PMN ≥250 cells/μL**) caused by translocation of gut enteric bacilli (_E. coli_), promoted by low ascitic protein (<1.0 g/dL) [Robbins 1 p. 200, Robbins 3 p. 242].
4. **Coagulopathy & Hemorrhagic Diathesis:** Failure to synthesize hepatic clotting factors (II, V, VII, IX, X, XI, XII, and fibrinogen) combined with thrombocytopenia, predisposing to spontaneous bruising and mucosal bleeding [Robbins 3 p. 238, 257].
5. **Hyperestrogenemia Manifestations:** Impaired hepatic degradation of circulating estrogens causes **spider angiomas** (pulsating cutaneous arterioles), **palmar erythema**, gynecomastia, and testicular atrophy in males [Robbins 3 p. 243].
6. **Malignant Transformation (Hepatocellular Carcinoma - HCC):** Dysplastic regenerative nodules undergo malignant progression into HCC at an annual incidence of 1% to 6% in cirrhotic patients [Robbins 3 p. 272, 283].

## 3. Prevention of Hepatitis B Infection [⭐ High-Yield]

Prevention of Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection involves **Active Immunization**, **Passive Immunoprophylaxis**, and **Infection Control Measures** [Davidson 1 p. 93, 100, Kdt4 p. 984, 985].

### A. Active Immunization (Hepatitis B Vaccine) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vaccine Composition:** Component vaccine containing recombinant Hepatitis B surface Antigen (**HBsAg**) expressed in yeast cells (_Saccharomyces cerevisiae_) and adsorbed onto an aluminum hydroxide adjuvant [Davidson 1 p. 100, Kdt4 p. 979, 984].
- **Dose & Route:**
    - **Adults (≥10 years):** **20 μg (1 mL) IM** in the **deltoid muscle** [Kdt4 p. 984].
    - **Infants & Children (<10 years):** **10 μg (0.5 mL) IM** in the anterolateral aspect of the thigh [Kdt4 p. 984].
    - _Note:_ Avoid gluteal injection due to reduced antibody response caused by fat deposition.
- **Vaccination Schedule:** Standard 3-dose regimen administered at **0, 1, and 6 months** [Kdt4 p. 984].
- **Target Efficacy:** Induces protective anti-HBs antibodies in **>95%** of recipients. Protective immunity is confirmed when serum **anti-HBs titer >10 mIU/mL** [Davidson 1 p. 100, Robbins 3 p. 265].
- **Indications:**
    1. Universal infant immunization (included in National Immunization Schedules / Pentavalent vaccine) [Kdt4 p. 979, 984].
    2. High-risk groups: Healthcare workers, blood bank personnel, hemodialysis patients, household/sexual contacts of HBsAg-positive individuals, and IV drug users [Davidson 1 p. 93, Kdt4 p. 984].

---

### B. Passive Immunization (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis - HBIG) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG):** Concentrated human immune globulin (10–18% solution) containing high titers of specific anti-HBs antibodies [Kdt4 p. 985].
- **Dosage & Administration:**
    - **Adults:** **1000 to 2000 IU IM** (or 32–48 IU/kg) administered within **24 hours to 7 days** post-exposure [Kdt4 p. 985].
    - **Perinatal Prevention (Infants of HBsAg+ Mothers):** Single dose of **0.5 mL (100–200 IU) IM** administered within **12 hours of birth** [Davidson 1 p. 93, Kdt4 p. 985].
- **Combined Active-Passive Immunization:** HBIG must be administered at a **separate anatomical site** (e.g., opposite arm/thigh) from the first dose of the HBV vaccine to avoid immune neutralization [Kdt4 p. 985].
- **Indications:** Accidental needle-stick injuries with HBsAg+ blood, acute sexual exposure, and newborns of HBsAg-positive mothers [Davidson 1 p. 100, Kdt4 p. 985].

---

### C. Infection Control & General Prevention Measures

1. **Screening of Blood & Organ Donors:** Mandatory screening of all donated blood units for HBsAg, anti-HBc, and HBV DNA to prevent transfusion-transmitted HBV [Davidson 1 p. 93, Robbins 3 p. 262].
2. **Sharps & Barrier Precautions:** Safe disposal of needles/syringes, strict sterilization of surgical and dental instruments, and routine use of gloves [Davidson 1 p. 93].
3. **Prevention of Vertical & Sexual Transmission:** Use of barrier contraception (condoms) and antiviral suppression (Tenofovir) in pregnant women with high HBV DNA viral load (>200,000 IU/mL) during the 3rd trimester [Davidson 1 p. 93, Kdt3 p. 751].

## 5. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In a newborn born to an HBsAg-positive mother, administering **Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG 0.5 mL IM) AND the first dose of Recombinant HBV Vaccine (10 μg IM) within 12 hours of birth at separate anatomical injection sites** reduces the vertical transmission rate of Hepatitis B from over 90% down to **less than 5%** [Davidson 1 p. 93, Kdt4 p. 985]!

---

## 6. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering the Hepatitis B vaccine in the **gluteal muscle (buttock)** in adults.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Injections into deep gluteal fat result in lower antigen deposition in vascular muscle tissue, leading to significantly lower anti-HBs seroconversion rates.
- **Resolution:** **Always administer the Hepatitis B vaccine in the DELTOID muscle in adults** (and the anterolateral thigh in infants) to ensure optimal immunogenicity and anti-HBs antibody titers >10 mIU/mL [Kdt4 p. 984]!

## 9. Quick Revision

- **Cirrhosis Complications:** Portal HTN causes ascites, esophageal varices, congestive splenomegaly (thrombocytopenia), and caput medusae [Robbins 3 p. 246, 253, 254]. Liver failure causes hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome, SBP, coagulopathy, and spider angiomas [Robbins 3 p. 237, 238, 242, 243].
- **HBV Vaccine Type:** Recombinant component HBsAg vaccine expressed in yeast [Davidson 1 p. 100, Kdt4 p. 984].
- **Vaccination Schedule:** 3 IM doses at **0, 1, and 6 months in deltoid muscle** (20 μg dose) [Kdt4 p. 984].
- **Protective Titer:** Serum **anti-HBs >10 mIU/mL** confers >95% protection [Davidson 1 p. 100, Robbins 3 p. 265].
- **HBIG Passive Protection:** Administer **HBIG (0.5 mL IM in infants within 12h; 1000–2000 IU in adults <24h–7d)** at a separate site from vaccine for post-exposure and perinatal prophylaxis [Davidson 1 p. 93, Kdt4 p. 985].

## 10. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Cirrhosis of the liver produces life-threatening complications through portal hypertension (ascites, variceal bleeding, splenomegaly) and hepatocellular failure (hepatic encephalopathy, HRS, SBP), while Hepatitis B infection is effectively prevented using recombinant HBsAg active vaccination (0, 1, 6 months) and HBIG passive immunoprophylaxis.**
