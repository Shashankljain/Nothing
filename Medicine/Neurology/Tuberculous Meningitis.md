# Tuberculous Meningitis

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Tuberculous Meningitis (TBM)** is an acute or subacute, life-threatening granulomatous meningoencephalitis caused by central nervous system infection with _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_ [Robbins 5 p. 1263]. It represents the most severe and lethal form of extrapulmonary tuberculosis, characterized by thick gelatinous exudates at the base of the brain, obliterative endarteritis, cranial nerve palsies, and hydrocephalus [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].

---

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Causative Organism

- **_Mycobacterium tuberculosis_:** An acid-fast, aerobic, non-motile, intracellular bacillus with a lipid-rich mycolic acid cell wall [Davidson 1 p. 588, Kdt 3 p. 818].

---

### B. High-Yield Risk Factors

- **HIV / AIDS Co-infection:** Increases the risk of developing active tuberculosis by 8-fold; HIV-associated TBM carries higher mortality and atypical presentations [Davidson 1 p. 1125, Kdt 3 p. 828].
- **Age Thresholds:** Young children (ages 1 to 5 years) following primary pulmonary infection, and older adults with age-related waning cell-mediated immunity [Davidson 1 p. 588, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Immunosuppression:** Prolonged systemic corticosteroid therapy, anti-TNF-α biologic administration, chemotherapy, organ transplantation, and severe malnutrition [Davidson 1 p. 588, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Socioeconomic Factors:** Overcrowding, poverty, alcoholism, and residency in endemic tuberculosis regions [Davidson 1 p. 588].

---

## 3. Clinical Staging Systems (MRC Staging of TBM) [⭐ High-Yield]

The **Medical Research Council (MRC)** clinical staging system predicts neurological prognosis and mortality in Tuberculous Meningitis:

|Stage|Clinical Description|Neurological Status|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Stage I (Early)**|Conscious, fully alert, non-specific constitutional symptoms (fever, headache, apathy)|**No focal neurological deficits**|
|**Stage II (Intermediate)**|Lethargic, confused, or drowsy; moderate meningismus|**Mild focal deficits** (Cranial nerve palsies—CN III, VI, VII, or mild hemiparesis)|
|**Stage III (Advanced)**|Stupor, deep coma, gross delirium, or decerebrate rigidity|**Severe deficits** (Dense hemiplegia, paraplegia, multiple CN palsies)|

## 4. Pathophysiology & Etiopathogenesis (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Primary Bacteraemia & Rich Focus Formation:** During primary pulmonary TB infection or miliary dissemination, blood-borne tubercle bacilli seed the central nervous system, forming small subpial or subependymal caseating granulomatous lesions in the brain cortex or meninges known as **Rich foci** [Davidson 1 p. 588, Robbins 2 p. 162, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
2. **Rupture into Subarachnoid Space:** Subsequent rupture or discharge of a Rich focus releases mycobacterial antigens and viable bacilli directly into the subarachnoid space filled with CSF [Robbins 5 p. 1263].
3. **Delayed Hypersensitivity & Basal Exudate Formation:** Mycobacterial lipoarabinomannan and proteins trigger a intense T-cell-mediated (type IV) delayed hypersensitivity reaction. This generates a dense, thick, gelatinous, and fibrinous exudate that characteristically accumulates at the **base of the brain (basal cisterns)**, effacing the interpeduncular fossa, optic chiasm, and brainstem [Robbins 2 p. 154, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
4. **Obliterative Endarteritis & Cerebral Ischemia:** Arteries traversing the subarachnoid basal exudate (especially branches of the Circle of Willis, middle cerebral artery, and lenticulostriate vessels) undergo marked intimal thickening, perivascular lymphocytic/plasma cell infiltration, and **obliterative endarteritis**, leading to vessel thrombosis and **ischemic brain infarction (stroke)** in the basal ganglia and internal capsule [Robbins 5 p. 1263].
5. **Cranial Nerve Entrapment:** The thick basal exudate encases and compresses cranial nerves traversing the basal cisterns, predominantly **CN III (Oculomotor), CN VI (Abducens), and CN VII (Facial)** [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
6. **CSF Pathway Obstruction & Hydrocephalus:** Basal exudate and chronic adhesive arachnoiditis block CSF flow through the foramina of Luschka/Magendie or impair arachnoid villi resorption, producing **communicating or obstructive hydrocephalus** [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Prodromal Phase (Insidious Onset over 2 to 4 Weeks) [⭐ Key Distinguishing Feature]:** Low-grade evening pyrexia, night sweats, malaise, anorexia, weight loss, listlessness, apathy, and persistent dull headache [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Meningeal Phase:** Progressive severe headache, persistent projectile vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia, and neck pain [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1120].
- **Advanced Neurological Phase:** Drowsiness, confusion, delirium, focal weakness (hemiparesis), seizures, and progressive coma [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].

---

### B. Physical Signs

1. **Meningism (Meningeal Irritation Signs):**
    - **Nuchal Rigidity (Neck Stiffness):** Resistance to passive forward neck flexion [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Positive Kernig's Sign:** Resistance and pain on passive extension of the knee when hip is flexed at 90° [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Positive Brudzinski's Sign:** Involuntary flexion of hips and knees upon passive neck flexion [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **Cranial Nerve Palsies [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **CN VI (Abducens) Palsy [Most Common]:** Inability to abduct the eye, producing horizontal diplopia [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
    - **CN III (Oculomotor) Palsy:** Ptosis, dilated unreactive pupil, and "down and out" eye deviation [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1120].
    - **CN VII (Facial) Palsy:** Lower motor neuron facial weakness [Davidson 1 p. 1120].
3. **Ophthalmic Signs:** **Choroidal Tubercles** (yellow-white raised retinal lesions on fundoscopy; highly specific sign of miliary/tuberculous dissemination) [Davidson 1 p. 588, 1120].
4. **Focal Deficits & Endocrine Alterations:** Hemiparesis/aphasia from middle cerebral artery endarteritis; hyponatremia due to **Syndrome of Inappropriate ADH Secretion (SIADH)** or Cerebral Salt Wasting [Davidson 1 p. 358].

## 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Examination [⭐ Gold Standard Diagnostic Test]

Lumbar puncture (LP) performed at L3/L4 interspace demonstrates characteristic TBM parameters:

```
                     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                     │          CSF ANALYSIS IN SUSPECTED TBM       │
                     └──────┬───────────────────────────────────────┘
                            │
   ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
   ▼                        ▼                        ▼
┌──────────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────────┐
│ APPEARANCE & CLOT    │ │ CELL COUNT & PROTEIN │ │ BIOCHEMISTRY & MOLECULAR
├──────────────────────┤ ├──────────────────────┤ ├──────────────────────┤
│ • Clear / Opalescent │ │ • Lymphocytosis      │ │ • Glucose <30 mg/dL  │
│ • Delicate "COBWEB"  │ │   (100–500 cells/μL) │ │   (CSF:Blood <0.3)   │
│   Fibrin Clot on     │ │ • Protein Elevated   │ │ • GeneXpert MTB/RIF  │
│   standing           │ │   (100–500 mg/dL)    │ │ • CSF ADA >10 IU/L   │
└──────────────────────┘ └──────────────────────┘ └──────────────────────┘
```

1. **Appearance & Cobweb Clot:** Clear or opalescent fluid; upon standing overnight at room temperature, it forms a delicate spiderweb-like fibrin network (**"Cobweb / Pellicle Clot"**) due to extremely high protein and fibrinogen content [Davidson 1 p. 1120].
2. **Cell Count & Differential:** Elevated total WBC count (**100 to 500 cells/μL**), with marked **Lymphocytic / Mononuclear cell predominance (>80%)** [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263]. _(Note: Very early TBM may show transient neutrophil presence)._
3. **CSF Protein:** **Markedly elevated (100 to 500 mg/dL)**, sometimes exceeding 1000 mg/dL in the presence of spinal block [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
4. **CSF Glucose:** **Markedly reduced (<30 to 40 mg/dL)**; CSF-to-blood glucose ratio is characteristically **<0.3 to 0.4**[Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
5. **CSF Adenosine Deaminase (ADA):** Elevated CSF ADA levels (**>10 IU/L**) offer high diagnostic sensitivity and specificity for TBM.
6. **Microscopy & Staining:** **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain** or auramine-rhodamine fluorescent stain on centrifuged large-volume CSF (at least 10 mL) demonstrates acid-fast bacilli (AFB) in ~30% to 50% of cases [Davidson 1 p. 592, 1120].
7. **Rapid Molecular Test — GeneXpert MTB/RIF (CBNAAT):** Automated real-time PCR detecting _M. tuberculosis_DNA and rifampicin resistance mutation within 2 hours [Davidson 1 p. 592].

---

### B. Neuroimaging (Contrast MRI / CT Brain)

- **Contrast-Enhanced MRI Brain [Test of Choice]:** Demonstrates the pathognomonic diagnostic triad of TBM:
    1. **Thick Basal Meningeal Enhancement** in the basal cisterns [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
    2. **Hydrocephalus** (dilated lateral and third ventricles) [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
    3. **Ischemic Brain Infarctions** in the basal ganglia, thalamus, and internal capsule ("tubercular zone") secondary to endarteritis [Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Tuberculoma:** Ring-enhancing intraparenchymal mass lesions with central caseous necrosis and surrounding vasogenic edema [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].

---

### C. Systemic Evaluation

- **Chest X-Ray:** Reveals evidence of active/healed pulmonary TB, hilar lymphadenopathy, or miliary "millet-seed" reticulonodular pattern in 30% to 50% of patients [Davidson 1 p. 588, 1120].
- **Tuberculin Skin Test (Mantoux Test):** Induration ≥5 mm in HIV-positive or ≥10 mm in immunocompetent individuals [Davidson 1 p. 588].

### A. Chemotherapy Regimen for TBM (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Total Duration of ATT:** Tuberculous meningitis requires a extended total treatment duration of **12 months** (unlike the 6-month regimen used for uncomplicated pulmonary TB) [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Intensive Phase (2 Months):** **2 HRZE** — Daily Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E) for 2 months [Kdt 3 p. 825]. _(Streptomycin S may replace Ethambutol in severe TBM due to superior CSF penetration through inflamed meninges)_ [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Continuation Phase (10 Months):** **10 HRE** (or **10 HR**) — Daily Isoniazid + Rifampicin + Ethambutol for an additional 10 months [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].

---

### B. Individual Anti-Tubercular Drug Profiles (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Isoniazid (H)

- **Name:** Isoniazid (INH) [Kdt 3 p. 818, 825].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 818). Prodrug activated by mycobacterial catalase-peroxidase enzyme (**KatG**). Active metabolite inhibits **InhA (enoyl-ACP reductase)** enzyme, blocking mycolic acid synthesis—an essential structural component of the mycobacterial cell wall [Kdt 3 p. 818].
    
    ```
    Isoniazid → Activated by KatG → Inhibits InhA Enoyl-ACP Reductase → Blocks Mycolic Acid Synthesis → Mycobacterial Cell Death
    ```
    
- **Target / Receptors:** KatG and InhA enoyl-ACP reductase enzymes.
- **Dose:** **5 mg/kg/day** (Usual adult dose: **300 mg PO once daily**) [Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablet / IV injection.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (OD) on an empty stomach.
- **Features:** Primary bactericidal drug; achieves **excellent CSF penetration (~100% of serum levels)** across both inflamed and uninflamed meninges [Kdt 3 p. 818, 825].
- **Side Effects:** **Peripheral neuropathy** (due to competition with pyridoxal phosphate; prevented by co-prescribing Pyridoxine), hepatotoxicity, rash, lupus-like syndrome [Kdt 3 p. 818, 825].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate immediately on Day 1; continue for the full **12 months** [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].

#### 2. Rifampicin (R)

- **Name:** Rifampicin / Rifampin [Kdt 3 p. 819, 825].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 819). Binds specifically to the **β-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase**, inhibiting transcription and RNA synthesis [Kdt 3 p. 819].
- **Target:** β-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
- **Dose:** **10 mg/kg/day** (Usual adult dose: **450 to 600 mg PO once daily**) [Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) capsules, 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals [Kdt 3 p. 819, 825].
- **Frequency:** Once daily (OD).
- **Features:** Powerful bactericidal and sterilizing agent; causes harmless **bright orange-red discoloration of urine, tears, and sweat** [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 819, 825].
- **Side Effects:** Hepatotoxicity, GI upset, potent hepatic CYP450 enzyme induction (reduces efficacy of oral contraceptives, warfarin, antiretrovirals) [Kdt 3 p. 819, 825].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate on Day 1; continue for the full **12 months** [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].

#### 3. Pyrazinamide (Z)

- **Name:** Pyrazinamide [Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 820). Converted by mycobacterial pyrazinamidase into active **pyrazinoic acid**, which accumulates in acidic intracellular phagolysosomes, disrupting bacterial membrane potential and transport mechanisms [Kdt 3 p. 820].
- **Dose:** **25 mg/kg/day** (Usual adult dose: **1500 mg PO once daily**) [Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Features:** Highly effective sterilizing drug against slow-multiplying intracellular bacilli; achieves **excellent CSF penetration** [Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].
- **Side Effects:** Hepatotoxicity (most hepatotoxic first-line drug), **hyperuricemia** (inhibits renal uric acid secretion, triggering gout), arthralgia [Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Given during the **first 2 months (Intensive Phase) only**; then discontinued [Kdt 3 p. 825].

#### 4. Ethambutol (E)

- **Name:** Ethambutol [Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 820). Inhibits **arabinosyl transferase (EmbB)**, blocking the polymerization of arabinogalactan in the mycobacterial cell wall [Kdt 3 p. 820].
- **Dose:** **15 mg/kg/day** (Usual adult dose: **800 to 1200 mg PO once daily**) [Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Features:** Tuberculostatic agent; prevents emergence of drug resistance [Kdt 3 p. 820].
- **Side Effects:** **Retrobulbar optic neuritis** (dose-dependent loss of visual acuity and green-red color discrimination) [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Included in intensive and continuation phases; stop immediately if visual toxicity develops [Kdt 3 p. 820, 825].

---

### C. Adjuvant Corticosteroid Protocol [⭐ Mandatory High-Yield Therapy]

- **Drug & Dose:** **Dexamethasone 0.4 mg/kg/day IV** (or **Prednisolone 60 mg/day PO**) for **4 weeks**, followed by a gradual tapering schedule over the next 4 weeks (total 6 to 8-week steroid course) [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Kdt 3 p. 828].
- **Mechanism & Benefits:** Corticosteroids suppress NF-κB-mediated transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β), reducing basal exudate formation, attenuating obliterative endarteritis, lowering ICP, and **significantly decreasing mortality and permanent neurological disability** [Davidson 1 p. 592, 1120, Kdt 3 p. 828].

---

### D. Pyridoxine (Vitamin B₆) Supplementation

- **Dose:** **25 to 50 mg PO once daily** throughout the entire course of ATT to prevent isoniazid-induced peripheral neuropathy [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hydrocephalus:** Communicating or obstructive hydrocephalus causing progressive intracranial hypertension [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
2. **Cerebral Ischemic Infarction (Stroke):** Caused by obliterative endarteritis of middle cerebral and lenticulostriate arteries, producing permanent hemiplegia [Robbins 5 p. 1263].
3. **Cranial Nerve Palsies & Blindness:** Entrapment of CN III, VI, and VII in basal exudate; optic atrophy following CN II compression [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
4. **Intraparenchymal Tuberculomas:** Expansion of caseating granulomas causing focal mass effect and seizures [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
5. **Chronic Spinal Arachnoiditis & Radiculopathy:** Exudative spread down the spinal subarachnoid space causing spinal cord compression, girdle pain, and paraplegia (Potts paraplegia) [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
6. **Hyponatremia:** Driven by SIADH or Cerebral Salt Wasting Syndrome [Davidson 1 p. 358].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Unrecognized and untreated TBM is **100% rapidly fatal** [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- Even with appropriate anti-tubercular therapy and steroids, overall mortality remains **15% to 30%**, rising to >50% in Stage III presentation [Davidson 1 p. 1120].
- Up to 30% of survivors suffer long-term neurological sequelae (cognitive impairment, hemiparesis, hydrocephalus, or visual loss) [Davidson 1 p. 1120].

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** The presence of a delicate **"Cobweb / Pellicle Clot"** forming in a clear or opalescent CSF sample standing overnight, combined with **lymphocytic pleocytosis, markedly high protein (>100 mg/dL), and severe hypoglycorrhachia (<30 mg/dL)**, is highly pathognomonic for Tuberculous Meningitis [Davidson 1 p. 1120]! Always initiate high-dose **Adjuvant Dexamethasone** alongside 4-drug ATT immediately—dexamethasone dramatically reduces basal endarteritis, stroke incidence, and mortality [Davidson 1 p. 592, 1120]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Waiting for CSF Acid-Fast Bacilli (AFB) smear or culture results before initiating Anti-Tubercular Therapy (ATT) in a patient with suspected TBM.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Direct CSF ZN smear has a low sensitivity (~30–50%), and mycobacterial cultures take 2 to 6 weeks to grow. Delaying treatment while waiting for laboratory confirmation leads to irreversible basal endarteritis, brain infarction, and fatal herniation.
- **Resolution:** **NEVER delay Anti-Tubercular Therapy while awaiting AFB smear or culture results!** Initiate empiric 4-drug ATT (HRZE) plus Dexamethasone immediately upon clinical suspicion and suggestive CSF findings [Davidson 1 p. 592, 1120]!

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Chronic/subacute granulomatous meningoencephalitis caused by _M. tuberculosis_ seeding from Rich foci [Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Classic CSF Profile:** Opalescent with **Cobweb clot**, **Lymphocytosis (100–500 cells/μL)**, **Markedly High Protein (>100–500 mg/dL)**, and **Low Glucose (<30 mg/dL)** [Davidson 1 p. 1120].
- **Neuroimaging Triad:** Basal meningeal enhancement, Hydrocephalus, and Basal ganglia ischemic infarcts [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
- **Standard ATT Duration:** **12 Months Total** (2 HRZE + 10 HRE) [Davidson 1 p. 592, Kdt 3 p. 825].
- **Mandatory Adjuvant:** **Dexamethasone 0.4 mg/kg/day IV** tapered over 6 to 8 weeks [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Kdt 3 p. 828].

## 16. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Tuberculous meningitis is a severe, subacute granulomatous meningoencephalitis caused by the rupture of subpial Rich foci into the subarachnoid space, presenting with an insidious prodrome, basal exudates, cranial nerve palsies, and hydrocephalus, diagnosed by lymphocytic hypoglycorrhachic CSF with a cobweb clot, and managed using a 12-month ATT regimen with adjuvant high-dose Dexamethasone.**
