# Lumbar Puncture (Spinal Tap)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Lumbar Puncture (LP)**, commonly termed a spinal tap, is an essential invasive diagnostic and therapeutic bedside procedure in which a hollow needle is introduced into the subarachnoid space of the lumbar spine (typically at the L3/L4 or L4/L5 interspace) to measure opening pressure, collect cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for biochemical, microbiological, and cytological analysis, or instill therapeutic and diagnostic agents [Davidson 1 p. 502, Kdt 2 p. 396].

---

## 2. Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Diagnostic Indications

1. **Central Nervous System Infections [⭐ Primary Indication]:**
    - Acute pyogenic (bacterial) meningitis [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
    - Tuberculous meningitis (TBM) [Davidson 1 p. 1120, Robbins 5 p. 1263].
    - Viral / aseptic meningitis and viral encephalitis (HSV-1, Enterovirus) [Robbins 5 p. 1262, 1264].
    - Fungal meningitis (Cryptococcal meningitis in HIV/AIDS) [Davidson 1 p. 1125, Robbins 5 p. 1268].
    - Neurosyphilis and Neuroborreliosis (Lyme disease) [Robbins 5 p. 1264].
2. **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):** Performed to detect **xanthochromia** (spectrophotometric breakdown product of hemoglobin) or persistent red blood cells when non-contrast CT (NCCT) head is normal or equivocal in a patient presenting with a sudden "thunderclap" headache (>12 hours after onset) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
3. **Autoimmune & Inflammatory Neurological Disorders:**
    - Multiple Sclerosis (detection of CSF oligoclonal IgG bands and elevated IgG index) [Robbins 5 p. 1274].
    - Guillain-Barré Syndrome (demonstration of **albuminocytological dissociation**—elevated CSF protein with normal cell count) [Robbins 5 p. 1140, 1268].
4. **Meningeal Malignancies:** Carcinomatous meningitis, leukemic infiltration, and primary CNS lymphoma (detection of malignant cells on CSF cytology) [Robbins 5 p. 1268].

---

### B. Therapeutic Indications

1. **Relief of Intracranial Hypertension:** Serial therapeutic CSF drainage to lower opening pressure in **Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH / Pseudotumor cerebri)** and **Cryptococcal Meningitis** [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1125].
2. **Intrathecal Pharmacotherapy:**
    - Administration of intrathecal chemotherapy (e.g., Methotrexate, Cytarabine) for hematological malignancies [Robbins 3 p. 147].
    - Intrathecal antifungal or antibiotic administration in refractory CNS infections.
3. **Spinal Anesthesia:** Injection of local anesthetics (e.g., Heavy Bupivacaine or Lignocaine) into the subarachnoid space for operative procedures below the umbilicus [Kdt 2 p. 395, 396].

---

### C. Radiological Indications

- **Myelography & Cisternography:** Intrathecal administration of radio-opaque contrast agents for spinal canal imaging or CSF leak localization [Davidson 1 p. 502].

### A. Absolute Contraindications

1. **Raised Intracranial Pressure (ICP) with Focal Mass Effect:** Intracranial space-occupying lesions (tumors, abscess, intracranial hemorrhage) or midline shift. Uncontrolled removal of lumbar CSF lowers intraspinal pressure relative to intracranial pressure, triggering **fatal transtentorial (uncal) or tonsillar cerebellar brain herniation**through the foramen magnum [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117, Kdt 2 p. 397, Robbins 5 p. 1244].
2. **Localized Infection / Sepsis at Injection Site:** Active cellulitis, furunculosis, or epidural abscess over the L3–L5 lumbar region (carries severe risk of introducing bacteria directly into the subarachnoid space) [Kdt 2 p. 397].
3. **Severe Vertebral Column Abnormalities:** Structural anatomical distortion (severe kyphoscoliosis or spinal fusion) preventing safe needle entry [Kdt 2 p. 397].

---

### B. Relative Contraindications (Require Pre-Procedure Optimization / Imaging)

1. **Coagulopathy & Bleeding Diathesis:** Thrombocytopenia (platelet count **<50,000/μL**), elevated INR (**>1.5**), or ongoing anticoagulant therapy (risk of spinal epidural hematoma leading to cauda equina compression) [Davidson 1 p. 408, Kdt 2 p. 397].
2. **Clinical Indications for Pre-LP CT Head Scan:** LP must be preceded by an urgent Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head scan if any of the following "red flag" features are present [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117]:
    - Focal neurological deficits (e.g., hemiparesis, cranial nerve palsy) [Davidson 1 p. 502]
    - Papilledema on fundoscopy [Davidson 1 p. 502]
    - New-onset seizures [Davidson 1 p. 502]
    - Altered level of consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale GCS **<10**) [Davidson 1 p. 502]
    - Immunocompromised state (HIV/AIDS, transplant recipients) [Davidson 1 p. 1125]
3. **Severe Cardiorespiratory Instability / Shock:** Postponed until vital signs are stabilized [Kdt 2 p. 397].

## 4. Procedure & Anatomical Layers Traversed [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Anatomical Landmarks & Patient Positioning

1. **Tuffier's Line (Intercristal Line):** An imaginary line connecting the highest points of both iliac crests crosses the spinous process of **L4** or the **L3/L4 interspace**.
2. **Safe Level of Entry:** Performed strictly at the **L3/L4 or L4/L5 interspace**.
    - _Anatomical Rationale:_ In adults, the spinal cord terminates as the conus medullaris at the **L1/L2 vertebral level** (L3 in infants). Entering below L2 ensures needle placement into the cauda equina sac where nerve roots move freely, eliminating the risk of spinal cord trauma [Kdt 2 p. 396].
3. **Patient Positioning Options:**
    - **Lateral Decubitus Position (Preferred Diagnostic Position):** Patient lies on edge of bed, curled up in "fetal position" with knees flexed to chest and chin tucked to chest. Opens up the interspinous spaces and allows accurate CSF opening pressure measurement [Kdt 2 p. 396].
    - **Sitting Position:** Patient sits on edge of bed leaning forward over a bedside table or pillow. Useful in obese patients; however, opening pressure cannot be accurately measured [Kdt 2 p. 396].

---

### B. Step-by-Step Technique & Anatomical Layers

1. **Aseptic Preparation:** Scrub lumbar skin with chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine in concentric circles; apply sterile drapes.
2. **Local Anesthesia:** Infiltrate 2 to 5 mL of **1% or 2% Lignocaine** into skin and subcutaneous tissues down to the supraspinous ligament [Kdt 2 p. 387, 396].
3. **Spinal Needle Insertion:** Insert a 22G to 25G spinal needle (Quincke cutting needle or Whitacre/Sprotte pencil-point needle) with stylet in place through the L3/L4 interspace, angled slightly cephalad (toward the umbilicus).
4. **Anatomical Layers Traversed in Order (Outward to Inward) [⭐ Exam Favorite]:**

```
Skin
  → Subcutaneous Tissue
    → Supraspinous Ligament
      → Interspinous Ligament
        → Ligamentum Flavum (Gives characteristic "Give" or "Pop" sensation)
          → Epidural Space (Epidural venous plexus)
            → Dura Mater
              → Arachnoid Mater
                → Subarachnoid Space (Target area containing CSF & Cauda Equina)
```

5. **Pressure Measurement & Sample Collection:**
    - Withdraw stylet; attach a manometer to measure **CSF Opening Pressure** (Normal: **50 to 180 mmH₂O** in lateral decubitus) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - Collect **2 to 3 mL of CSF per tube** into 3 to 4 sequential sterile, numbered tubes for:
        - _Tube 1:_ Biochemistry (Protein, Glucose) [Davidson 1 p. 8].
        - _Tube 2:_ Microbiology (Gram stain, ZN stain, India ink, Cultures, GeneXpert) [Davidson 1 p. 8, 1120].
        - _Tube 3:_ Cytology & Cell count / Differential [Davidson 1 p. 8].
        - _Tube 4:_ Viral PCR / Special serology / Oligoclonal bands [Davidson 1 p. 8, Robbins 5 p. 1274].
6. **Procedure Termination:** Reinsert stylet prior to needle withdrawal (prevents nerve root entrapment or dural tissue bridging); apply sterile pressure dressing.

## 5. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Post-Lumbar Puncture Headache (PLPH) [⭐ Most Common Complication]:**
    - **Incidence:** Occurs in **10% to 30%** of patients within 24 to 48 hours [Kdt 2 p. 396].
    - **Mechanism:** Persistent leak of CSF through the dural puncture defect into the epidural space exceeds CSF production rate, lowering CSF volume/pressure and causing mechanical traction on pain-sensitive meninges and cranial blood vessels [Kdt 2 p. 396].
    - **Clinical Features:** Classic **postural headache** (frontal/occipital throbbing headache that markedly worsens upon sitting or standing and is **rapidly relieved by lying flat**) accompanied by neck stiffness, nausea, and tinnitus.
    - **Prevention:** Use small-bore pencil-point needles (22G to 25G Whitacre/Sprotte), align cutting needle bevel parallel to dural fibers, reinsert stylet before needle removal, and maintain flat bed rest [Kdt 2 p. 396].
    - **Treatment:** Strict supine bed rest, vigorous oral/IV hydration, oral analgesics, IV caffeine, and **Epidural Blood Patch** (autologous blood injected into epidural space to seal dural hole) if persistent (>48 hours).
2. **Brain Herniation (Tonsillar / Transtentorial Herniation) [⭐ Most Fatal Complication]:**
    - Uncontrolled LP performed in the presence of raised ICP / mass lesion drops lumbar subarachnoid pressure, causing downward pressure gradient and displacement of cerebellar tonsils through foramen magnum, compressing medullary respiratory centers [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1244].
3. **Infection:** Iatrogenic pyogenic meningitis, spinal epidural abscess, or discitis due to breakdown of aseptic technique [Kdt 2 p. 396].
4. **Spinal / Epidural Hematoma & Nerve Root Trauma:** Traumatic bleeding from epidural venous plexus, particularly in coagulopathic patients, causing cauda equina syndrome or radicular pain [Kdt 2 p. 396].
5. **Traumatic Tap (Bloody Tap):** Accidental puncture of epidural venous plexus during needle insertion.
    - _Distinguishing Fact:_ In a traumatic tap, CSF blood clears progressively from Tube 1 to Tube 4, supernatant is clear after centrifugation, and no xanthochromia is seen; in SAH, blood is uniform across all tubes and xanthochromia is present [Davidson 1 p. 502].
6. **Subarachnoid Epidermoid Tumor:** Late complication caused by displacement of viable skin epidermal plugs into the subarachnoid space when LP is performed without a stylet.

## 7. Dosage Rule (Local Anesthetic for LP)

### Lignocaine / Lidocaine Hydrochloride

- **Name:** Lignocaine (Lidocaine) 1% or 2% Solution [Kdt 2 p. 387, 396].
- **Mechanism of Action:** Amide-linked local anesthetic. Blocks **voltage-gated Na⁺ channels** from the inner axoplasmic side, preventing Na⁺ influx, slowing depolarization, and blocking action potential conduction in local sensory nerve fibers [Kdt 2 p. 387].
- **Dose for LP Infiltration:** **2 to 5 mL of 1% or 2% plain Lignocaine** injected subcutaneously and into interspinous ligaments [Kdt 2 p. 387, 396].
- **Maximum Safe Single Dose:** **4.5 mg/kg** (Maximum 300 mg plain lignocaine) [Kdt 2 p. 387].
- **Route:** Subcutaneous (SC) infiltration.
- **Features:** Rapid onset (1 to 3 minutes), duration 30 to 60 minutes; excellent local analgesia [Kdt 2 p. 387].

## 8. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always reinsert the **needle stylet before withdrawing the spinal needle**! Withdrawing the spinal needle without the stylet in place can trap arachnoid mater or cauda equina nerve rootlets within the needle bevel, pulling them through the dural defect and increasing the incidence of **dural tissue bridging, nerve root trauma, and severe post-LP headache** [Kdt 2 p. 396]!

---

## 9. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Performing an immediate Lumbar Puncture in a patient with suspected bacterial meningitis who presents with a focal deficit or papilledema, vs. delaying antibiotic therapy while waiting for a CT scan.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Performing LP in the presence of raised ICP with focal mass effect triggers fatal brain herniation. However, antibiotic administration must NEVER be delayed for imaging.
- **Resolution:** If CT Head is required before LP (due to papilledema, GCS <10, or focal signs), **draw blood cultures immediately and administer empiric IV Ceftriaxone + Dexamethasone BEFORE sending the patient to the CT suite [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117]!**

## 12. Quick Revision

- **Safe Level of LP:** **L3/L4 or L4/L5 interspace** (below conus medullaris L1/L2) [Davidson 1 p. 502, Kdt 2 p. 396].
- **Landmark:** **Tuffier's Line** (intersects L4 spinous process) [Kdt 2 p. 396].
- **Normal Opening Pressure:** **50 to 180 mmH₂O** (measured in lateral decubitus) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Most Fatal Contraindication:** Raised ICP with mass effect (risk of **tonsillar brain herniation**) [Davidson 1 p. 502, Kdt 2 p. 397].
- **Most Common Complication:** **Post-LP postural headache** (prevented by small pencil-point needles and reinserting stylet) [Kdt 2 p. 396].
