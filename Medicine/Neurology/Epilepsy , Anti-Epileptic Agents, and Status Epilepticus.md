[[C25-Neurology]]
# Epilepsy , Anti-Epileptic Agents, and Status Epilepticus

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Seizure:** A brief, paroxysmal, transient episode of neurological dysfunction (motor, sensory, autonomic, or cognitive) caused by abnormal, excessive, and hypersynchronous electrical discharges of a population of cerebral cortical neurons [Kdt 1 p. 437].
- **Epilepsy:** A chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent (≥2), unprovoked seizure episodes due to underlying paroxysmal cerebral dysrhythmia [Kdt 1 p. 437].
- **Status Epilepticus [⭐ Medical Emergency]:** A life-threatening state where seizure activity persists for **>30 minutes**, or where **two or more discrete seizures occur without complete interictal recovery of consciousness between episodes** [Davidson 1 p. 186, Kdt 1 p. 450]. (Clinically, emergency treatment is initiated if continuous seizure activity lasts **>5 minutes** [Kdt 1 p. 450]).

### A. Primary / Idiopathic Epilepsy (~50–60% of cases)

- Genetic channelopathies affecting voltage-gated Na⁺, Ca²⁺, or K⁺ channels, or GABA_A receptors, creating inherent neuronal hyperexcitability without structural brain abnormalities [Kdt 1 p. 438, Robbins 5 p. 1240].

### B. Secondary / Symptomatic Epilepsy

1. **CNS Infections & Parasitic Granulomas:** **Neurocysticercosis** (_Taenia solium_ cysts; most common cause of acquired epilepsy in developing countries), tuberculoma, viral encephalitis (HSV-1), and bacterial meningitis [Kdt 4 p. 1005, Robbins 5 p. 1261, 1265].
2. **Cerebrovascular Disease:** Ischemic stroke, intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) [Robbins 5 p. 1253, 1257, 1260].
3. **Space-Occupying Lesions (SOL):** Primary brain tumors (gliomas, gangliogliomas, meningiomas) and vascular metastases [Robbins 5 p. 1272, 1293].
4. **Head Trauma:** Post-traumatic cortical scarring, depressed skull fractures, and cerebral contusions [Kdt 1 p. 451, Robbins 5 p. 1249].
5. **Metabolic & Toxic Derangements:** Severe **hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL)**, **hypocalcemia (serum Ca²⁺ <8.4 mg/dL)**, hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L), uremia, hepatic encephalopathy, alcohol withdrawal, and organophosphate poisoning [Davidson 1 p. 182, Kdt 1 p. 415, 883].

## 3. Classification of Epilepsy & Seizures (ILAE Classification) [⭐ High-Yield]

Seizures are classified based on clinical semiology and electroencephalographic (EEG) origin into **Generalized Seizures**(diffuse bilateral cortical onset) and **Focal / Partial Seizures** (unilateral localized onset) [Kdt 1 p. 437, 438]:

```

                            │        CLASSIFICATION OF SEIZURES        │
                            └────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                                 │
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   ▼                                                                                           ▼
┌──────────────────────────────┐                                            ┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ I. GENERALIZED SEIZURES      │                                            │ II. FOCAL / PARTIAL SEIZURES │
│    (Bilateral origin)        │                                            │     (Unilateral origin)      │
├──────────────────────────────┤                                            ├──────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Tonic-Clonic (GTCS)       │                                            │ 1. Simple Partial (SPS)      │
│ 2. Absence (Petit Mal)       │                                            │    (Awareness preserved)     │
│ 3. Myoclonic                 │                                            │ 2. Complex Partial (CPS)     │
│ 4. Atonic / Akinetic         │                                            │    (Awareness impaired)      │
│ 5. Infantile Spasms          │                                            │ 3. Secondarily Generalized   │
└──────────────────────────────┘                                            └──────────────────────────────┘
```

### I. Generalized Seizures (Diffuse Bilateral Brain Involvement)

1. **Generalized Tonic-Clonic Seizures (GTCS / Grand Mal):** Characterized by sudden loss of consciousness, laryngeal cry, extensor tonic rigidity (10–30s), rhythmic clonic jerking (30–60s) with lateral tongue biting, frothing, incontinence, and post-ictal sleep/confusion [Kdt 1 p. 437].
2. **Absence Seizures (Petit Mal / Minor Epilepsy):** Prevalent in children (ages 4–12); brief (5–15s) momentary loss of awareness, sudden staring, and eye blinking, without loss of postural tone. EEG pathognomonic finding: **bilaterally synchronous 3 Hz spike-and-wave pattern** [Kdt 1 p. 437, 443].
3. **Myoclonic Seizures:** Sudden, shock-like, brief involuntary contractions of muscle groups or whole body without prolonged loss of consciousness [Kdt 1 p. 437].
4. **Atonic / Akinetic Seizures (Drop Attacks):** Sudden brief loss of postural tone causing the patient to collapse instantly, carrying a high risk of head trauma [Kdt 1 p. 437].
5. **Infantile Spasms (West Syndrome):** Brief flexor or extensor spasms in infants, associated with intellectual disability and chaotic **hypsarrhythmia** on EEG [Kdt 1 p. 437, 450].

---

### II. Focal / Partial Seizures (Unilateral Localized Origin)

1. **Simple Partial Seizures (SPS):** Motor jerking (Jacksonian march) or localized sensory/autonomic disturbances **without impairment of consciousness** [Kdt 1 p. 437].
2. **Complex Partial Seizures (CPS / Temporal Lobe Epilepsy):** Starts with an aura (epigastric rising sensation, olfactory/auditory hallucinations), followed by **impairment of consciousness**, bizarre automatisms (lip smacking, chewing, swallowing), and post-ictal amnesia [Kdt 1 p. 437, 438].
3. **Partial Seizures Secondarily Generalized:** Focal seizure that spreads bilaterally across thalamocortical networks to evolve into a full generalized tonic-clonic fit [Kdt 1 p. 438].

### B. Classification Based on Mechanism of Action [KDT p. 440]

1. **Prolongation of Voltage-Gated Na⁺ Channel Inactivation:**
    - Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Sodium Valproate, Lamotrigine, Topiramate, Zonisamide, Lacosamide [Kdt 1 p. 440].
2. **Inhibition of T-type Ca²⁺ Currents (Thalamic Pacemaker):**
    - Ethosuximide, Sodium Valproate, Zonisamide [Kdt 1 p. 440, 443].
3. **Facilitation of GABA-Mediated Cl⁻ Influx (Inhibitory Enhancement):**
    - _GABA_A Allosteric Modulators:_ Barbiturates (Phenobarbitone), Benzodiazepines (Lorazepam, Diazepam, Clobazam, Clonazepam) [Kdt 1 p. 430, 440].
    - _GABA Transaminase (GABA-T) Inhibitor:_ Vigabatrin [Kdt 1 p. 440, 448].
    - _GABA Transporter-1 (GAT-1) Inhibitor:_ Tiagabine [Kdt 1 p. 440, 448].
    - _GABA Synthesis / Release Enhancers:_ Sodium Valproate, Gabapentin, Pregabalin [Kdt 1 p. 440, 446].
4. **Inhibition of Excitatory Glutamatergic Synapses / Receptors:**
    - _SV2A Binding (Inhibits Presynaptic Glutamate Exocytosis):_ **Levetiracetam** [Kdt 1 p. 440, 447].
    - _AMPA / NMDA Glutamate Receptor Blockers:_ Phenobarbitone, Topiramate, Lamotrigine, Perampanel [Kdt 1 p. 440, 447].

## 5. Pathophysiology of Status Epilepticus (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Failure of Seizure Termination Mechanisms:** Prolonged seizure activity (>5 minutes) exhausts endogenous inhibitory GABAergic mechanisms (due to GABA_A receptor endocytosis/desensitization) while upregulating presynaptic AMPA/NMDA excitatory glutamate receptors [Kdt 1 p. 440, 450].
2. **Systemic Metabolic & Autonomic Crisis (Phase 1: First 30 minutes):** Massive autonomic discharge causes severe hypertension, tachycardia, hyperthermia, hyperglycemia, and profuse diaphoresis [Kdt 1 p. 450].
3. **Systemic Decompensation & Cerebral Hypoxia (Phase 2: >30 minutes):** Autonomic exhaustion leads to hypotension, cardiac dysrhythmias, severe mixed lactic/metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalemia, cerebral edema, and elevated ICP [Kdt 1 p. 450].
4. **Excitotoxic Neuronal Death:** Uncontrolled massive intracellular Ca²⁺ influx via NMDA receptors triggers irreversible enzymatic cell death (apoptosis/necrosis) in the hippocampus (CA1/CA3), amygdala, and cerebellar cortex [Robbins 5 p. 1253, 1275].

## 6. Clinical Features of Status Epilepticus [⭐ High-Yield]

Status Epilepticus presents as a progressive multi-organ emergency:

- **Convulsive Status Epilepticus (Generalized):**
    - Persistent, continuous, unbroken tonic-clonic convulsions or discrete fits recurring rapidly without recovery of consciousness between episodes [Kdt 1 p. 450].
    - **Cyanosis & Respiratory Compromise:** Spasm of respiratory and laryngeal muscles causes asphyxia, central cyanosis, and chest congestion [Kdt 1 p. 437, 450].
    - **Hyperpyrexia:** Core body temperature rises markedly (>39 °C to 41 °C) due to continuous, violent skeletal muscle contractions [Kdt 1 p. 450].
    - **Autonomic Storm:** Initial severe hypertension and tachycardia progressing to circulatory collapse, hypotension, and fatal cardiac arrhythmias [Davidson 1 p. 182, Kdt 1 p. 450].
    - **Oral / Airway Features:** Pink frothy sputum at the lips, severe lateral tongue biting lacerations, and heavy pooling of airway secretions [Davidson 1 p. 181, Kdt 1 p. 437].
- **Non-Convulsive Status Epilepticus (NCSE):** Continuous subtle twilight state, confusion, or automatisms with continuous epileptiform discharges on EEG, requiring high index of suspicion [Davidson 1 p. 181].

## 7. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Capillary Blood Glucose [⭐ First Mandatory Bedside Test]:** Performed instantly to detect **severe hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL)**, the most rapidly reversible cause of status epilepticus [Davidson 1 p. 182, Kdt 1 p. 450].
2. **Continuous Electroencephalography (EEG):** Confirms non-convulsive status epilepticus, evaluates background suppression, and monitors burst-suppression patterns during anesthetic coma [Davidson 1 p. 181, Kdt 1 p. 450].
3. **Emergent Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head / MRI Brain:** Identifies structural triggers (acute intracranial hemorrhage, stroke, space-occupying lesions, head trauma, or neurocysticercosis) [Davidson 1 p. 186, Kdt 4 p. 1005].
4. **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) & Serum Electrolytes:** Detects severe lactic acidosis, hyponatremia (<120 mEq/L), hypocalcemia (Ca²⁺ <8.4 mg/dL), and hyperkalemia secondary to rhabdomyolysis [Davidson 1 p. 182, 358].
5. **Serum Antiepileptic Drug (AED) Levels:** Evaluates compliance or sudden withdrawal of AEDs in known epileptic patients [Kdt 1 p. 449].
6. **Lumbar Puncture (LP):** Mandatory if acute CNS infection (pyogenic or viral meningitis/encephalitis) is suspected, performed after ruling out raised ICP on CT scan [Robbins 5 p. 1261].

### Step-by-Step Emergency Protocol & Dosage Rules

#### Step 1: Initial Resuscitation Phase (0 to 5 Minutes)

1. **Airway & Positioning:** Position patient in lateral recovery position; clear airway secretions; insert nasopharyngeal airway; administer high-flow **100% Oxygen** [Kdt 1 p. 449, 450].
2. **Venous Access & Bedside Testing:** Establish two wide-bore IV lines; draw blood for emergency labs and AED levels [Kdt 1 p. 450].
3. **Hypoglycemia Correction:** Check capillary glucose instantly; inject **20 to 50 mL of 50% Dextrose IV** (along with 100 mg Thiamine IV in alcoholics) [Kdt 1 p. 450].

---

#### Step 2: First-Line Benzodiazepine Phase (5 to 20 Minutes) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Lorazepam [⭐ Drug of Choice for Status Epilepticus]

- **Name:** Lorazepam [Kdt 1 p. 430, 450].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 430, 450). High-potency benzodiazepine. Binds to the **allosteric BZD site on the GABA_A receptor-Cl⁻ channel complex**, enhancing the affinity of GABA for its receptor. Increases the **frequency of Cl⁻ channel opening**, inducing membrane hyperpolarization and rapid suppression of cortical seizure activity [Kdt 1 p. 430, 450].
    
    ```
    Lorazepam → Binds GABA_A BZD Site → ↑ Cl- Channel Opening Frequency → Hyperpolarization → Rapid Seizure Termination
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** GABA_A receptor chloride channel complex.
- **Dose:** **4 mg IV in adults (0.1 mg/kg in children)**, injected at a maximum rate of **2 mg/min**. Can be repeated once after 10 minutes if seizures persist [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) slow injection.
- **Frequency:** Single dose; repeat once after 10 minutes if needed.
- **Features:** **First choice drug for status epilepticus**; controls 75–90% of cases; produces a sustained action (6–12 hours) due to lower lipid solubility and slower redistribution compared to diazepam; lower risk of local thrombophlebitis [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Side Effects:** Respiratory depression, hypotension, sedation, ataxia, respiratory arrest [Kdt 1 p. 430, 450].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Inject immediately if seizure activity continues >5 minutes; stop once seizure terminates.

##### 2. Diazepam (First-Line Alternative)

- **Name:** Diazepam [Kdt 1 p. 430, 450].
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates GABA-mediated Cl⁻ influx via GABA_A receptors [Kdt 1 p. 430].
- **Dose:** **10 mg IV (0.2–0.3 mg/kg)** injected at **2 mg/min**. Can be repeated once after 10 minutes (maximum 100 mg/day) [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Features & Drawbacks:** Rapid onset (<2 minutes); however, anticonvulsant action wanes quickly after **20 minutes due to rapid tissue redistribution**, requiring follow-up long-acting AEDs; causes thrombophlebitis [Kdt 1 p. 430, 450].

---

#### Step 3: Second-Line Control Phase (20 to 40 Minutes) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### 1. Fosphenytoin [⭐ Preferred Second-Line Prodrug]

- **Name:** Fosphenytoin [Kdt 1 p. 439, 450].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 439, 440, 450). Water-soluble phosphate ester prodrug of phenytoin. Converted rapidly by blood phosphatases to active phenytoin, which **prolongs the inactivated state of voltage-gated neuronal Na⁺ channels**, suppressing high-frequency repetitive action potential firing [Kdt 1 p. 440, 450].
    
    ```
    Fosphenytoin → Cleaved by Phosphatases → Phenytoin → Prolongs Na+ Channel Inactivation → Suppresses Repetitive Detonation
    ```
    
- **Receptors / Ion Channels:** Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.
- **Dose:** **15 to 20 mg PE/kg (Phenytoin Equivalents) IV infusion**, administered at a rate of **100 to 150 mg PE/min**under continuous ECG and BP monitoring [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) infusion in 0.9% Normal Saline or 5% Dextrose.
- **Frequency:** Single loading dose.
- **Features:** Preferred over phenytoin because it is water-soluble (pH 7.4), does not cause tissue necrosis or purple glove syndrome upon extravasation, can be infused 3-times faster, and causes fewer cardiac arrhythmias [Kdt 1 p. 439, 450].
- **Side Effects:** Hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias, paresthesias, pruritus [Kdt 1 p. 440, 450].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Administer immediately after benzodiazepine administration, regardless of whether seizures have stopped, to establish long-term seizure control [Kdt 1 p. 450].

##### 2. Phenytoin Sodium (Alternative)

- **Dose:** **15 to 20 mg/kg IV infusion**, administered strictly at a maximum rate of **25 to 50 mg/min** in 0.9% Normal Saline under continuous ECG monitoring [Kdt 1 p. 450, 451].
- _Warning:_ Must NEVER be mixed with Dextrose solutions (causes rapid precipitation) or injected rapidly (causes severe hypotension and fatal cardiac arrhythmias) [Kdt 1 p. 450, 451].

##### 3. Phenobarbitone Sodium (Alternative Second-Line)

- **Dose:** **10 to 20 mg/kg IV injection**, administered at **50 to 100 mg/min** (maximum 1000 mg) [Kdt 1 p. 443, 451].

---

#### Step 4: Refractory Status Epilepticus Phase (>40 Minutes) [⭐ High-Yield]

If seizure activity persists despite Lorazepam + Fosphenytoin within 40 minutes, transfer the patient immediately to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU), intubate, and initiate general anesthesia [Kdt 1 p. 451]:

1. **Continuous IV Midazolam Infusion:** Loading dose **0.2 mg/kg IV slow bolus**, followed by continuous infusion at **0.05 to 2.0 mg/kg/hr** [Kdt 1 p. 451].
2. **Continuous IV Propofol Infusion:** Loading dose **1.0 to 2.0 mg/kg IV**, followed by continuous infusion at **2.0 to 10 mg/kg/hr** [Kdt 1 p. 451].
3. **IV Thiopentone Sodium:** Loading dose **3.0 to 5.0 mg/kg IV**, followed by continuous infusion at **1.0 to 5.0 mg/kg/hr** under continuous EEG monitoring targeting burst-suppression [Kdt 1 p. 425, 451].

## 10. Complications & Prognosis

1. **Excitotoxic Brain Injury:** Irreversible neuronal loss in hippocampus, leading to permanent cognitive decline and temporal lobe epilepsy [Robbins 5 p. 1253, 1275].
2. **Systemic Complications:** Severe lactic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis with acute tubular necrosis (ATN), neurogenic pulmonary edema, hyperthermia, and cardiac arrhythmias [Davidson 1 p. 182, Kdt 1 p. 450].
3. **Mortality:** Overall acute mortality ranges between **10% and 20%**, rising sharply if treatment is delayed beyond 1 hour [Kdt 1 p. 450].

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** **IV Lorazepam (4 mg at 2 mg/min)** is the single undisputed first-choice drug for terminating acute status epilepticus! Because of its lower lipid solubility compared to diazepam, Lorazepam redistributes slowly from the brain, maintaining a sustained therapeutic anticonvulsant concentration for **6 to 12 hours**(versus only 20 minutes for Diazepam) [Kdt 1 p. 450]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Phenytoin Sodium IV mixed with 5% Dextrose infusion** or injecting it rapidly (>50 mg/min).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Phenytoin is highly alkaline (pH 12) and insoluble in acidic Dextrose solutions, causing instant micro-crystallization in the IV tubing. Rapid IV injection causes severe myocardial depression, hypotension, and fatal cardiac arrest.
- **Resolution:** **Phenytoin must strictly be diluted ONLY in 0.9% Normal Saline and infused at a maximum rate of 50 mg/min [Kdt 1 p. 450, 451]!** Alternatively, use **Fosphenytoin**, which is completely compatible with Dextrose and can be infused 3-times faster (150 mg PE/min) [Kdt 1 p. 439, 450]!

- **Labels in Order:**
    1. **Voltage-Gated Na⁺ Channels:** Blocked by **Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Valproate, Lamotrigine**.
    2. **Presynaptic SV2A Protein:** Bound and inhibited by **Levetiracetam** (decreases glutamate release).

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Status Epilepticus Definition:** Seizure activity lasting **>30 minutes** or ≥2 seizures without interictal recovery [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Absence Seizure EEG Hallmark:** **Bilaterally synchronous 3 Hz spike-and-wave pattern** [Kdt 1 p. 437, 443].
- **First Choice Drug for Status Epilepticus:** **IV Lorazepam 4 mg (0.1 mg/kg) at 2 mg/min** [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Second-Line Drug:** **IV Fosphenytoin 15–20 mg PE/kg** (preferred over Phenytoin) [Kdt 1 p. 450].
- **Phenytoin Dilution Rule:** Dilute strictly in **0.9% Normal Saline ONLY** (never Dextrose) and infuse at **<50 mg/min** [Kdt 1 p. 450, 451].

> **Status epilepticus is a medical emergency defined by continuous seizure activity lasting >30 minutes or recurrent seizures without interval consciousness recovery, requiring immediate airway stabilization, prompt first-line termination with IV Lorazepam (4 mg at 2 mg/min), and long-term control using IV Fosphenytoin or Phenytoin.**
