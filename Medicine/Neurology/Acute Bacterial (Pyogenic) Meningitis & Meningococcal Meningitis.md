# Acute Bacterial (Pyogenic) Meningitis & Meningococcal Meningitis

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Acute Pyogenic (Bacterial) Meningitis** is a life-threatening, acute suppurative infection of the leptomeninges (pia mater and arachnoid mater) and the subarachnoid space filled with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), characterized by a marked neutrophilic inflammatory response, cerebral edema, and elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP) [Robbins 5 p. 1261].

**Meningococcal Meningitis:** A specific fulminant epidemic subtype of pyogenic meningitis caused by the Gram-negative diplococcus _Neisseria meningitidis_, frequently accompanied by meningococcemia, purpuric skin rash, and septic shock [Robbins 2 p. 161, Robbins 5 p. 1261].

---

1. **Neonates (<1 Month Old):** **Group B Streptococcus** (_Streptococcus agalactiae_; ~50% of cases), _Escherichia coli_(K1 capsular strain), and _Listeria monocytogenes_ [Robbins 5 p. 1261, Davidson 1 p. 1117].
2. **Infants & Young Children (1 Month to 15 Years):** **Streptococcus pneumoniae** (Pneumococcus; m/c), _Neisseria meningitidis_, and _Haemophilus influenzae type b_ (dramatically reduced in developed nations following conjugate Hib vaccination) [Robbins 5 p. 1261, Davidson 1 p. 1117].
3. **Adolescents & Young Adults (15 to 29 Years) [⭐ Meningococcal Peak]:** **Neisseria meningitidis**(Meningococcus; capsular serogroups A, B, C, W, Y); spreads via respiratory droplets in crowded environments (e.g., college dormitories, military barracks) [Robbins 2 p. 161, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
4. **Older Adults (>50 Years) & Alcoholics:** **Streptococcus pneumoniae** (m/c) and _Listeria monocytogenes_ [Robbins 5 p. 1261, Davidson 1 p. 1117].
5. **Post-Neurosurgical / Trauma / Immunocompromised:** _Staphylococcus aureus_, _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_, _Klebsiella pneumoniae_, and _Acinetobacter_ species [Robbins 5 p. 1261].

## 3. Etiopathogenesis & Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Nasopharyngeal Colonization:** Pathogenic bacteria (_N. meningitidis_ or _S. pneumoniae_) attach to nasopharyngeal epithelial cells via pili/fimbriae and IgA proteases [Robbins 2 p. 161, 162].
2. **Mucosal Invasion & Bacteraemia:** Bacteria breach the mucosal barrier, entering the bloodstream. Bacterial capsular polysaccharides resist complement-mediated lysis (membrane attack complex C5–C9) and phagocytosis [Robbins 2 p. 162].
3. **Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) Crossing:** Circulating bacteria seed the subarachnoid space by traversing cerebral capillary endothelial cells or the choroid plexus plexus epithelium [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
4. **Intense Inflammatory Cascade in CSF:** Rapid bacterial multiplication in the subarachnoid space triggers lysis, releasing **endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide LPS)** and **peptidoglycan cell wall fragments**. These induce astrocytes and microglia to release pro-inflammatory cytokines (**TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6**) [Robbins 2 p. 161, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
5. **Neutrophil Recruitment & Exudate Formation:** Cytokines upregulate endothelial adhesion molecules, causing massive transmigration of polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs) into the CSF, generating a thick, purulent subarachnoid exudate [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
6. **Vasogenic & Cytotoxic Edema + Increased ICP:** Breakdown of tight junctions increases BBB permeability (vasogenic edema), while purulent exudate impairs arachnoid villi CSF resorption (communicating hydrocephalus) and causes cerebral vasculitis/thrombosis, culminating in severe intracranial hypertension and brain herniation [Robbins 5 p. 1261].

## 4. Pathology & Morphology [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Macroscopic (Gross) Pathology

- **Engorged Leptomeninges:** Leptomeningeal vessels stand out prominently; surface of the brain is hyperemic and edematous [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Purulent Subarachnoid Exudate:** A thick, creamy, yellow-green suppurative exudate covers the brain surface within the subarachnoid space [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Anatomical Exudate Distribution Pattern [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - _Pneumococcal Meningitis:_ Exudate is densest over the **cerebral convexities near the superior sagittal sinus**[Robbins 5 p. 1261].
    - _H. influenzae Meningitis:_ Exudate is characteristically concentrated at the **base of the brain** [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
    - _Meningococcal Meningitis:_ Exudate is widespread, involving both the convexities and basal cisterns.
- **Ventriculitis:** In severe or fulminant cases, infection extends into the cerebral ventricles [Robbins 5 p. 1261].

---

### B. Microscopic Pathology

- **Neutrophilic Infiltration:** Subarachnoid space is densely packed with polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs), fibrin, and cellular debris [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Perivascular Spread:** Inflammatory infiltrate extends along Virchow-Robin spaces surrounding penetrating blood vessels [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Vasculitis & Venous Thrombosis:** Neutrophilic infiltration of leptomeningeal vein walls produces phlebitis, intimal proliferation, and secondary venous thrombosis, leading to **hemorrhagic cortical venous infarction**[Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Microvascular Identification:** Gram stain of tissue sections reveals intra- and extracellular Gram-negative coffee-bean diplococci (_N. meningitidis_) or Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci (_S. pneumoniae_) [Robbins 2 p. 160, Robbins 3 p. 185, Robbins 5 p. 1261].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Classical Symptom Triad

1. **High Fever (>38 °C to 40 °C):** Abrupt onset accompanied by shaking chills and rigors [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].
2. **Severe Generalized Headache:** Continuous, bursting headache caused by meningeal stretching and raised ICP [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].
3. **Meningism (Meningeal Irritation Signs):** Marked **nuchal rigidity (neck stiffness)**, photophobia, and phonophobia [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].

---

### B. Physical Signs of Meningeal Irritation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Nuchal Rigidity:** Inability to passively flex the neck forward to touch the chin to the chest due to painful spasm of extensor neck muscles [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Positive Kernig's Sign:** With patient supine, flex the hip and knee to 90°. Resistance or severe back/hamstring pain upon passive extension of the knee beyond 135° constitutes a positive test [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Positive Brudzinski's Sign:** Passive flexion of the neck induces involuntary, reflex flexion of the hips and knees [Davidson 1 p. 502].

### C. Specific Features of Meningococcal Meningitis

- **Petechial / Purpuric Rash:** Pathognomonic non-blanching maculopapular, petechial, or ecchymotic skin rash on trunk, lower extremities, and mucous membranes (present in 50–75% of meningococcal cases; driven by circulating endotoxin and dermal microvascular thrombosis) [Davidson 1 p. 1117, Robbins 4 p. 201].
- **Fulminant Meningococcemia (Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome):** Overwhelming septic shock, widespread purpura fulminans, Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC), and **bilateral hemorrhagic destruction of the adrenal glands**, leading to acute adrenal insufficiency and rapid death within hours [Robbins 4 p. 201, Robbins 5 p. 1261].

---

### D. Signs of Raised Intracranial Pressure & Cerebral Dysfunction

- Irritability, confusion, delirium, stupor, and deep coma [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- Repeated projectile vomiting without antecedent nausea.
- Focal or generalized tonic-clonic seizures (in 20–30% of cases) [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- Cranial nerve palsies (CN III, IV, VI, VIII) and papilledema (late feature) [Davidson 1 p. 502].

### B. Microbiological & Diagnostic Tests

1. **CSF Gram Stain:** Identifies Gram-negative kidney-bean diplococci (_N. meningitidis_), Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci (_S. pneumoniae_), or Gram-positive rods (_Listeria_) in 60–90% of untreated cases [Robbins 2 p. 160, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
2. **CSF & Blood Cultures:** Blood cultures are positive in 50–80% of bacterial meningitis cases and should be drawn immediately [Davidson 1 p. 1117].
3. **CSF Latex Agglutination & Multiplex PCR:** Detects bacterial capsular antigens (_N. meningitidis_, _S. pneumoniae_, _H. influenzae_) rapidly within 1 hour, especially if antibiotics were administered prior to LP [Davidson 1 p. 6, 1117].
4. **Computed Tomography (CT Head) [⭐ Pre-LP Rule]:** Mandatory **prior to performing Lumbar Puncture** if focal neurological deficits, papilledema, new-onset seizures, or GCS <10 are present, to rule out mass effect and prevent fatal transtentorial brain herniation [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].

### A. Immediate Resuscitation & Empirical Antibiotic Protocol

- **Rule of Gold Standard Execution:** **NEVER delay antibiotic administration for a CT scan or Lumbar Puncture!** If CT is required before LP, draw blood cultures immediately and administer IV steroids + empirical antibiotics before sending the patient for CT [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Ceftriaxone (First-Line High-Dose Parenteral Cephalosporin) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Ceftriaxone [Kdt 3 p. 778, 781].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 778, 781, 1012). Third-generation, broad-spectrum β-lactam cephalosporin. Binds covalently to **Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs)** on bacterial cell walls, inhibiting the transpeptidation enzyme step of peptidoglycan synthesis. Triggers bacterial autolytic enzymes, producing rapid cell wall breakdown and bactericidal death. Achieves high, sustained CSF concentrations across inflamed meninges.
    
    ```
    Ceftriaxone → Binds Bacterial PBPs → Inhibits Peptidoglycan Transpeptidation → Cell Wall Lysis → Bactericidal CSF Sterilization
    ```
    
- **Receptors / Targets:** Bacterial Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs).
- **Dose:** **Adults:** **2.0 g IV twice daily (12-hourly)** (or 4.0 g IV single daily dose). **Children:** 75 to 100 mg/kg/day IV divided 12-hourly [Kdt 3 p. 778, 781, 1012].
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) slow injection over 15 to 30 minutes.
- **Frequency:** 12-hourly (twice daily) or 24-hourly.
- **Features:** Drug of choice for empirical management of bacterial meningitis and specific meningococcal/pneumococcal disease; excellent CSF penetration; long half-life (t½ ~8 hours) [Kdt 3 p. 778, 781].
- **Side Effects:** Hypersensitivity rashes, pseudolithiasis (biliary sludging), diarrhea, hypoprothrombinemia, neutropenia [Kdt 3 p. 778].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate immediately upon clinical suspicion; continue for **7 days** (_N. meningitidis_), **10 to 14 days** (_S. pneumoniae_), or **21 days** (_L. monocytogenes_ / Gram-negative bacilli) [Kdt 3 p. 778, 781].

#### 2. Dexamethasone (Adjuvant Anti-Inflammatory Corticosteroid) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Dexamethasone [Kdt 1 p. 289, Davidson 1 p. 1117].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 289). Potent synthetic glucocorticoid. Binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors, translocating to the nucleus to inhibit NF-κB transcription. Suppresses synthesis of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) triggered by antibiotic-induced bacterial lysis, reducing subarachnoid neutrophilic inflammation, vasogenic edema, and ICP [Davidson 1 p. 1117].
    
    ```
    Dexamethasone → Inhibits NF-kB → Suppresses TNF-alpha & IL-1beta → Attenuates Meningeal Inflammation → Prevents Hearing Loss
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Intracellular Glucocorticoid Receptors (GR).
- **Dose:** **10 mg IV** (Children: 0.15 mg/kg IV) administered **15 to 20 minutes BEFORE or CONCURRENTLY WITH the first dose of IV antibiotic**, repeated as **10 mg IV every 6 hours for 4 days** [Davidson 1 p. 1117].
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Frequency:** Every 6 hours (4 times daily) for 4 days.
- **Features:** Significantly reduces severe neurological sequelae (especially **sensorineural hearing loss**) and mortality in _S. pneumoniae_ and _N. meningitidis_ meningitis [Davidson 1 p. 1117].
- **Side Effects:** Hyperglycemia, peptic ulceration, immunosuppression, transient hypertension [Kdt 1 p. 289].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Must be given before or with the first antibiotic dose; **do NOT start if antibiotics were initiated >4 hours prior**; stop after 4 days [Davidson 1 p. 1117].

#### 3. Specific Pathogen Targeted Regimens

- **Meningococcal Meningitis (_N. meningitidis_):** **IV Ceftriaxone 2 g IV 12-hourly** OR **Benzylpenicillin G 2.4 g (4 million units) IV 4-hourly** for 7 days [Kdt 3 p. 781, Davidson 1 p. 102].
- **Pneumococcal Meningitis (_S. pneumoniae_):** **IV Ceftriaxone 2 g IV 12-hourly + IV Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg 8 to 12-hourly** for 10 to 14 days [Kdt 3 p. 781].
- **Listeria Meningitis (_L. monocytogenes_):** **IV Ampicillin 2 g IV 4-hourly + IV Gentamicin 5 mg/kg/day** for 21 days [Davidson 1 p. 1078, Kdt 3 p. 781].

---

### C. Prophylaxis for Close Contacts of Meningococcal Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

Indicated for household contacts, healthcare workers exposed to airway secretions, and nursery contacts:

- **Rifampicin:** **600 mg PO twice daily for 2 days** (Children: 10 mg/kg PO BD for 2 days) [Davidson 1 p. 102, 1117].
- **Ciprofloxacin:** **500 mg PO single dose** (Adults) [Kdt 3 p. 111].
- **Ceftriaxone:** **250 mg IM single dose** (Preferred in pregnant contacts) [Kdt 3 p. 778].

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Acute Complications

1. **Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome:** Fulminant meningococcemia causing purpura fulminans, DIC, septic shock, and bilateral adrenal hemorrhagic necrosis [Robbins 4 p. 201, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
2. **Cerebral Edema & Herniation:** Rapid elevation of ICP causing transtentorial (uncal) or tonsillar brain herniation [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
3. **Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis & Vasculitis:** Suppurative phlebitis causing hemorrhagic cortical infarction and focal stroke deficits [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
4. **Subdural Empyema & Brain Abscess:** Localized suppurative collections [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
5. **Seizures & Status Epilepticus.**

---

### B. Chronic Long-Term Sequelae

1. **Sensorineural Hearing Loss (Deafness):** Caused by direct suppurative labyrinthitis or inflammation of CN VIII (occurs in 10–20% of pneumococcal cases) [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
2. **Obstructive / Communicating Hydrocephalus:** Leptomeningeal and arachnoid villi fibrosis (**chronic adhesive arachnoiditis**) blocking CSF flow [Robbins 5 p. 1261, 1262].
3. **Cognitive Impairment, Intellectual Deficits, & Epilepsy.**

---

## 10. Prognosis

- With prompt parenteral antibiotic and dexamethasone therapy, overall mortality is reduced to <10% for meningococcal disease, though pneumococcal meningitis carries a higher mortality (~20%) [Robbins 2 p. 162, Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- Delayed presentation (>24 hours) or presence of Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome carries >50% mortality.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always administer **Dexamethasone (10 mg IV) 15 to 20 minutes BEFORE or WITH the very first dose of IV antibiotic** in suspected pyogenic meningitis! Antibiotic-induced bacterial lysis releases massive amounts of endotoxin and peptidoglycan in the subarachnoid space, triggering an explosive inflammatory surge; pre-treating with dexamethasone blunts this cytokine wave, dramatically reducing the risk of permanent **sensorineural hearing loss (CN VIII damage) and neurological deficits** [Davidson 1 p. 1117]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Delaying parenteral antibiotic administration to perform a CT Head scan or waiting for Lumbar Puncture (LP) results in a patient with suspected bacterial meningitis.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Antibiotic delay directly increases mortality and permanent brain damage. LP can be performed safely after antibiotic administration, or CT can be done after antibiotics are running.
- **Resolution:** **NEVER delay antibiotic therapy for CT or LP!** If CT is required to rule out raised ICP before LP, **draw blood cultures immediately and give IV Ceftriaxone + Dexamethasone BEFORE sending the patient to the CT suite [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117]!**

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Classic Clinical Triad:** Fever (>38 °C), Severe Headache, and Meningism (Nuchal rigidity, Kernig's +, Brudzinski's +) [Davidson 1 p. 502, 1117].
- **Pathognomonic Meningococcal Sign:** Non-blanching **petechial or purpuric rash** on trunk and limbs [Davidson 1 p. 1117].
- **Pathognomonic CSF Profile:** Turbid CSF, **Neutrophils (PMNs >80%)**, **Elevated Protein (>100 mg/dL)**, and **Markedly Low Glucose (<40 mg/dL)** [Robbins 5 p. 1261].
- **Empirical Drug Protocol:** **IV Ceftriaxone 2 g 12-hourly + IV Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg 8–12-hourly** (plus IV Ampicillin if >50 years) [Kdt 3 p. 778, 781].
- **Critical Adjuvant Rule:** **Dexamethasone 10 mg IV** given 15–20 mins BEFORE or WITH the first antibiotic dose for 4 days [Davidson 1 p. 1117].

## 16. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Acute pyogenic (bacterial) meningitis is a life-threatening suppurative infection of the leptomeninges and subarachnoid space—predominantly caused by Neisseria meningitidis in adolescents and Streptococcus pneumoniae in adults—presenting with fever, headache, meningism, and petechial rash, diagnosed by neutrophilic pleocytosis and low CSF glucose, and managed urgently with IV Ceftriaxone and adjuvant Dexamethasone.**
