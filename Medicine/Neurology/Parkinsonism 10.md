# Parkinsonism — Etiology, Pathophysiology, Clinical Features, and Management

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Parkinsonism** is an extrapyramidal motor disorder characterized by the clinical cardinal triad of **resting tremor, rigidity, and bradykinesia (hypokinesia)**, accompanied by secondary manifestations such as posture/gait instability, mask-like facies, micrographic handwriting, and sialorrhea [Kdt 2 p. 451].

**Parkinson's Disease (PD)** is a progressive, chronic neurodegenerative disorder resulting from the selective degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta (SN-PC)**, leading to severe dopamine depletion in the striatum (caudate nucleus and putamen) [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451].

---

### A. Primary / Idiopathic Parkinson's Disease (~80% to 90% of cases)

- **Aging:** Single major risk factor; incidence increases sharply after age 60 [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451].
- **Genetic Predisposition:**
    - _Autosomal Dominant Mutations:_ **SNCA gene** (encoding α-synuclein) and **LRRK2 gene** (leucine-rich repeat kinase 2) [Robbins 5 p. 1282, 1283].
    - _Autosomal Recessive Mutations:_ **PINK1, Parkin, and DJ-1 genes** (impairing mitochondrial mitophagy and ubiquitin-proteasome degradation) [Robbins 5 p. 1282, 1283].
    - _Lysosomal Storage Gene:_ Heterozygous mutations in **GBA (glucocerebrosidase)** gene increase PD risk 20-fold [Robbins 1 p. 119, Robbins 5 p. 1283].

---

### B. Secondary (Symptomatic) Parkinsonism

1. **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism [⭐ Most Common Secondary Cause]:**
    - _D₂ Receptor Blockers:_ Typical antipsychotics (Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine) and prokinetic antiemetics (Metoclopramide, Levosulpiride) [Kdt 2 p. 451, 470].
    - _Central Monoamine Depleters:_ Reserpine, Tetrabenazine, and Methyldopa [Davidson 1 p. 6, Kdt 2 p. 451].
2. **Neurotoxin-Induced:** **MPTP (1-methyl-4-phenyl-1,2,3,6-tetrahydropyridine)**—a synthetic opioid contaminant selectively metabolized by MAO-B into MPP⁺, destroying SN-PC dopaminergic neurons; environmental pesticide exposure (rotenone, paraquat) [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451, 458].
3. **Vascular / Arteriosclerotic Parkinsonism:** Multiple lacunar infarcts involving deep basal ganglia structures [Robbins 5 p. 1255, Kdt 2 p. 451].
4. **Metabolic Disorders:** **Wilson's Disease** (chronic copper accumulation in basal ganglia/substantia nigra causing spastic dystonia and tremor) [Robbins 3 p. 124, Kdt 2 p. 451, Kdt 4 p. 1001].
5. **Post-Encephalitic & Post-Traumatic:** Sequela of von Economo's encephalitis lethargica or repetitive head trauma ("dementia pugilistica") [Robbins 5 p. 1249, Kdt 2 p. 451].

---

### C. Atypical Parkinsonian Syndromes (Parkinson-Plus) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Progressive Supranuclear Palsy (PSP):** Tauopathy characterized by truncal rigidity, frequent falls, pseudobulbar palsy, and **vertical supranuclear gaze palsy** [Robbins 5 p. 1284].
2. **Multiple System Atrophy (MSA):** Synucleinopathy featuring parkinsonism, cerebellar ataxia, and severe autonomic failure (orthostatic hypotension) [Robbins 5 p. 1284].
3. **Corticobasal Degeneration (CBD):** Asymmetric extrapyramidal rigidity with cortical signs (apraxia, alien limb phenomenon) [Robbins 5 p. 1284].

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Selective SN-PC Neuronal Degeneration:** Progressive loss of pigmented catecholaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta reduces dopamine delivery to the striatum [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451]. Symptoms emerge when **>60% to 80% of dopaminergic neurons are lost** [Kdt 2 p. 451].
2. **Striatal Neurotransmitter Imbalance:** Loss of inhibitory dopamine (DA) creates relative cholinergic (**Acetylcholine - ACh**) overactivity within striatal interneurons, disturbing motor tone coordination [Kdt 2 p. 451, 454].
3. **Basal Ganglia Circuitry Disruption:**
    - _Direct Pathway (D₁-mediated):_ Inactivated due to lack of DA stimulation → Decreased direct inhibition of internal globus pallidus (GP-i) / substantia nigra pars reticulata (SN-PR) [Kdt 2 p. 454].
    - _Indirect Pathway (D₂-mediated):_ Disinhibited due to loss of DA suppression → Increased glutamatergic drive from subthalamic nucleus to GP-i/SN-PR [Kdt 2 p. 454].
    - _Net Effect:_ Excessive GABAergic output from GP-i/SN-PR inhibits thalamocortical motor projections, blunting voluntary movement execution (bradykinesia/rigidity) [Kdt 2 p. 454].
4. **Histopathological Hallmark — Lewy Bodies [⭐ Pathognomonic Morphology]:**
    - Intracytoplasmic, single or multiple, round, eosinophilic inclusions with a dense core and pale halo in surviving SN-PC neurons [Robbins 5 p. 1283].
    - Ultrastructurally composed of aggregated **α-synuclein** filaments and ubiquitin [Robbins 5 p. 1282, 1283].

## 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Cardinal Motor Triad [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                             ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                             │        CARDINAL PARKINSONIAN TRIAD       │
                             └────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                                  │
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   ▼                                              ▼                                              ▼
┌──────────────────────────────┐               ┌──────────────────────────────┐               ┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. RESTING TREMOR            │               │ 2. RIGIDITY                  │               │ 3. BRADYKINESIA              │
├──────────────────────────────┤               ├──────────────────────────────┤               ├──────────────────────────────┤
│ • 4–6 Hz frequency           │               │ • Increased muscle tone      │               │ • Slowness of movement       │
│ • "Pill-rolling" character   │               │ • "Lead-pipe" rigidity       │               │ • Difficulty initiating motion│
│ • Present at rest; abates    │               │ • "Cogwheel" rigidity        │               │ • Micrographia, Hypophonia   │
│   with voluntary movement    │               │   (superimposed tremor)      │               │ • Mask-like facies           │
└──────────────────────────────┘               └──────────────────────────────┘               └──────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Resting Tremor ("Pill-Rolling" Tremor):**
    - Frequency of **4 to 6 Hz**; asymmetric onset in hands or fingers [Kdt 2 p. 451].
    - Alternating flexion-extension or supination-pronation movement ("pill-rolling").
    - Prominent at rest, exaggerated by emotional stress, and **disappears during voluntary movement and sleep**[Kdt 2 p. 451].
2. **Rigidity:**
    - Uniform, velocity-independent resistance to passive limb movement felt equally in flexor and extensor muscle groups (**"Lead-Pipe Rigidity"**) [Kdt 2 p. 451].
    - Superimposition of resting tremor on lead-pipe rigidity produces a ratchet-like sensation (**"Cogwheel Rigidity"**) [Kdt 2 p. 451].
3. **Bradykinesia / Hypokinesia [⭐ Most Disabling Feature]:**
    - Slowness in initiating and executing voluntary movements, with progressive decline in amplitude/speed during repetitive actions [Kdt 2 p. 451].
    - **Mask-like Facies (Hypomimia):** Unblinking, expressionless face with widened palpebral fissures [Kdt 2 p. 451].
    - **Micrographia:** Handwriting becomes progressively small, cramped, and illegible.
    - **Hypophonia & Monotonous Speech:** Soft, low-volume, slurred speech without normal cadence.

---

### B. Secondary Motor & Non-Motor Manifestations

- **Postural Instability & Festinating Gait:**
    - Stooped, flexed posture with loss of postural reflexes, predisposing to frequent forward falls [Kdt 2 p. 451].
    - **Festinating Gait:** Patient takes short, rapid, shuffling steps with diminished arm swing, appearing to chase their center of gravity [Kdt 2 p. 451].
- **Autonomic Dysfunction:** Sialorrhea (drooling due to impaired swallowing), seborrheic dermatitis (excessive oily skin secondary to dopamine deficiency), constipation, urinary urgency, and orthostatic hypotension [Robbins 4 p. 125, Robbins 5 p. 1284, Kdt 2 p. 451, 455].
- **Neuropsychiatric Features:** Depression, anxiety, sleep disturbances (REM sleep behavior disorder), anosmia (early non-motor sign), and cognitive decline progressing to **Dementia with Lewy Bodies** in 10-15% of advanced cases [Robbins 5 p. 1283, Kdt 2 p. 451].

---

## 5. Diagnosis & Evaluation

- **Clinical Diagnosis:** Primarily clinical, based on the presence of at least two of the three cardinal motor signs (resting tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia) in the absence of secondary causes, confirmed by a **dramatic symptomatic response to Levodopa challenge** [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451].
- **Neuroimaging (MRI Brain):** Normal in idiopathic PD; excludes stroke, hydrocephalus, or structural basal ganglia lesions.
- **Functional Imaging (DaTscan / SPECT):** Shows reduced presynaptic dopamine transporter binding in the striatum.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Levodopa + Carbidopa (Co-Careldopa) [⭐ Gold Standard]

- **Name:** Levodopa + Carbidopa (Co-careldopa) [Kdt 2 p. 453, 457].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 451, 453, 455, 457). Levodopa is the immediate metabolic precursor of dopamine. It crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) via the L-amino acid active transporter and is decarboxylated into dopamine by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase in surviving striatal neurons. **Carbidopa** is a peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor that **does not cross the BBB**; it prevents peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, increasing central levodopa brain entry from 1-2% to ~10%, prolonging levodopa t½, and markedly reducing peripheral side effects (nausea, vomiting, cardiac arrhythmias) [Kdt 2 p. 453, 455, 457].
    
    ```
    Oral Levodopa + Carbidopa → Carbidopa Blocks Peripheral Decarboxylation → ↑ Levodopa BBB Entry → Striatal Conversion to DA → Activates D1 & D2 Receptors
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Striatal D₁ and D₂ dopamine receptors [Kdt 2 p. 453].
- **Dose:** **Initial:** Levodopa 100 mg + Carbidopa 10 mg or 25 mg PO TDS. **Usual Maintenance:** Levodopa 400 to 800 mg/day + Carbidopa 75 to 100 mg/day given in 3 to 4 divided doses [Kdt 2 p. 457].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets / Controlled-Release (CR) tablets, taken after meals to reduce GI irritation [Kdt 2 p. 455, 457].
- **Frequency:** 3 to 4 times daily (TDS or QID).
- **Features:** Most effective antiparkinsonian therapy available; resolves hypokinesia and rigidity first, followed by resting tremor [Kdt 2 p. 451, 453].
- **Side Effects:**
    - _Early / Dose-Related:_ Nausea, vomiting (via CTZ stimulation), postural hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias [Kdt 2 p. 455, 456].
    - _Late / Long-Term (>2–5 years):_ **Dyskinesias** (facial tics, choreoathetosis), **motor fluctuations** ("wearing-off" effect, "on-off" phenomenon), and psychiatric symptoms (hallucinations, psychosis) [Kdt 2 p. 456, 461].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate when motor symptoms begin to impair daily functional activities/lifestyle [Kdt 2 p. 460]. **Never discontinue abruptly** (risk of precipitating severe rigidity and neuroleptic malignant-like syndrome) [Kdt 2 p. 461].

#### 2. Ropinirole / Pramipexole (Dopamine Agonists)

- **Name:** Ropinirole / Pramipexole [Kdt 2 p. 457, 458].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 457, 458). Non-ergoline, selective **D₂/D₃ dopamine receptor agonists**. Act directly on postsynaptic striatal dopamine receptors independently of surviving presynaptic SN-PC dopaminergic neurons [Kdt 2 p. 457].
    
    ```
    Ropinirole / Pramipexole → Direct Postsynaptic Striatal D2/D3 Activation → Bypasses Degenerated SN-PC Neurons → Smooth Motor Control
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** D₂ and D₃ dopamine receptors.
- **Dose:**
    - _Ropinirole:_ Start **0.25 mg PO TDS**, titrated weekly up to **1.0 to 2.0 mg PO TDS** (maximum 8 mg TDS) [Kdt 2 p. 458].
    - _Pramipexole:_ Start **0.125 mg PO TDS**, titrated up to **0.5 to 1.5 mg PO TDS** [Kdt 2 p. 458].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets.
- **Frequency:** Thrice daily (TDS).
- **Features:** Preferred initial monotherapy in younger patients (<60 years) to delay levodopa-induced dyskinesias; longer acting than levodopa [Kdt 2 p. 458, 460, 461].
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, postural hypotension, hallucinations, daytime sleepiness / sudden sleep attacks, and **impulse control disorders** (pathological gambling, hypersexuality, excessive shopping) [Kdt 2 p. 458].
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line in younger patients or as adjuvant to levodopa in advanced cases [Kdt 2 p. 458, 461].

#### 3. Rasagiline / Selegiline (MAO-B Inhibitors)

- **Name:** Rasagiline / Selegiline [Kdt 2 p. 458, 459, 460].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 458, 459). Selective, irreversible inhibitors of **Monoamine Oxidase-B (MAO-B)** in the brain. Retards intracerebral degradation of dopamine, prolonging dopamine action in the striatum [Kdt 2 p. 458].
    
    ```
    Rasagiline → Irreversible Central MAO-B Inhibition → Blocks Striatal Dopamine Degradation → Enhances & Prolongs Levodopa Action
    ```
    
- **Receptors / Enzymes:** MAO-B isoenzyme.
- **Dose:**
    - _Rasagiline:_ **1.0 mg PO once daily (OD)** in the morning [Kdt 2 p. 460].
    - _Selegiline:_ **5.0 mg PO twice daily (BD)** (with breakfast and lunch) [Kdt 2 p. 458].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO).
- **Frequency:** Once daily (Rasagiline) or twice daily (Selegiline).
- **Features:** Mild antiparkinsonian benefit alone in early cases; reduces "wearing-off" fluctuations when added to levodopa [Kdt 2 p. 458, 461]. Rasagiline is preferred because it is 5-times more potent and is not metabolized into amphetamine metabolites [Kdt 2 p. 460].
- **Side Effects:** Postural hypotension, nausea, insomnia (selegiline), confusion [Kdt 2 p. 459].

#### 4. Entacapone (COMT Inhibitor)

- **Name:** Entacapone [Kdt 2 p. 459, 460].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 459, 460). Selective, reversible inhibitor of **Catechol-O-Methyl Transferase (COMT)** acting in peripheral tissues. Blocks peripheral methylation of levodopa to 3-O-methyldopa (3-OMD), extending levodopa t½ and increasing brain penetration [Kdt 2 p. 459, 460].
- **Dose:** **200 mg PO administered concurrently with each dose of Levodopa-Carbidopa** (maximum 1600 mg/day) [Kdt 2 p. 460].
- **Features:** Adjuvant in advanced PD with "wearing-off" motor fluctuations; causes harmless **yellow-orange discoloration of urine** [Kdt 2 p. 460].

#### 5. Trihexyphenidyl / Benzhexol (Central Anticholinergic)

- **Name:** Trihexyphenidyl [Kdt 2 p. 453, 460].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 453, 460). Blocks central **muscarinic cholinergic receptors** in the striatum, reducing relative cholinergic overactivity caused by dopamine depletion [Kdt 2 p. 453, 460].
- **Dose:** **1.0 to 2.0 mg PO twice to thrice daily (BD/TDS)**, gradually increased up to 6–10 mg/day [Kdt 2 p. 460].
- **Features:** Most effective drug for controlling **resting tremor** and sialorrhea; **only drug class effective in drug-induced parkinsonism** [Kdt 2 p. 460].
- **Side Effects:** Dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision, memory impairment, and confusion (avoid in elderly >65 years) [Kdt 2 p. 460].

---

### B. Surgical Management (Deep Brain Stimulation)

- **Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS):** High-frequency electrical stimulation electrodes implanted into the **subthalamic nucleus (STN)** or **internal globus pallidus (GP-i)**; indicated for advanced PD patients experiencing severe levodopa-induced dyskinesias or motor fluctuations [Robbins 5 p. 1282].

---

## 8. Complications & Prognosis

- **Motor Complications:** Levodopa-induced dyskinesias (choreoathetosis), "wearing-off" end-of-dose failure, and "on-off" motor oscillations [Kdt 2 p. 456, 461].
- **Non-Motor Complications:** Severe freezing of gait, recurrent falls with hip fractures, aspiration pneumonia (terminal event), and progressive dementia [Robbins 5 p. 1282, 1283].
- **Prognosis:** Progressive disorder; pharmacological therapy significantly improves functional quality of life and extends productive survival by 5 to 10 years [Kdt 2 p. 451, 460].

---

## 9. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Central Anticholinergics (Trihexyphenidyl) are the **ONLY drugs effective for treating Drug-Induced Parkinsonism** (caused by antipsychotics like Haloperidol or Metoclopramide)! **NEVER administer Levodopa for drug-induced parkinsonism**, because the postsynaptic striatal D₂ receptors are completely blocked by the offending neuroleptic, rendering levodopa ineffective while risking severe exacerbation of psychosis [Kdt 2 p. 460, 471]!

---

## 10. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B₆)** concurrently with **Levodopa alone** (without Carbidopa).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Pyridoxal phosphate is an essential cofactor for peripheral dopa-decarboxylase. Co-administering Vitamin B₆ markedly enhances peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, drastically reducing the amount of levodopa available to cross the BBB into the brain [Kdt 2 p. 455, 456].
- **Resolution:** **Pyridoxine reverses the therapeutic effect of Levodopa alone!** However, this B₆ interaction **DOES NOT occur when Levodopa is combined with Carbidopa (Co-careldopa)**, as Carbidopa potently inhibits peripheral decarboxylase regardless of B₆ levels [Kdt 2 p. 456, 457]!

## 13. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Neurodegenerative disorder caused by loss of SN-PC dopaminergic neurons projecting to striatum [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451].
- **Cardinal Triad:** Resting tremor (4-6 Hz), Rigidity (lead-pipe/cogwheel), and Bradykinesia [Robbins 5 p. 1282, Kdt 2 p. 451].
- **Pathognomonic Inclusion:** **Lewy Bodies** (intracytoplasmic eosinophilic α-synuclein aggregates) [Robbins 5 p. 1282, 1283].
- **Gold Standard Drug:** **Levodopa + Carbidopa (Co-careldopa)**; Carbidopa blocks peripheral dopa-decarboxylase [Kdt 2 p. 453, 457].
- **Drug-Induced Treatment:** **Trihexyphenidyl** (Central Anticholinergic) [Kdt 2 p. 460].

## 14. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative extrapyramidal disorder characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and the presence of α-synuclein Lewy bodies, presenting with resting tremor, rigidity, and bradykinesia, and managed primarily with Levodopa-Carbidopa combination therapy.**
