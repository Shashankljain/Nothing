# Hepatitis A Viral Infection — Diagnostic Investigations

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Hepatitis A Virus (HAV) Infection** is a self-limited acute inflammatory liver disease caused by the non-enveloped, single-stranded RNA picornavirus, **Hepatitis A Virus** [Robbins 3 p. 238, 268]. Unlike Hepatitis B and C, HAV infection **never progresses to chronic hepatitis or a persistent carrier state** [Robbins 3 p. 241, 268].

---

## 2. Etiology & Transmission [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Virology:** Small (27 nm), non-enveloped, positive-strand ssRNA virus belonging to the _Picornaviridae_ family (_Hepatovirus_ genus) [Robbins 3 p. 238, 268].
- **Mode of Transmission:** **Fecal-oral route** via ingestion of contaminated water, food, or raw/steamed shellfish (oysters, mussels, clams) in unsanitary conditions [Robbins 3 p. 237, 241].
- **Incubation Period:** 2 to 6 weeks (average 3 to 4 weeks) [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
- **Viral Shedding:** Shed in stool for **2 to 3 weeks before and 1 week after the onset of clinical jaundice**, representing the period of peak infectivity [Robbins 3 p. 237, 241].

---

## 3. Pathophysiology & Serological Sequence (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

HAV enters hepatocytes via the **HAVcr-1 (TIM-1)** glycoprotein receptor [Robbins 3 p. 238]. The virus is not directly cytopathic; hepatocellular damage is driven by host **CD8⁺ Cytotoxic T-Lymphocytes and Natural Killer (NK) cells**recognizing viral antigens on infected hepatocytes [Robbins 3 p. 238].

## 4. Clinical Features

- **Prodromal (Pre-icteric) Phase:** Low-grade fever, severe fatigue, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, right upper quadrant abdominal pain, and loss of taste for tobacco [Robbins 3 p. 239, 271].
- **Icteric Phase:** Dark tea-colored urine, pale clay-colored stools, scleral icterus, cutaneous jaundice, and tender hepatomegaly [Robbins 3 p. 239, 271, 283].

---

### A. Specific Diagnostic Serology [⭐ Gold Standard]

1. **IgM anti-HAV (Immunoglobulin M Anti-Hepatitis A Virus Antibody) [⭐ Pathognomonic Marker]:**
    - **Clinical Significance:** The definitive **Gold Standard test** for diagnosing acute Hepatitis A infection [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
    - **Timing:** Appears simultaneously with the onset of clinical symptoms and transaminase elevation (2 to 6 weeks post-exposure) [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
    - **Duration:** Persists in high titers for **3 to 6 months** during acute illness and convalescence before declining to undetectable levels [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
2. **IgG anti-HAV (Immunoglobulin G Anti-Hepatitis A Virus Antibody):**
    - **Clinical Significance:** Indicates **past exposure, complete clinical recovery, or successful vaccination**, conferring lifelong protective immunity against re-infection [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
    - **Timing:** Replaces IgM anti-HAV during late convalescence and persists for life [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
    - _Note:_ Total anti-HAV assays measure both IgM and IgG; a positive total anti-HAV in the absence of IgM confirms immunity.

---

### B. Biochemical Liver Function Tests (LFTs) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Serum Aminotransferases (ALT & AST):**
    - **Marked Elevation (>10 to 100 times normal; typically >1000 U/L)** reflecting acute hepatocellular necrosis and membrane leakage [Robbins 3 p. 234, 271].
    - **Characteristically ALT > AST** (ALT/AST ratio >1.0) during acute hepatitis [Robbins 3 p. 282].
    - Peak enzyme levels coincide with the onset of overt clinical jaundice.
2. **Serum Bilirubin:**
    - Elevated total serum bilirubin (typically **5 to 20 mg/dL**), with a predominant rise in **conjugated (direct) bilirubin** due to impaired canalicular excretion [Robbins 3 p. 271, 283].
3. **Serum Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) & Gamma-Glutamyl Transpeptidase (GGT):**
    - Normal or mildly elevated (<3 times upper limit of normal) in typical cases [Robbins 3 p. 271].
    - Markedly elevated in the **prolonged cholestatic variant** of HAV infection [Robbins 3 p. 247].

---

### C. Coagulation & Hematological Profile

1. **Prothrombin Time (PT) / INR [⭐ Critical Prognostic Marker]:**
    - Remains normal or only slightly prolonged in uncomplicated acute HAV [Robbins 3 p. 271].
    - **Significant PT prolongation (INR >1.5)** is the single most sensitive indicator of massive liver necrosis and impending **Acute Fulminant Liver Failure** (occurs in 0.1% to 0.3% of HAV cases) [Robbins 3 p. 234, 239, 268].
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
    - Mild leukopenia with relative lymphocytosis. Atypical lymphocytes may be present on peripheral blood smear [Davidson 1 p. 24, Robbins 3 p. 271].

---

### D. Stool & Urine Examination

1. **Urinalysis:** Dark tea-colored urine caused by early **conjugated bilirubinuria** and elevated urobilinogen, appearing 1 to 5 days before visible scleral icterus [Robbins 3 p. 271].
2. **Fecal HAV Antigen / RT-PCR:** HAV virions and viral RNA detectable in stool by Reverse Transcription-Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) during late incubation (2 to 3 weeks pre-jaundice); reserved mainly for epidemiological investigations [Robbins 3 p. 237, 241].

## 7. Management & Prevention (Brief Overview)

- **Management:** Purely supportive care (rest, high-calorie nutrition, avoidance of alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs like paracetamol) as >99% resolve spontaneously [Robbins 3 p. 239, Kdt 3 p. 854].
- **Immunoprophylaxis:** Inactivated HAV vaccine (2 doses at 0 and 6–12 months IM) confers long-term protection [Robbins 3 p. 247]. Post-exposure prophylaxis with single-dose HAV vaccine or standard immune globulin (0.02 mL/kg IM) within 2 weeks of exposure [Davidson 1 p. 100].

---

## 8. Complications & Prognosis

- **Prognosis:** Excellent; >99% recover completely within 3 to 6 months with lifelong immunity [Robbins 3 p. 239].
- **Complications:** Acute fulminant liver failure (0.1–0.3%), prolonged cholestatic hepatitis, and relapsing hepatitis within 6 months [Robbins 3 p. 239, 247].

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** IgM anti-HAV appears **at the exact onset of clinical symptoms and transaminase elevation**! Therefore, a single positive **IgM anti-HAV ELISA test** in a patient presenting with acute jaundice and elevated ALT (>1000 U/L) is 100% diagnostic of acute Hepatitis A infection, requiring no additional confirmatory viral tests [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Mistaking a positive **Total anti-HAV** antibody test for active acute Hepatitis A infection.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Total anti-HAV measures both IgM and IgG antibodies. Since IgG anti-HAV persists for life after exposure or vaccination, total anti-HAV remains positive permanently in immune individuals.
- **Resolution:** **Never rely on total anti-HAV to diagnose acute infection!** Diagnosis of **ACUTE Hepatitis A strictly requires a positive IgM anti-HAV** test [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268]!

---

### Quick Revision

- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test:** **IgM anti-HAV** (positive at symptom onset, persists 3 to 6 months) [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
- **Past Exposure / Immunity Marker:** **IgG anti-HAV** (persists for life) [Robbins 3 p. 239, 268].
- **LFT Pattern:** Marked ALT and AST surge (>1000 U/L) with **ALT > AST** [Robbins 3 p. 234, 271].
- **Prognostic Marker:** **Prothrombin Time / INR >1.5** (signals fulminant liver failure) [Robbins 3 p. 234, 239].
- **Chronicity:** **Zero (0%)** chronicity or carrier state [Robbins 3 p. 241, 268].

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Hepatitis A viral infection is an enterically transmitted, self-limited acute hepatitis diagnosed definitively by the presence of serum IgM anti-HAV antibodies alongside marked transaminase elevation (>1000 U/L, ALT > AST) and conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.**
