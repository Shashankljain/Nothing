# Migraine Headache

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Migraine** is a chronic, paroxysmal, neurovascular brain disorder characterized by recurrent attacks of severe, pulsating/throbbing, usually unilateral headache lasting 4 to 72 hours. It is frequently accompanied by nausea, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia, and cutaneous allodynia, and is preceded by visual or sensory aura in ~20% of cases [Kdt 1 p. 192]. It predominantly affects young adults (ages 15 to 45 years), with a marked female preponderance (female-to-male ratio ≈3:1) [Kdt 1 p. 192].

1. **Migraine without Aura (Common Migraine):** Accounts for ~80% of cases; headache lacks focal neurological warning signs and develops directly [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Migraine with Aura (Classic Migraine):** Accounts for ~20% of cases; headache is immediately preceded by transient, reversible focal neurological symptoms (**aura**) lasting 10 to 30 minutes [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].
3. **Chronic Migraine / Status Migrainosus:** Debilitating migraine headache occurring on ≥15 days per month for >3 months, or a severe intractable attack lasting >72 hours despite therapy [Kdt 1 p. 192, 193].

## 3. Etiology / Triggers & Risk Factors in Young Adults [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Genetic Susceptibility

- Strong hereditary background; ~70% of migraine patients have a positive first-degree family history [Kdt 1 p. 192].

### B. Precipitating Triggers in Young Adults

1. **Dietary Factors:** Tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, red wine, chocolate, processed meats), caffeine withdrawal, artificial sweeteners, and prolonged fasting / missed meals [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Hormonal Fluctuations:** Estrogen withdrawal during early stages of menstruation (**menstrual migraine**) or initiation of oral contraceptive pills (OCPs) [Kdt 1 p. 192].
3. **Psychological & Lifestyle Factors:** Emotional stress, anxiety, sleep deprivation or oversleeping (weekend migraine), physical exhaustion, and dehydration [Kdt 1 p. 192].
4. **Environmental Factors:** Bright flashing lights, loud noise, strong perfumes/odors, weather changes, and prolonged screen time [Kdt 1 p. 192].

## 4. Pathophysiology (Trigeminovascular System) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Cortical Spreading Depression (CSD):** A slow, self-propagating wave of neuronal and glial cell depolarization across the cerebral cortex, responsible for the initial visual aura and release of inflammatory mediators [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Trigeminovascular Activation:** CSD stimulates primary sensory nerve afferents of the **trigeminal ganglion (V₁ ophthalmic division)** innervating dural and meningeal blood vessels [Kdt 1 p. 192, 555].
3. **Neuropeptide Release & Neurogenic Inflammation:** Activated trigeminal nerve endings release potent vasodilator neuropeptides into perivascular spaces:
    - **Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP)** [Kdt 1 p. 192, 555]
    - **Substance P (SP)** and **Neurokinin A** [Kdt 1 p. 192, 554]
4. **Perivascular Vasodilation & Plasma Protein Extravasation:** CGRP causes intense dilatation of cranial blood vessels and carotid arterio-venous shunts, while Substance P induces plasma protein extravasation, mast cell degranulation, and perivascular neurogenic edema [Kdt 1 p. 192, 555].
5. **Serotonergic (5-HT) Dysregulation:** Initial surging release of 5-HT from cerebral platelets causes vasoconstriction, followed by rapid renal excretion (5-HIAA), leading to systemic 5-HT depletion and secondary, uncontrolled cranial vessel dilation [Kdt 1 p. 188, 192].
6. **Central Sensitization:** Continuous nociceptive input to the trigeminal nucleus caudalis in the brainstem lowers pain thresholds, causing hyperalgesia and cutaneous allodynia [Kdt 1 p. 192].

### A. Phase 1: Prodrome (Occurs 24 to 48 Hours Before Attack)

- Unexplained fatigue, repetitive yawning, food cravings (chocolate/sweets), irritability, depression, or neck stiffness [Kdt 1 p. 192].

---

### B. Phase 2: Aura (Lasts 10 to 30 Minutes; Absent in Common Migraine) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Visual Aura [Most Common]:**
    - **Scintillating Scotoma:** A expanding area of blindness surrounded by shimmering, glittering lights [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].
    - **Fortification Spectra (Teichopsia):** Zig-zag lines resembling the wall outline of a medieval fortress [Davidson 1 p. 1010].
    - Flashes of light (photopsia), blurred vision, or micropsia/macropsia ("Alice in Wonderland" syndrome) [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Sensory & Motor Aura:** Unilateral paresthesias (tingling spreading from fingers to face), numbness, or mild dysphasia [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].

---

### C. Phase 3: Headache Phase (Lasts 4 to 72 Hours) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Pain Character:** Typically **unilateral** (frontotemporal or retro-orbital, though it can alternate sides between attacks) and **pulsating / throbbing** in quality [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Intensity:** Moderate to severe; noticeably **aggravated by routine physical activity** (walking, climbing stairs) [Kdt 1 p. 192].
3. **Associated Symptoms:**
    - **Gastric Stasis, Nausea, and Vomiting:** Gastric tone and peristalsis are markedly reduced, delaying oral drug absorption [Kdt 1 p. 192, 558].
    - **Hypersensitivity:** Marked **photophobia** (intolerance to light) and **phonophobia** (intolerance to sound), forcing the patient to seek a dark, quiet room to lie flat [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].
    - **Cutaneous Allodynia:** Non-painful stimuli (touching hair, brushing, wearing glasses) feel painful due to central sensitization [Kdt 1 p. 192].

---

### D. Phase 4: Postdrome (Recovery Phase)

- Feeling drained, fatigued, confused, or washed out, accompanied by scalp tenderness and mild muscle aches lasting 24 hours [Kdt 1 p. 192].

## 6. Diagnosis & Diagnostic Criteria

### Diagnostic Evaluation

- **Clinical Diagnosis:** Diagnosis is strictly clinical based on patient history and physical examination [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].
- **ICHD-3 Diagnostic Criteria for Migraine without Aura:**
    - At least **5 attacks** fulfilling the following criteria:
        1. Headache attacks lasting **4 to 72 hours** [Kdt 1 p. 192].
        2. Headache has at least **2 of the following 4 characteristics**:
            - Unilateral location [Kdt 1 p. 192]
            - Pulsating / throbbing quality [Kdt 1 p. 192]
            - Moderate or severe pain intensity [Kdt 1 p. 192]
            - Aggravation by or causing avoidance of routine physical activity [Kdt 1 p. 192]
        3. During headache at least **1 of the following**:
            - Nausea and/or vomiting [Kdt 1 p. 192]
            - Photophobia and phonophobia [Kdt 1 p. 192]
- **Neuroimaging (MRI / CT Brain):** Indicated only in the presence of "red flag" features (sudden thunderclap onset, focal neurological deficits, onset >50 years, or papilledema) to exclude organic intracranial lesions [Davidson 1 p. 1010].

### A. General & Non-Pharmacological Measures

- **Trigger Avoidance:** Maintain a headache diary to identify and eliminate personal triggers (specific foods, irregular sleep, stress) [Kdt 1 p. 192, 194].
- **Acute Attack Rest:** Rest in a quiet, darkened, well-ventilated room with a cold compress applied to the forehead [Davidson 1 p. 1010, Kdt 1 p. 192].

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Sumatriptan [⭐ Gold Standard First-Line Specific Antimigraine Drug]

- **Name:** Sumatriptan [Kdt 1 p. 193, 194].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 187, 193, 194). Selective agonist at **5-HT₁B and 5-HT₁D receptors**.
    
    - Activates postjunctional **5-HT₁B receptors** on dilated cranial blood vessels and carotid arterio-venous shunts, causing selective vasoconstriction [Kdt 1 p. 187, 193, 194].
    - Activates presynaptic **5-HT₁D autoreceptors** on trigeminal nerve endings, inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides (**CGRP**, Substance P) and attenuating neurogenic inflammation and plasma protein extravasation [Kdt 1 p. 187, 193, 194].
    
    ```
    Sumatriptan → Activates 5-HT1B Receptors (Cranial Vasoconstriction) + Activates 5-HT1D Receptors (Blocks CGRP Release) → Rapid Relief of Migraine
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** 5-HT₁B and 5-HT₁D receptors [Kdt 1 p. 187, 193, 194].
- **Dose:** **Oral:** **50 to 100 mg PO** taken at the onset of headache; may repeat once after 2 hours if pain recurs (maximum 200 mg in 24 hours) [Kdt 1 p. 194]. **Subcutaneous (SC):** **6 mg SC** (acts within 10–20 minutes) [Kdt 1 p. 194]. **Nasal Spray:** **25 mg per nostril** [Kdt 1 p. 194].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablet / Subcutaneous (SC) injection / Nasal spray [Kdt 1 p. 194].
- **Frequency:** Single dose at onset; may repeat once after 2 to 4 hours if necessary [Kdt 1 p. 194].
- **Features:** Drug of choice for acute moderate-to-severe migraine attacks; suppresses accompanying nausea and vomiting (unlike ergotamine) [Kdt 1 p. 193, 194].
- **Side Effects:** Chest tightness/heaviness, feeling of heat, flushing, dizziness, tingling in limbs, coronary vasospasm, and mild rise in blood pressure [Kdt 1 p. 194].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Take immediately at the onset of headache phase; **contraindicated in ischemic heart disease (angina, MI), uncontrolled hypertension, peripheral vascular disease, and pregnancy** [Kdt 1 p. 194]. Do NOT co-administer with ergotamine within 24 hours [Kdt 1 p. 194].

#### 2. Ergotamine + Caffeine [⭐ Second-Line Antimigraine Combination]

- **Name:** Ergotamine Tartrate (+ Caffeine) [Kdt 1 p. 190, 191, 193].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 190, 191, 193, 516). Partial agonist at **5-HT₁A/1B/1D receptors**, α-adrenergic receptors, and dopamine receptors [Kdt 1 p. 190, 193]. Produces sustained constriction of dilated cranial arteries and carotid A-V shunts [Kdt 1 p. 190, 193]. **Caffeine (100 mg)** enhances oral absorption of ergotamine and adds synergistic cranial vasoconstriction by blocking adenosine A₂A receptors [Kdt 1 p. 193, 516].
- **Dose:** **1 to 2 mg PO/sublingual** at onset, repeated 1 mg every 30 minutes until pain is relieved (maximum 6 mg per attack / 10 mg per week) [Kdt 1 p. 191, 193].
- **Features & Drawbacks:** Inexpensive; however, accentuates nausea/vomiting, carries a risk of ergotism (peripheral ischemia/gangrene), and can cause **rebound medication-overuse headache** upon chronic use [Kdt 1 p. 191, 193].

#### 3. Metoclopramide / Domperidone (Adjuvant Antiemetic)

- **Name:** Metoclopramide / Domperidone [Kdt 1 p. 192, 558, 710].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 558, 710). Blocks central and peripheral dopamine D₂ receptors. Reverses migraine-induced gastric stasis, accelerates gastric emptying, and restores oral absorption of co-administered analgesics and triptans [Kdt 1 p. 192, 558].
- **Dose:** **Metoclopramide 10 mg PO/IV** or **Domperidone 10 to 20 mg PO** given 15 minutes before oral antimigraine medications [Kdt 1 p. 192, 238].

---

### C. Prophylactic Management Protocols (For Chronic / Frequent Attacks) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications for Prophylaxis:** Recommended when patients experience **≥2 to 3 disabling attacks per month**, when acute attacks fail to respond to triptans, or when triptans are contraindicated [Kdt 1 p. 194].
- **Goal:** Reduce frequency, severity, and duration of migraine attacks by ≥50% [Kdt 1 p. 194, 195].

#### 1. Propranolol [⭐ First-Line Drug for Migraine Prophylaxis]

- **Name:** Propranolol [Kdt 1 p. 164, 195].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 164, 195). Non-selective **β₁ and β₂ adrenergic receptor blocker**. Reduces cerebral vasodilatory responsiveness, inhibits catecholamine-induced cranial vasodilation, and decreases central cortical hyperexcitability [Kdt 1 p. 164, 195].
- **Dose:** Start with **40 mg PO twice daily (BD)**, gradually increased up to **80 to 160 mg PO BD** as tolerated [Kdt 1 p. 195].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets [Kdt 1 p. 195].
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (BD).
- **Features:** Most widely used and effective prophylactic agent; reduces attack frequency in up to 70% of patients [Kdt 1 p. 195]. Therapeutic benefit manifests after 4 weeks of continuous use [Kdt 1 p. 195].
- **Side Effects:** Fatigue, bradycardia, hypotension, cold extremities, sleep disturbances / vivid dreams, and **bronchospasm in asthmatics** [Kdt 1 p. 164, 195].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate for frequent attacks; continue for 6 months before assessing tapering [Kdt 1 p. 194, 195]. **Contraindicated in bronchial asthma, AV block, and severe bradycardia** [Kdt 1 p. 164].

#### 2. Alternative Prophylactic Agents

- **Amitriptyline (Tricyclic Antidepressant):** **25 to 50 mg PO at bedtime (HS)**; excellent for patients with co-existing depression, tension headache, or insomnia [Kdt 1 p. 195, 492].
- **Flunarizine (Ca²⁺ Channel Blocker):** **5 to 10 mg PO once daily (OD)** at night; cerebro-selective Ca²⁺ channel blocker that reduces intracellular Ca²⁺ overload [Kdt 1 p. 195]. Causes sedation and weight gain [Kdt 1 p. 195].
- **Topiramate / Sodium Valproate (Anticonvulsants):** **Topiramate 25 to 50 mg PO BD** or **Valproic acid 400 to 1200 mg/day PO**; reduces cortical spreading depression [Kdt 1 p. 195, 448].
- **Erenumab (CGRP Receptor Monoclonal Antibody) [⭐ Novel Specific Agent]:**
    - **Mechanism:** Fully human monoclonal antibody that selectively blocks the **CGRP receptor** [Kdt 1 p. 195, 555].
    - **Dose & Route:** **70 mg to 140 mg SC once monthly** via prefilled auto-injector [Kdt 1 p. 195, 555].

## 9. Complications

1. **Status Migrainosus:** Debilitating migraine attack lasting >72 hours, requiring hospitalization, IV hydration, IV antiemetics, and parenteral steroids [Kdt 1 p. 192].
2. **Medication-Overuse Headache (MOH / Rebound Headache):** Chronic daily headache resulting from frequent, overuse of simple analgesics (>15 days/month) or triptans/ergots (>10 days/month) [Kdt 1 p. 192, 193].
3. **Migrainous Infarction (Stroke):** Ischemic brain injury occurring during a prolonged migraine aura, confirmed on neuroimaging [Davidson 1 p. 1010].
4. **Persistent Aura Without Infarction:** Aura symptoms persisting for >1 week without evidence of brain infarction on MRI [Davidson 1 p. 1010].

---

## 10. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always co-prescribe a prokinetic antiemetic like **Metoclopramide (10 mg PO/IV)** alongside oral analgesics or triptans during an acute migraine attack! Migraine causes severe hypomotility and **gastric stasis**, which severely delays or abolishes the intestinal absorption of oral antimigraine tablets; metoclopramide reverses gastric stasis and accelerates drug absorption, markedly hastening pain relief [Kdt 1 p. 192, 558]!

---

## 11. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing **Propranolol** for acute termination of an ongoing migraine attack, or co-administering **Sumatriptan and Ergotamine within 24 hours**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Propranolol is purely a prophylactic agent used to prevent future attacks over weeks; it has no acute abortive value. Co-administering triptans and ergotamine causes additive, severe vasospasm.
- **Resolution:** **Propranolol is strictly for long-term PROPHYLAXIS only!** For acute attack termination, use **Sumatriptan** or NSAIDs, and **NEVER combine Sumatriptan with Ergotamine within a 24-hour window [Kdt 1 p. 194, 195]!**

## 14. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Paroxysmal neurovascular headache lasting 4 to 72 hours, unilateral, throbbing, with photo/phonophobia [Kdt 1 p. 192].
- **Key Pathophysiological Mediator:** **Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP)** released from trigeminal afferents causing dural neurogenic inflammation [Kdt 1 p. 192, 555].
- **Gold Standard Acute Drug:** **Sumatriptan** (50–100 mg PO / 6 mg SC); 5-HT₁B/1D agonist [Kdt 1 p. 193, 194].
- **Gold Standard Prophylactic Drug:** **Propranolol** (40–160 mg/day PO); non-selective β-blocker [Kdt 1 p. 164, 195].
- **Novel Targeted Prophylactic:** **Erenumab** (70–140 mg SC once monthly); anti-CGRP receptor monoclonal antibody [Kdt 1 p. 195, 555].
