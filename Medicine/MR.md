# Mitral Regurgitation — Etiology and Clinical Features

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Mitral Regurgitation (MR)**—also termed **Mitral Incompetence** or **Mitral Insufficiency**—is a valvular heart disorder characterized by the abnormal retrograde backflow of blood from the Left Ventricle (LV) into the Left Atrium (LA) during ventricular systole [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 557].

It results from structural breakdown or functional impairment of one or more of the five anatomical components of the **mitral valve apparatus**:

1. Valve leaflets (anterior and posterior)
2. Mitral valve annulus
3. Chordae tendineae
4. Papillary muscles (anterolateral and posteromedial)
5. Underlying left ventricular myocardium [Robbins p. 557, 560].

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

The etiology of mitral regurgitation is broadly categorized into **Primary (Organic)** causes (intrinsic structural disease of leaflets or chordae) and **Secondary (Functional)** causes (secondary to left ventricular remodeling, dilation, or ischemic dysfunction) [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 557]. Furthermore, it is classified by clinical acuity into **Acute** and **Chronic**MR:

### A. Primary (Organic) Causes

1. **Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP / Myxomatous Degeneration) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - The single most common cause of primary MR in developed countries [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 559].
    - Characterized by myxomatous expansion of the spongiosa layer and attenuation of the fibrosa layer, creating enlarged, redundant, "floppy" leaflets that billow into the left atrium during systole.
    - Can occur sporadically or in association with connective tissue disorders such as **Marfan's Syndrome**(FBN1 mutation) or Ehlers-Danlos syndrome [Davidson p. 508, Robbins p. 559].
2. **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - The predominant cause of MR in developing nations (including the Indian subcontinent and Africa) [Davidson p. 525, Robbins p. 560].
    - Recurrent post-inflammatory carditis leads to fibrous thickening, cuspal deformity, shortening of chordae tendineae, and fusion of commissures, preventing complete systolic leaflet apposition [Robbins p. 561].
3. **Infective Endocarditis (IE):**
    - Causes **acute severe MR** via bacterial erosion, cusp perforation, or destruction of chordae tendineae and valve leaflets [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 563].
4. **Mitral Annular Calcification (MAC):**
    - Degenerative calcific deposits along the fibrous mitral annulus in elderly patients (more common in females and hypertensives), interfering with normal systolic annular sphincter-like contraction [Robbins p. 559].
5. **Rupture of Chordae Tendineae:**
    - May occur spontaneously in floppy myxomatous valves, secondary to infective endocarditis, or following blunt chest trauma [Davidson p. 526].

### B. Secondary (Functional) Causes

1. **Ischemic Heart Disease (IHD) & Post-MI Papillary Muscle Dysfunction:**
    - **Acute Papillary Muscle Rupture:** Occurs 3 to 7 days post-MI (most commonly posteromedial papillary muscle in inferior MI due to single-blood-supply from RCA), causing catastrophic, acute, severe MR [Davidson p. 496, Robbins p. 546].
    - **Chronic Ischemic MR:** Localized LV wall motion abnormalities and post-MI scar tissue displace papillary muscles outward, causing tethering of structurally normal valve leaflets [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 546].
2. **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) & LV Dilation:**
    - Global left ventricular enlargement distorts papillary muscle geometry and dilates the mitral ring (annular dilation), preventing normal leaflet coaptation during systole [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 568].

## 3. Classification & Hemodynamic Comparison [⭐ High-Yield]

Mitral Regurgitation presents in two distinct clinical and pathological forms based on the rapidity of onset:

|Feature|Acute Mitral Regurgitation|Chronic Mitral Regurgitation|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Onset / Duration**|Abrupt (minutes to hours)|Insidious (years to decades)|
|**Common Causes**|Papillary muscle rupture post-MI, infective endocarditis, acute chordal rupture|Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP), Rheumatic Heart Disease, Dilated Cardiomyopathy|
|**Left Atrial Size & Compliance**|Normal LA size, **Low LA compliance**|**Massively dilated LA**, High LA compliance|
|**Left Atrial Pressure**|**Precipitous, extreme rise in LAP** (large 'v' waves)|Mild to moderate rise in LAP (LA accommodates volume)|
|**Clinical Presentation**|**Acute pulmonary edema & cardiogenic shock**|Slowly progressive exertional dyspnea, fatigue, Atrial Fibrillation|
|**Surgical Urgency**|Emergency surgical intervention required|Elective repair/replacement based on symptoms, LVEF, and LV dimensions|

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Systolic Regurgitation:** During ventricular systole, blood ejected from the Left Ventricle divides into two pathways: forward stroke volume into the high-resistance aorta, and regurgitant volume backward into the low-pressure Left Atrium [Davidson p. 526].
2. **Left Atrial & Left Ventricular Volume Overload:** During diastole, the regurgitant volume plus normal pulmonary venous return flows back into the LV. This chronic **volume overload** causes progressive LV dilation [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 557].
3. **Eccentric Hypertrophy:** To accommodate the large end-diastolic volume and maintain forward stroke volume, the LV undergoes compensatory **eccentric hypertrophy** (addition of sarcomeres in series) [Robbins p. 542].
4. **Left Atrial Dilation & Atrial Fibrillation:** Chronic volume load drives left atrial stretch, hypertrophy, and massive LA dilation. LA dilation disrupts atrial architecture, predisposing to **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)** and intra-atrial mural thrombus formation [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 556].
5. **Decompensation & Heart Failure:** Over time, progressive myocyte damage and interstitial fibrosis reduce LV contractility. Left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) rises, backing up into pulmonary veins, causing **pulmonary venous congestion**, reactive pulmonary arterial hypertension, and secondary right heart failure [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 542].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Chronic Mitral Regurgitation

- **Asymptomatic Phase:** Patients with chronic MR remain completely asymptomatic for many years or decades due to effective compensatory LV dilation and eccentric hypertrophy [Davidson p. 526].
- **Fatigue & Easy Fatigability:** Early symptom caused by reduced forward cardiac output during exertion [Davidson p. 526].
- **Exertional Dyspnea:** Gradually progressive breathlessness on exertion as elevated LVEDP is transmitted backward into the pulmonary vasculature [Davidson p. 526].
- **Orthopnea & Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND):** Occurs as LV decompensation advances and pulmonary venous congestion worsens [Davidson p. 526].
- **Palpitations:** Secondary to paroxysmal or permanent **Atrial Fibrillation** or premature atrial/ventricular ectopic beats [Davidson p. 526].
- **Right Heart Failure Symptoms:** Ankle edema, right hypochondrial discomfort (hepatic congestion), and abdominal distension (ascites) late in the disease [Davidson p. 526].

#### 2. Acute Mitral Regurgitation

- Presents as a sudden, catastrophic cardiovascular emergency with **acute severe dyspnea, flash pulmonary edema, hypotension, and cardiogenic shock** [Davidson p. 496, 526].

#### 1. General & Peripheral Examination

- **Arterial Pulse:** Sharp, brisk, hyperdynamic pulse with a rapid upstroke (because the LV empties rapidly into two outlets during systole: the low-pressure LA and the aorta) [Davidson p. 442, 526]. Pulse is **irregularly irregular** if Atrial Fibrillation has developed [Davidson p. 442].
- **Jugular Venous Pulse (JVP):** Normal initially; elevated JVP with prominent 'v' wave if pulmonary hypertension and secondary tricuspid regurgitation develop [Davidson p. 443, 526].

#### 2. Precordial Inspection & Palpation

- **Apex Beat Location:** Displaced laterally and inferiorly (e.g., 6th intercostal space outside the mid-clavicular line) due to left ventricular enlargement [Davidson p. 442, 526].
- **Apex Beat Character:** Hyperdynamic, forceful, and **"rocking"** in character, representing LV volume overload (contrasts with the discrete, pressure-overload heaving apex of aortic stenosis) [Davidson p. 442, 526].
- **Systolic Thrill:** Palpable high-frequency thrill at the apex in severe organic MR (patient in left lateral position) [Davidson p. 442].
- **Parasternal Heave:** Left parasternal heave indicating Right Ventricular Hypertrophy if secondary pulmonary hypertension is present [Davidson p. 442].

#### 3. Auscultation (Classic Signs) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **First Heart Sound (S1):** **Soft or absent S1** at the apex, because impaired leaflet apposition prevents forceful S1 closure [Davidson p. 450, 526].
2. **Pansystolic (Holosystolic) Murmur [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Timing:** High-pitched, blowing pansystolic murmur that begins immediately with S1 and extends throughout systole up to S2 (often obscuring S2) [Davidson p. 450, 526].
    - **Location:** Best heard at the cardiac **apex** using the diaphragm of the stethoscope with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position [Davidson p. 450, 526].
    - **Radiation:** Characteristically radiates into the **left axilla** and infrascapular region (high-velocity jet direction) [Davidson p. 450, 526].
    - **Dynamic Changes:** Intensity increases with handgrip (increased afterload) and leg raise; unchanged on inspiration (distinguishes from Tricuspid Regurgitation).
3. **Mid-Systolic Click + Late Systolic Murmur (in MVP):**
    - Floppy prolapsing leaflets snap back into the LA in mid-systole (producing a high-pitched **mid-systolic click**), followed by a late systolic regurgitant murmur [Davidson p. 450, 526, Robbins p. 560].
4. **Third Heart Sound (S3) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - A prominent **apical S3** is present in severe MR, occurring in early diastole due to abrupt deceleration of massive blood volume entering the dilated LV from the LA [Davidson p. 450, 526].
5. **Short Mid-Diastolic Flow Murmur:**
    - A short, low-pitched mid-diastolic flow murmur may follow S3 at the apex, caused by hyperdynamic increased blood flow across the mitral orifice during early diastole (does not indicate coexisting mitral stenosis) [Davidson p. 450, 526].

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **Left Atrial Enlargement (P Mitrale):** Broad, notched P wave in lead II (>0.12 sec) and biphasic P wave in V1 [Davidson p. 449, Marrow Vol 2 p. 383].
- **Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH):** Tall R waves in V5–V6 and deep S waves in V1–V2 (Sokolow-Lyon voltage criteria >35 mm) with left axis deviation [Davidson p. 449, Marrow Vol 2 p. 383].
- **Atrial Fibrillation:** Total absence of P waves with irregular QRS intervals [Davidson p. 452, Marrow Vol 2 p. 404].

### B. Chest X-Ray

- **Cardiomegaly:** Marked cardiac enlargement due to combined **Left Ventricular and Left Atrial dilation**[Davidson p. 451, 526].
- **Left Atrial Enlargement Signs:** Double density shadow along the right cardiac border, straightening of the left cardiac border, and subcarinal angle widening (>90°) [Davidson p. 451].
- **Pulmonary Venous Congestion:** Hazy perihilar "bat-wing" alveolar shadowing and Kerley B lines in acute/decompensated MR [Davidson p. 451, 526].

### C. Echocardiography & Doppler (Gold Standard Investigation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):**
    - Defines structural etiology (flail leaflet, prolapse, chordal rupture, MAC, or rheumatic thickening) [Davidson p. 526].
    - Measures LV end-systolic diameter (LVESD), LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), and Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) [Davidson p. 526].
- **Color Flow Doppler:**
    - Visualizes the regurgitant jet area into the LA, measures **vena contracta width (≥7 mm indicates severe MR)**, and calculates effective regurgitant orifice area (EROA ≥0.4 cm²) [Davidson p. 451, 526].
- **Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE):**
    - Gold standard for precise anatomical mapping prior to surgical repair and excluding vegetations/thrombi [Davidson p. 451, 526].

### D. Cardiac Catheterisation

- Demonstrates prominent **giant 'v' waves** in the Pulmonary Artery Wedge Pressure (PAWP) or LA pressure tracing during systole [Davidson p. 526].

### A. Medical Management

- **Diuretics (Furosemide 20–80 mg PO/IV):** Reduces intravascular volume, relieving dyspnea and pulmonary congestion [Davidson p. 526, KDT p. 627].
- **Vasodilators / Afterload Reductions (ACE Inhibitors - Enalapril 2.5–10 mg BD):** Decreases systemic vascular resistance, enhancing forward stroke volume and reducing regurgitant volume [Davidson p. 526, KDT p. 562].
- **Rate Control & Anticoagulation in AF:** Beta-blockers (Metoprolol) or Digoxin for rate control, plus **Warfarin (INR 2.0–3.0)** for stroke prevention [Davidson p. 526].

### B. Surgical Management (Definitive Treatment)

- **Mitral Valve Repair (Annuloplasty Ring + Leaflet/Chordal Repair):** Treatment of choice over replacement due to lower operative mortality, preserved LV function, and no requirement for long-term anticoagulation [Davidson p. 526].
- **Mitral Valve Replacement (MVR):** Indicated when valve leaflets are severely destroyed or calcified [Davidson p. 526].
- **Class I Surgical Indications:**
    1. Symptomatic severe primary MR [Davidson p. 526].
    2. Asymptomatic severe primary MR with **LVEF ≤60%** or **LVESD ≥40 mm** [Davidson p. 526].
    3. New-onset Atrial Fibrillation or secondary pulmonary hypertension (PASP >50 mmHg) [Davidson p. 526].

## 9. Complications

1. **Left Ventricular Failure (HFrEF):** Irreversible myocyte exhaustion from chronic volume overload [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 542].
2. **Atrial Fibrillation (AF):** Occurs in >40% of chronic MR cases secondary to left atrial stretch [Davidson p. 526].
3. **Systemic Thromboembolism & Stroke:** Caused by mural thrombi forming in dilated LA during AF [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 556].
4. **Infective Endocarditis:** Turbulent regurgitant jets damage endocardium, predisposing to bacterial seeding [Davidson p. 526, Robbins p. 563].
5. **Pulmonary Hypertension & Cor Pulmonale:** Passive pulmonary venous congestion leading to reactive arteriolar remodeling and right heart failure [Davidson p. 526].

---

## 10. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always distinguish the **Pansystolic Murmur of Mitral Regurgitation** from **Tricuspid Regurgitation** using **Carvallo's Sign**!
> 
> - On deep inspiration, negative intrathoracic pressure increases venous return to the right heart, **accentuating the murmur of Tricuspid Regurgitation (Positive Carvallo Sign)**.
> - The murmur of **Mitral Regurgitation remains unchanged or decreases** during inspiration!

---

## 11. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Relying solely on the **intensity of the pansystolic murmur** to gauge the severity of Mitral Regurgitation.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Murmur intensity correlates poorly with regurgitant volume (a soft murmur can occur in severe acute MR due to rapid equalization of LV and LA pressures).
- **Resolution:** True physical examination markers of **SEVERE Mitral Regurgitation** are:
    1. A displaced, hyperdynamic, "rocking" apex beat
    2. A soft or absent S1
    3. A prominent **Apical Third Heart Sound (S3)**
    4. A short mid-diastolic flow murmur following S3

- **R** — **R**ocking, hyperdynamic displaced apex beat

- **R** — **R**apid, hyperdynamic pulse / **R**isk of Atrial Fibrillation
- **G** — **G**allop rhythm: Prominent **S3** sound + **Soft S1**

Visualize walking through 4 stations at a grand garden fountain:

1. **Loci 1 (The Leaking Back-Valve):** Water spraying backward from a high-pressure pump into a soft upper basin (**Systolic regurgitation from LV into LA**).
2. **Loci 2 (The Rocking Base):** The heavy stone base of the fountain rocking vigorously side to side (**Hyperdynamic, rocking apex beat**).
3. **Loci 3 (The Blowing Axilla Jet):** A high-pressure water jet blowing sideways into an armpit-shaped alcove (**Blowing pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla**).
4. **Loci 4 (The Third Splash):** A heavy third splash sounding right after the main valve shuts (**Prominent S3 gallop sound**).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Retrograde systolic blood flow from LV to LA due to mitral valve incompetence.
- **Most Common Causes:** Mitral Valve Prolapse (developed nations), Rheumatic Heart Disease (developing nations), Post-MI Papillary Muscle Rupture (acute severe MR).
- **Classic Auscultation Quadrad:** Soft S1 + High-pitched blowing Pansystolic Murmur radiating to axilla + Prominent apical S3 + Short mid-diastolic flow murmur.
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Tool:** Doppler Echocardiography (quantifies regurgitant jet, vena contracta ≥7 mm, and LVEF/LVESD dimensions).
- **Surgical Treatment of Choice:** Mitral Valve Repair (annuloplasty ring) preferred over total valve replacement.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Mitral regurgitation is a chronic or acute valvular disorder characterized by retrograde systolic blood flow from the left ventricle into the left atrium, resulting in progressive volume overload, left ventricular eccentric hypertrophy, and a classic apical blowing pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla.**
