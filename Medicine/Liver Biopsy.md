# Liver Biopsy: Indications and Complications

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Liver Biopsy** is an invasive diagnostic procedure involving the extraction of a cylindrical core of hepatic tissue for microscopic, histopathological, immunohistochemical, and molecular evaluation [Robbins 3 p. 254]. It remains the gold standard for diagnosing diffuse or focal parenchymal liver disorders, grading necroinflammatory activity, and staging fibrous tissue architecture [Robbins 3 p. 254, 295].

---

## 2. Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Diagnostic Indications

1. **Unexplained Abnormal Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Persistent, unexplained elevation of serum transaminases (ALT/AST) or alkaline phosphatase (ALP) lasting >6 months despite negative non-invasive serological and radiological workup [Davidson 1 p. 8, Robbins 3 p. 236].
2. **Autoimmune & Cholestatic Liver Diseases:** Diagnostic confirmation of **Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH)** (showing interface hepatitis and dense portal plasma cell infiltrates), **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)** (florid duct lesions with granulomatous destruction of interlobular ducts), or **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)** (periductal "onion-skin" fibrosis) [Robbins 3 p. 256, 271, 272].
3. **Metabolic & Storage Diseases:** Quantification of hepatic copper in **Wilson's Disease** (>250 μg/g dry weight), iron overload in **Hereditary Hemochromatosis** (Perls' Prussian blue staining / Hepatic Iron Index), and Alpha-1 Antitrypsin deficiency (PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules) [Robbins 3 p. 263, 265, Kdt4 p. 945].
4. **Focal Liver Lesions & Neoplasms:** Evaluation of indeterminate liver masses, atypical hepatocellular adenomas, or suspected metastatic carcinoma when imaging (CT/MRI) is non-diagnostic [Robbins 3 p. 288, 289].
5. **Pyrexia of Unknown Origin (PUO) & Granulomas:** Identifying granulomatous infections (tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, brucellosis, histoplasmosis, or visceral leishmaniasis) [Davidson 1 p. 8].
6. **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):** Assessing patterns of hepatotoxicity (microvesicular steatosis, cholestasis, or confluent necrosis) [Robbins 3 p. 257].

### B. Prognostic & Staging Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Chronic Viral Hepatitis (HBV & HCV):** Grading necroinflammatory activity (METAVIR/Ishak grade) and staging bridging fibrosis and cirrhosis (Stage F0 to F4) to guide antiviral therapy [Robbins 3 p. 254].
2. **Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) / NASH:** Distinguishing simple steatosis from **Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis (NASH)** (showing hepatocyte ballooning, Mallory-Denk bodies, and pericellular "chicken-wire" fibrosis) [Robbins 3 p. 258, 262].
3. **Alcoholic Liver Disease:** Assessing severity of alcoholic hepatitis and perivenular pericellular scarring [Robbins 3 p. 261, 262].

### C. Post-Transplant & Vascular Indications

1. **Allograft Dysfunction:** Distinguishing acute/chronic cellular rejection (portal infiltrates, endotheliitis, bile duct injury) from viral infection (CMV, EBV) or disease recurrence [Robbins 2 p. 209].
2. **Vascular Disorders:** Diagnosis of Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome (SOS/VOD) or Budd-Chiari syndrome when Doppler USG is inconclusive [Robbins 3 p. 281, 282].

## 3. Techniques & Routes of Liver Biopsy [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Percutaneous Route (Standard Transthoracic / Subcostal):**
    - Performed using a 16-gauge or 18-gauge Tru-Cut / Menghini needle inserted through the 8th or 9th intercostal space in the mid-axillary line under real-time ultrasound guidance during held mid-expiration [Davidson 1 p. 8].
2. **Transjugular Liver Biopsy (TJLB) [⭐ High-Yield Special Route]:**
    - Access via right internal jugular vein; catheter guided under fluoroscopy into the right hepatic vein, and needle passed through the vein wall into liver parenchyma.
    - **Indications for TJLB:** Uncorrected coagulopathy (INR >1.5), severe thrombocytopenia (platelets <50,000/μL), massive ascites, peliosis hepatis, or vascular tumors [Robbins 3 p. 282].
3. **Laparoscopic / Surgical Biopsy:** Performed under direct vision during staging laparoscopy; allows precise sampling of surface nodules and peritoneal lesions [Davidson 1 p. 8].

---

## 4. Pre-Biopsy Risk Assessment & Contraindications [⭐ High-Yield]

### Absolute Contraindications

1. **Severe Uncorrected Coagulopathy:** Prothrombin Time (PT) INR >1.5 or aPTT >1.5 times control [Davidson 1_compressed_401-800 p. 28, Kdt 3 p. 665].
2. **Severe Thrombocytopenia:** Platelet count **<50,000/μL** [Davidson 1_compressed_401-800 p. 28].
3. **Hydatid Cyst of Liver (_Echinococcus granulosus_):** Strict contraindication due to high risk of cyst rupture causing lethal **anaphylactic shock** and peritoneal dissemination [Robbins 2 p. 200].
4. **Vascular Neoplasms / Large Hemangiomas:** High risk of uncontrollable intra-abdominal hemorrhage [Robbins 3 p. 287].
5. **Inability to Cooperate or Hold Breath** (for percutaneous route).

### Relative Contraindications

- **Massive Ascites:** Fluids cushion needle entry (perform paracentesis first or select Transjugular route) [Robbins 3 p. 243].
- **Extrahepatic Biliary Obstruction / Ascending Cholangitis:** High risk of bile peritonitis and Gram-negative sepsis [Robbins 3 p. 266].
- **Right Pleural Empyema / Subphrenic Abscess.**

## 6. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Complications are divided into **Minor (Common, Self-Limiting)** and **Major (Life-Threatening)**:

### A. Minor Complications (~30% of cases)

1. **Transient Right Upper Quadrant Pain:** Localized ache radiating to the right shoulder (diaphragmatic irritation); resolves within 2 to 4 hours with simple analgesics.
2. **Vasovagal Reaction:** Transient hypotension and bradycardia immediately following puncture.
3. **Small Subcapsular / Intraparenchymal Hematoma:** Asymptomatic micro-hematomas visible on post-procedural USG.

### B. Major Complications (~0.5% to 1.0% of cases; Mortality ~0.01% to 0.1%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Intra-abdominal Hemorrhage & Hemoperitoneum [⭐ Most Common Major Complication]:**
    - Laceration of intercostal arteries, portal vein branches, or capsular vessels.
    - Manifests as rapid tachycardia, hypotension, severe abdominal pain, and drop in hemoglobin.
2. **Hemobilia:**
    - Arteriobiliary fistula formation causing hemorrhage into the biliary tree.
    - **Quincke's Triad of Hemobilia:** **Jaundice, Right Upper Quadrant Pain, and Upper GI Bleeding (Melena/Hematemesis)**.
3. **Bile Peritonitis & Biliary Leak:** Puncture of dilated intrahepatic bile ducts or gallbladder, causing chemical peritonitis and shock [Robbins 3 p. 266].
4. **Pneumothorax / Hemothorax:** Accidental penetration of the pleural space above the costodiaphragmatic recess.
5. **Visceral Perforation:** Accidental puncture of gallbladder, colon, kidney, or stomach.
6. **Bacteremia & Septic Shock:** Direct entry of bacteria into systemic circulation (especially in unrecognized ascending cholangitis) [Robbins 3 p. 266].

## 8. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Positioning the patient in the **Right Lateral Decubitus Position for 2 to 4 hours**immediately following a percutaneous liver biopsy applies continuous mechanical compression of the puncture site against the abdominal wall, reducing post-biopsy capsular hemorrhage and subcapsular hematoma formation by over 75%!

---

## 9. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Canceling a necessary liver biopsy in a cirrhotic patient with severe coagulopathy (INR >1.8, Platelets 30,000/μL) and massive ascites.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Percutaneous biopsy is strictly contraindicated in this setting due to catastrophic hemoperitoneum risk.
- **Resolution:** **Switch to Transjugular Liver Biopsy (TJLB)!** TJLB safely enters the liver via the hepatic vein. Any capsular bleeding drains back into the venous circulation rather than the peritoneal cavity, while simultaneously measuring the **Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG)**!

## 12. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Definitive procedure to obtain hepatic tissue for grading necroinflammation and staging fibrosis [Robbins 3 p. 254].
- **Key Indications:** Unexplained LFT elevation, chronic viral hepatitis staging (HBV/HCV), AIH/PBC/PSC diagnosis, NASH vs steatosis, Wilson's/Hemochromatosis, PUO, DILI [Robbins 3 p. 254, 256, 258, 263].
- **Absolute Contraindication:** Uncorrected coagulopathy (INR >1.5, Platelets <50,000/μL) and **Hydatid Cyst**(anaphylaxis risk) [Robbins 2 p. 200, Kdt 3 p. 665].
- **Special Route in Coagulopathy/Ascites:** **Transjugular Liver Biopsy (TJLB)** [Robbins 3 p. 282].
- **Quincke's Triad (Hemobilia):** Jaundice + Right Upper Quadrant Pain + Upper GI Bleeding.

## 13. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Liver biopsy is the definitive diagnostic modality for evaluating parenchymal liver disease, grading necroinflammatory activity, and staging fibrosis, performed percutaneously or via a transjugular route to prevent bleeding complications in coagulopathic states.**
