# Cirrhosis of Liver

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Cirrhosis of the Liver** is a chronic, diffuse, irreversible end-stage liver disease characterized by progressive hepatic parenchymal destruction, loss of normal lobular architecture, widespread bridging fibrosis, and the formation of regenerative parenchymal nodules surrounded by dense collagenous fibrous bands [Robbins 3 p. 556, 560].

- **Alcoholic Cirrhosis (Laennec's Cirrhosis):** A specific subtype resulting from chronic, heavy alcohol consumption (typically >80 g/day in males, >20–40 g/day in females over 10 to 20 years) [Robbins 3 p. 560]. Pathologically, it is characterized by micronodular cirrhosis (nodules <3 mm), steatosis, ballooning degeneration, Mallory-Denk bodies, and pericellular ("chicken-wire") fibrosis [Robbins 3 p. 560, 561].
- **Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy:** A complex, life-threatening neuropsychiatric syndrome complicating severe hepatic dysfunction or portosystemic shunting. It is characterized by metabolic brain dysfunction ranging from subtle confusion and asterixis to deep coma, driven primarily by elevated serum ammonia (NH₃) levels crossing the blood-brain barrier [Robbins 3 p. 554, Kdt 3 p. 724, 996].

1. **Alcoholic Liver Disease (60–70% of cases):** Prolonged excessive consumption of ethyl alcohol (>80 g/day in men, >20–40 g/day in women for >10–20 years) [Robbins 3 p. 560].
2. **Chronic Viral Hepatitis:** Chronic infection with Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) ± Hepatitis D Virus (HDV), or Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) [Robbins 3 p. 556].
3. **Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) / Steatohepatitis (NASH):** Hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, obesity, and type 2 diabetes mellitus [Robbins 3 p. 560, 562].
4. **Autoimmune & Biliary Diseases:**
    - _Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC):_ Autoimmune destruction of small interlobular bile ducts (95% Antimitochondrial Antibody [AMA] positive) [Robbins 3 p. 558, 563].
    - _Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC):_ Fibrosing inflammation of intra- and extrahepatic bile ducts (strongly associated with Ulcerative Colitis) [Robbins 3 p. 563].
    - _Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH):_ Type 1 (ANA / ASMA positive) or Type 2 (Anti-LKM-1 positive) [Robbins 3 p. 558].
5. **Metabolic & Genetic Disorders:**
    - _Wilson's Disease:_ Autosomal recessive mutation in ATP7B gene leading to copper toxic accumulation in liver and basal ganglia [Robbins 3 p. 562, Kdt4 p. 945].
    - _Hereditary Hemochromatosis:_ HFE gene mutation producing systemic iron overload ("bronze diabetes") [Robbins 3 p. 562].
    - _Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency:_ PiZZ phenotype leading to misfolded protein accumulation [Robbins 3 p. 556].
6. **Vascular & Cardiac Causes:** Budd-Chiari syndrome (hepatic vein outflow obstruction), chronic passive congestion ("nutmeg liver" secondary to right-sided heart failure / constrictive pericarditis), Sinusoidal Obstruction Syndrome [Robbins 3 p. 554, 564, 565].
7. **Drugs & Hepatotoxins:** Chronic administration of methotrexate, isoniazid, amiodarone, or excessive retinoids [Robbins 3 p. 559].

## 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Morphological Classification

1. **Micronodular Cirrhosis:** Regenerative nodules are small and uniform (<3 mm diameter), bounded by thin, regular fibrous septa. Typical of **Alcoholic Cirrhosis (Laennec's Cirrhosis)**, hemochromatosis, and biliary obstruction [Robbins 3 p. 561].
2. **Macronodular Cirrhosis:** Regenerative nodules are large and variable (>3 mm to several centimeters), separated by broad, irregular fibrous scars. Typical of chronic viral hepatitis (HBV/HCV) and post-necrotic cirrhosis [Robbins 3 p. 557].
3. **Mixed Cirrhosis:** Exhibits features of both micronodular and macronodular patterns.

### B. Functional / Clinical Staging

1. **Compensated Cirrhosis:** Asymptomatic or mild non-specific symptoms; liver synthetic capacity preserved; absence of major portal hypertensive complications.
2. **Decompensated Cirrhosis:** Manifested by life-threatening complications: Jaundice, Ascites, Variceal Bleeding, Hepatic Encephalopathy, or Hepatorenal Syndrome.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Ethanol Oxidation & Metabolic Redox Shift:** Ethanol is oxidized in hepatocytes by **Alcohol Dehydrogenase (ADH)** to **Acetaldehyde**, reducing NAD to NADH [Robbins 2 p. 163, Robbins 3 p. 560]. High NADH/NAD ratio inhibits fatty acid beta-oxidation, causing triglyceride accumulation (**Steatosis**) and lactic acidosis [Robbins 2 p. 163, Robbins 3 p. 560].
2. **Oxidative Stress & Hepatocyte Injury:** Induction of CYP2E1 generates Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS), causing lipid peroxidation of hepatocyte membranes [Robbins 2 p. 163, Robbins 3 p. 560]. Acetaldehyde forms protein adducts, damaging cytoskeletal intermediate filaments to form **Mallory-Denk bodies** [Robbins 3 p. 554, 560].
3. **Stellate Cell Activation & Fibrogenesis:** Injured hepatocytes, ROS, and gut-derived endotoxins (LPS via TLR4 signaling) activate resident **Hepatic Stellate Cells (Ito Cells)** in the Space of Disse [Robbins 3 p. 555, 560]. Stellate cells transform into contractile myofibroblasts, secreting massive amounts of **Type I and III Collagen**, replacing normal fenestrated sinusoidal matrix with dense fibrous bands (**Pericellular / "Chicken-wire" Fibrosis**) [Robbins 3 p. 555, 561].
4. **Portal Hypertension:** Sinusoidal narrowing, perivenular scarring, and expanding regenerative nodules increase resistance to portal blood flow [Robbins 3 p. 555, 557]. Splanchnic vasodilation (driven by nitric oxide) increases portal inflow, elevating portal pressure (>10–12 mmHg) [Robbins 3 p. 557].
5. **Pathophysiology of Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - _Hyperammonemia:_ Impaired hepatic conversion of ammonia to urea via the urea cycle, combined with portosystemic shunting, causes gut-derived **Ammonia (NH₃)** to surge in systemic circulation [Robbins 3 p. 554, Kdt 3 p. 724, 996].
    - _Astrocytic Swelling & Cerebral Edema:_ Lipid-soluble NH₃ crosses the blood-brain barrier. Astrocytes detoxify NH₃ by reacting it with glutamate to form **Glutamine**. Intracellular glutamine accumulation acts as an osmolyte, causing **astrocytic swelling, cerebral edema, and raised ICP** [Robbins 3 p. 554].
    - _Precipitating Triggers:_ Gastrointestinal hemorrhage (protein load in gut), electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemic alkalosis secondary to loop diuretics, converting NH₄⁺ → NH₃), infections (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis), constipation, high-protein intake, or sedatives [Kdt 3 p. 724, 996, 1014].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Constitutional & Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, nausea, morning vomiting, weight loss, muscle wasting, fatigue, weakness, and right upper quadrant dull abdominal discomfort [Robbins 3 p. 556, 561].
- **Decompensation Manifestations:** Abdominal distension (ascites), leg edema, jaundice, dark tea-colored urine, hematemesis, or melena (from esophageal varices) [Robbins 3 p. 554, 557].
- **Encephalopathy Symptoms:** Reversed sleep rhythm (daytime drowsiness, nocturnal insomnia), apathy, euphoria, memory impairment, slurred speech, confusion, lethargy, progressing to stupor and coma [Robbins 3 p. 554, Kdt 3 p. 724, 996].

#### 1. Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease (Hyperestrogenemia & Metabolic Failure)

- **Spider Angiomas (Spider Naevi):** Central pulsating arteriole with radiating fine capillary legs, found on sun-exposed skin above the nipple line (face, neck, chest, arms) [Robbins 3 p. 556].
- **Palmar Erythema:** Mottled vascular reddening of the hypothenar and thenar eminences [Robbins 3 p. 556].
- **Endocrine Signs:** Gynecomastia, loss of chest and axillary hair, and testicular atrophy in males; amenorrhea or oligomenorrhea in females [Robbins 3 p. 556, 557].
- **Nail & Palmar Signs:** Terry's nails (proximal white opacity with distal pink zone), leuconychia, and **Dupuytren's contracture** (palmar fascial thickening, highly characteristic of alcoholic etiology) [Robbins 3 p. 556, Davidson 1_compressed_401-800 p. 1220].
- **Bilateral Parotid Gland Swelling:** Painless parotid hypertrophy secondary to chronic alcoholism.

#### 2. Signs of Portal Hypertension & Decompensation

- **Ascites & Peripheral Edema:** Abdominal distension with fluid thrill and shifting dullness; bilateral pitting leg and sacral edema [Robbins 3 p. 557].
- **Caput Medusae:** Engorged, dilated subcutaneous veins radiating outward from the umbilicus [Robbins 3 p. 557].
- **Splenomegaly:** Congestive enlargement of the spleen [Robbins 3 p. 557].
- **Fetor Hepaticus:** Sweetish, musty, fecal breath odor caused by volatile dimethyl sulfide [Robbins 3 p. 554].

#### 3. Signs of Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy [⭐ Pathognomonic Signs]

- **Asterixis ("Flapping Tremor"):** Rapid, non-rhythmic, jerky extension-flexion movements of the wrists and metacarpophalangeal joints elicited when arms are held extended with dorsiflexed wrists [Robbins 3 p. 554].
- **Neurological Findings:** Hyperreflexia, bilateral extensor plantar responses (Babinski sign), muscular rigidity, dysarthria, and fluctuating levels of consciousness [Robbins 3 p. 554].

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Laboratory Investigations

1. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):**
    - _Serum AST & ALT:_ Elevated. In **Alcoholic Cirrhosis**, **AST : ALT ratio >2 : 1** (due to pyridoxal-5'-phosphate deficiency restricting ALT synthesis and mitochondrial AST toxicity) [Robbins 3 p. 561].
    - _Serum Bilirubin:_ Elevated total and conjugated bilirubin (jaundice).
    - _Serum ALP & GGT:_ Elevated. **Gamma-Glutamyl Transpeptidase (GGT)** is markedly elevated in active alcohol intake [Robbins 3 p. 561].
    - _Serum Albumin:_ Decreased (<3.5 g/dL) reflecting loss of hepatic synthetic capacity [Robbins 3 p. 557, 561].
    - _Prothrombin Time (PT) / INR:_ Prolonged PT (INR >1.5) due to impaired synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X [Robbins 3 p. 554, 561].
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
    - Macrocytic anemia (alcohol toxicity / folate deficiency) or microcytic anemia (variceal blood loss).
    - **Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL) & Leukopenia:** Secondary to hypersplenism [Robbins 3 p. 557].
3. **Electrolytes & Renal Function:**
    - _Hyponatremia (Na⁺ <130 mEq/L):_ Hypervolemic hemodilution.
    - _Hypokalemia (K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L):_ Secondary hyperaldosteronism and loop diuretic use [Kdt 3 p. 724, 996].
    - Elevated Blood Urea & Serum Creatinine (indicates Hepatorenal Syndrome) [Robbins 3 p. 554].
4. **Blood Ammonia (NH₃):** Elevated serum ammonia (>80–100 μg/dL) confirming Hepatic Encephalopathy [Robbins 3 p. 554].

### B. Diagnostic Staging Systems [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Child-Pugh Scoring System (Mnemonic: "P-A-R-I-S")

Evaluates 5 parameters (1 to 3 points each):

|Parameter|1 Point|2 Points|3 Points|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Serum Bilirubin (mg/dL)**|<2.0|2.0 to 3.0|>3.0|
|**Serum Albumin (g/dL)**|>3.5|2.8 to 3.5|<2.8|
|**Prothrombin Time (INR)**|<1.7|1.7 to 2.3|>2.3|
|**Ascites**|Absent|Mild / Controlled|Moderate / Severe|
|**Hepatic Encephalopathy**|None|Grade I – II|Grade III – IV|

- **Class A (5–6 points):** Well-compensated disease (100% 1-year survival).
- **Class B (7–9 points):** Moderately compromised disease (80% 1-year survival).
- **Class C (10–15 points):** Decompensated end-stage disease (45% 1-year survival).

#### 2. West-Haven Staging of Hepatic Encephalopathy

- **Grade I:** Trivial lack of awareness, euphoria/anxiety, shortened attention span; mild asterixis.
- **Grade II:** Lethargy, apathy, disorientation to time, obvious personality change; **asterixis easily present**.
- **Grade III:** Somnolence to semi-stupor, responsive to verbal stimuli, confusion, disorientation to place; asterixis present.
- **Grade IV:** **Coma** (unresponsive to verbal or painful stimuli).

---

### C. Imaging & Histopathology

1. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) with Doppler:** Demonstrates shrunken nodular liver, coarsened echotexture, splenomegaly, ascites, dilated portal vein (>13 mm), and hepatofugal portal blood flow [Davidson 1_compressed_401-800 p. 451].
2. **Transient Elastography (FibroScan):** Measures liver stiffness (kPa >12.5 kPa indicates cirrhosis).
3. **Upper GI Endoscopy (UGIE):** Identifies esophageal/gastric varices and portal hypertensive gastropathy [Robbins 3 p. 557].
4. **Liver Biopsy (Gold Standard Confirmation):** Shows micronodular regenerative nodules surrounded by fibrous septa, Mallory-Denk bodies, and steatosis [Robbins 3 p. 560, 561].

### A. Emergency Stabilization for Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy

1. **Airway Protection:** Grade III or IV coma patients require ICU transfer and **endotracheal intubation** to prevent aspiration [Robbins 3 p. 554, Kdt 2 p. 421].
2. **Correction of Precipitating Causes [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - _GI Hemorrhage:_ Perform emergency variceal band ligation; purge blood from gut.
    - _Infections:_ Screen for Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP); give empirical IV Ceftriaxone (1–2 g IV daily).
    - _Electrolyte Imbalances:_ Correct hypokalemia and metabolic alkalosis immediately [Kdt 3 p. 724, 996].
    - _Sedatives:_ Discontinue all CNS depressants, hypnotics, and opioids [Kdt 2 p. 421, Kdt 3 p. 627].

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Lactulose (First-Line Osmotic Disaccharide) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Lactulose.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 724, 725, 996, 997). Non-absorbable disaccharide (fructose + galactose). Passed unabsorbed into the colon, where colonic bacteria ferment it into short-chain fatty acids (lactic and acetic acids).
    
    - _Acidification of Colon (pH ↓ to 5.0):_ Converts diffusible gaseous Ammonia (NH₃) into non-absorbable Ammonium ions (NH₄⁺), trapping it in the gut lumen (**Ammonia Trapping**).
    - _Bacterial Suppression:_ Low pH suppresses ammoniagenic colonic bacteria (e.g., _E. coli_).
    - _Osmotic Purging:_ Promotes rapid colonic evacuation of nitrogenous waste.
    
    ```
    Lactulose → Colonic Bacterial Fermentation → Lowered Colonic pH (5.0) → NH3 + H+ → NH4+ (Trapped) + Rapid Evacuation
    ```
    
- **Receptors / Target:** Colonic microflora and luminal pH.
- **Dose:**
    - _Acute Encephalopathy (Oral/NG Tube):_ **30 mL to 45 mL PO / via NG Tube every 1 to 2 hours** until bowel movement occurs, then adjusted to **15 mL to 30 mL PO 8-hourly (TID)** to achieve 2 to 3 soft stools daily.
    - _Retention Enema (in Comatose Patients):_ **300 mL Lactulose mixed with 700 mL water / normal saline**, retained in colon for 30–60 minutes 6-hourly.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral solution, Nasogastric tube, or Retention Enema.
- **Frequency:** 1–2 hourly initially, then 8-hourly maintenance.
- **Features:** Gold standard therapy; reduces blood ammonia by 25–50% [Kdt 3 p. 724].
- **Side Effects:** Flatulence, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, dehydration, hypernatremia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon confirming HE; titrate to maintain 2–3 soft stools daily; continue long-term for secondary prevention.

#### 2. Rifaximin (Non-Absorbable Antibiotic) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Rifaximin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 733, 734). Minimally absorbed (<0.4%) oral rifamycin derivative. Inhibits bacterial RNA synthesis by binding to the β-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase. Eradicates ammoniagenic gut bacteria (_Enterobacteriaceae_, _Clostridium_ species), reducing intestinal ammonia production without systemic side effects.
    
    ```
    Rifaximin → Inhibits Bacterial RNA Polymerase in Gut → Eradicates Ammoniagenic Bacteria → Decreased Blood NH3 Synthesis
    ```
    
- **Dose:** **550 mg PO twice daily (BD)** or **400 mg PO three times daily (TID)**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (550 mg) or 8-hourly (400 mg).
- **Features:** First-line add-on to lactulose; significantly reduces HE recurrence rates.
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, flatulence, abdominal pain, headache.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in acute HE unresponsive to lactulose alone or in combination from onset; maintain chronically.

#### 3. Potassium Chloride (KCl) Infusion (Correction of Hypokalemia)

- **Name:** Potassium Chloride.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Kdt 3 Textbook p. 724, 996). Reverses hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Hypokalemia increases renal tubular production of NH₃, while alkalosis converts ionized NH₄⁺ into free lipid-soluble NH₃ gas that crosses the blood-brain barrier into the CNS.
    
    ```
    KCl Infusion → Reverses Hypokalemic Alkalosis → Shift of Free NH3 to Ionized NH4+ → Decreased CNS Penetration
    ```
    
- **Dose:** **20 to 40 mEq KCl per liter of IV Normal Saline** (infused at ≤10–20 mEq/hour under cardiac monitoring).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous infusion.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate if serum K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L; stop when K⁺ normalizes (4.0–4.5 mEq/L).

#### 4. L-Ornithine L-Aspartate (LOLA)

- **Dose:** **20 g IV infusion daily** in 500 mL 5% Dextrose over 4–6 hours. Provides substrates for urea cycle and glutamine synthesis.

### C. Long-Term Management of Alcoholic Cirrhosis & Portal Hypertension

1. **Total Alcohol Abstinence [⭐ High-Yield]:** Absolute alcohol cessation halts fibrosis progression and improves 5-year survival from 50% to >90% [Robbins 3 p. 561]. Use Acamprosate (666 mg PO TID) or Baclofen (5 mg PO TID) for dependence [Kdt 2 p. 421].
2. **Management of Ascites:**
    - _Dietary Sodium Restriction:_ Restrict sodium to **<2 g/day (<88 mEq/day)**.
    - _Combination Diuretics:_ **Spironolactone** (100–400 mg PO OD) plus **Furosemide** (40–160 mg PO OD) in a **100 mg : 40 mg ratio** to maintain normokalemia [Kdt 3 p. 627, 996].
    - _Therapeutic Paracentesis:_ Co-infuse **IV Human Albumin (6 to 8 g per liter of ascitic fluid removed)** if >5 L drained.
3. **Primary Prophylaxis of Variceal Bleeding:** Non-selective beta-blockers (**Propranolol 20–40 mg PO BD** or Carvedilol 6.25 mg PO OD) or Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL) [Davidson 1_compressed_401-800 p. 450, Robbins 3 p. 557].
4. **Definitive Treatment:** **Liver Transplantation** (indicated for Child-Pugh Class C disease after 6 months of documented abstinence).

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Variceal Hemorrhage:** Rupture of esophagogastric varices causing severe upper GI bleeding (~30% mortality per episode) [Robbins 3 p. 557, 561].
2. **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Metabolic neuropsychiatric deterioration [Robbins 3 p. 554].
3. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Functional renal failure caused by intense renal vasoconstriction secondary to splanchnic vasodilation (Type 1 rapidly progressive; Type 2 insidious) [Robbins 3 p. 554].
4. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP):** Infection of ascitic fluid (**PMN count ≥250 cells/μL**), usually by _E. coli_[Robbins 3 p. 557].
5. **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Annual risk of 1% to 6% in cirrhotic livers [Robbins 3 p. 561].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **With Total Alcohol Abstinence:** 5-year survival in compensated alcoholic cirrhosis approaches **90%** [Robbins 3 p. 561].
- **With Continued Drinking:** 5-year survival drops sharply to **50% to 60%** [Robbins 3 p. 561].
- Decompensation events (HE, variceal bleeding, HRS, SBP) reduce 1-year survival to <50%.

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In patients with cirrhotic ascites and hepatic encephalopathy, NEVER initiate rapid, aggressive diuresis with loop diuretics alone! High-dose furosemide induces severe **hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**. In alkalosis (pH >7.4), ammonium ions (NH₄⁺) convert into lipid-soluble un-ionized ammonia gas (NH₃), which rapidly crosses the blood-brain barrier into the central nervous system, directly triggering or worsening **Acute Hepatic Encephalopathy**! Always combine **Spironolactone** with Furosemide in a 100 mg : 40 mg ratio and maintain serum potassium above 4.0 mEq/L [Kdt 3 p. 724, 996, 1014].

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Neomycin** or **Morphine / Sedatives** to manage agitation or restless behavior in a patient with alcoholic cirrhosis presenting with acute hepatic encephalopathy.
- **Distinguishing Fact:**
    - Cirrhotic patients have markedly increased CNS sensitivity to depressants, and normal doses of morphine, barbiturates, or benzodiazepines can precipitate **fatal hepatic coma** [Kdt 2 p. 421, Kdt 3 p. 627, 669].
    - Neomycin is obsolete due to severe ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity.
- **Resolution:** **Sedatives, hypnotics, and opioids are STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in Hepatic Encephalopathy**! Agitation must be managed by treating the underlying hyperammonemia using **Lactulose and Rifaximin**, along with airway protection.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** End-stage diffuse liver disease with parenchymal necrosis, micronodular regeneration (<3 mm), and dense fibrosis.
- **Alcoholic LFT Hallmark:** **AST : ALT ratio >2 : 1** with elevated GGT [Robbins 3 p. 561].
- **Encephalopathy Pathogenesis:** Gut-derived **Ammonia (NH₃)** crosses blood-brain barrier → astrocytic glutamine accumulation → cerebral edema [Robbins 3 p. 554].
- **Key Encephalopathy Sign:** **Asterixis ("flapping tremor")** on wrist dorsiflexion [Robbins 3 p. 554].
- **Core Encephalopathy Treatment:** **Lactulose** (30–45 mL 1–2 hourly initially to target 2–3 soft stools/day) + **Rifaximin** (550 mg PO BD) + **Correction of Hypokalemia** [Kdt 3 p. 724, 734, 996].

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Cirrhosis of the liver is the end-stage consequence of chronic hepatic injury characterized by diffuse parenchymal destruction, micronodular regeneration, and bridging fibrosis, leading to portal hypertension and life-threatening acute hepatic encephalopathy driven by hyperammonemia.**
