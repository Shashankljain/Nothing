# Hypersplenism

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Hypersplenism** is a secondary clinical syndrome resulting from splenomegaly, characterized by an exaggerated physiological function of the spleen that leads to premature destruction and excessive sequestration of one or more formed elements of blood [Robbins 3 p. 132, 134].

It is defined strictly by the classic **Triad of Hypersplenism**:

1. **Splenomegaly** (palpable or radiologically demonstrated enlargement of the spleen) [Robbins 3 p. 134].
2. **Peripheral Cytopenia(s)** involving one or more cellular blood lines (**Anemia**, **Leukopenia/Neutropenia**, or **Thrombocytopenia**) [Robbins 3 p. 132, 134].
3. **Compensatory Bone Marrow Hyperplasia** of the corresponding precursor cell lines (erythroid, granulocytic, or megakaryocytic series) [Robbins 3 p. 187].

- **Definitive Criterion:** Reversibility and correction of peripheral cytopenias following **splenectomy** [Robbins 3 p. 154, 190].

### A. Congestive States (Portal Hypertension) [⭐ Most Common Cause]

- **Cirrhosis of the Liver:** Alcoholic cirrhosis, viral hepatitis B/C, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) [Robbins 3 p. 138, 139].
- **Vascular Obstruction:** Extrahepatic portal vein thrombosis, splenic vein thrombosis (pancreatitis/carcinoma), Budd-Chiari syndrome [Robbins 3 p. 138].
- **Cardiac Causes:** Severe right-sided heart failure, constrictive pericarditis [Robbins 3 p. 139, 141].

### B. Infections

- **Protozoal Infections:** Chronic _Plasmodium falciparum / vivax_ malaria (Tropical Splenomegaly Syndrome), Visceral Leishmaniasis (_Kala-azar_) [Robbins 3 p. 139].
- **Helminthic Infections:** _Schistosoma mansoni_ ("pipe-stem" periportal fibrosis) [Robbins 3 p. 138, 139].
- **Bacterial & Viral Infections:** Tuberculosis, Typhoid fever, Infectious Endocarditis, Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV) [Robbins 3 p. 139, 144].

### C. Lymphohematogenous & Neoplastic Disorders

- **Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (MPNs):** Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML), Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF), Polycythemia Vera [Robbins 3 p. 115, 127, 139].
- **Lymphoid Neoplasms:** Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas, Hodgkin Lymphoma, Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL), Hairy Cell Leukemia [Robbins 3 p. 108, 139].
- **Hemolytic Anemias:** Hereditary Spherocytosis, Thalassemia Major, Sickle Cell Disease (early stage before autosplenectomy), Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA) [Robbins 3 p. 139, 154, 165].

### D. Immunologic & Inflammatory Conditions

- **Felty Syndrome:** Triad of Rheumatoid Arthritis, Splenomegaly, and Neutropenia [Robbins 3 p. 109].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)** and **Sarcoidosis** [Robbins 3 p. 139, 140].

### E. Storage Diseases & Infiltrative Disorders

- **Gaucher Disease**, **Niemann-Pick Disease**, and **Splenic Amyloidosis** [Robbins 3 p. 139].

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Splenic Enlargement & Pooling:** The normal spleen holds approximately **30 to 40 mL of red blood cells** and **30% to 40% of the total circulating platelet mass** [Robbins 3 p. 132]. In splenomegaly, splenic pooling surges dramatically: up to **80% to 90% of the total body platelet mass** and large fractions of RBCs and WBCs become trapped within the red pulp [Robbins 3 p. 132, 184].
2. **Slowing of Microcirculation (Erythrostasis):** Elevated venous portal pressure causes sinusoidal dilation, thickening of basement membranes, and congestion of the cords of Billroth [Robbins 3 p. 140]. Blood transit from splenic cords into sinusoids is markedly delayed [Robbins 3 p. 140, 154].
3. **Premature Destruction & Phagocytosis:** Prolonged exposure to active splenic cords depletes glucose, reduces cellular pH, and impairs RBC membrane deformability [Robbins 3 p. 154]. Macrophages of the mononuclear phagocyte system ingest and destroy these trapped blood elements (**extravascular destruction**) [Robbins 3 p. 134, 140, 150].
4. **Compensatory Bone Marrow Hyperplasia:** Peripheral blood cytopenias trigger feedback release of hematopoietic growth factors (Erythropoietin, Thrombopoietin, and Granulocyte Colony-Stimulating Factor [G-CSF]), stimulating intense precursor proliferation in the bone marrow [Robbins 3 p. 96, 151, 187].

## 4. Clinical Features

- **Mechanical Symptoms:** Left upper quadrant abdominal discomfort, dragging sensation, and early satiety secondary to gastric compression by the enlarged spleen [Robbins 3 p. 115, 134].
- **Features of Anemia:** Progressive fatigue, exertion-induced dyspnea, pallor, tachycardia, and weakness [Robbins 3 p. 134].
- **Features of Thrombocytopenia:** Mucocutaneous bleeding, petechiae, purpura, ecchymoses, epistaxis, and gingival bleeding [Robbins 3 p. 113, Marrow Vol 1 p. 171].
- **Features of Neutropenia / Leukopenia:** Increased susceptibility to recurrent bacterial infections, furunculosis, and painful oral aphthous ulcerations [Robbins 2 p. 101, Robbins 3 p. 109].
- **Etiology-Specific Features:** Jaundice, ascites, caput medusae (Cirrhosis); high fever with rigors (Malaria); joint deformity (Felty syndrome) [Robbins 3 p. 109, 139].

---

## 5. Diagnosis / Laboratory Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Peripheral Blood Evaluation

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):**
    - **Thrombocytopenia:** Platelet count **<100,000/μL** (most common and earliest hematological manifestation) [Robbins 3 p. 132, Marrow Vol 1 p. 170].
    - **Leukopenia / Neutropenia:** Total Leukocyte Count (TLC) **<4,000/μL** and Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) **<1,500/μL** [Robbins 2 p. 83, 101, Robbins 3 p. 132].
    - **Anemia:** Normocytic normochromic anemia (Hemoglobin **<10 to 12 g/dL**) [Robbins 3 p. 134].
    - **Pancytopenia:** Simultaneous reduction across all three hematopoietic cell lines [Robbins 3 p. 108, 134].
2. **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS):**
    - Reticulocytosis (polychromasia) reflecting active marrow compensatory response [Robbins 3 p. 152].
    - Presence of megathrombocytes (large platelets) [Robbins 3 p. 187].
    - Etiological Clues: Microspherocytes (Hereditary Spherocytosis), teardrop red cells / dacryocytes (Myelofibrosis), or ring-stage trophozoites (Malaria) [Robbins 3 p. 126, 154].

---

### B. Bone Marrow Examination

- **Bone Marrow Aspirate & Biopsy:**
    - Demonstrates **Normocellular or Hypercellular Marrow** with compensatory hyperplasia of affected cell series (erythroid hyperplasia, increased megakaryocytes, or granulocytic precursors) [Robbins 3 p. 187].
    - Essential to exclude primary marrow failure (Aplastic Anemia) and aleukemic leukemias [Robbins 3 p. 113, 178].

---

### C. Imaging & Diagnostic Confirmation

1. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) with Doppler:** Confirms spleen size (splenic length **>13 cm** or weight **>500 g**), assesses portal vein diameter (**>13 mm** in portal hypertension), and detects splenic vein thrombosis [Robbins 3 p. 139].
2. **Radionuclide Scintillation Imaging (Cr⁵¹-labeled RBCs):** Demonstrates shortened red cell survival and localized splenic sequestration.

### A. Management of Underlying Cause (First-Line Strategy)

- Treatment must focus primarily on correcting the underlying cause of splenomegaly (e.g., antimalarials for chronic malaria, antiviral/TIPS for portal hypertension in cirrhosis, TKIs like Imatinib for CML) [Robbins 3 p. 115, 139].

---

### B. Indications for Splenectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Severe, Symptomatic Cytopenias:**
    - Refractory transfusion-dependent anemia [Robbins 3 p. 154].
    - Severe neutropenia (ANC **<500/μL**) with recurrent life-threatening infections [Robbins 2 p. 101].
    - Severe thrombocytopenia (platelets **<20,000/μL**) with spontaneous mucosal bleeding [Robbins 3 p. 184].
2. **Massive, Painful Splenomegaly:** Causing severe mechanical abdominal discomfort, early satiety, or impending splenic rupture [Robbins 3 p. 115, 144].

---

### C. Pre-Splenectomy Mandatory Vaccination Protocol (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

To prevent fatal **Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection (OPSI)**, mandatory immunizations must be administered at least **2 weeks prior to elective splenectomy** (or 2 weeks post-emergency splenectomy) [Davidson 1 p. 971, Robbins 3 p. 133, Marrow Vol 1 p. 105]:

#### 1. Pneumococcal Vaccination Protocol

- **Vaccines:** 13-valent Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (**PCV13**) followed by 23-valent Pneumococcal Polysaccharide Vaccine (**PPSV23**) 8 weeks later [Robbins 3 p. 133].
- **Dose & Route:** **0.5 mL IM** single dose in deltoid muscle [Kdt4 p. 984].
- **Features:** Protects against _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ (most common OPSI pathogen) [Robbins 3 p. 133].

#### 2. Meningococcal Vaccination Protocol

- **Vaccine:** Quadrivalent Meningococcal Conjugate Vaccine (**MenACWY**) + Serogroup B Meningococcal Vaccine (**MenB**) [Robbins 3 p. 133].
- **Dose & Route:** **0.5 mL IM** single dose [Kdt4 p. 984].

#### 3. Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) Vaccination

- **Vaccine:** Conjugate Hib Vaccine [Robbins 3 p. 133].
- **Dose & Route:** **0.5 mL IM** single dose [Kdt4 p. 984].

---

### D. Alternative Interventions (High-Risk Surgical Candidates)

- **Partial Splenic Embolization (PSE):** Interventional radiological occlusion of splenic artery branches using gelfoam particles; reduces splenic mass while preserving sufficient tissue for immunocompetence.
- **Splenic Irradiation:** Low-dose palliative radiotherapy for massive splenomegaly in primary myelofibrosis.

---

## 8. Complications

1. **Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection (OPSI) / Post-Splenectomy Sepsis:** Rapidly fatal fulminant bacteremia caused by encapsulated organisms (_Streptococcus pneumoniae_, _Neisseria meningitidis_, _Haemophilus influenzae_) [Robbins 3 p. 133, 158].
2. **Portal / Splenic Vein Thrombosis:** Post-splenectomy rebound hypercoagulability and reactive thrombocytosis [Marrow Vol 1 p. 105].
3. **Severe Spontaneous Hemorrhage:** Secondary to refractory thrombocytopenia [Robbins 3 p. 184].

---

## 9. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Thrombocytopenia is the single most sensitive and earliest peripheral blood marker of hypersplenism! When a jaundiced patient with cirrhosis develops a falling platelet count (<100,000/μL) despite normal white cell and red cell counts, it signals the onset of portal hypertension-induced congestive hypersplenism [Robbins 3 p. 132, 184]!

---

## 10. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Performing emergency splenectomy for hypersplenism without administering pre-operative immunizations.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Asplenic individuals lose the primary filtration site for un-opsonized bacteria and lack splenic marginal zone B-lymphocytes required for antibody production against polysaccharide capsules.
- **Resolution:** **Never perform elective splenectomy without vaccinating against encapsulated bacteria (Pneumococcus, Meningococcus, Hib) at least 2 weeks prior to surgery**! Post-splenectomy patients must also receive prophylactic oral Penicillin V daily [Robbins 3 p. 133]!

## 11. Diagram Guide

### Diagram : Mechanism of Splenic Sequestration in Hypersplenism

_(Reference in text: See Section 3 - Pathophysiology)_

- **Type:** Labeled Microvascular Flow Diagram.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Cords of Billroth opening into Splenic Sinusoids flanked by Splenic Macrophages.
- **Labels in Order:**
    1. **Penicilliary Arterioles:** Blood entering cords of Billroth under high portal pressure.
    2. **Congested Cords of Billroth:** Trapping of RBCs, WBCs, and 80-90% of total platelet mass.
    3. **Splenic Macrophages:** Engulfment and extravascular destruction of trapped blood cells.
    4. **Sinusoidal Exit:** Reduced outflow of blood elements into splenic vein → Peripheral Cytopenia.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"High portal venous pressure causes erythrostasis in the cords of Billroth, triggering phagocytosis of blood elements by splenic macrophages."_.

## 13. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Syndrome of splenomegaly + peripheral cytopenia(s) + normal/hypercellular bone marrow + correction by splenectomy [Robbins 3 p. 132, 134].
- **Most Common Cause:** Congestive splenomegaly secondary to **Cirrhosis of the liver** (Portal HTN) [Robbins 3 p. 138, 139].
- **Platelet Sequestration:** Normal spleen sequesters 30-40% of platelets; enlarged spleen sequesters up to **80-90%**[Robbins 3 p. 132].
- **Bone Marrow Finding:** Normocellular or **Hypercellular** with compensatory hyperplasia of affected cell lines [Robbins 3 p. 187].
- **Mandatory Pre-Splenectomy Protocol:** Vaccinate against _S. pneumoniae_, _N. meningitidis_, and _H. influenzae_ at least **2 weeks before elective surgery** to prevent OPSI [Davidson 1 p. 971, Robbins 3 p. 133].

## 14. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Hypersplenism is a secondary hematological syndrome characterized by splenomegaly, peripheral blood cytopenias resulting from excessive splenic sequestration and macrophage phagocytosis, and compensatory bone marrow hyperplasia, definitively corrected by treating the underlying cause or performing splenectomy.**
