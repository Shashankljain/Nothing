# Cardiac Tamponade

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Cardiac Tamponade** is an acute, life-threatening hemodynamic emergency characterized by the compression of all four cardiac chambers caused by the rapid or excessive accumulation of fluid, blood, pus, or gas within the pericardial space.

This accumulation elevates intrapericardial pressure above normal limits (-2 to +3 mmHg), impeding normal diastolic cardiac filling, severely reducing stroke volume, and culminating in **obstructive cardiogenic shock** and cardiovascular collapse [Davidson p. 544, Robbins p. 554].

---

1. **Traumatic / Hemorrhagic Causes:**
    - Penetrating chest trauma (stab or gunshot wounds) or severe blunt chest impacts causing hemopericardium [Robbins p. 554].
2. **Cardiovascular Disasters:**
    - **Acute Myocardial Rupture:** Rupture of the left ventricular free wall following acute transmural myocardial infarction (typically 3–5 days post-MI) [Davidson p. 493, Robbins p. 546].
    - **Type A Aortic Dissection:** Retrograde dissection of the ascending aorta tearing through the aortic root into the pericardial sac [Davidson p. 506, Robbins p. 538].
3. **Infectious & Inflammatory Pericarditis:**
    - Tuberculous pericarditis (major cause in developing nations), purulent/bacterial pericarditis, viral pericarditis, and uremic pericarditis in end-stage renal disease [Davidson p. 542, 544].
    - Autoimmune conditions: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Rheumatoid Arthritis, and Dressler's syndrome (post-MI autoimmune pericarditis) [Davidson p. 542].
4. **Malignancy (Metastatic Pericardial Effusion):**
    - Metastatic spread from lung carcinoma, breast carcinoma, Hodgkin/Non-Hodgkin lymphoma, or melanoma [Davidson p. 542].
5. **Iatrogenic Causes:**
    - Perforation of cardiac chambers or coronary arteries during percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), electrophysiology ablation, central venous catheter insertion, or temporary pacemaker placement [Davidson p. 544].

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Intrapericardial Pressure Surge:** Under physiological conditions, the normal pericardial sac contains ~15–50 mL of serous fluid. When fluid accumulates rapidly (even as little as 150–200 mL in acute hemopericardium) or exceeds the stretch compliance of the parietal pericardium in chronic effusions (>1000 mL), **intrapericardial pressure rises sharply** [Robbins p. 554].
2. **Diastolic Chamber Compression:** High intrapericardial pressure exceeds normal diastolic filling pressures of the thin-walled right atrium and right ventricle, causing **diastolic collapse of the right-sided cardiac chambers**[Davidson p. 544].
3. **Impaired Diastolic Filling & Equilization of Pressures:** Venous return to the heart is severely restricted. Diastolic filling pressures across all four chambers (RA, RV, LA, LV) elevate and **equalize** with intrapericardial pressure (~15–25 mmHg).
4. **Ventricular Interdependence & Pulsus Paradoxus:** Because the total heart volume is fixed within the rigid pericardial sac, inspiration increases venous return to the right heart, driving the interventricular septum to bulge sharply to the left. This restricts Left Ventricular (LV) filling and stroke volume during inspiration, producing **Pulsus Paradoxus** (systolic BP drop >10 mmHg on inspiration) [Davidson p. 448, 544].
5. **Obstructive Cardiogenic Shock:** Decreased LV end-diastolic volume causes a dramatic fall in stroke volume and cardiac output. Compensatory sympathetic activation induces intense peripheral vasoconstriction and tachycardia, but eventual uncorrected pump failure leads to profound hypotension, tissue hypoperfusion, and death [Davidson p. 544, Robbins p. 535].

## 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Severe Exertional / Rest Dyspnea:** Rapidly progressive breathlessness and tachypnea [Davidson p. 544].
- **Chest Tightness / Retrosternal Pressure:** Sensation of retrosternal oppression or dull ache [Davidson p. 544].
- **Anxiety & Restlessness:** Feeling of impending doom (_angor animi_) secondary to acute cerebral hypoperfusion [Davidson p. 544].
- **Fatigue & Dizziness / Syncope:** Due to profound low cardiac output and systemic hypotension [Davidson p. 544].

#### 1. Beck's Classic Diagnostic Triad [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hypotension:** Low systemic blood pressure with a **narrow pulse pressure** (e.g., 85/70 mmHg) due to severely reduced stroke volume.
2. **Grossly Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP):** Prominent neck vein distension due to obstructed venous entry into the right heart.
3. **Muffled / Distant Heart Sounds:** Muffled cardiac auscultation caused by the insulating layer of pericardial fluid surrounding the heart.

#### 2. Pulsus Paradoxus [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition:** An exaggerated decrease in systolic blood pressure of **>10 mmHg during normal inspiration**[Davidson p. 448, 544].
- **Clinical Bedside Detection:** Measured using a sphygmomanometer. As the cuff is slowly deflated, the point where Korotkoff sounds are heard _only during expiration_ is noted, followed by the point where sounds are heard continuously throughout both inspiration and expiration. The difference between these two pressures represents the degree of pulsus paradoxus.
- **Palpation:** In severe cases, the radial pulse becomes weak or completely impalpable during inspiration.

#### 3. Jugular Venous Pulse Waveform Analysis

- **Prominent 'x' Descent:** Preserved or accentuated systolic 'x' descent (representing atrial relaxation during ventricular systole).
- **Absent or Blunted 'y' Descent:** The diastolic 'y' descent is **absent or severely blunted** because rapid early diastolic filling of the right ventricle is completely restricted by high intrapericardial pressure [Davidson p. 443].
- **Kussmaul's Sign:** Paradoxical rise or failure of JVP to fall on inspiration (uncommon in classic tamponade, but seen in loculated effusion or constrictive-effusive pericarditis) [Davidson p. 544].

## 5. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Echocardiography (Gold Standard Bedside Investigation) [⭐ High-Yield]

Echocardiography is the definitive diagnostic modality for immediate bedside evaluation:

- **Pericardial Effusion:** Visualizes an echo-free (anechoic) space surrounding the heart [Davidson p. 544].
- **Right Ventricular (RV) Diastolic Collapse:** Early diastolic indentation or collapse of the thin RV free wall (highly specific sign) [Davidson p. 544].
- **Right Atrial (RA) Late-Diastolic Collapse:** End-diastolic collapse of the RA lasting >1/3 of the cardiac cycle (earliest sign).
- **Dilated Inferior Vena Cava (IVC):** Plethoric IVC with **loss of normal respiratory collapse** (<50% collapse on inspiration), reflecting severe systemic venous congestion.
- **Doppler Inflow Variation:** Marked respiratory variation in transvalvular flow (>25–30% drop in mitral inflow velocity during inspiration and >40% drop in tricuspid inflow during expiration).
- **"Swinging Heart":** To-and-fro swinging motion of the heart within a large circumferential effusion.

---

### B. 12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG)

1. **Low-Voltage QRS Complexes:** Reduced QRS amplitude (<5 mm in limb leads, <10 mm in precordial leads) caused by the insulating effect of fluid [Davidson p. 544].
2. **Electrical Alternans:** Pathognomonic beat-to-beat variation in QRS amplitude and axis caused by the swinging motion of the heart inside the fluid-filled sac [Davidson p. 544].
3. **Sinus Tachycardia:** Compensatory mechanism to maintain cardiac output [Davidson p. 544].

---

### C. Chest X-Ray (PA View)

- **Water-Bottle Heart / Flask-Shaped Silhouette:** Symmetric, globular enlargement of the cardiac silhouette with sharp margins (seen in large, subacute effusions >200–300 mL; chest X-ray may look entirely normal in rapid, small-volume acute hemopericardium) [Davidson p. 544].
- Clear lung fields (absence of pulmonary edema helps differentiate tamponade from acute LV heart failure).

## 7. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Cardiac tamponade is an **absolute medical emergency**. Immediate decompression of the pericardial space is mandatory to restore cardiac filling and systemic circulation.

### A. Emergency Medical Resuscitative Measures (Bridge to Decompression)

#### 1. Intravenous Volume Expansion (First-Line Bridge)

- **Name:** Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl Infusion).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (Davidson Textbook p. 206, 544). Increases intravascular volume and right ventricular filling pressure (preload), temporarily counteracting high intrapericardial pressure to maintain stroke volume and cardiac output.
    
    ```
    IV 0.9% Normal Saline → ↑ Intravascular Volume → ↑ Intracardiac Filling Pressure > Intrapericardial Pressure → Temporary Maintenance of Stroke Volume
    ```
    
- **Dose:** **500 to 1000 mL IV rapid bolus** of 0.9% Normal Saline.
- **Dosage / Route:** Rapid Intravenous infusion via wide-bore cannulae.
- **Frequency:** Single rapid bolus, repeatable based on BP response while setting up pericardiocentesis.
- **Features:** Crucial temporizing measure to prevent cardiac arrest while preparing equipment.
- **Side Effects:** Hypernatremia, hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis if massive volumes used.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon clinical/echocardiographic confirmation of tamponade with hypotension; stop once pericardiocentesis successfully drains the fluid.

#### 2. Inotropic Vasopressor Support

- **Name:** Dobutamine or Adrenaline (Epinephrine).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 132, 619). Stimulates cardiac β₁-adrenoceptors to increase myocardial contractility and heart rate, augmenting cardiac output.
- **Dose:** Dobutamine **2.5 to 10 μg/kg/min IV infusion**; or Adrenaline **2 to 10 μg/min IV infusion**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Continuous IV infusion via central or large peripheral line.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated if profound hypotension persists despite IV fluid resuscitation.

#### 3. STRICT CONTRAINDICATIONS & WARNINGS [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Diuretics (Furosemide) and Vasodilators (Nitrates):** **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**! Decreasing preload or systemic vascular resistance causes immediate, catastrophic loss of cardiac filling and sudden death.
- **Positive Pressure Mechanical Ventilation (IPPV / Intubation):** Avoid if possible. Positive intrathoracic pressure severely decreases venous return to the compressed right heart, triggering immediate cardiovascular collapse. If intubation is mandatory, give aggressive IV fluid boluses prior to induction.

---

### B. Definitive Emergency Decompression Procedures [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Percutaneous Needle Pericardiocentesis (Echocardiography-Guided)

- **Procedure:**
    1. Patient positioned in a 30° to 45° semi-recumbent position (allows fluid to collect inferiorly).
    2. Under real-time echocardiographic guidance, an 18-gauge long pericardiocentesis needle is inserted in the **subxiphoid region** (between the xiphoid process and left costal margin at a 30° to 45° angle, directed toward the left shoulder).
    3. A soft pigtail catheter is introduced over a guidewire into the pericardial sac and left on gravity drainage until output drops <25 mL/day.
- **Result:** Removal of even **as little as 20 to 50 mL of fluid** produces a dramatic drop in intrapericardial pressure, immediately restoring blood pressure and cardiac output!

#### 2. Surgical Decompression (Pericardial Window / Emergency Thoracotomy)

- **Indications:**
    1. Traumatic hemopericardium or free wall rupture post-MI.
    2. Type A Aortic Dissection with pericardial rupture (needle aspiration may worsen arterial bleeding; emergency surgical repair required) [Davidson p. 506, 544].
    3. Purulent, viscous, or loculated pericardial effusions.
    4. Recurrent malignant effusions.

## 8. Complications

1. **Obstructive Cardiogenic Shock & Death:** Secondary to complete cessation of diastolic filling.
2. **Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) Cardiac Arrest:** Cardiac electrical rhythm persists on monitor, but mechanical contraction is abolished due to extreme compression.
3. **Pericardiocentesis Complications:** Coronary artery laceration, right ventricular puncture/laceration, pneumothorax, ventricular arrhythmias, or vasovagal syncope [Davidson p. 544].
4. **Post-Decompression Pulmonary Edema:** Rapid evacuation of large effusions can cause acute right-sided volume overload overloading the pulmonary circulation.

---

## 9. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Removal of even **as little as 20 to 50 mL of fluid** during pericardiocentesis can be life-saving! Because the pericardial pressure-volume compliance curve is extremely steep at high volumes, aspirating a small volume produces a precipitous drop in intrapericardial pressure below right-sided diastolic pressure, resulting in an immediate, dramatic jump in blood pressure.

---

## 10. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **IV Furosemide (Diuretic)** or **Sublingual Nitrates** to a patient with suspected Cardiac Tamponade who presents with dyspnea, distended neck veins, and low blood pressure.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In cardiac tamponade, high intrapericardial pressure requires high systemic venous filling pressure (preload) to force blood into the compressed right heart.
- **Resolution:** **Diuretics and Nitrates are STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in Cardiac Tamponade**! Reducing preload destroys the critical filling pressure gradient, causing rapid circulatory collapse and cardiac arrest. Always give **IV Normal Saline boluses** as a supportive bridge to pericardiocentesis!

## 13. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Life-threatening compression of heart chambers due to high intrapericardial pressure from fluid/blood accumulation.
- **Classic Clinical Triad:** Beck's Triad = Hypotension + Raised JVP + Muffled heart sounds.
- **Key Bedside Sign:** Pulsus Paradoxus (systolic BP drop >10 mmHg during inspiration).
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Tool:** Echocardiography showing RV early-diastolic collapse and RA late-diastolic collapse.
- **ECG Hallmarks:** Low voltage QRS complexes + Electrical Alternans.
- **Definitive Treatment:** Emergency Echocardiogram-guided Subxiphoid Pericardiocentesis.
- **Strict Contraindication:** Diuretics, Nitrates, and forced positive pressure ventilation.
