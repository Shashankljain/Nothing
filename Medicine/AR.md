# Aortic Regurgitation

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Aortic Regurgitation (AR)**—also termed **Aortic Incompetence** or **Aortic Insufficiency**—is a valvular heart disorder characterized by the abnormal retrograde backflow of blood from the aorta into the Left Ventricle (LV) during diastole, resulting from incompetence of the aortic valve cusps or pathological dilatation of the aortic root.

---

### A. Primary Valvular Causes

1. **Congenital Bicuspid Aortic Valve:** The most common congenital structural cause, predisposing to early fibrous degradation, calcification, or prolapse.
2. **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD):** Post-inflammatory scarring leads to cuspal retraction, fibrotic thickening, and failure of central coaptation during diastole.
3. **Infective Endocarditis (IE):** Bacterial vegetation erosion or direct perforation of an aortic valve cusp causes **acute, severe, life-threatening AR**.
4. **Traumatic / Senile Degeneration:** Blunt chest trauma or age-related calcific leaflet tearing.

### B. Aortic Root & Wall Dilatation Causes

1. **Marfan's Syndrome & Hereditary Connective Tissue Disorders:** Fibrillin-1 deficiency causes cystic medial degeneration, progressive aortic root dilatation, and separation of aortic valve cusps.
2. **Type A Aortic Dissection:** An intimal tear dissecting retrogradely into the aortic root disrupts the attachment of valve cusps.
3. **Syphilitic Aortitis (Tertiary Syphilis):** Endarteritis obliterans of the vasa vasorum leads to calcification and marked aneurysmal dilatation of the ascending aorta, expanding the aortic valve ring.
4. **Seronegative Arthropathies:** Ankylosing spondylitis, Reiter's syndrome, and Rheumatoid Arthritis causing aortitis.
5. **Systemic Hypertension:** Long-standing hypertension causing secondary ectasia of the ascending aorta.

## 3. Classification (Acute vs Chronic AR) [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Acute Aortic Regurgitation|Chronic Aortic Regurgitation|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Onset**|Sudden, precipitous (minutes to hours)|Insidious (years to decades)|
|**Primary Causes**|Infective endocarditis (cusp perforation), Type A aortic dissection, trauma|Rheumatic heart disease, Bicuspid valve, Marfan's syndrome, Syphilis|
|**LV Size & Compliance**|Normal LV chamber size; **low LV compliance**|**Massively dilated LV** ("cor bovinum"); high LV compliance|
|**Pulse Pressure**|Normal or near-normal pulse pressure|**Markedly widened pulse pressure**|
|**Classic Peripheral Signs**|**ABSENT or MASKED** due to tachycardia|**PROMINENT** (water-hammer pulse, Corrigan's, Quincke's, etc.)|
|**Clinical Presentation**|**Flash pulmonary edema & cardiogenic shock**|Long asymptomatic phase, palpitations, exertional dyspnea, angina|

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Diastolic Regurgitation & Volume Overload:** Incompetence of the aortic valve allows blood to flow retrogradely from the high-pressure aorta into the Left Ventricle during diastole. The LV receives both normal pulmonary venous return from the left atrium AND the regurgitant aortic volume.
2. **Left Ventricular Eccentric Hypertrophy:** Total stroke volume increases significantly (often doubling to 150–200 mL/beat). To accommodate this massive volume overload, the LV undergoes compensatory **eccentric hypertrophy**(addition of sarcomeres in series) and chamber dilatation.
3. **Wide Pulse Pressure & Peripheral Hyperdynamic Circulation:** A huge forward stroke volume produces a high peak Systolic Blood Pressure (e.g., 160–180 mmHg). Rapid diastolic run-off back into the LV and out to the peripheral circulation causes a precipitous drop in Diastolic Blood Pressure (e.g., <40–50 mmHg), creating a **wide pulse pressure** and hyperdynamic peripheral circulatory state.
4. **Myocardial Ischemia (Angina Pectoris):** Coronary artery perfusion occurs predominantly during diastole. A low aortic diastolic pressure drastically reduces coronary perfusion pressure. Combined with increased oxygen demand from a massively hypertrophied LV, this induces subendocardial ischemia and **angina pectoris** (especially nocturnal angina).
5. **LV Decompensation & Heart Failure:** Over time, myocyte exhaustion and interstitial fibrosis impair contractility. Left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) rises, leading to **pulmonary venous congestion**, dyspnea, and overt heart failure.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Long Asymptomatic Phase:** Patients with chronic AR remain symptom-free for decades due to effective compensatory LV chamber expansion.
2. **Pounding Heartbeat / Palpitations:** Prominent awareness of the heartbeat (especially when lying on the left side) caused by the hyperdynamic stroke volume striking the chest wall.
3. **Exertional Dyspnea & Fatigue:** Gradually progressive breathlessness on exertion as LV compliance decreases and LVEDP rises.
4. **Orthopnea & Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND):** Features of advanced left ventricular decompensation and pulmonary venous congestion.
5. **Angina Pectoris (Nocturnal Angina):** Anginal chest pain occurring at rest or night when heart rate slows, prolonging diastole and worsening diastolic blood pressure drop and coronary hypoperfusion.

#### 1. Pulse & Arterial Blood Pressure [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Collapsing / Water-Hammer Pulse (Corrigan's Pulse):** High-amplitude bounding pulse with a rapid upstroke and precipitous collapse. Best demonstrated by palpating the radial artery with the patient's arm elevated above heart level.
- **Wide Pulse Pressure:** Elevated Systolic BP (e.g., 160–180 mmHg) with markedly reduced or zero Diastolic BP (e.g., 160/40 or 150/0 mmHg).
- **Pulsus Bisferiens:** Double-peaked systolic pulse contour felt in major arteries (combining ejection and backflow components).

#### 2. Eponymous Peripheral Vascular Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Corrigan's Sign:** Prominent, visible dancing/pulsating carotid arteries in the neck.
2. **De Musset's Sign:** Rhythmic nodding of the head synchronized with each heartbeat.
3. **Quincke's Sign:** Alternating capillary flushing and blanching visible in the nail beds upon gentle pressure.
4. **Duroziez's Sign:** A double murmur ("to-and-fro" systolic and diastolic bruit) heard over the femoral artery when compressed proximal and distal with a stethoscope.
5. **Traube's Sign ("Pistol-Shot" Sound):** Booming "pistol-shot" sound heard over the femoral arteries during systole.
6. **Hill's Sign:** Popliteal cuff systolic BP exceeding brachial cuff systolic BP by **>20 mmHg** (mild: 20–40 mmHg; moderate: 40–60 mmHg; severe: **>60 mmHg**).
7. **Müller's Sign:** Rhythmic pulsation of the uvula and soft palate during systole.
8. **Becker's Sign:** Visible pulsation of retinal arterioles on fundoscopy.
9. **Mayne's Sign:** Drop in diastolic BP >15 mmHg upon elevating the arm.

#### 3. Precordial Inspection & Palpation

- **Apex Beat Location:** Displaced laterally and inferiorly (e.g., 6th or 7th intercostal space at the anterior axillary line) due to massive LV enlargement.
- **Apex Beat Character:** Hyperdynamic, forceful, and **"thrusting"** in character (volume overload apex; contrasts with the discrete pressure-overload apex of aortic stenosis).

#### 4. Auscultation (Classic Murmurs) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Early Diastolic Murmur (EDM) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Quality & Pitch:** High-pitched, blowing, decrescendo diastolic murmur beginning immediately after S2.
    - **Best Heard:** Best audited along the left sternal border at the 3rd/4th intercostal space (**Erb's point**) using the diaphragm of the stethoscope, with the patient **sitting forward in held expiration**. _(Note: If AR is due to primary aortic root dilatation, the EDM is heard best along the right sternal border)._
2. **Midsystolic Ejection Flow Murmur:**
    - A mid-systolic flow murmur heard at the aortic area due to the massive forward stroke volume ejected across the aortic valve (does not imply coexisting aortic stenosis).
3. **Austin Flint Murmur [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - A soft, low-pitched, rumbling mid-diastolic or presystolic murmur heard at the cardiac apex.
    - **Mechanism:** Produced by the severe regurgitant aortic jet impinging directly onto the anterior leaflet of the mitral valve, causing partial closure and functional mitral stenosis.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH):** Tall R waves in V5–V6 and deep S waves in V1–V2 (Sokolow-Lyon criteria >35 mm) with left axis deviation.
- **Volume Overload Pattern:** Prominent Q waves with tall, upright T waves in lateral leads (V5–V6, I, aVL).

### B. Chest X-Ray

- **Cardiomegaly:** Marked enlargement of the left ventricle producing a classic **"boot-shaped" heart** (_coeur en sabot_).
- **Aortic Dilatation:** Prominence and dilatation of the ascending aorta and aortic knuckle.
- **Pulmonary Congestion:** Hazy perihilar opacities in acute or decompensated AR.

### C. Echocardiography & Doppler (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **2D & Color-Flow Doppler Echocardiography:**
    - Direct visualization of the regurgitant jet area into the LV.
    - Measures **vena contracta width (≥6 mm indicates severe AR)** and regurgitant fraction (>50%).
    - Demonstrates high-frequency diastolic fluttering of the anterior mitral valve leaflet.
    - Measures LV End-Systolic Diameter (LVESD) and LV End-Diastolic Diameter (LVEDD).
- **Indication for Surgery Threshold:** Asymptomatic severe AR with **LVESD ≥50–55 mm** or **LVEF ≤50%**.

### D. Cardiac Catheterisation & Aortography

- Confirms severity of regurgitation, quantifies aortic root dilatation, and evaluates coexisting coronary artery disease prior to surgery.

### A. Medical Management

- **Afterload-Reducing Vasodilators:** Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine 5–10 mg OD) or ACE Inhibitors (Enalapril 2.5–10 mg BD) to reduce systemic vascular resistance, enhancing forward stroke volume and reducing regurgitant volume.
- **Diuretics (Furosemide 20–80 mg PO/IV):** Indicated for pulmonary venous congestion and fluid overload.
- **Etiological Therapy:** Appropriate parenteral antibiotics for Infective Endocarditis or Penicillin G for Syphilitic Aortitis.

### B. Surgical Intervention (Definitive Treatment) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Aortic Valve Replacement (AVR):** Surgical removal of diseased valve cusps and placement of a mechanical or bioprosthetic valve.
- **Composite Aortic Root Replacement (Bentall Procedure):** Indicated when AR is caused by marked aortic root dilatation (e.g., Marfan's syndrome or aortic aneurysm).
- **Surgical Indications [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    1. All symptomatic patients with severe AR (dyspnea, angina, or heart failure).
    2. Asymptomatic severe AR with Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction **LVEF ≤50%**.
    3. Asymptomatic severe AR with Left Ventricular End-Systolic Diameter **LVESD ≥50 to 55 mm**.

## 9. Complications

1. **Left Ventricular Failure:** Progressive myocyte decompensation leading to irreversible HFrEF.
2. **Infective Endocarditis:** Turbulent high-velocity regurgitant jet damages endocardium, predisposing to bacterial colonization.
3. **Aortic Dissection & Rupture:** In patients with root dilatation (Marfan's or aortic aneurysms).
4. **Sudden Cardiac Death:** Secondary to fatal ventricular arrhythmias in dilated, ischemic myocardium.

---

## 10. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always differentiate the **Austin Flint Murmur** of severe Aortic Regurgitation from the **Mid-Diastolic Murmur of Organic Mitral Stenosis**!
> 
> - **Austin Flint Murmur:** Soft, low-pitched apical mid-diastolic murmur; **NO Opening Snap (OS)**, **NO Loud S1**, and disappears following Aortic Valve Replacement!
> - **Organic Mitral Stenosis:** Low-pitched rumbling diastolic murmur at apex; **Loud S1 PRESENT**, **Opening Snap PRESENT**, and exhibits **P Mitrale** on ECG!

## 11. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Expecting to find classic peripheral vascular signs (water-hammer pulse, wide pulse pressure) in a patient presenting with **ACUTE Severe Aortic Regurgitation** (e.g., secondary to aortic dissection or infective endocarditis).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In acute AR, the un-adapted, non-compliant LV cannot dilate suddenly to accommodate the regurgitant volume. LVEDP rises precipitously, cutting off regurgitant flow early in diastole.
- **Resolution:** In **Acute AR**, pulse pressure is **normal or near-normal**, the early diastolic murmur is **short or soft**, and classic peripheral signs are **COMPLETELY ABSENT**! The patient presents directly with severe tachycardia, flash pulmonary edema, and cardiogenic shock.

## 13. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

### Mnemonic for Eponymous Peripheral Signs of AR: "C-A-R-D-I-A-C"

- **C** — **C**orrigan's pulse (water-hammer pulse) / **C**apillary pulsations (Quincke's sign)
- **A** — **A**ustin Flint murmur (functional mitral stenosis)
- **R** — **R**hythmic head nodding (De Musset's sign)
- **D** — **D**uroziez's double murmur over femoral artery
- **I** — **I**ncreased popliteal BP (Hill's sign >20 mmHg)
- **A** — **A**ortic shooting sound ("pistol-shot" Traube's sign)
- **C** — **C**ervical & uvular pulsations (Müller's sign)

### Memory Palace: "The High-Pressure Water Tower"

Visualize walking through 4 loci at a bursting water tower:

1. **Loci 1 (The Pounding Radial Pipe):** A pipe expanding and collapsing violently under your hand when raised overhead (**Water-hammer / Corrigan's pulse**).
2. **Loci 2 (The Nodding Statue):** A stone statue rhythmically nodding its head with every pulse wave (**De Musset's sign**).
3. **Loci 3 (The Flashing Glass Window):** A glass pane flushing red and white at its edges (**Quincke's capillary pulsation**).
4. **Loci 4 (The High-Pitched Blowing Valve):** A relief valve making a high-pitched, blowing, decrescendo hiss as water leaks backward (**Early diastolic decrescendo murmur**).

### Quick Revision

- **Primary Causes:** Bicuspid valve, Rheumatic heart disease, Infective endocarditis, Aortic dissection, Marfan's syndrome.
- **Hemodynamic Hallmark:** Wide pulse pressure with high systolic and low/zero diastolic BP.
- **Key Auscultatory Signs:** High-pitched blowing early diastolic decrescendo murmur at Erb's point + Austin Flint apical murmur.
- **Classic Peripheral Signs:** Water-hammer pulse, Corrigan's, Quincke's, Duroziez's, Traube's, and Hill's sign.
- **Surgical Indications:** Symptomatic severe AR, or asymptomatic with LVEF ≤50% or LVESD ≥50–55 mm.

> **Aortic regurgitation is a valvular or aortic root disorder characterized by retrograde diastolic blood flow from the aorta into the left ventricle, producing massive volume overload, eccentric hypertrophy, a hyperdynamic wide pulse pressure circulation with classic peripheral vascular signs, and a high-pitched blowing early diastolic murmur.**
