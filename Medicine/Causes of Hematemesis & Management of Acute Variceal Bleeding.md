[[C22- Hepatology]]


## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hematemesis:** The vomiting of overt blood or altered blood ("coffee-ground" material) originating from a bleeding source in the gastrointestinal tract proximal to the ligament of Treitz (Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding) [Robbins 1 p. 159, Robbins 2 p. 199, 205].
- **Acute Variceal Bleeding:** Massive, life-threatening upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage resulting from the spontaneous rupture of dilated subepithelial/submucosal esophagogastric varices, driven by severe portal hypertension with a Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) ≥12 mmHg [Robbins 2 p. 201, 203, Kdt 3 p. 643].

### A. Esophageal Causes

1. **Esophageal Varices [⭐ Major Cause ~20–30%]:** Rupture of dilated submucosal veins secondary to portal hypertension (alcoholic cirrhosis, chronic viral hepatitis, schistosomiasis) [Robbins 2 p. 201, 226].
2. **Mallory-Weiss Syndrome (~10%):** Longitudinal mucosal tears near the gastroesophageal junction caused by violent retching or vomiting following acute alcohol intoxication [Robbins 2 p. 199].
3. **Erosive / Reflux Esophagitis & Severe Esophageal Ulcers** [Robbins 2 p. 200].
4. **Esophageal Carcinoma (Squamous Cell Carcinoma or Adenocarcinoma) & Aorto-Esophageal Fistula**[Robbins 2 p. 200].
5. **Boerhaave Syndrome:** Transmural esophageal perforation with severe chest pain and mediastinitis [Robbins 2 p. 199].

### B. Gastric Causes

1. **Peptic Ulcer Disease (Gastric Ulcer) [⭐ Most Common Cause ~50%]:** Erosion into underlying mucosal and submucosal blood vessels [Robbins 2 p. 205, 215].
2. **Acute Gastritis & Stress-Related Mucosal Injury:** Stress ulcers in critically ill ICU patients (Curling ulcers in severe burns; Cushing ulcers in intracranial injury) [Robbins 2 p. 205, 208].
3. **NSAID, Aspirin, & Alcohol-Induced Gastropathy:** Direct cellular injury and inhibition of protective prostaglandin synthesis (PGE₂) [Robbins 2 p. 205, Kdt 1 p. 93].
4. **Dieulafoy Lesion:** Abnormally large, tortuous submucosal arteriole eroding through gastric mucosa near the lesser curvature [Robbins 2 p. 207].
5. **Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia (GAVE / "Watermelon Stomach"):** Longitudinal mucosal stripes of ectatic mucosal capillaries containing microthrombi [Robbins 2 p. 207].
6. **Gastric Adenocarcinoma & Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GIST).**

### C. Duodenal Causes

1. **Duodenal Ulcer:** Erosion into the gastroduodenal artery along the posterior wall of the first part of the duodenum [Robbins 2 p. 213, 215].
2. **Duodenitis & Periampullary / Ampullary Carcinoma.**
3. **Aorto-Enteric Fistula:** Erosion of a prosthetic aortic graft into the 3rd or 4th part of the duodenum.

### D. Vascular & Systemic Coagulopathic Causes

1. **Thrombocytopenia & Platelet Dysfunction:** Severe ITP, TTP, HUS, or aplastic anemia [Robbins 1 p. 159, Robbins 3 p. 184].
2. **Coagulopathies & Anticoagulant Toxicity:** Warfarin overdose, Factor VIII/IX deficiency (Hemophilia A/B), DIC, or liver synthetic failure [Robbins 1 p. 159, Kdt 3 p. 662, Kdt4 p. 142].
3. **Hereditary Hemorrhagic Telangiectasia (Osler-Weber-Rendu Disease):** Autosomal dominant vascular malformations causing mucocutaneous and GI bleeding [Robbins 2 p. 171].

## 3. Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding is broadly classified based on source into two clinical categories:

|Feature|Variceal Upper GI Bleeding|Non-Variceal Upper GI Bleeding|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Proportion**|~20% to 30% of total cases|~70% to 80% of total cases|
|**Primary Causes**|Esophageal and Gastric Varices|Peptic Ulcer Disease, Mallory-Weiss tears, Gastritis, Dieulafoy lesion|
|**Nature of Bleeding**|Sudden, massive, painless arterial/venous gush|Variable (mild oozing to catastrophic ulcer hemorrhage)|
|**Mortality Risk**|**High (~15% to 20% per episode)** [Robbins 2 p. 203]|Lower (~5% to 10% overall)|
|**Background Disease**|**Portal Hypertension / Cirrhosis** [Robbins 2 p. 201]|NSAID use, _H. pylori_ infection, physiological stress|

## 4. Pathophysiology of Variceal Bleeding (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Portal Hypertension:** Sinusoidal or pre-sinusoidal obstruction (cirrhosis) elevates intrahepatic vascular resistance, causing portal venous pressure to rise [Robbins 2 p. 201].
2. **Portosystemic Collateral Formation:** When the Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) rises >5 mmHg, blood is diverted through low-resistance collateral channels connecting the left gastric (coronary) vein to the intercostal/azygos veins at the esophagogastric junction [Robbins 2 p. 201, 226].
3. **Submucosal Venous Congestion:** High pressure expands and distends thin-walled submucosal esophageal veins (**Esophageal Varices**) [Robbins 2 p. 201].
4. **Critical Rupture Threshold:** When **HVPG reaches ≥12 mmHg**, excessive vessel wall tension causes sudden spontaneous rupture of dilated varices, triggering explosive, painless hematemesis [Robbins 2 p. 201, 203].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Symptoms:**
    - Sudden, painless, profuse vomiting of bright red blood (hematemesis) or dark "coffee-ground" fluid.
    - Passage of dark, black, tarry stools (melena).
    - Postural lightheadedness, weakness, syncope, and confusion [Davidson 1 p. 7, Robbins 2 p. 199].
- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Hemodynamic Shock:** Tachycardia (pulse >100/min), hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg), cold clammy extremities, and oliguria [Davidson 1 p. 7].
    - **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease:** Jaundice, spider angiomas, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, ascites, splenomegaly, caput medusae, and flapping tremor (asterixis) [Robbins 2 p. 171, 226].

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Emergent Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (UGIE) [⭐ Gold Standard]:** Diagnostic and therapeutic; performed within 12 hours of presentation to identify the exact bleeding site, confirm variceal rupture (active spurting, oozing, or red wale marks), and execute endoscopic band ligation [Davidson 1 p. 52, Robbins 2 p. 203].
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Serial hemoglobin and hematocrit monitoring; thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL) indicates hypersplenism [Robbins 3 p. 182, 225].
3. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs) & Coagulation Profile:** Decreased serum albumin, elevated bilirubin, prolonged PT/INR (>1.5) reflecting hepatic synthetic failure [Robbins 3 p. 224, Kdt 3 p. 662].
4. **Blood Grouping & Cross-matching:** Mandatory preparation for blood product transfusions.
5. **Abdominal Ultrasonography (USG) with Doppler:** Demonstrates portal vein enlargement (>13 mm), splenomegaly, ascites, or portal vein thrombosis.

### A. Immediate Resuscitation & Stabilization Protocols

1. **Airway Protection:** Keep patient semi-recumbent; intubate comatose patients to prevent pulmonary aspiration of blood [Davidson 1 p. 6, Robbins 2 p. 165].
2. **Restrictive Transfusion Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]:** Fluid resuscitation with crystalloids; transfuse packed RBCs (PRBCs) strictly targeting a **restrictive Hemoglobin threshold of 7.0 to 8.0 g/dL**. _Over-transfusing increases portal venous pressure and directly triggers rebleeding!_
3. **Prophylactic Antibiotic Therapy:** Administer **IV Ceftriaxone 1 g OD** for 7 days to prevent SBP, septicemia, and rebleeding [Kdt 4 p. 140, 142].
4. **Acid Suppression:** High-dose IV PPI (**Pantoprazole 80 mg IV bolus followed by 8 mg/hr infusion**) to stabilize clot formation [Kdt 3 p. 702].

---

### B. Vasoactive Pharmacotherapy (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Terlipressin [⭐ First-Line Gold Standard]

- **Name:** Terlipressin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 643). Synthetic prodrug of vasopressin (8-lysine vasopressin). Slowly cleaved in vivo by endothelial peptidases to release active vasopressin. Acts on **vascular V₁ receptors** on splanchnic vascular smooth muscle, producing potent, selective vasoconstriction of mesenteric arterioles. This reduces splanchnic blood inflow into the portal system, lowers portal venous pressure, and promotes hemostasis.
    
    ```
    Terlipressin → Cleaved to Vasopressin → Splanchnic V1 Receptor Activation → Splanchnic Arteriolar Vasoconstriction → Reduced Portal Pressure & Hemostasis
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Vasopressin V₁ receptors.
- **Dose:** **2 mg IV bolus every 4 hours** initially for 24 to 48 hours; once bleeding is controlled, reduce dose to **1 mg IV every 4 hours** for up to 3 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Frequency:** Every 4 hours.
- **Features:** First-line vasoactive agent; **the only drug proven to reduce overall mortality** in acute variceal bleeding [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Side Effects:** Abdominal cramps, diarrhea, hyponatremia, peripheral tissue ischemia, hypertension, bradycardia, myocardial ischemia [Kdt 3 p. 644].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon clinical suspicion of variceal bleed; stop 24 to 48 hours after complete hemostasis (maximum duration 5 days).

#### 2. Octreotide

- **Name:** Octreotide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 94, 95, 643, 644). Synthetic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin. Binds to **somatostatin receptors (SSTR2/SSTR5)** on splanchnic vessels, inhibiting the release of vasodilatory peptides (such as glucagon and VIP). Induces selective splanchnic vasoconstriction, lowering portal vein pressure.
    
    ```
    Octreotide → Somatostatin SSTR2/SSTR5 Binding → Inhibits Glucagon & VIP Release → Selective Splanchnic Vasoconstriction → ↓ Portal Pressure
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Somatostatin SSTR2 and SSTR5 receptors.
- **Dose:** **50 μg IV rapid bolus**, followed immediately by a **continuous IV infusion of 25 to 50 μg/hour** for 2 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** IV bolus followed by continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion over 48 to 120 hours.
- **Features:** Excellent safety profile; preferred alternative when Terlipressin is unavailable or contraindicated in ischemic heart disease [Kdt 3 p. 94, 644].
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, abdominal cramps, transient hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia, gallstones on long-term use [Kdt 3 p. 95].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at presentation; maintain for 2 to 5 days.

#### 3. Propranolol (Secondary Prophylaxis — Start AFTER Recovery)

- **Name:** Propranolol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 157, Kdt 3 p. 585). Non-selective beta-blocker. Dual mechanism: **β₁-blockade** reduces cardiac output; **β₂-blockade** blocks splanchnic vasodilation, allowing **unopposed α₁-adrenergic vasoconstriction** of splanchnic arterioles, reducing portal inflow and keeping HVPG <10 mmHg.
- **Dose:** **20 mg to 40 mg PO BD**, titrated every 3 to 5 days to achieve a resting heart rate of **55 to 60 beats/min**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO).
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (BD).
- **Features:** Gold-standard oral drug for long-term secondary prophylaxis to prevent rebleeding.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, bronchospasm, cold extremities.
- **When to Start / Stop:** **DO NOT start during acute active bleeding!** Initiate 5 to 7 days after acute bleeding has completely resolved; continue **indefinitely**.

---

### C. Endoscopic & Rescue Interventions

1. **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL / Banding) [⭐ Gold Standard]:**
    - Performed during UGIE within 12 hours. Elastic rubber bands are deployed around variceal trunks, inducing ischemic thrombosis, sloughing, and fibrous obliteration [Davidson 1 p. 51, Robbins 2 p. 202].
2. **Balloon Tamponade (Bridge Therapy):**
    - **Sengstaken-Blakemore (S-B) Tube:** Double-balloon catheter (gastric balloon inflated with 200–250 mL air; esophageal balloon inflated to 30–45 mmHg pressure).
    - Provides direct mechanical compression over bleeding varices.
    - _Precaution:_ Temporary bridge therapy only; **do not leave inflated >24 hours** to avoid esophageal tissue necrosis and perforation [Robbins 2 p. 199, 202].
3. **Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS) [Rescue Therapy]:**
    - Percutaneous expandable metal stent placed between hepatic vein and intrahepatic portal vein branch, decompressing portal pressure [Robbins 2 p. 202]. Reserved for refractory bleeding.

## 9. Complications

1. **Hypovolemic Shock & Cardiac Arrest:** From rapid blood loss [Davidson 1 p. 7, Robbins 1 p. 157].
2. **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Digestion of blood proteins in the gut surges systemic ammonia (NH₃) levels [Robbins 2 p. 224].
3. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) & Sepsis** [Robbins 2 p. 225].
4. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Oliguric renal failure driven by extreme splanchnic vasodilation.
5. **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Secondary to vomiting blood with altered consciousness [Davidson 1 p. 12, Robbins 2 p. 165].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Acute variceal hemorrhage carries a **15% to 20% 6-week mortality rate** [Robbins 2 p. 203].
- Over **50% of untreated survivors experience recurrent hemorrhage within 1 year** [Robbins 2 p. 203].

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** When resuscitating a patient with acute variceal bleeding, **NEVER over-transfuse to achieve a normal hemoglobin level (>9–10 g/dL)**! Over-transfusing with PRBCs or aggressive crystalloids expands intravascular volume, sharply raises central venous and portal pressures, and directly breaks fresh hemostatic clots—precipitating catastrophic, fatal **rebleeding of esophageal varices** [Robbins 2 p. 203]! Always enforce a **restrictive transfusion strategy targeting a Hemoglobin of 7.0 to 8.0 g/dL**!

---

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Propranolol** or other Beta-Blockers during the **ACUTE active bleeding episode** of esophageal varices.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Beta-blockers reduce cardiac output and blood pressure. Administering them during acute ongoing hemorrhage worsens hypovolemic shock and blunts compensatory tachycardia.
- **Resolution:** **Propranolol is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED during acute active bleeding!** Management during acute bleeding requires **Vasoactive Vasoconstrictors (Terlipressin / Octreotide)** and **EVL**. Propranolol is initiated ONLY for secondary prophylaxis **5 to 7 days AFTER acute bleeding has completely stopped** [Kdt 1 p. 157, Kdt 3 p. 585].

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Hematemesis = vomiting of blood/coffee-ground material from upper GI tract (proximal to ligament of Treitz) [Robbins 2 p. 199].
- **Key Causes:** Peptic Ulcer Disease (most common non-variceal), Esophageal Varices (most common variceal), Mallory-Weiss tears, NSAID/Stress gastritis, Dieulafoy lesion [Robbins 2 p. 199, 201, 205, 207].
- **First-Line Vasoactive Drug:** **Terlipressin** (2 mg IV q4h) — only drug proven to reduce mortality in variceal bleeding [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Gold Standard Endoscopic Treatment:** **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL)** within 12 hours [Davidson 1 p. 51, Robbins 2 p. 203].
- **Transfusion Threshold:** Restrictive target Hb of **7.0 to 8.0 g/dL** to prevent portal pressure spikes [Robbins 2 p. 203].
- **Prophylaxis Rule:** Propranolol is for **prevention/secondary prophylaxis AFTER recovery**, never during acute bleeding [Kdt 1 p. 157].
