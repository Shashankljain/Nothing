# Myasthenia Gravis — Etiology, Clinical Features, Investigations, and Management

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Myasthenia Gravis** is an autoimmune neuromuscular junction disorder characterized by painless, fluctuating weakness and easy fatigability of skeletal muscles upon repeated activity, with partial or complete recovery after rest [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 169]. It is caused by autoantibodies directed primarily against **postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (AChR / N_M)** or sarcolemmal **muscle-specific receptor tyrosine kinase (MuSK)** at the motor endplate [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 170].

---

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Autoimmune Pathogenesis

- **Anti-AChR Autoantibodies (~85% of cases):** Pathogenic IgG autoantibodies directed against the α-subunit of postsynaptic nicotinic N_M acetylcholine receptors [Robbins 1 p. 136, Robbins 5 p. 170].
- **Anti-MuSK Autoantibodies (~10–15% of cases):** Autoantibodies targeting muscle-specific receptor tyrosine kinase, essential for AChR clustering on the postsynaptic membrane [Robbins 5 p. 170].
- **Anti-LRP4 Antibodies:** Present in a subset of seronegative cases [Robbins 5 p. 170].

---

### B. Thymic Pathology Association [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Thymic Follicular Hyperplasia (65% to 75% of cases):** Characterized by the appearance of B-cell germinal centers within the thymus; predominant in younger female patients [Robbins 3 p. 147, Robbins 5 p. 173].
2. **Thymoma (10% to 15% of cases):** Epithelial tumor of the thymus gland; more prevalent in older male patients [Robbins 3 p. 148, Robbins 5 p. 173].
3. **Thymic Myoid Cells:** Stromal myoid cells in the thymus express muscle-like AChR surface antigens, acting as the primary site of autoantibody sensitization [Kdt 1 p. 121, Robbins 5 p. 173].

---

### C. Bimodal Age Distribution & Triggers

- **Bimodal Peak:** Young adults (ages 20 to 30 years; Female : Male ratio ≈2:1) and older adults (ages 60 to 70 years; Male predominance) [Robbins 5 p. 170].
- **Exacerbating Drug Triggers:** Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin), D-penicillamine, Beta-blockers, Procainamide, Quinine, and Neuromuscular blocking agents [Kdt 1 p. 122, Medicine Marrow E8 Vol 3 p. 764].

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Autoantibody Production:** Sensitized B-cells produce circulating IgG autoantibodies against postsynaptic N_M acetylcholine receptors [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 170].
2. **Mechanisms of Postsynaptic Receptor Loss:**
    - **Complement-Mediated Membrane Destruction:** Anti-AChR antibodies fix complement (C5b–C9 membrane attack complex), causing focal lysis and flattening/simplification of postsynaptic endplate folds [Robbins 1 p. 136, Robbins 5 p. 171].
    - **Accelerated Receptor Endocytosis:** Antibody cross-linking speeds up endocytosis and lysosomal degradation of AChRs [Robbins 1 p. 136, Robbins 5 p. 171].
    - **Functional Receptor Blockade:** Direct steric hinderance of the acetylcholine binding site [Robbins 1 p. 136, Robbins 5 p. 171].
3. **Subthreshold Endplate Potential (EPP):** Available functional N_M receptors drop to **≤1/3 of normal** [Kdt 1 p. 120]. During repetitive nerve activity, presynaptic acetylcholine release naturally declines; in MG, EPP falls below the threshold required to trigger a muscle action potential, causing progressive muscle fatigue [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 171].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Cardinal Symptom

- **Fluctuating Muscle Weakness & Easy Fatigability:** Muscle power is normal or near-normal upon waking in the morning, but progressively deteriorates with sustained activity or toward the evening (**worsens with exertion, improves with rest**) [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 174].

---

1. **Extraocular & Eyelid Muscles [Initial Site in >60–80%]:**
    - **Ptosis:** Asymmetric drooping of upper eyelids, accentuated by sustained upward gaze (>45 seconds) [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 174].
    - **Diplopia:** Binocular double vision due to extraocular muscle paresis [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 174].
    - **Pupillary Reflexes:** **STRICTLY NORMAL (Pupils are spared)**, as autonomic smooth muscles are unaffected [Davidson 1 p. 1088, Robbins 5 p. 174].
2. **Bulbar & Facial Muscles:**
    - **Dysphagia & Chewing Fatigue:** Difficulty swallowing liquids/solids and jaw fatigue while chewing food [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 174].
    - **Dysarthria & Rhinolalia:** Speech becomes slurred and nasal after prolonged conversation [Kdt 1 p. 120].
    - **Apathetic / Snarling Facies:** Inability to smile normally due to facial muscle weakness [Kdt 1 p. 120].
3. **Limb & Trunk Muscles:**
    - **Symmetrical Proximal Muscle Weakness:** Arms affected more than legs (difficulty combing hair, reaching overhead, or rising from a chair) [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 174, 179].
    - **Neck Extensor Weakness:** Causes "head drop" [Robbins 5 p. 174].
4. **Respiratory Muscles (Myasthenic Crisis) [⭐ Medical Emergency]:** Weakness of the diaphragm and intercostal muscles leading to acute respiratory failure [Kdt 1 p. 121, Robbins 5 p. 174].

## 6. Diagnosis & Laboratory Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Bedside & Pharmacological Tests

1. **Edrophonium (Tensilon) Test [⭐ Diagnostic Ameliorative Test]:**
    - **Procedure:** Administer 2 mg IV test dose of Edrophonium (short-acting AChE inhibitor). If no adverse reaction occurs within 30–60 seconds, administer the remaining 8 mg IV [Kdt 1 p. 121, 997].
    - **Positive Result:** Rapid (within 30 seconds), dramatic, transient (lasts 5 to 10 minutes) improvement in muscle strength and reversal of ptosis/diplopia [Kdt 1 p. 121, 997].
2. **Neostigmine Test:** **1.5 mg IM/IV Neostigmine** co-administered with **Atropine 0.6 mg IV** (to block muscarinic side effects); produces improvement lasting 1 to 2 hours if Edrophonium is unavailable [Kdt 1 p. 122, 997].
3. **Ice Pack Test:** Application of an ice pack over the ptotic eyelid for 2 minutes results in significant improvement of ptosis (cold temperature inhibits acetylcholinesterase enzyme activity) [Kdt 1 p. 121].

---

### B. Serological Autoantibody Testing [⭐ Gold Standard Confirmation]

- **Serum Anti-AChR Antibodies:** Highly specific (>99%); positive in **85% of generalized MG** and **50% of ocular MG** [Kdt 1 p. 122, Robbins 5 p. 170].
- **Serum Anti-MuSK Antibodies:** Positive in 40% to 50% of AChR-seronegative patients [Robbins 5 p. 170].

---

### C. Electrophysiological Studies

1. **Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS) Test:** Low-frequency (2 to 3 Hz) electrical stimulation of motor nerves shows a characteristic **decremental compound muscle action potential (CMAP) response (>10% drop)** between the 1st and 5th action potentials [Robbins 5 p. 175].
2. **Single-Fiber Electromyography (SFEMG) [⭐ Most Sensitive Test]:** Demonstrates increased "jitter" (variability in transmission time between two muscle fibers) and neuromuscular blocking (sensitivity >95%) [Robbins 5 p. 175].

---

### D. Radiologic Imaging

- **Contrast-Enhanced CT / MRI Chest:** Mandatory to evaluate for **Thymoma** or thymic follicular hyperplasia [Robbins 3 p. 148, Robbins 5 p. 173].

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Pyridostigmine [⭐ First-Line Symptomatic Anticholinesterase Agent]

- **Name:** Pyridostigmine Bromide [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 118, 119, 120). Reversible carbamate **acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitor**. Reversibly carbamylates the esteratic site of AChE enzyme at the neuromuscular junction, preventing the hydrolysis of endogenous acetylcholine. Preserves released ACh at the synaptic cleft, allowing accumulated ACh molecules to repeatedly bind and activate remaining functional postsynaptic N_M receptors [Kdt 1 p. 118, 119, 120].
    
    ```
    Pyridostigmine → Inhibits AChE Enzyme → Prevents ACh Hydrolysis → ↑ Synaptic ACh Accumulation → Activates Remaining Postsynaptic NM Receptors
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Nicotinic N_M receptors at muscle endplate [Kdt 1 p. 118, 120].
- **Dose:** **60 mg PO thrice to 4-times daily (TDS/QID)** (Dose titrated according to individual muscle weakness response; range 60 mg to 240 mg/day) [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets.
- **Frequency:** 3 to 4 times daily (every 4 to 6 hours) [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **Features:** Preferred first-line drug for long-term maintenance; longer duration of action (3 to 6 hours) than neostigmine, less potent dose-for-dose, and produces smoother muscle control with fewer muscarinic side effects [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **Side Effects:** Excessive muscarinic stimulation (abdominal cramps, diarrhea, hypersalivation, lacrimation, sweating, bradycardia, bronchospasm—controlled by adding oral Atropine) [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate upon clinical/serological diagnosis; adjust dose carefully. **Overdose causes Cholinergic Crisis** (persistent endplate depolarization) [Kdt 1 p. 121].

#### 2. Prednisolone (Immunosuppressive Glucocorticoid) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Prednisolone [Kdt 1 p. 121, Kdt 2 p. 316].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 121, 316). Suppresses B-cell autoantibody synthesis against N_M receptors, reduces T-helper cell stimulation, and promotes the synthesis of new postsynaptic N_M receptors [Kdt 1 p. 121].
- **Dose:** **30 to 60 mg/day PO** (1 to 2 mg/kg/day) until clinical remission is achieved (~80% remission rate), then gradually tapered to **10 mg PO daily or on alternate days** as maintenance [Kdt 1 p. 121, Kdt 2 p. 316].
- **Features:** Used when anticholinesterases fail to achieve adequate muscle strength, or post-thymectomy [Kdt 1 p. 121, Kdt 2 p. 316].

#### 3. Azathioprine (Second-Line Immunosuppressant)

- **Dose:** **50 to 150 mg/day PO (1.5 to 2.5 mg/kg/day)**; inhibits purine synthesis and T/B lymphocyte proliferation. Slow onset (takes 3 to 6 months to act); excellent for long-term steroid-sparing maintenance [Kdt 1 p. 121, Kdt 4 p. 1000].

---

### B. Surgical Management (Thymectomy) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications:** Recommended for **all patients with Thymoma** and all younger patients (<60 years) with moderate-to-severe generalized MG [Kdt 1 p. 121, Robbins 5 p. 175, Kdt 4 p. 998].
- **Outcome:** Removes the source of AChR antigen sensitization, producing gradual clinical improvement and complete disease remission in up to 50% of cases [Kdt 1 p. 121, Kdt 4 p. 998].

---

## 9. Complications

1. **Myasthenic Crisis:** Acute life-threatening respiratory muscle paralysis requiring ICU ventilation [Kdt 1 p. 121, Robbins 5 p. 174].
2. **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Secondary to bulbar muscle weakness, impaired cough, and swallowing difficulty [Davidson 1 p. 17, Robbins 5 p. 174].
3. **Cholinergic Crisis:** Anticholinesterase overdose causing depolarizing neuromuscular block and autonomic collapse [Kdt 1 p. 121].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Modern therapeutic strategies (pyridostigmine, thymectomy, steroids, and plasmapheresis) have reduced overall mortality from >30% to **<5%** [Robbins 5 p. 175].

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always perform the **2 mg IV Edrophonium (Tensilon) Test** to differentiate between a **Myasthenic Crisis** and a **Cholinergic Crisis** in a patient presenting with acute respiratory deterioration! If muscle strength **improves**, it is a Myasthenic Crisis (under-dosing); if weakness **worsens or remains unchanged**, it is a Cholinergic Crisis (anti-ChE overdose causing persistent depolarizing block) [Kdt 1 p. 121]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Confusing Myasthenia Gravis with **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS)**, or treating a Cholinergic Crisis by increasing the dose of Pyridostigmine.
- **Distinguishing Fact:**
    - In **Myasthenia Gravis**, repetitive muscle use **WORSENS weakness**, pupils are spared, and autoantibodies target **postsynaptic AChR** [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 170, 174].
    - In **LEMS**, repetitive muscle use **IMPROVES strength (post-tetanic potentiation)**, autonomic dry mouth is present, and autoantibodies target **presynaptic P/Q-type Ca²⁺ channels** (often paraneoplastic with Small Cell Lung Cancer) [Robbins 5 p. 176].
- **Resolution:** **In a Cholinergic Crisis, IMMEDIATELY WITHHOLD all anticholinesterase drugs**, intubate the patient, and administer **IV Atropine** to antagonize muscarinic toxicity [Kdt 1 p. 121]!

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Autoimmune NMJ disorder driven by anti-AChR or anti-MuSK autoantibodies causing fatigable muscle weakness [Kdt 1 p. 120, Robbins 5 p. 169, 170].
- **Thymic Associations:** **Thymic Follicular Hyperplasia (65–75%)** and **Thymoma (10–15%)** [Robbins 3 p. 147, 148, Robbins 5 p. 173].
- **Diagnostic Gold Standards:** Anti-AChR antibody serology, Single-Fiber EMG (increased jitter), and **Edrophonium 2 mg IV test** [Kdt 1 p. 121, 122, Robbins 5 p. 170, 175].
- **First-Line Symptomatic Drug:** **Pyridostigmine 60 mg PO TDS/QID** (Reversible AChE inhibitor) [Kdt 1 p. 119, 120].
- **Crisis Management:** Mechanical ventilation + **Plasmapheresis OR IVIG (2 g/kg)** + IV Methylprednisolone [Kdt 1 p. 121].

---

## 16. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Myasthenia gravis is an autoimmune neuromuscular junction disorder caused by autoantibodies against postsynaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors or MuSK—frequently associated with thymic hyperplasia or thymoma—presenting with painless, fluctuating extraocular, bulbar, and limb muscle weakness that worsens with exertion, diagnosed by anti-AChR serology and the Edrophonium test, and managed with Pyridostigmine, thymectomy, prednisolone, and plasmapheresis.**
