# Intracerebral Hemorrhage

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH)**—also termed primary intraparenchymal hemorrhage—is a catastrophic vascular subtype of stroke defined as acute extravasation of blood directly into the brain parenchyma resulting from the non-traumatic rupture of small intraparenchymal arterioles, capillaries, or medium-sized cerebral arteries [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].

It accounts for **10% to 15% of all stroke events**, carrying the highest morbidity and acute 30-day mortality (30% to 50%) among all cerebrovascular accidents [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].

---

### A. Primary Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Chronic Systemic Hypertension [⭐ Most Common Overall Cause (~50–60%)]:**
    - Causes small vessel arteriolosclerosis in deep penetrating lenticulostriate branches of the Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) and thalamoperforating vessels [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].
    - Predominantly causes **Ganglionic (Deep) Hemorrhages** in the putamen, thalamus, pons, and cerebellum [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].
2. **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) [⭐ Most Common Cause of Lobar Hemorrhage in Elderly]:**
    - Characterized by progressive deposition of **β-amyloid (Aβ40)** peptides in the tunica media and adventitia of small-to-medium-sized meningeal and cortical cerebral blood vessels [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].
    - Weakens vessel walls, leading to recurrent, superficial **Lobar Hemorrhages** (cortex and subcortical white matter) [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].
    - Strongly associated with **ApoE ε2 and ε4 alleles** and Alzheimer's disease [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].

---

### B. Secondary Causes & Risk Factors

1. **Anticoagulant & Thrombolytic Therapy:** 5- to 10-fold increased risk with Warfarin (target INR >3.0), DOACs (Dabigatran, Apixaban, Rivaroxaban), or post-rt-PA infusion [Davidson 1 p. 461, Kdt 3 p. 625].
2. **Vascular Malformations:** Arteriovenous Malformations (AVMs), Cavernous Angiomas (Cavernomas), and Saccular (Berry) Aneurysms [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].
3. **Intracranial Neoplasms:** High-grade Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM) or vascular metastases (**Melanoma, Renal Cell Carcinoma, Choriocarcinoma, Thyroid Carcinoma**) [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
4. **Hematological / Coagulation Disorders:** Severe thrombocytopenia (platelets <20,000/μL), hemophilia A/B, leukemia, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) [Robbins 1 p. 116, Robbins 5 p. 1257].
5. **Illicit Sympathomimetic Drug Use:** Cocaine, amphetamines, or ecstasy causing transient severe hypertensive surges and vessel rupture [Kdt 1 p. 146, Robbins 5 p. 1257].
6. **Central Nervous System Vasculitis:** Primary Angiitis of the CNS, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), or Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) [Robbins 5 p. 1253, 1257].

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Arteriolosclerotic Weakening & Charcot-Bouchard Microaneurysms:** Chronic systemic hypertension induces **hyaline arteriolosclerosis** (lipohyalinosis) in small deep penetrating arteries (40 to 900 μm diameter) [Robbins 5 p. 1255, 1257]. Concentric smooth muscle loss and elastic lamina fragmentation create microscopic outpouchings called **Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms** [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
2. **Vessel Rupture & Hematoma Expansion:** Under high intravascular arterial pressure, Charcot-Bouchard aneurysms rupture, producing rapid blood extravasation directly into the brain parenchyma [Robbins 5 p. 1257]. The expanding hematoma dissects along white matter tracts.
3. **Mass Effect & Primary Mechanical Injury:** The expanding hematoma compresses surrounding brain parenchyma, causing physical tissue destruction, neuronal disruption, and elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP) [Robbins 5 p. 1244, 1257].
4. **Perilesional Edema & Secondary Ischemic Injury:** Thrombin release, hemoglobin breakdown products (cytotoxic iron/hemin), and neuroinflammation break down the blood-brain barrier, inducing severe **vasogenic and cytotoxic peri-hematomal edema** [Robbins 5 p. 1244, 1257].
5. **Cerebral Herniation & Duret Hemorrhage:** Uncontrolled ICP elevation causes transtentorial (uncal) or tonsillar herniation, displacing the brainstem downward and causing tearing of midline brainstem penetrating vessels (**Duret Hemorrhages in pons/midbrain**), leading to coma and death [Robbins 5 p. 1244, 1271].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. General Presentation (Common to All Sites)

- **Abrupt Onset During Activity:** Symptoms characteristically develop suddenly during waking hours, physical exertion, or emotional stress [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **Severe Headache & Vomiting:** Caused by acute elevation of Intracranial Pressure (ICP) and meningeal irritation (present in >60% of cases, distinguishing ICH from ischemic stroke) [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **Smoothly Progressive Deficits:** Neurological deficits evolve continuously over minutes to hours as the hematoma expands.
- **Rapid Deterioration of Consciousness:** Early lethargy progressing to stupor and coma due to raised ICP and brainstem displacement [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **Severe Systolic Hypertension:** Blood pressure is markedly elevated (systolic BP >180 to 220 mmHg) driven by the **Cushing Reflex** (hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respiration) [Davidson 1 p. 502].

1. **Putaminal Hemorrhage (50% to 60% of cases) [⭐ Most Common Site]:**
    - Contralateral dense hemiplegia and hemisensory loss (due to internal capsule compression) [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
    - Conjugate eye deviation **towards the side of the lesion** ("looking at the lesion").
    - Homonymous hemianopia and dysphasia (if dominant hemisphere involved).
2. **Thalamic Hemorrhage (15% to 20%):**
    - Contralateral hemisensory loss greater than motor deficit.
    - Characteristic ocular signs: Downward and inward eye deviation (**"peering at the tip of the nose"**), unreactive pupils.
3. **Pontine Hemorrhage (10% to 15%) [⭐ Most Lethal]:**
    - Rapid coma within minutes, quadriplegia, and decerebrate posturing.
    - **Pathognomonic Triad:** **Pinpoint reactive pupils**, **hyperpyrexia** (due to central autonomic disruption), and **quadriplegia** [Davidson 1 p. 502].
4. **Cerebellar Hemorrhage (10%) [⭐ Surgical Emergency]:**
    - Sudden occipital headache, severe vertigo, vomiting, and marked gait ataxia [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - Absence of hemiplegia; presence of ipsilateral facial weakness and gaze palsy.
    - _Critical Risk:_ Rapid compression of 4th ventricle causing acute obstructive hydrocephalus and brainstem herniation [Davidson 1 p. 502].
5. **Lobar Hemorrhage (15% to 20%):**
    - Localized focal deficits corresponding to the affected lobe (e.g., parietal → sensory loss; occipital → hemianopia; frontal → motor/behavioral).
    - High frequency of early focal **seizures** [Robbins 5 p. 1257].

## 6. Diagnosis & Laboratory Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Neuroimaging Protocols [⭐ Gold Standard]

1. **Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head [⭐ First-Line Investigation of Choice]:**
    - **Sensitivity:** **100% sensitive** for acute intracerebral hemorrhage [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Findings:** Hyperdense (bright white) parenchymal collection with surrounding hypodense peri-hematomal edema and mass effect (sulcal effacement, ventricular compression, midline shift) [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1257].
    - **CT "Spot Sign":** Foci of contrast extravasation within the hematoma during CT Angiography, indicating active ongoing bleeding and high expansion risk.
2. __Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Brain - T2_ / Gradient Echo / SWI):_*
    - Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI) detects acute blood breakdown products (deoxyhemoglobin/hemosiderin) and identifies cortical "microbleeds" characteristic of Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
3. **CT Angiography (CTA) / Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA):**
    - Mandatory in young, non-hypertensive patients or lobar bleeds to rule out underlying Arteriovenous Malformations (AVMs), aneurysms, or dural venous sinus thrombosis [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].

---

### B. Systemic Laboratory Panel

- **Capillary Blood Glucose [⭐ Mandatory Bedside Test]:** Excludes severe hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL) or hyperglycemia [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT):** Screens for anticoagulant-induced hemorrhage (INR >1.5) or hemophilia [Robbins 1 p. 116].
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Evaluates platelet count (thrombocytopenia <50,000/μL) and hemoglobin [Robbins 1 p. 116].
- **Renal & Liver Function Tests:** Identifies underlying uremia or cirrhotic coagulopathy.
- **Toxicology Screen:** Detects cocaine, amphetamines, or sympathomimetic agents.

### A. Emergency Resuscitation & ICU Protocol

1. **Airway Protection & Ventilation:** Secure airway via endotracheal intubation in patients with depressed level of consciousness (**Glasgow Coma Scale GCS ≤8**) to prevent aspiration pneumonia and hypercapnic cerebral vasodilation [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **Positioning & Venous Drainage:** Maintain head elevation at **30 degrees** in midline position to facilitate cerebral venous drainage and reduce ICP [Robbins 5 p. 1244].
3. **Glycemic & Temperature Control:** Maintain normoglycemia (blood glucose 140 to 180 mg/dL) and aggressive normothermia (paracetamol / cooling blankets) as hyperthermia accelerates peri-hematomal ischemic cell death [Davidson 1 p. 502].

---

### B. Coagulopathy Reversal Protocols

- **Warfarin Reversal:** Administer **Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC)** (50 U/kg IV) plus **Vitamin K (Phytomenadione 10 mg IV slow infusion)** to achieve INR <1.4 immediately.
- **Heparin Reversal:** **Protamine Sulfate** (1 mg per 100 units of Heparin administered).
- **Dabigatran Reversal:** **Idarucizumab** (5 g IV).
- **Factor Xa Inhibitors Reversal:** **Andexanet Alfa** (or 4-factor PCC).
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Transfuse platelet concentrates if platelet count <50,000/μL.

---

### C. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Labetalol (First-Line Antihypertensive) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Labetalol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 165, Kdt 3 p. 619). Combined competitive **α₁- and non-selective β₁/β₂-adrenergic receptor blocker** (α:β blockade ratio of 1:7 for IV). Produces arteriolar vasodilation (via α₁-blockade) while blunting reflex tachycardia and maintaining cardiac output (via β₁-blockade). Does not increase Intracranial Pressure (ICP) or cause cerebral arterial dilation.
    
    ```
    Labetalol → Combined α1 + β1/β2 Blockade → Arteriolar Vasodilation without Reflex Tachycardia → Rapid BP Reduction (Preserved ICP)
    ```
    
- **Target / Receptors:** α₁-, β₁-, and β₂-adrenergic receptors.
- **Dose:** **10 to 20 mg IV slow injection over 2 minutes**. Repeat **20 to 80 mg IV every 10 minutes** as needed (max cumulative dose 300 mg), OR continuous IV infusion at **1.0 to 2.0 mg/min**, titrated to target BP.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) bolus or continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** 10-minute bolus intervals or continuous infusion.
- **Features:** Drug of choice in acute stroke and intracranial hemorrhage; rapid onset (2 to 5 minutes); safe in altered sensorium as it does not cause sedation [Kdt 3 p. 619].
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm (in asthmatics), heart block, postural dizziness [Kdt 1 p. 165, Kdt 3 p. 619].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately when SBP >150 to 220 mmHg to achieve target **SBP <140 mmHg** within 1 hour; maintain for 24 to 48 hours.

#### 2. Nicardipine (Alternative First-Line Antihypertensive) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Nicardipine.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 595, Kdt 3 p. 619). Second-generation dihydropyridine **L-type Calcium Channel Blocker (CCB)** with selective vascular smooth muscle arteriolar dilator action. Rapidly lowers systemic vascular resistance without reducing cerebral arterial blood flow.
    
    ```
    Nicardipine → Vascular L-type Ca2+ Channel Blockade → Smooth Muscle Relaxation → Decreased Systemic Vascular Resistance
    ```
    
- **Target / Receptors:** Vascular L-type voltage-gated calcium channels.
- **Dose:** **5.0 mg/hr continuous IV infusion** initially; titrate upward by 2.5 mg/hr every 5 to 15 minutes up to a maximum dose of **15.0 mg/hr** until target SBP is achieved.
- **Dosage / Route:** Continuous IV infusion using a dedicated infusion pump.
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion.
- **Features:** Highly predictable, titratable, short-acting IV hypotensive; replacing nitroprusside in emergency stroke management due to lower toxicity [Kdt 3 p. 619].
- **Side Effects:** Reflex tachycardia, flushing, headache, phlebitis at infusion site [Kdt 3 p. 619].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate at presentation to maintain SBP between 130 and 140 mmHg.

#### 3. Mannitol 20% Solution (Osmotic Diuretic for Raised ICP) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Mannitol (20% Solution).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 638). Freely filtered osmotic diuretic. Elevates plasma osmolality, establishing an osmotic gradient that draws water out of edematous brain parenchyma across an intact blood-brain barrier into the intravascular space, thereby rapidly reducing cerebral edema, brain tissue volume, and **Intracranial Pressure (ICP)** [Kdt 3 p. 638].
    
    ```
    Mannitol 20% IV → ↑ Intravascular Osmolality → Osmotic Water Shift from Brain Parenchyma to Blood → ↓ Cerebral Edema & ICP
    ```
    
- **Dose:** **0.5 to 1.0 g/kg (2.5 to 5.0 mL/kg of 20% solution) IV bolus infused over 15 to 30 minutes**. Can be repeated at doses of **0.25 to 0.5 g/kg IV every 4 to 6 hours**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) rapid infusion.
- **Frequency:** Every 4 to 6 hours as needed (guided by serum osmolality).
- **Features:** Rapid onset of ICP reduction (15 to 30 minutes); duration of action 1 to 6 hours [Kdt 3 p. 638].
- **Side Effects:** Dehydration, hypernatremia, acute renal failure (if serum osmolality >320 mOsm/kg), electrolyte depletion, rebound ICP elevation [Kdt 3 p. 638].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start when clinical signs of raised ICP (papilledema, GCS drop) or transtentorial herniation emerge; **stop if serum osmolality exceeds 320 mOsm/kg** or renal failure develops [Kdt 3 p. 638].

---

### D. Surgical Interventions

1. **Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy & Hematoma Evacuation [⭐ Absolute Indication]:** Mandatory emergency life-saving procedure for **Cerebellar Hemorrhages >3 cm in diameter** or those causing brainstem compression and 4th ventricular hydrocephalus [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **External Ventricular Drain (EVD) Placement:** Indicated for acute intraventricular hemorrhage causing obstructive hydrocephalus [Robbins 5 p. 1244].
3. **Surgical Evacuation of Superficial Lobar Hemorrhages:** Considered for large lobar hematomas (>30 mL within 1 cm of cortical surface) with progressive neurological deterioration.

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hematoma Expansion:** Occurs in 30% of patients within the first 6 hours; major predictor of mortality.
2. **Intraventricular Extension & Obstructive Hydrocephalus:** Rupture into lateral ventricles occurs in 40% of ganglionic bleeds [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
3. **Cerebral Herniation Syndromes:** Transtentorial (uncal) and tonsillar herniation causing irreversible brainstem necrosis [Robbins 5 p. 1244].
4. **Peri-Hematomal Ischemia & Vasogenic Edema:** Peaks at 3 to 5 days post-bleed [Robbins 5 p. 1244].
5. **Post-Stroke Seizures:** Common in lobar hemorrhages [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
6. **Systemic Complications:** Aspiration pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) / pulmonary embolism, and neurogenic pulmonary edema.

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Overall 30-day mortality remains high (**30% to 50%**), with half of deaths occurring within the first 48 hours [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **ICH Score** (predicts 30-day mortality based on GCS, ICH volume >30 mL, intraventricular blood, infratentorial origin, and age ≥80 years).
- Functional recovery among survivors is gradual, requiring dedicated neurorehabilitation.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In any patient presenting with acute spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage, **rapidly lower the Systolic Blood Pressure to a target of <140 mmHg (specifically between 130 and 140 mmHg) within the FIRST HOUR using IV Labetalol or Nicardipine**! Early aggressive BP control directly halts early hematoma expansion without compromising cerebral perfusion pressure, cutting absolute mortality and severe disability rates significantly!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Aspirin, Heparin, or Thrombolytics (rt-PA)** to a patient presenting with acute stroke symptoms before obtaining neuroimaging.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Clinical presentation of ischemic stroke and intracerebral hemorrhage can be identical. Administering antiplatelets or thrombolytics in ICH causes catastrophic hematoma expansion and instant death.
- **Resolution:** **NEVER administer Aspirin, Clopidogrel, Heparin, or rt-PA until a Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head has definitively ruled out intracerebral hemorrhage [Davidson 1 p. 502, Kdt 3 p. 625]!**

    1. **Putamen & Internal Capsule (Deep):** Large dark hemorrhagic mass in basal ganglia (**Putaminal Bleed - 50–60%**).
    2. **Thalamus (Deep):** Hemorrhage adjacent to 3rd ventricle (**Thalamic Bleed**).
    3. **Lobar Cortex (Superficial):** Hemorrhage in subcortical white matter of parietal/occipital lobe (**Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy Bleed**).
    4. **Ventricular Extension:** Rupture of deep hematoma into lateral ventricle.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Ganglionic hemorrhages occur deep in the putamen/thalamus due to hypertensive lipohyalinosis, whereas lobar hemorrhages occur superficially in the cortex due to Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy."_.

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Most Common Cause Overall:** **Chronic Hypertension** causing hyaline arteriolosclerosis & Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms in deep ganglionic sites (Putamen > Thalamus > Pons > Cerebellum) [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **Most Common Cause of Lobar Bleeds in Elderly:** **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)** [Robbins 5 p. 1257].
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test:** **Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head** (hyperdense lesion) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Blood Pressure Target:** Lower SBP rapidly to **<140 mmHg** using **IV Labetalol** (10–20 mg IV) or **IV Nicardipine**(5–15 mg/hr) [Kdt 3 p. 619].
- **Emergency Surgical Indication:** **Cerebellar Hemorrhage >3 cm** with brainstem compression or hydrocephalus [Davidson 1 p. 502].

## 16. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Intracerebral hemorrhage is a catastrophic stroke subtype characterized by non-traumatic arterial rupture into the brain parenchyma—driven predominantly by hypertensive Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms in deep structures or cerebral amyloid angiopathy in lobar regions—requiring immediate Non-Contrast CT diagnosis, rapid BP lowering to SBP <140 mmHg, and osmotic or surgical decompression.**
