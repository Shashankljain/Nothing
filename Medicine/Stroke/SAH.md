# Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)** is an acute, life-threatening form of cerebrovascular bleed characterized by the extravasation of blood into the subarachnoid space—the anatomical space between the arachnoid mater and pia mater surrounding the brain and spinal cord [Robbins 5 p. 1258, Davidson 1 p. 502].

- **Spontaneous (Non-Traumatic) SAH:** Most commonly results from the sudden rupture of a congenital or acquired saccular ("berry") intracranial aneurysm (~80% to 85% of spontaneous cases) [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
- **Traumatic SAH:** Head trauma is the single most common cause of subarachnoid hemorrhage overall [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1284].

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Etiology

1. **Ruptured Saccular ("Berry") Aneurysm (80% to 85% of spontaneous cases) [⭐ Most Common Cause of Spontaneous SAH]:**
    - Thin-walled outpouchings arising at arterial branch points along the Circle of Willis [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].
    - **Anatomical Distribution of Saccular Aneurysms:**
        - **Anterior Communicating Artery (~40%):** Most common single site [Robbins 5 p. 1259, 1260].
        - **Middle Cerebral Artery Bifurcation (~34%)** [Robbins 5 p. 1259, 1260].
        - **Internal Carotid / Posterior Communicating Artery Junction (~20%)** [Robbins 5 p. 1259, 1260].
        - **Basilar Artery Bifurcation / Posterior Circulation (~4%)** [Robbins 5 p. 1259, 1260].
2. **Vascular Malformations (5% to 10%):** Arteriovenous Malformations (AVMs) and Cavernous Angiomas [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].
3. **Rupture of Intraparenchymal Hemorrhage:** Extension of deep hypertensive bleeds into the ventricular system or subarachnoid space [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
4. **Other Intracranial Aneurysms:** Fusiform (atherosclerotic, basilar artery), mycotic (infective endocarditis), traumatic, and dissecting aneurysms [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
5. **Systemic & Coagulation Disorders:** Anticoagulant therapy, severe thrombocytopenia, or bleeding diatheses [Robbins 5 p. 1258].

---

### B. High-Yield Risk Factors

- **Modifiable Risk Factors:** Systemic hypertension, cigarette smoking, and heavy alcohol ingestion [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
- **Genetic & Connective Tissue Associations:** Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD), Marfan syndrome, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (Type IV), and Coarctation of the Aorta [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
- **Aneurysm Size Threshold:** Aneurysms >10 mm in diameter carry a 50% per year risk of spontaneous rupture [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
- **Precipitating Triggers:** Acute surges in Intracranial Pressure (ICP), such as straining at stool, severe coughing, or sexual intercourse [Robbins 5 p. 1260].

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Structural Wall Defect & Outpouching:** Saccular aneurysms lack a smooth muscle layer (tunica media) and internal elastic lamina; the wall consists only of thickened hyalinized intima and adventitia [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
2. **Aneurysmal Rupture & Extravasation:** High arterial blood pressure breaches the apex of the sac, blasting blood directly into the subarachnoid space [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].
3. **Acute Intracranial Pressure Surge:** Arterial blood under high pressure floods basal cisterns, triggering a rapid spike in Intracranial Pressure (ICP) and transient cerebral hypoperfusion [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].
4. **Secondary Cerebral Vasospasm (Days 3 to 14) [⭐ Major Complication]:** Degradation of subarachnoid blood releases spasmogenic compounds (oxyhemoglobin, endothelin-1, thromboxane A₂). This induces severe vasoconstriction of major cerebral arteries in the Circle of Willis, causing **Delayed Ischemic Neurological Deficits (DIND)** and secondary brain infarction [Robbins 5 p. 1260, Kdt 3 p. 643].
5. **Meningeal Fibrosis & Hydrocephalus:** Meningeal inflammation during the healing phase leads to arachnoid granulation fibrosis, impairing CSF resorption and triggering **hydrocephalus** [Robbins 5 p. 1260].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Sudden "Thunderclap" Headache [⭐ Cardinal Symptom]:** Instantaneous, excruciating headache reaching maximum intensity within seconds to 5 minutes ("the worst headache of my life") [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1260].
- **Transient or Persistent Loss of Consciousness (LOC):** Occurs in 50% of patients at rupture onset due to acute ICP spike [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
- **Nausea & Repeated Vomiting:** Secondary to elevated intracranial pressure [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Photophobia, Phonophobia, & Neck Pain/Stiffness** [Davidson 1 p. 502].

---

### B. Physical Signs

1. **Meningism (Meningeal Irritation Signs):**
    - **Nuchal Rigidity (Neck Stiffness):** Resistance to passive neck flexion [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Positive Kernig's Sign:** Pain/resistance on passive knee extension when hip is flexed at 90° [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Positive Brudzinski's Sign:** Involuntary flexion of hips and knees upon passive neck flexion.
2. **Ophthalmologic Signs [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Subhyaloid (Preretinal) Hemorrhages:** Flame-shaped blood collections between retina and vitreous membrane on fundoscopy [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - **Oculomotor (3rd Cranial Nerve) Palsy:** Ptosis, dilated unreactive pupil, and "down and out" eye deviation (indicates expanding or ruptured **Posterior Communicating Artery Aneurysm**) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
    - Papilledema (late feature of raised ICP) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
3. **Focal Neurological Deficits:** Hemiparesis, dysphasia, or cranial nerve palsies due to intra-parenchymal blood extension or delayed vasospasm [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
4. **Neurogenic Autonomic Dysfunction:** Tachycardia, hypertension, neurogenic pulmonary edema, and ECG changes (T-wave inversions, ST depression, prolonged QT interval) driven by a massive sympathetic surge [Davidson 1 p. 502].

### A. Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head [⭐ First-Line Test of Choice]

- **Sensitivity:** **>95% sensitive** when performed within 6 hours of symptom onset [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Diagnostic Findings:** Hyperdense (bright white) blood collections filling the basal cisterns, Circle of Willis, Sylvian fissures, and cerebral sulci [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1260].

---

### B. Lumbar Puncture (LP) [⭐ Mandatory if CT is Negative]

Indicated when clinical suspicion of SAH remains high despite a normal CT scan (best performed **>12 hours** after headache onset) [Davidson 1 p. 502]:

1. **Xanthochromia [⭐ Pathognomonic LP Finding]:** Yellowish discoloration of the CSF supernatant after centrifugation, caused by enzymatic breakdown of hemoglobin into **bilirubin and hemosiderin**. Confirms true subarachnoid hemorrhage and differentiates it from a traumatic tap [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **CSF Parameters:** Uniformly elevated RBC count across sequential tubes (Tube 1 to Tube 3) and elevated opening pressure (>200 mmH₂O) [Davidson 1 p. 502].

---

### C. Vascular Imaging (CTA / DSA) [⭐ Gold Standard]

- **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) / CT Angiography (CTA):** Definitive gold standard for anatomical localization, sizing, and structural characterization of the ruptured aneurysm or AVM prior to intervention [Davidson 1 p. 502].

### A. General & Initial Supportive Measures

1. **Bed Rest & Environment:** Strict bed rest in a quiet, dark room with head elevated 30° to lower intracranial pressure [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **Airway & Ventilation:** Endotracheal intubation for GCS ≤8 or severe neurogenic pulmonary edema [Davidson 1 p. 502].
3. **Blood Pressure Target:** Maintain Systolic BP **<160 mmHg** (or MAP <110 mmHg) prior to aneurysm securing using **IV Labetalol** or **IV Nicardipine** to prevent rebleeding [Kdt 3 p. 619].
4. **Prevention of Straining:** Administer stool softeners (docusate) and antiemetics to prevent acute spikes in ICP [Robbins 5 p. 1260].

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Nimodipine [⭐ First-Line Drug for Prevention of Cerebral Vasospasm]

- **Name:** Nimodipine [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 643). Lipophilic dihydropyridine (DHP) calcium channel blocker. Efficiently crosses the blood-brain barrier due to high lipid solubility and selectively binds to **L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** on cerebral vascular smooth muscle. Inhibits calcium influx, preventing and reversing delayed cerebral vasospasm and protecting neurons from ischemic injury.
    
    ```
    Nimodipine → Crosses BBB → Blocks L-type Ca2+ Channels in Cerebral Vessels → Prevents Vasospasm → Reduces Delayed Ischemic Deficits
    ```
    
- **Target / Receptors:** L-type voltage-gated calcium channels.
- **Dose:** **60 mg PO every 4 hours** (or 30 mg PO every 2 hours if hypotensive).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) capsules or via nasogastric (NG) tube.
- **Frequency:** Every 4 hours (6 times daily).
- **Features:** The **only drug approved and proven to reduce neurological deficits and overall mortality** from cerebral vasospasm post-SAH [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Side Effects:** Systemic hypotension, flushing, headache, nausea, heart rate changes [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate within 96 hours of SAH onset; continue continuously for **21 days (3 weeks)** [Kdt 3 p. 643].

---

### C. Definitive Surgical & Interventional Management (Securing Aneurysm)

Should be performed within **24 to 72 hours** to prevent catastrophic rebleeding:

1. **Endovascular Coiling [⭐ Preferred Modern Technique]:** Platinum microcoils deployed into the aneurysm sac via femoral endovascular catheterization, inducing thrombosis and sealing the sac.
2. **Neurosurgical Microvascular Clipping:** Craniotomy and placement of a metallic clip across the neck of the aneurysm [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
3. **External Ventricular Drain (EVD):** Placed for acute obstructive hydrocephalus.

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Aneurysmal Rebleeding:** Highest risk within the first 24 hours (up to 20%); carries >50% mortality per episode [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
2. **Cerebral Vasospasm & Delayed Ischemic Neurological Deficit (DIND):** Peaks between **days 4 and 14** post-bleed [Robbins 5 p. 1260, Kdt 3 p. 643].
3. **Hydrocephalus:**
    - _Acute:_ Obstructive hydrocephalus caused by blood clots blocking CSF pathways [Robbins 5 p. 1260, 1271].
    - _Chronic:_ Communicating hydrocephalus caused by meningeal fibrosis of arachnoid granulations [Robbins 5 p. 1260, 1271].
4. **Hyponatremia:** Caused by **Cerebral Salt Wasting Syndrome (CSWS)** or SIADH [Davidson 1 p. 358].
5. **Seizures & Neurogenic Pulmonary Edema.**

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **Initial Rupture Mortality:** 25% to 50% of patients die with the first aneurysmal rupture [Robbins 5 p. 1260].
- Survivors face significant risks of rebleeding and delayed cerebral vasospasm; early aneurysm securing combined with Nimodipine therapy improves functional recovery.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** A negative non-contrast head CT performed >6 hours after headache onset CANNOT rule out Subarachnoid Hemorrhage! In a patient with a convincing history of a thunderclap headache and a normal CT, performing a **Lumbar Puncture >12 hours post-onset to test for Xanthochromia (yellow CSF supernatant from hemoglobin breakdown)** is mandatory to rule out SAH [Davidson 1 p. 502]!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Nimodipine via Intravenous (IV) injection**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** IV administration of oral Nimodipine solution causes severe systemic vasodilation, profound hypotension, and fatal cardiac arrest!
- **Resolution:** **Nimodipine must STRICTLY be administered ORALLY or via Nasogastric (NG) tube! NEVER give Nimodipine intravenously!**

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Extravasation of blood into the subarachnoid space; spontaneous cases caused by ruptured saccular berry aneurysms (80-85%) [Robbins 5 p. 1258].
- **Cardinal Symptom:** Sudden, excruciating **"Thunderclap" headache** reaching peak intensity within seconds [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1260].
- **Pathognomonic LP Finding:** **Xanthochromia** (yellow CSF supernatant >12 hours post-onset) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Vasospasm Prevention Drug:** **Nimodipine 60 mg PO q4h for 21 days** [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Definitive Treatment:** **Endovascular coiling** or surgical clipping within 24 to 72 hours.
