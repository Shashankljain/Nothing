# Stroke — Risk Factors, Etiology & Clinical Management

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident - CVA):** Rapidly developing clinical signs of focal (or global) disturbance of cerebral function, lasting **>24 hours** or leading to death, with no apparent cause other than that of vascular origin (ischemic or hemorrhagic) [Robbins 5 p. 1254].
- **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA):** A transient episode of neurological dysfunction caused by focal brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischemia, **without acute infarction**, where clinical symptoms completely resolve within **<24 hours**(typically within 1 hour) [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1254].

---

## 2. Risk Factors & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Classification of Stroke

1. **Ischemic Stroke (~80% to 85% of cases):** Resulting from acute arterial occlusion due to thromboembolism or in situ thrombosis [Robbins 5 p. 1253, 1254].
    - _Thromboembolic:_ Emboli originating from cardiac mural thrombi (Atrial Fibrillation) or carotid atheromatous plaques [Robbins 5 p. 1253].
    - _Thrombotic / Small Vessel:_ In situ thrombosis over ruptured atheroma or hyaline arteriolosclerosis (Lacunar Infarcts) [Robbins 5 p. 1255, 1260].
2. **Hemorrhagic Stroke (~15% to 20% of cases):** Resulting from vascular rupture [Robbins 5 p. 1254, 1257].
    - _Intraparenchymal Hemorrhage:_ Rupture of small penetrating arteries secondary to chronic hypertension or Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA) [Robbins 5 p. 1257, 1260].
    - _Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):_ Rupture of saccular ("berry") aneurysms in the Circle of Willis [Robbins 5 p. 1258, 1260].

#### 1. Non-Modifiable / Constitutional Risk Factors

- **Increasing Age:** Single most powerful independent non-modifiable risk factor; stroke incidence doubles for each decade after age 55 [Davidson 1 p. 376, Robbins 1 p. 484].
- **Gender / Sex:** Men have a higher baseline incidence in middle age; premenopausal women are relatively protected by endogenous estrogen, but the sex gap closes post-menopause [Davidson 1 p. 376, Robbins 1 p. 484].
- **Family History & Genetics:** Positive family history of premature stroke or cardiovascular disease; polygenic inheritance of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, or specific genetic traits (e.g., Factor V Leiden mutation, Prothrombin G20210A, CADASIL) [Davidson 1 p. 376, Robbins 1 p. 484, 485].
- **Ethnicity:** Elevated incidence in South Asian, African-American, and Japanese populations [Davidson 1 p. 376, 510].
- **Prior History:** Previous TIA or stroke carries a high recurrent stroke risk [Davidson 1 p. 460].

---

#### 2. Modifiable Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hypertension [⭐ Single Most Important Modifiable Risk Factor]:**
    - Elevates stroke risk by >60% [Robbins 1 p. 485].
    - Causes hyaline arteriolosclerosis in deep penetrating lenticulostriate vessels, leading to lacunar infarcts and deep ganglionic intraparenchymal hemorrhages [Robbins 5 p. 1255, 1257, 1260].
2. **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) & Cardiac Embolic Sources:**
    - AF causes stasis of blood in the left atrial appendage, leading to thromboembolism [Davidson 1 p. 460].
    - Accounts for ~15% of all strokes; stroke risk is quantified using the **CHA₂DS₂-VASc Score** [Davidson 1 p. 460, 461].
    - Other cardiac sources: recent Myocardial Infarction (mural thrombus), Valvular Heart Disease (rheumatic mitral stenosis), Dilated Cardiomyopathy, and Infective Endocarditis [Davidson 1 p. 460, Robbins 5 p. 1253].
3. **Cigarette Smoking:**
    - Doubles the risk of ischemic stroke [Robbins 1 p. 485, Robbins 2 p. 415].
    - Nicotine and toxins promote endothelial injury, arterial wall inflammation, platelet aggregation, and accelerate carotid atherosclerosis [Robbins 1 p. 485, Robbins 2 p. 415].
4. **Diabetes Mellitus:**
    - Increases stroke risk by 1.5 to 4-fold [Davidson 1 p. 755, 756].
    - Induces accelerated macrovascular atherosclerosis and endothelial dysfunction [Davidson 1 p. 755, Robbins 1 p. 485].
5. **Dyslipidemia / Hypercholesterolemia:**
    - Elevated serum LDL-C, total cholesterol, or Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] and depressed HDL-C accelerate atheroma formation in large elastic and muscular arteries (carotid bifurcation, middle cerebral artery) [Davidson 1 p. 376, Robbins 1 p. 485].
6. **Carotid Artery Stenosis & Peripheral Arterial Disease:**
    - Atherosclerotic narrowing of internal carotid arteries shedding micro-emboli distally into the Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) territory [Davidson 1 p. 502, Robbins 5 p. 1253].
7. **Pro-Thrombotic & Hypercoagulable States:**
    - Primary: Factor V Leiden mutation, Antithrombin III, Protein C or S deficiency [Robbins 1 p. 116, 117].
    - Secondary: Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS), oral contraceptive use, hyperestrogenic states, active malignancy [Robbins 1 p. 117].
8. **Lifestyle & Environmental Factors:**
    - Physical inactivity (doubles risk), central obesity, heavy alcohol intake (>3 drinks/day), high dietary sodium, and Obstructive Sleep Apnea Syndrome (OSAS) [Davidson 1 p. 376, 510, 686].

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Vascular Occlusion / Rupture:** Atherosclerotic plaque disruption, cardiogenic thromboembolism, or hypertensive vessel rupture reduces regional Cerebral Blood Flow (CBF) below critical thresholds (<10 to 12 mL/100 g/min) [Robbins 5 p. 1253].
2. **Ischemic Core vs Penumbra:**
    - **Ischemic Core:** Region of rapid, irreversible neuronal cell death via necrosis due to acute ATP depletion [Robbins 5 p. 1253].
    - **Ischemic Penumbra:** Surrounding hypoperfused zone of metabolically active but non-functional tissue that can be salvaged by rapid reperfusion (thrombolysis/thrombectomy) [Robbins 5 p. 1253].
3. **Excitotoxicity & Neuronal Injury:** Failure of energy-dependent Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps leads to neuronal depolarization, excessive presynaptic glutamate release, calcium (Ca²⁺) influx, and apoptotic/necrotic cascade [Robbins 5 p. 1253].

## 4. Clinical Features

- **Sudden Onset Focal Neurological Deficit:**
    - **Motor:** Contralateral hemiparesis or hemiplegia (face, arm, leg).
    - **Sensory:** Contralateral hemisensory loss.
    - **Speech / Language:** Dysphasia / Aphasia (expressive Broca's or receptive Wernicke's), dysarthria.
    - **Visual:** Homonymous hemianopia, amaurosis fugax (monocular blindness).
    - **Cerebellar / Brainstem:** Ataxia, vertigo, diplopia, cranial nerve palsies, altered consciousness [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **F-A-S-T Screening Tool:** **F**ace drooping, **A**rm weakness, **S**peech difficulty, **T**ime to call emergency services.

---

## 5. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Non-Contrast CT Head [⭐ First-Line Gold Standard]:** Mandatory initial imaging to differentiate **Ischemic Stroke** (hypodense area developing over hours) from **Intracranial Hemorrhage** (hyperdense blood visible immediately) before starting antiplatelet or thrombolytic therapy [Davidson 1 p. 502].
2. **Diffusion-Weighted MRI Brain (DWI-MRI):** Highly sensitive test for detecting hyperacute ischemic stroke within minutes of onset.
3. **CT / MR Angiography & Carotid Doppler:** Evaluates vessel occlusion site and quantifies internal carotid artery stenosis [Davidson 1 p. 502].
4. **Capillary Blood Glucose [⭐ Mandatory Bedside Test]:** Excludes **hypoglycemia** (most common stroke mimic).
5. **12-Lead ECG & Echocardiography:** Identifies Atrial Fibrillation, recent MI, or cardiac mural thrombi [Davidson 1 p. 460].
6. **Laboratory Panel:** Complete Blood Count, PT/INR, aPTT, lipid profile, HbA1c, and renal/liver panels.

### A. Acute Reperfusion Therapy (Ischemic Stroke)

1. **Intravenous Thrombolysis (IV Alteplase / rt-PA) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Dose:** **0.9 mg/kg IV** (max dose 90 mg; 10% given as IV bolus over 1 minute, remaining 90% infused over 60 minutes).
    - **Time Window:** Administered within **3.0 to 4.5 hours** of symptom onset in eligible patients after excluding hemorrhage on NCCT [Kdt 3 p. 625, 626].
2. **Endovascular Mechanical Thrombectomy (EMT):** Indicated for large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the anterior circulation within **6 to 24 hours**.

---

### B. Secondary Prevention & Risk Factor Modification Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Antiplatelet Therapy (Non-Valvular Ischemic Stroke)

- **Aspirin:** **75 mg to 150 mg PO once daily (OD)** (Initial loading dose **300 mg PO** within 24 to 48 hours of stroke onset, after excluding hemorrhage) [Davidson 1 p. 461, Kdt 3 p. 625].
- **Clopidogrel:** **75 mg PO once daily (OD)** (Used as an alternative or combined as Dual Antiplatelet Therapy [DAPT] for 21 days in high-risk TIA / minor stroke) [Davidson 1 p. 461, Kdt 3 p. 625].

#### 2. Anticoagulation Therapy (Stroke with Atrial Fibrillation)

- **Warfarin / DOACs (Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban):** Indicated for patients with AF and **CHA₂DS₂-VASc score ≥2** [Davidson 1 p. 460, 461].
- **Warfarin Target:** Target **INR 2.0 to 3.0** [Davidson 1 p. 461].

#### 3. Statin Therapy (Intensive Lipid Lowering)

- **Atorvastatin:** **40 mg to 80 mg PO once daily at bedtime** (Target LDL-C <70 mg/dL) [Davidson 1 p. 376, 510].

#### 4. Antihypertensive & Glycemic Control

- **Blood Pressure Control:** Target BP **<130/80 mmHg** in chronic secondary prevention using ACE inhibitors (Perindopril / Ramipril) plus Thiazide diuretics [Davidson 1 p. 510].
- **Glycemic Target:** Target HbA1c **<7.0%** [Davidson 1 p. 755].

## 8. Complications & Prognosis

- **Complications:** Cerebral edema with herniation, hemorrhagic transformation of ischemic infarct, aspiration pneumonia, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) / pulmonary embolism, post-stroke depression, and spasticity.
- **Prognosis:** Stroke is a leading cause of long-term disability worldwide. Early thrombolysis and dedicated stroke unit care significantly reduce mortality and functional dependency.

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always measure capillary blood glucose immediately in every patient presenting with acute focal neurological deficits! **Hypoglycemia (<40 to 50 mg/dL)** can perfectly mimic acute ischemic stroke (hemiparesis, dysphasia) and must be ruled out and corrected before performing advanced neuroimaging or initiating thrombolysis!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering antiplatelet agents (Aspirin) or IV Thrombolytics (rt-PA) immediately based solely on clinical examination of stroke symptoms.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Clinical examination alone cannot reliably distinguish ischemic stroke from intraparenchymal hemorrhage. Administering thrombolytics or aspirin in a hemorrhagic stroke is fatal.
- **Resolution:** **NEVER administer Aspirin, Clopidogrel, or rt-PA until a Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head has definitively excluded intracranial hemorrhage!**

## 13. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Focal neurological deficit lasting >24 hours of vascular etiology [Robbins 5 p. 1254].
- **Single Most Important Modifiable Risk Factor:** **Hypertension** (raises risk >60%; causes lacunar & ganglionic bleeds) [Robbins 1 p. 485, Robbins 5 p. 1255, 1260].
- **Major Cardiac Cause:** **Atrial Fibrillation** (15% of strokes; managed by CHA₂DS₂-VASc stratification) [Davidson 1 p. 460, 461].
- **First-Line Diagnostic Imaging:** **Non-Contrast CT (NCCT) Head** (rules out hemorrhage) [Davidson 1 p. 502].
- **Acute Reperfusion Golden Window:** **IV Alteplase (rt-PA 0.9 mg/kg) within 3.0 to 4.5 hours** of symptom onset [Kdt 3 p. 625, 626].

## 14. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Stroke is an acute vascular neurological emergency defined by focal cerebral deficits lasting >24 hours, driven predominantly by modifiable risk factors like hypertension, atrial fibrillation, smoking, and diabetes, requiring urgent non-contrast CT imaging and time-critical reperfusion therapy.**
