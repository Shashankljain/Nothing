# Antiarrhythmic Drugs

## 1. Definition

**Antiarrhythmic drugs** are therapeutic agents used to prevent, suppress, or terminate irregularities of cardiac rhythm by altering the electrophysiological properties of cardiac muscle cells—specifically automaticity, excitability, conduction velocity, and effective refractory period (ERP).

---

## 2. Key Classification & Features (Vaughan Williams System) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Class I — Sodium (Na⁺) Channel Blockers (Membrane Stabilizing Agents):** Bind to fast voltage-gated Na⁺ channels and decrease the Phase 0 depolarization slope (dv/dt).
    - _Subclass Ia:_ Moderately depresses Phase 0 upstroke and prolongs Action Potential Duration (APD) and ERP (e.g., Quinidine, Procainamide, Disopyramide).
    - _Subclass Ib:_ Weakly depresses Phase 0 upstroke and shortens APD/ERP; selective for inactivated Na⁺ channels in ischemic Purkinje fibers (e.g., Lidocaine, Mexiletine).
    - _Subclass Ic:_ Markedly depresses Phase 0 upstroke and severely slows His-Purkinje conduction with minimal effect on APD (e.g., Flecainide, Propafenone).
- **Class II — Anti-adrenergic Agents (β-Blockers):** Suppress sympathetic-mediated ectopic automaticity and impede AV nodal conduction by decreasing the slope of Phase 4 diastolic depolarization and prolonging AV nodal ERP (e.g., Propranolol, Metoprolol, Bisoprolol, Esmolol).
- **Class III — Potassium (K⁺) Channel Blockers (Agents Widening Action Potential):** Prolong repolarization (Phase 3) and APD by blocking myocardial delayed-rectifier K⁺ channels, thereby increasing tissue ERP and extinguishing re-entrant circuits (e.g., Amiodarone, Sotalol, Dronedarone, Dofetilide, Ibutilide).
- **Class IV — Calcium (Ca²⁺) Channel Blockers:** Inhibit slow-channel inward Ca²⁺ current in SA and AV nodes, decreasing SA node automaticity, slowing AV conduction velocity, and prolonging PR interval (e.g., Verapamil, Diltiazem).
- **Unclassified / Miscellaneous Antiarrhythmic Agents:**
    - _Adenosine:_ Activates A₁ GPCRs in AV node to open K⁺ channels, causing transient AV block to terminate >90% of PSVT episodes.
    - _Digoxin:_ Enhances central vagal tone to slow AV nodal conduction in Atrial Fibrillation.
    - _Atropine:_ Muscarinic M₂ blocker used to treat severe sinus bradycardia and vagal AV block.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Class I (Na⁺ Blockers):** Class Ia prolongs APD; Class Ib shortens APD; Class Ic slows conduction without altering APD.
- **Class II (β-Blockers):** Decreases Phase 4 SA/AV node automaticity and prevents rate acceleration.
- **Class III (K⁺ Blockers):** Delays Phase 3 repolarization, prolonging APD and ERP (risk of Torsades de pointes, except Amiodarone).
- **Class IV (Ca²⁺ Blockers):** Suppresses Ca²⁺-dependent SA/AV nodal depolarization (Verapamil/Diltiazem).
- **Acute PSVT Termination:** IV Adenosine is the drug of choice.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Antiarrhythmic drugs are electrophysiologically active pharmacological agents classified under the Vaughan Williams system into sodium channel blockers (Class I), beta-blockers (Class II), potassium channel blockers (Class III), and calcium channel blockers (Class IV) that restore normal cardiac rhythm or control ventricular response rate.**
