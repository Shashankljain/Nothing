# Essential & Accelerated Hypertension

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Hypertension (HTN)** is defined as a sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure where the systolic blood pressure (SBP) is **≥140 mmHg** and/or the diastolic blood pressure (DBP) is **≥90 mmHg** (based on JNC-8, BHS, and ESC/EHS guidelines) [or ≥130/80 mmHg as per ACC/AHA criteria].

- **Essential (Primary) Hypertension:** Sustained elevation of blood pressure without an identifiable underlying secondary cause, accounting for **90–95%** of all clinical cases.
- **Secondary Hypertension:** High blood pressure directly attributable to an underlying identifiable renal, endocrine, vascular, or pharmacological etiology, accounting for **5–10%** of cases.
- **Accelerated (Malignant) Hypertension:** A severe, life-threatening clinical presentation characterized by a rapid, marked elevation of blood pressure (typically SBP >180–200 mmHg, DBP >120–130 mmHg) accompanied by accelerated microvascular damage (**fibrinoid necrosis** of small arteries and arterioles), intravascular thrombosis, and rapidly progressive target organ damage (retinopathy with papilledema, acute renal failure, hypertensive encephalopathy, or acute heart failure).

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Causes of Essential Hypertension (Multifactorial)

Essential hypertension results from a complex interaction between non-modifiable genetic factors and modifiable environmental influences:

- **Genetic Susceptibility:** Polygenic inheritance explaining 40–60% of blood pressure variance (e.g., genetic variants affecting renal sodium handling and the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis).
- **High Dietary Sodium Intake:** Excessive salt intake exceeding renal excretion capacity promotes intravascular volume expansion.
- **Obesity & Centripetal Adiposity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²):** Increases sympathetic drive, activates RAAS, and promotes renal compression.
- **Heavy Alcohol Consumption & Cigarette Smoking:** Causes acute adrenergic discharge, structural arterial stiffening, and endothelial dysfunction.
- **Physical Inactivity & Chronic Psychosocial Stress.**
- **Intrauterine Growth Restriction & Low Birth Weight:** Leads to reduced nephron endowment at birth.

1. **Renal Diseases (Most Common Secondary Group):**
    - _Renal Parenchymal Disease:_ Acute glomerulonephritis, Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD), Diabetic Nephropathy, Adult Polycystic Kidney Disease (APKD).
    - _Renovascular Disease:_ **Renal Artery Stenosis** secondary to atherosclerotic plaque (older males) or Fibromuscular Dysplasia (young females).
    - _Renin-secreting tumors_ (Juxtaglomerular cell tumor).
2. **Endocrine Diseases:**
    - **Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's Syndrome / Bilateral Adrenal Hyperplasia):** Autonomous aldosterone secretion causing Na⁺ retention and K⁺ wasting.
    - **Pheochromocytoma:** Catecholamine-secreting tumor of adrenal medulla producing paroxysmal or sustained hypertension with the classic triad of headache, sweating, and palpitations.
    - **Cushing's Syndrome:** Excess cortisol activating glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptors.
    - **Acromegaly:** Excess Growth Hormone causing sodium retention and vascular hypertrophy.
    - **Thyroid Disorders:** Thyrotoxicosis (isolated systolic HTN) and Primary Hypothyroidism (diastolic HTN).
    - **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH):** 11β-hydroxylase or 17α-hydroxylase deficiency.
3. **Vascular / Cardiovascular Causes:**
    - **Coarctation of the Aorta:** Congenital narrowing producing upper limb hypertension with radio-femoral delay and weak lower limb pulses.
4. **Drug-Induced & Exogenous Toxins:**
    - Oral Contraceptive Pills (estrogen-induced hepatic angiotensinogen production), NSAIDs (inhibition of renal vasodilatory prostaglandins), Systemic Glucocorticoids, Sympathomimetics (cocaine, amphetamines, decongestants), Cyclosporine, Erythropoietin, and Licorice ingestion (inhibits 11β-HSD2).
5. **Pregnancy-Related:**
    - Preeclampsia, Eclampsia, Gestational Hypertension.

### B. ACC/AHA 2017 Categories

- Normal: <120/<80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120–129/<80 mmHg
- Stage 1 Hypertension: 130–139 OR 80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 Hypertension: ≥140 OR ≥90 mmHg

### C. Hypertensive Crises

- **Hypertensive Urgency:** Severe BP elevation (SBP >180 mmHg, DBP >120 mmHg) **WITHOUT** evidence of acute target organ damage. Managed with oral antihypertensives over 24–48 hours.
- **Hypertensive Emergency / Accelerated Hypertension:** Severe BP elevation **WITH** ongoing, rapidly progressive target organ damage (papilledema, encephalopathy, acute pulmonary edema, acute aortic dissection, acute kidney injury). Requires immediate ICU admission and parenteral IV antihypertensive therapy.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Essential Hypertension Pathophysiology

1. **Defective Renal Pressure-Natriuresis:** Impaired renal sodium excretion at normal arterial pressures leads to salt and water retention.
2. **Volume-Vasoconstriction Coupling:**
    - Increased extracellular fluid volume → ↑ Cardiac Output (initially).
    - Tissue hyperperfusion triggers an intrinsic autoregulatory vascular response → widespread arteriolar **vasoconstriction**.
    - Long-term elevated pressure induces vascular smooth muscle cell hypertrophy, hyaline arteriosclerosis, and narrowing of vessel lumina, shifting the primary hemodynamic abnormality to elevated **Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR)**.

### B. Accelerated / Malignant Hypertension Pathophysiology

1. A sudden, drastic spike in blood pressure exceeds the upper limit of cerebral and systemic vascular autoregulation.
2. **Endothelial Shear Stress & Injury:** Severe pressure mechanical trauma damages endothelial cell linings, increasing vascular permeability.
3. **Fibrinoid Necrosis & Microthrombosis:** Plasma proteins (including fibrin) enter the damaged vessel wall, causing **fibrinoid necrosis** of arterioles. Platelet aggregation and microvascular thrombosis cause focal luminal obliteration.
4. **Ischaemic Vicious Cycle:** Intravascular thrombosis leads to tissue ischemia, triggering further renin release from ischemic kidneys, driving blood pressure even higher in a self-perpetuating cycle.

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Essential Hypertension

- **Symptoms:**
    - **Asymptomatic ("The Silent Killer"):** The vast majority of patients have no symptoms for years until diagnosed incidentally or presenting with a target organ complication.
    - Nonspecific symptoms (when present): Early morning suboccipital headache, throbbing dizziness, tinnitus, epistaxis, fatigue, and palpitations.
- **Physical Signs:**
    - **Blood Pressure:** Elevated SBP/DBP measured under standardized conditions.
    - **Cardiovascular Examination:** Forceful, heaving, leftward-displaced apex beat (Left Ventricular Hypertrophy), **accentuated aortic component of second heart sound (loud A₂)**, and **Fourth Heart Sound (S₄ gallop)** due to atrial contraction into a stiff LV.
    - **Fundoscopy (Hypertensive Retinopathy - Scheie's / Keith-Wagener Classification) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
        - _Grade 1:_ Arteriolar thickening, tortuosity, and silver/copper wiring.
        - _Grade 2:_ Grade 1 + Arteriovenous (AV) nipping / crossing compression.
        - _Grade 3:_ Grade 2 + Retinal hemorrhages (flame-shaped) and "cotton wool" exudates (retinal infarcts).
        - _Grade 4:_ Grade 3 + **Papilledema** (optic disc swelling) (pathognomonic for accelerated/malignant HTN). (See **Diagram 1**).

### B. Secondary Hypertension Clinical Clues

- **Renal Artery Stenosis:** Epigastric/flank abdominal bruits, sudden worsening of BP after starting ACE inhibitors.
- **Coarctation of Aorta:** Upper extremity hypertension with weak/delayed femoral pulses (**radio-femoral delay**) and systolic murmur over interscapular back.
- **Pheochromocytoma:** Paroxysmal attacks of severe headache, sweating, palpitations, pallor, and postural hypotension.
- **Cushing's Syndrome:** Truncal obesity, moon facies, purple abdominal striae, proximal muscle weakness.
- **Conn's Syndrome:** Hypertension with spontaneous or diuretic-induced hypokalemia and muscle weakness.

## 6. Diagnosis / Baseline Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Diagnostic Objectives

1. Obtain accurate, representative BP measurements (confirming sustained HTN).
2. Screen for underlying secondary causes of hypertension.
3. Detect pre-existing target organ damage (TOD).
4. Quantify overall 10-year cardiovascular risk.

---

### B. Mandatory Baseline Investigations (Essential HTN Workup)

1. **Urinalysis:**
    - **Dipstick & Microscopy:** Evaluates for proteinuria / albuminuria (microalbuminuria is the earliest marker of target organ renal damage), hematuria, and urinary casts (glomerular disease).
2. **Renal Function Tests & Electrolytes:**
    - **Serum Creatinine, Blood Urea, and eGFR:** Assesses hypertensive nephrosclerosis or underlying CKD.
    - **Serum Sodium & Potassium:** Hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis in unmedicated patients suggests Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's).
3. **Fasting Plasma Glucose & HbA1c:** Detects co-existing Diabetes Mellitus (a major cardiovascular risk multiplier).
4. **Fasting Serum Lipid Profile:** Measures Total Cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, and Triglycerides.
5. **Serum Uric Acid:** Baseline measurement prior to initiating thiazide diuretic therapy.
6. **12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) Criteria:**
        - _Sokolow-Lyon Index:_ \(\text{S in V}_1 + \text{R in V}_5\text{ or V}_6 > 35\text{ mm}\).
        - _Cornell Voltage:_ \(\text{R in aVL} + \text{S in V}_3 > 28\text{ mm (men) or } >20\text{ mm (women)}\).
    - Evaluates for LV "strain" pattern (ST depression & T-wave inversion in V₅–V₆), prior ischaemic Q waves, or Atrial Fibrillation.
7. **Thyroid Function Tests (Serum TSH):** Excludes occult hypothyroidism or thyrotoxicosis.

---

### C. Specialized / Secondary Investigations (Indicated in young patients <40 yrs, resistant HTN, or suspected secondary cause)

- **Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitoring (ABPM) / Home BP Monitoring (HBPM):** 24-hour automated monitoring to rule out "White-Coat Hypertension" (clinic BP ≥140/90, ABPM <135/85) or "Masked Hypertension".
- **Echocardiography:** Gold standard to quantify Left Ventricular Mass Index, concentric wall thickness, and diastolic dysfunction.
- **Renal Ultrasound & Duplex Doppler / CT Renal Angiography:** Assesses kidney size, polycystic changes, and Renal Artery Stenosis.
- **Plasma Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio (ARR):** Screening test for Primary Hyperaldosteronism (elevated PAC with suppressed PRA).
- **24-Hour Urinary Fractional Metanephrines / Plasma Free Metanephrines:** Diagnostic screening for Pheochromocytoma.
- **24-Hour Urinary Free Cortisol / Overnight Dexamethasone Suppression Test:** Screens for Cushing's syndrome.

## 8. Management of Accelerated / Malignant Hypertension [⭐ High-Yield]

Accelerated hypertension is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospitalization (preferably in an HDU/ICU) and parenteral antihypertensive therapy.

### A. Hemodynamic Principles & Safety Targets

- **Avoid Rapid Blood Pressure Drops:** Sudden drastic drops in BP overwhelm altered cerebral and renal autoregulation curves (See **Diagram 2**), precipitating ischemic stroke, cerebral infarction, blindness, or acute coronary insufficiency.
- **Therapeutic Goal:** Lower Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by **no more than 20% to 25% within the first 1 to 2 hours**, then gradually to **160/100 mmHg over the next 2 to 6 hours**, and normalize over 24 to 48 hours.
- _Exception to Slow Reduction Rule:_ Acute Aortic Dissection requires rapid reduction of SBP to <120 mmHg within 20 minutes!

---

### B. Parenteral (IV) Antihypertensive Protocols in Accelerated Phase

#### 1. Labetalol (Combined α + β Blocker) [First-Line Choice]

- **Mechanism:** Blocks α₁ and non-selective β-receptors (α:β ratio = 1:7 IV). Reduces TPR without altering cardiac output or cerebral blood flow.
- **Dose & Administration:**
    - _IV Bolus:_ 20–40 mg IV over 2 minutes; repeat 20–80 mg every 10 minutes (maximum total dose 300 mg).
    - _IV Infusion:_ 1–2 mg/min titrated to response.
- **Features:** Drug of choice in hypertensive encephalopathy, acute stroke, aortic dissection, and preeclampsia. Does NOT increase intracranial pressure.
- **Contraindications:** Asthma/COPD, 2nd/3rd-degree heart block, acute decompensated heart failure.

#### 2. Nicardipine (Dihydropyridine CCB)

- **Mechanism:** Potent, highly vasoselective L-type calcium channel blocker causing arteriolar vasodilation.
- **Dose & Administration:** Initiate at 5 mg/hour IV infusion; increase by 2.5 mg/hr every 15 minutes up to a maximum of 15 mg/hour.
- **Features:** Highly titratable, predictable response; safe in heart failure and renal impairment.

#### 3. Sodium Nitroprusside (Direct Arteriolar + Venodilator)

- **Mechanism:** Releases Nitric Oxide (NO) directly, causing immediate dilation of resistance and capacitance vessels.
- **Dose & Administration:** 0.3 to 1.0 μg/kg/min IV infusion, titrated upward every 5 minutes (maximum 10 μg/kg/min). Requires continuous intra-arterial BP monitoring.
- **Side Effects / Toxicity:** Metabolism produces **cyanide and thiocyanate**, causing metabolic acidosis, confusion, and convulsions if infused >48 hours or in renal failure.

#### 4. Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN Infusion)

- **Dose:** 5–20 μg/min IV infusion. Preferred in accelerated HTN complicated by **Acute Coronary Syndrome** or **Acute Left Ventricular Failure with Pulmonary Edema**.

    - _Thiazides & Thiazide-like:_ Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone, Indapamide.

    - _Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors:_ Captopril, Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril, Perindopril.
    - _Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs):_ Losartan, Candesartan, Valsartan, Telmisartan, Olmesartan.

    - _Dihydropyridines (DHPs):_ Amlodipine, Felodipine, Cilnidipine, Lacidipine, Lercanidipine, Nifedipine SR.

    - _Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers):_ Metoprolol, Atenolol, Bisoprolol, Propranolol, Nebivolol.

    - _Alpha₁-Selective Blockers:_ Prazosin, Terazosin, Doxazosin.
    - _Central Sympatholytics:_ Clonidine, Alpha-Methyldopa.
5. **Vasodilators:**
    - _Arteriolar Dilators:_ Hydralazine, Minoxidil.
    - _Arteriolar + Venodilator:_ Sodium Nitroprusside.

---

### Stepwise Management Strategy (NICE 2011 / JNC-8 Guidelines)

- **Step 1 (Initial Monotherapy):**
    - _Patients <55 years (Non-Black):_ **ACE Inhibitor or ARB** (Class **A**).
    - _Patients ≥55 years OR Black (any age):_ **Calcium Channel Blocker** (Class **C**) OR Thiazide-like Diuretic (Class **D**).
- **Step 2 (Dual Combination):**
    - Combine **A + C** (ACE-I/ARB + CCB) OR **A + D** (ACE-I/ARB + Diuretic).
- **Step 3 (Triple Combination):**
    - Combine **A + C + D** (ACE-I/ARB + CCB + Thiazide-like Diuretic).
- **Step 4 (Resistant Hypertension):**
    - Add Spironolactone (if serum K⁺ <4.5 mmol/L) OR higher-dose thiazide OR Alpha/Beta-blocker.

## 10. Dosage Rule (Representative Core Antihypertensives)

### 1. Enalapril (ACE Inhibitor Prototype)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 532; Davidson p. 532). Prodrug hydrolyzed in liver to active enalaprilat. Inhibits ACE enzyme → prevents conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II. Suppresses vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, and myocyte remodeling. Inhibits kininase II, raising vasodilatory bradykinin levels.
- **Dose:** Initial starting dose 2.5–5 mg PO OD; usual maintenance dose 10–20 mg PO OD (maximum 40 mg/day).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line drug in young hypertensives, diabetics, heart failure, and CKD with proteinuric nephropathy.
- **Side Effects:** Dry persistent cough (bradykinin accumulation), hyperkalemia, first-dose hypotension, acute GFR drop, angioedema.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in diabetic/proteinuric HTN. **Contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis, hyperkalemia (K⁺ >5.5 mmol/L), and pregnancy**.

### 2. Amlodipine (Dihydropyridine CCB)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 595, 608; Davidson p. 533). Slow-acting, long-duration dihydropyridine CCB. Blocks voltage-gated L-type Ca²⁺ channels in vascular smooth muscle cells, causing smooth muscle relaxation and decreased Total Peripheral Resistance.
- **Dose:** Initial dose 2.5–5 mg PO OD; maintenance dose 5–10 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** First-line choice in elderly patients, isolated systolic HTN, and black patients. Unaffected by food; smooth 24-hour action.
- **Side Effects:** Ankle pretibial edema (transcapillary pressure shift), flushing, headache, dizziness, lower esophageal sphincter relaxation.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line monotherapy in patients ≥55 years. Safe in asthma, COPD, and peripheral vascular disease.

### 3. Chlorthalidone (Thiazide-like Diuretic)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 605, 630; Davidson p. 533). Inhibits Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter (NCCT) in early distal convoluted tubule → increases urinary Na⁺ and H₂O excretion. Long-term action reduces intracellular vascular smooth muscle Na⁺, decreasing responsiveness to vasoconstrictors.
- **Dose:** 12.5 mg to 25 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily in the morning.
- **Features:** Long plasma half-life (40–50 hours) providing superior 24-hour BP control compared to hydrochlorothiazide.
- **Side Effects:** Hypokalemia, hyperuricemia (gout precipitation), hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, hyponatremia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line step-1/step-2 drug. Ineffective and avoided in severe renal failure (eGFR <30 mL/min; replace with loop diuretics).

### 4. Telmisartan (Angiotensin II Receptor Blocker - ARB)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 536; Davidson p. 533). Highly selective, competitive antagonist of AT₁ receptors. Blocks all Ang II actions (vasoconstriction, aldosterone release) without affecting bradykinin degradation. Has additional PPAR-γ agonistic properties.
- **Dose:** 20 mg to 80 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Longest terminal elimination half-life (~24 hours) among ARBs. Zero risk of dry cough.
- **Side Effects:** Hypotension, hyperkalemia, renal impairment.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line alternative in patients intolerant to ACE inhibitors due to cough. **Contraindicated in pregnancy**.

### 5. Alpha-Methyldopa (Central Sympatholytic)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 611; Davidson p. 535). Prodrug converted in brain to α-methylnoradrenaline, which stimulates central presynaptic α₂-adrenoceptors in the nucleus tractus solitarius, reducing central sympathetic outflow to heart and vessels.
- **Dose:** 250 mg PO BD/TDS, increased to 500 mg TDS (maximum 2 g/day).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Two to three times daily.
- **Features:** **Drug of choice for chronic hypertension in pregnancy** (safety proven over decades).
- **Side Effects:** Sedation, mental depression, dry mouth, Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia, drug fever, hepatitis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Drug of choice in pregnancy-induced HTN. Stop if autoimmune hemolytic anemia or liver dysfunction develops.

## 11. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Cardiovascular:** Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH), Congestive Heart Failure (HFrEF & HFpEF), Coronary Artery Disease (Angina, Acute MI), Atrial Fibrillation.
2. **Cerebrovascular:** Ischemic Stroke, Intracerebral Hemorrhage, Transient Ischaemic Attack (TIA), Hypertensive Encephalopathy, Multi-Infarct Dementia.
3. **Renal:** Hyaline Arteriosclerosis, Benign Nephrosclerosis, Accelerated Malignant Nephrosclerosis, Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD).
4. **Vascular & Visual:** Aortic Dissection, Peripheral Arterial Disease, Hypertensive Retinopathy (Grades 1–4), Central Retinal Vein Thrombosis.

### Memory Palace: "The High-Pressure Boiler Room"

To remember the management of Accelerated Hypertension, walk through 4 stations in a high-pressure boiler room:

1. **Station 1 (The Exploding Red Pressure Gauge):** A boiler gauge redlining above **180/120 mmHg** with sparks flying (**Accelerated HTN with Fibrinoid Necrosis**).
2. **Station 2 (The Slow Valve Release Wheel):** A safety officer carefully turns a heavy wheel to lower pressure by **only 20–25% in the first 2 hours** (**Controlled MAP reduction to prevent cerebral ischemia**).
3. **Station 3 (The Labetalol Dual Emergency Hose):** Firefighters using a combined α+β blue hose (**IV Labetalol 20–40 mg bolus**) to suppress flames without stopping the main cooling pump.
4. **Station 4 (The Glass Fundus Window):** Inspecting a soot-covered window showing flame-like cracks and a swollen central dial (**Grade 4 Retinopathy with Papilledema**).

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In managing a patient with **Accelerated / Malignant Hypertension**, NEVER lower the blood pressure to normotensive levels rapidly in the first few hours! Chronic high blood pressure shifts the cerebral blood flow autoregulation curve to the right. Dropping blood pressure too quickly or excessively (e.g., giving sublingual nifedipine) causes catastrophic cerebral hypoperfusion, resulting in ischemic stroke, occipital blindness, or acute myocardial infarction. Always aim for a controlled 20–25% MAP reduction over the first 1–2 hours.

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Combining an **ACE Inhibitor (e.g., Enalapril) with an Angiotensin Receptor Blocker (e.g., Losartan)** for dual RAAS blockade in hypertension.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Large multicentric clinical trials (e.g., ONTARGET) proved that combining an ACE-I with an ARB provides NO additional cardiovascular or BP-lowering benefit, but significantly increases the risk of severe **hyperkalemia, acute kidney injury, and syncope**. Guidelines (JNC-8, NICE, ESC) strictly **CONTRAINDICATE**the combined routine use of ACE inhibitors and ARBs.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition & Criteria:** HTN defined as sustained SBP ≥140 and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg. Essential HTN = 90–95% cases; Secondary = 5–10%.
- **Accelerated HTN:** SBP >180 / DBP >120 mmHg with fibrinoid necrosis and Grade 3/4 retinopathy (papilledema).
- **Baseline Investigations:** Urinalysis (proteinuria), Serum Creatinine/Electrolytes, Fasting Glucose, Lipid Profile, and 12-lead ECG (LVH criteria).
- **Accelerated HTN Management:** IV Labetalol or IV Nicardipine; target 20–25% MAP reduction in first 1–2 hours (avoid rapid drop).
- **First-Line Drug Classes:** ACE-I / ARB (young/diabetics/CKD), CCB (elderly/black patients), Thiazide-like Diuretics (Chlorthalidone/Indapamide).

> **Hypertension is a chronic, polygenic, and environment-driven cardiovascular disorder characterized by sustained arterial pressure elevation, where progressive arteriolar wall remodeling drives target organ damage, and acute severe surges precipitate fibrinoid microvascular necrosis in accelerated phase disease.**
