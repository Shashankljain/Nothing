# Acute Myocardial Infarction

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)** is defined according to the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction as myocardial necrosis caused by acute, prolonged myocardial ischemia. Diagnostic criteria require the detection of a rise and/or fall of cardiac biomarker values (preferably **cardiac troponin T or I**), with at least one value above the 99th percentile upper reference limit (URL), accompanied by at least one of the following clinical features:

1. Symptoms of myocardial ischemia.
2. New or presumed new significant ST-segment/T-wave (ST–T) changes or new left bundle branch block (LBBB) on 12-lead ECG.
3. Development of pathological Q waves on ECG.
4. Imaging evidence of new loss of viable myocardium or new regional wall motion abnormality (RWMA).
5. Identification of an intracoronary thrombus by angiography or autopsy.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary Etiology

- **Type 1 MI (Atherothrombotic Occlusion - ~90% of cases):** Plaque rupture, erosion, or fissuring of a vulnerable atheromatous plaque in an epicardial coronary artery, triggering platelet aggregation and superadded occlusive thrombus formation.
- **Type 2 MI (Supply-Demand Mismatch):** Myocardial necrosis in the absence of acute atherothrombosis, caused by severe coronary vasospasm (Prinzmetal angina), coronary embolism, acute aortic dissection, severe anemia, tachyarrhythmias, or profound hypotension.

#### 1. Non-Modifiable (Constitutional) Risk Factors

- **Age:** Risk rises progressively with age; >45 years in men and >55 years in women.
- **Gender:** Males are at significantly higher risk during middle age; post-menopausal females lose estrogenic protection and risk equalizes.
- **Family History:** Positive family history of premature CAD (<50–55 years in first-degree male relative, <55–65 years in female relative) increases risk up to 8-fold.
- **Genetics:** Polygenic inheritance of hypercholesterolemia, hypertension, and vascular reactivity.

#### 2. Modifiable Major Risk Factors

- **Cigarette Smoking:** The single most important modifiable risk factor; doubles mortality from ischemic heart disease by inducing endothelial dysfunction, platelet activation, and vascular inflammation.
- **Hypertension:** Systolic BP ≥140 mmHg or Diastolic BP ≥90 mmHg increases ischemic heart disease risk by ~60% via endothelial injury and elevated left ventricular afterload.
- **Hyperlipidemia / Dyslipidemia:** Elevated serum total cholesterol and Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL-C >130–160 mg/dL) combined with low High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL-C <40 mg/dL in men, <50 mg/dL in women) accelerates atherogenesis.
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** A potent "CAD risk equivalent" that doubles MI risk, induces microvascular disease, and predisposes to silent/painless MI.
- **Obesity & Sedentary Lifestyle:** Central/truncal obesity (BMI >25 kg/m²) and physical inactivity double CAD risk.
- **Inflammatory & Hemostatic Markers:** Elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP >3 mg/dL), Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)], and hyperfibrinogenemia.

## 3. Classification

### A. Clinical & Electrocardiographic Classification

1. **ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):** Complete, acute occlusive thrombus of an epicardial coronary artery producing transmural full-thickness myocardial ischemia and necrosis.
2. **Non-ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI):** Subtotal or transient thrombotic occlusion producing partial-thickness (subendocardial) necrosis without ST-elevation.

### B. Pathological Classification (Universal Definition Types)

- **Type 1:** Spontaneous MI due to primary coronary atherothrombotic plaque rupture/erosion.
- **Type 2:** Secondary MI to ischemia from increased oxygen demand or decreased supply.
- **Type 3:** Cardiac death with symptoms suggestive of MI before biomarkers are obtained.
- **Type 4a / 4b:** PCI-related MI / Stent thrombosis associated MI.
- **Type 5:** Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG)-related MI.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Plaque Disruption:** Physical stress or intraplaque inflammation causes fissuring, erosion, or rupture of a thin-capped vulnerable atheromatous plaque in a large epicardial coronary artery (most commonly Left Anterior Descending [LAD] artery ~45–50%, Right Coronary Artery [RCA] ~30–40%, Left Circumflex [LCx] ~15–20%).
2. **Thrombus Formation:** Subendothelial collagen and necrotic plaque contents are exposed to blood flow → platelet adhesion, aggregation, and activation (releasing TXA₂ and ADP) → coagulation cascade activation → occlusive fibrin-platelet thrombus.
3. **Ischemic Cascade:** Sudden blood flow deprivation causes cessation of aerobic metabolism within seconds → intracellular ATP depletion & lactic acid accumulation → loss of myocardial contractility within 1 minute.
4. **Irreversible Necrosis Wavefront:** Irreversible myocyte sarcolemmal disruption (necrosis) begins after 20 to 30 minutes of severe ischemia, originating in the subendocardium and progressing outward toward the epicardium over 6 to 12 hours (the "wavefront phenomenon").

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Severe Cardiac Chest Pain:** The cardinal symptom. Heavy, crushing, squeezing, or constricting retrosternal chest pain lasting >20 minutes; radiates to the neck, jaw, throat, left shoulder, epigastrium, or both arms. Pain is **not relieved by rest or sublingual nitroglycerin**.
- **Autonomic Symptoms:** Profuse sweating (diaphoresis), pallor, nausea, and vomiting (due to vagal stimulation, especially in inferior MI).
- **Dyspnea & Orthopnea:** Breathlessness due to impaired myocardial contractility and pulmonary congestion.
- **Apprehension:** Marked anxiety and intense "fear of impending death" (_angor animi_).
- **Silent / Painless MI:** Occurs in up to 20–25% of patients, particularly in elderly individuals and patients with long-standing diabetes mellitus (due to autonomic neuropathy).

### B. Physical Signs

- **Sympathetic Drive:** Tachycardia, elevated blood pressure, pallor, cold clammy extremities.
- **Vagal Drive (Inferior MI):** Bradycardia, hypotension, vomiting.
- **Impaired LV Dysfunction:** Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg), oliguria, narrow pulse pressure, displaced diffuse apical impulse, **soft S₁**, **S₃ gallop** (early left heart failure), and bilateral fine inspiratory basal pulmonary crackles/crepitations.
- **Low-Grade Fever:** Appears within 24 to 48 hours secondary to myocardial tissue necrosis and acute inflammatory response.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (12-Lead ECG)

- **STEMI Criteria:** New ST-segment elevation at the J-point in two contiguous leads of **≥1 mm (0.1 mV)** in limb leads or **≥2 mm (0.2 mV)** in chest leads (V₂–V₃), or **new Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)**.
- **ECG Evolution:** Hyperacute tall T waves → convex ST-segment elevation → T-wave inversion → development of pathological Q waves (>1 mm wide, >25% of R-wave depth).
- **Anatomic Localization:**
    - _Antero-septal / Anterolateral (LAD):_ V₁–V₄ / V₅–V₆, I, aVL.
    - _Inferior (RCA):_ II, III, aVF.
    - _Lateral (LCx):_ I, aVL, V₅–V₆.
    - _Right Ventricular (RCA):_ ST elevation in right-sided lead V₄R.

### B. Cardiac Biomarkers [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cardiac Troponins (cTnT & cTnI):** The gold-standard diagnostic biomarker.
    - _Onset:_ Rises within **3 to 6 hours** post-infarction.
    - _Peak:_ Peaks at **24 to 48 hours**.
    - _Duration:_ Remains elevated for **10 to 14 days**.
- **Creatine Kinase-MB (CK-MB):** Rises in 4–8 hours, peaks at 24 hours, normalizes in 48–72 hours. Highly valuable for detecting **early re-infarction** occurring within 28 days.

### C. Imaging & Laboratory Workup

- **Bedside Echocardiography:** Identifies regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA), estimates LV ejection fraction, and excludes mechanical complications.
- **Coronary Angiography:** Gold-standard investigation for anatomical localization and immediate revascularization.
- **Routine Labs:** Full blood count (leukocytosis peak day 1), ESR/CRP (elevated), serum lipid profile (measured within 24 hours of admission).

### A. Immediate Pre-Hospital & Emergency Resuscitative Measures (MONA / BASH Protocol)

1. **Oxygenation:** Administer oxygen (2–4 L/min via nasal cannula) **ONLY if SpO₂ is <90%** or if patient is in respiratory distress.
2. **Analgesia & Anxiolysis:** **IV Morphine Sulphate 5–10 mg** (or Diamorphine 2.5–5 mg) slow IV titrated with **Metoclopramide 10 mg IV** antiemetic.
3. **Sublingual Nitrates:** Sublingual GTN (0.5 mg tablet / spray) up to 3 doses 5 mins apart for chest pain relief (if SBP >90 mmHg; **avoid in Right Ventricular Infarction**).
4. **Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) Loading:**
    - **Aspirin:** **300 mg** chewable non-enteric tablet immediately.
    - **P2Y₁₂ Inhibitor:** **Ticagrelor 180 mg** PO OR **Prasugrel 60 mg** PO (if undergoing PCI) OR **Clopidogrel 300–600 mg** PO.
5. **High-Intensity Statin Loading:** **Atorvastatin 80 mg** PO or **Rosuvastatin 40 mg** PO immediately.

---

### B. Reperfusion Therapy (Golden Window <12 Hours) [⭐ High-Yield]

Reperfusion must be instituted as quickly as possible (_"Time is Myocardium"_).

#### 1. Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) [First-Line Choice]

- **Indication:** Preferred revascularization mode for all STEMI patients presenting within 12 hours of symptom onset.
- **Target Time:** First Medical Contact (FMC) to wire crossing **<90 to 120 minutes**.
- **Efficacy:** Restores vessel patency in >95% of patients, Halves heart failure rates, and achieves 1-year survival >95%.

#### 2. Thrombolytic / Fibrinolytic Therapy [Alternative Strategy]

- **Indication:** Indicated in STEMI when primary PCI cannot be performed within 120 minutes of diagnosis, provided symptom onset is <12 hours and no contraindications exist.
- **Fibrinolytic Agents:**
    - **Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA):** Fibrin-specific, single IV bolus **0.5 mg/kg over 10 seconds**.
    - **Alteplase (rt-PA):** Accelerated 90-minute IV infusion (15 mg bolus → 50 mg over 30 mins → 35 mg over 60 mins).
    - **Streptokinase:** 1.5 million IU IV in 100 mL saline over 1 hour (non-fibrin specific, antigenic).
- **Absolute Contraindications [⭐ High-Yield]:** Prior intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke within 3 months, known structural cerebrovascular lesion/aneurysm, active internal bleeding, aortic dissection, or severe closed head/facial trauma within 3 months.
- **Pharmaco-Invasive Approach:** If thrombolysis is successful (>70% ST resolution at 60–90 mins), perform routine coronary angiography/PCI within 2 to 24 hours.

---

### C. In-Hospital Pharmacotherapy Started Within First 24 Hours [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Parenteral Anticoagulation

- **Low Molecular Weight Heparin (Enoxaparin):** **30 mg IV bolus** followed by **1 mg/kg SC twice daily**. Alternative: **Fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC daily**. Continued for up to 8 days or until revascularization.

#### 2. Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers)

- **Initiation:** Administer oral **Metoprolol 25–50 mg BD** or **Bisoprolol 2.5–5 mg OD** within the first 24 hours in all hemodynamically stable patients.
- **Benefit:** Reduces myocardial oxygen demand, limits infarct size, prevents ventricular arrhythmias, and reduces short- and long-term mortality.
- **Contraindications in Acute MI:** Heart failure/pulmonary edema, hypotension (SBP <105 mmHg), bradycardia (HR <65 bpm), or severe asthma.

#### 3. Renin-Angiotensin System Blockade (ACE Inhibitors / ARBs)

- **Initiation:** Oral **Enalapril 2.5 mg BD** or **Ramipril 1.25–2.5 mg OD** started within the first 24 hours once hemodynamically stable.
- **Benefit:** Arrests pathological ventricular remodeling, prevents progressive LV dilation, reduces incidence of heart failure, and improves long-term survival.
- **ARBs:** **Valsartan 40 mg BD** or **Candesartan 4 mg OD** if intolerant to ACEi (cough).

### A. Cardiac Arrhythmias (Most Common Early Complication ~90%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Ventricular Fibrillation (VF):** Occurs in 5–10% of patients; primary cause of sudden death in the first 24 hours. Requires immediate unsynchronized DC shock (150–200 J biphasic) + CPR.
- **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) & Accelerated Idioventricular Rhythm (AIVR):** AIVR is common upon successful reperfusion and usually benign.
- **Sinus Bradycardia:** Very common in **Inferior MI** due to heightened vagal tone and RCA ischemia to SA node; treated with **IV Atropine 0.6–1.2 mg** if symptomatic.
- **Atrioventricular (AV) Block:**
    - _Inferior MI:_ Transient 1° / 2° Mobitz I AV block; good escape rhythm; responds to Atropine.
    - _Anterior MI:_ Infra-Hisian 2° Mobitz II or 3° Complete AV block indicating massive septal infarction; requires immediate **Temporary Transvenous Pacemaker** insertion.
- **Atrial Fibrillation (AF):** Occurs in 10–15% secondary to atrial ischemia or elevated LV filling pressures.

### B. Acute Pump Failure & Hemodynamic Complications

- **Acute Left Ventricular Failure / Pulmonary Edema:** Develops when >25% of LV myocardium is damaged. Managed with IV Furosemide, nitrates, and CPAP.
- **Cardiogenic Shock:** Occurs when **>40% of LV myocardium undergoes necrosis**. Characterized by SBP <90 mmHg, cardiac index <2.2 L/min/m², oliguria, and cold clammy peripheries. Requires emergency PCI, inotropes (dobutamine), and Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP).
- **Right Ventricular Infarction (RVMI):** Associated with inferior MI (RCA occlusion). Classic triad: **Hypotension, clear lung fields, and markedly elevated JVP**. Treated with **IV fluid expansion** (avoid nitrates and diuretics).

### C. Early Mechanical Complications (Days 1 to 7)

- **Acute Mitral Regurgitation:** Caused by posteromedial papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture (RCA branch involvement). Presents with acute pulmonary edema, cardiogenic shock, and a new pansystolic murmur. Diagnosis confirmed by Echocardiography; requires urgent surgical repair.
- **Ventricular Septal Rupture (VSR):** Rupture of necrotic interventricular septum. Presents with sudden shock, right heart failure, and a **new harsh pansystolic murmur with thrill** at the left lower sternal border. Treated with emergency surgical repair.
- **Left Ventricular Free Wall Rupture:** Rupture of necrotic myocardium leading to rapidly fatal hemopericardium, cardiac tamponade, and pulseless electrical activity (PEA).

### D. Early Pericarditis

- **Fibrinous Pericarditis:** Occurs on **days 2 to 3** in transmural MI due to epicardial inflammation. Characterized by sharp positional chest pain (worse lying flat, better leaning forward) and an audible **pericardial friction rub**. Treated with high-dose Aspirin (avoid NSAIDs/steroids as they impair scar healing).

## 10. Dosage Rule (Core Acute MI Medications)

### 1. Aspirin (Antiplatelet / COX-1 Inhibitor)

- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly acetylates COX-1 enzyme in platelets, suppressing Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) synthesis for the 7–10 day platelet lifespan.
- **Dose:** **300 mg** chewable loading dose; **75 to 100 mg PO OD** daily maintenance.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (chewed for rapid absorption).
- **Frequency:** Single loading dose, then once daily.
- **Features:** Reduces mortality by 25% in acute MI.
- **Side Effects:** Gastric irritation, peptic ulceration, bleeding.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately at presentation; continue indefinitely.

### 2. Ticagrelor (P2Y₁₂ Receptor Antagonist)

- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversibly binds and blocks ADP P2Y₁₂ receptors on platelets, inhibiting ADP-induced platelet aggregation.
- **Dose:** **180 mg** loading dose; **90 mg PO BD** maintenance.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Loading stat, then twice daily.
- **Features:** Superior to clopidogrel in reducing ACS mortality (PLATO trial).
- **Side Effects:** Dyspnea, bleeding, ventricular pauses.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at presentation; continue for 12 months.

### 3. Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA Fibrinolytic)

- **Mechanism of Action:** Genetically engineered recombinant tissue plasminogen activator that selectively converts clot-bound plasminogen to plasmin.
- **Dose:** **0.5 mg/kg** (or weight-based 30–50 mg) single IV bolus over 10 seconds.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous single bolus.
- **Frequency:** Single stat dose.
- **Features:** Fastest fibrinolytic administration; ideal for pre-hospital thrombolysis.
- **Side Effects:** Major bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage (~0.5–1%).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Give within 12 hours of STEMI if primary PCI is delayed >120 mins.

### 4. Metoprolol (Beta-1 Selective Blocker)

- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitively blocks β₁-adrenoceptors, reducing heart rate, myocardial contractility, and oxygen consumption.
- **Dose:** **25 to 50 mg** PO twice daily (titrated to 100 mg BD).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily.
- **Features:** Limits infarct size and prevents ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, heart failure exacerbation.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start within 24 hours once hemodynamically stable.

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In a patient presenting with an **Inferior Wall MI**, always record right-sided precordial ECG leads (**V₃R and V₄R**) before administering sublingual Nitrates or Morphine! If ST-elevation is present in V₄R (indicating **Right Ventricular Infarction**), nitrates, morphine, and diuretics are **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** as they drop RV preload, precipitating profound, catastrophic hypotension. The primary treatment for RVMI hypotension is **rapid IV 0.9% Normal Saline fluid expansion**!

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Thrombolytic Therapy (Tenecteplase / Streptokinase)** to a patient presenting with a **Non-ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI)** or Unstable Angina.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** NSTEMI is caused by subtotal/non-occlusive platelet-rich thrombi. Clinical trials demonstrate that thrombolytics in NSTEMI afford **NO benefit and significantly increase mortality and intracranial hemorrhage risk**.
- **Resolution:** Thrombolysis is indicated **ONLY in STEMI** (when primary PCI is unavailable). NSTEMI is managed medically with antiplatelets, anticoagulants, and early inpatient coronary angiography/PCI.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Myocardial necrosis with troponin elevation + clinical/ECG evidence of ischemia.
- **Crucial Risk Factors:** Smoking (most modifiable), Hypertension, Hyperlipidemia, Diabetes, Male gender, Family history.
- **Reperfusion Goal:** Primary PCI (<120 mins) or Thrombolysis with Tenecteplase (<12 hours) to halt transmural necrosis wavefront.
- **First 24-Hour Medications:** Aspirin 300 mg + Ticagrelor 180 mg + Atorvastatin 80 mg + LMWH + Oral Beta-Blocker & ACE Inhibitor (when stable).
- **Early Complications:** Ventricular Fibrillation (major early death cause ~90% arrhythmias), Cardiogenic shock (>40% LV loss), Papillary muscle rupture (acute MR), and VSR.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Acute myocardial infarction is an ischemic necrosis of the myocardium triggered by acute coronary atherothrombosis, requiring rapid diagnosis via 12-lead ECG and cardiac troponins, immediate emergency reperfusion therapy within the golden window, and intensive early complication management.**
