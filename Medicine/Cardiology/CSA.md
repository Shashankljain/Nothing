# Chronic Stable Angina

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Angina Pectoris** is a clinical pain syndrome resulting from transient myocardial ischaemia, which occurs whenever there is an adverse imbalance between myocardial oxygen supply and myocardial oxygen demand.

**Chronic Stable Angina (Classical / Exertional Angina)** is characterized by paroxysmal, predictable, and reproducible episodes of substernal chest discomfort or dyspnoea provoked by physical exertion, cold exposure, heavy meals, or emotional stress, and promptly relieved within 2 to 5 minutes by rest or sublingual nitroglycerin. It is caused by chronic, fixed stenosing coronary atherosclerosis obstructing >70% of the cross-sectional luminal area of large epicardial conducting coronary arteries, without acute plaque rupture or active thrombus formation.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary Etiology

1. **Coronary Atherosclerosis (Dominant Cause - >90%):** Fixed, calcified, or fibrofatty atheromatous plaque in epicardial vessels causing fixed structural luminal stenosis.
2. **Fixed Non-Atherosclerotic Causes:** Calcific Aortic Stenosis, Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM), Syphilitic or autoimmune Aortitis, and Coronary Vasculitis.
3. **Microvascular Angina (Syndrome X):** Typical exertional angina with objective evidence of myocardial ischaemia on stress testing, but angiographically normal epicardial coronary arteries (due to microvascular endothelial dysfunction).
4. **Aggravating / Precipitating Factors:** Severe anaemia (reduced O₂ carrying capacity), thyrotoxicosis (increased metabolic O₂ demand), uncontrolled hypertension, and tachyarrhythmias.

### B. Risk Factors for Coronary Artery Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Non-Modifiable:** Age (>45 years in men, >55 years in women), male gender, family history of premature CAD (first-degree male relative <55 yrs, female <65 yrs).
- **Modifiable:**
    - Cigarette smoking / tobacco use.
    - Systemic Hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg).
    - Dyslipidaemia (elevated LDL-C, low HDL-C, high triglycerides).
    - Diabetes Mellitus and Insulin Resistance.
    - Obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²) and physical inactivity.

---

## 3. Classification (Canadian Cardiovascular Society - CCS Grading)

The Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) scoring system grades the functional severity of angina based on effort tolerance:

- **Class I:** Angina occurs only with strenuous, rapid, or prolonged physical activity; no limitation during ordinary activity.
- **Class II:** Slight limitation of ordinary activity; angina occurs on walking rapidly, climbing stairs, or walking uphill in cold air/stress.
- **Class III:** Marked limitation of ordinary physical activity; angina occurs on walking 1 to 2 blocks on level ground or climbing one flight of stairs at a normal pace.
- **Class IV:** Inability to perform any physical activity without discomfort, or angina present at rest.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Fixed Luminal Occlusion:** Atherosclerotic plaque occludes **>70% of the luminal area** of a conducting epicardial coronary artery. Resting blood flow remains adequate due to compensatory autoregulatory vasodilation of downstream resistance arterioles.
2. **Exertional Mismatch:** Physical exertion or emotional stress increases sympathetic tone, raising heart rate, systolic blood pressure, and myocardial contractility (increasing myocardial O₂ demand).
3. **Exhaustion of Coronary Reserve:** Because epicardial resistance is fixed, maximal downstream arteriolar vasodilation is reached and cannot increase blood flow further.
4. **Subendocardial "Crunch":** Myocardial ischaemia causes the left ventricle to fail to relax completely in diastole, raising Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Pressure (LVEDP) from a normal 5 mmHg up to 25 mmHg. High LVEDP compresses intramyocardial perforating vessels during diastole ("subendocardial crunch"), worsening subendocardial ischaemia. (See **Diagram 1**).
5. **Pain Pathway:** Anaerobic metabolism generates metabolic end-products (adenosine, bradykinin, lactate, H⁺ ions), which stimulate local sympathetic afferent nerve endings (C8–T4 dermatomes), producing referred anginal chest pain.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Site:** Central retrosternal or precordial chest discomfort; occasionally felt behind or to the left of the mediastinum.
- **Character:** Described as a heavy pressure, squeezing, tight band, crushing, or burning sensation ("an elephant standing on the chest").
- **Radiation:** Radiates along C8–T4 dermatomes to the left shoulder, inner aspect of the left arm, neck, jaw, teeth, epigastrium, or interscapular area.
- **Precipitating Factors:** Physical exertion, emotional stress, exposure to cold air, heavy meals ("the 4 E's").
- **Duration:** Typically brief, lasting **2 to 15 minutes** (strictly <20–30 minutes; pain >30 minutes indicates Acute Coronary Syndrome/MI).
- **Relieving Factors:** Prompt relief within **2 to 5 minutes by rest** or sublingual Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN).
- **Angina Equivalents:** Exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, lightheadedness, or diaphoresis (common in elderly and diabetic patients with autonomic neuropathy).

### B. Signs

- **General Examination:** Usually unremarkable at rest. During an active attack, the patient may appear anxious, pale, diaphoresis may be present, and pulse rate/BP may be elevated.
- **Cardiovascular Examination:** Look for underlying risk factors or complications:
    - Xanthomas, xanthelasma, or arcus senilis (hyperlipidaemia).
    - Peripheral arterial disease or carotid bruits.
    - Transient **Fourth Heart Sound (S₄ gallop)** during an ischaemic episode due to reduced LV compliance.
    - Transient apical pansystolic murmur of Mitral Regurgitation secondary to papillary muscle dysfunction during ischaemia.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Baseline Laboratory Investigations

- **Full Blood Count:** Excludes anaemia (which aggravates ischaemia) and leucocytosis.
- **Fasting Blood Glucose & HbA1c:** Screens for Diabetes Mellitus.
- **Fasting Lipid Profile:** Measures total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, and triglycerides.
- **Renal & Thyroid Function Tests:** Excludes hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, and renal dysfunction.

### B. Electrocardiogram (ECG) & Non-Invasive Tests

#### 1. Baseline 12-Lead Resting ECG

- Normal in >50% of patients between attacks.
- May show non-specific ST-T wave changes, evidence of prior MI (pathological Q waves), or Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH).
- ECG recorded _during an active attack_ demonstrates transient horizontal or downsloping **ST-segment depression ≥1 mm** or T-wave inversion, resolving with pain relief.

#### 2. Exercise Stress ECG / Treadmill Test (TMT) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **First-line diagnostic & risk-stratification investigation** using the standard **Bruce Protocol**.
- **Diagnostic Threshold:** Horizontal or downsloping **ST-segment depression ≥1 mm (or ≥2 mm for high risk)**measured 80 ms after the J-point.
- **High-Risk Features on TMT:** Ischaemia occurring early (Stage 1 or 2 of Bruce protocol), fall in BP during exercise, widespread/marked ST depression (>2 mm), or exercise-induced ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Contraindications:** Acute Coronary Syndrome, rest angina, severe calcific aortic stenosis, decompensated heart failure, severe hypertension.

#### 3. Stress Imaging Studies (Echo / Perfusion Scanning)

- **Stress Echocardiography (Dobutamine / Exercise):** Indicated in patients with mobility issues or baseline ECG abnormalities (LBBB, WPW syndrome, LVH). Identifies stress-induced regional wall motion abnormalities.
- **Myocardial Perfusion Scintigraphy (SPECT / PET with ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Tetrofosmin or Thallium):** Demonstrates **reversible perfusion defects** (uptake defect present during stress that normalizes at rest). (A persistent defect both at rest and stress indicates scar/prior MI).

#### 4. Computed Tomography Coronary Angiography (CTCA)

- High negative predictive value; recommended as initial diagnostic investigation in patients with low-to-intermediate likelihood of CAD or inconclusive exercise ECG.

#### 5. Invasive Coronary Angiography (CAG) [Gold Standard]

- Invasive diagnostic gold standard for visualizing epicardial lumen stenosis.
- Indicated in high-risk patients on stress testing, refractory symptoms despite medical therapy, or when revascularization (PCI / CABG) is considered.

### A. Non-Pharmacological & Lifestyle Measures

- **Smoking Cessation:** Mandatory stopping of all tobacco products.
- **Diet & Exercise:** Regular aerobic exercise up to, but not beyond, the point of chest discomfort (30 mins/day, 3–5 times/week); low-saturated-fat diet (<7% total calories); weight loss.
- **Avoidance of Triggers:** Avoid sudden unaccustomed exertion, heavy meals, and cold exposure.

---

### B. Disease-Modifying / Prognosis-Improving Drugs

#### 1. Aspirin (Antiplatelet Agent)

- **Name:** Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 680; Davidson p. 491). Irreversibly acetylates and inhibits Cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) enzyme in platelets, preventing Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) synthesis and inhibiting platelet aggregation.
- **Dose:** 75 mg to 150 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Reduces risk of MI and vascular death in all CAD patients; continued indefinitely.
- **Side Effects:** Dyspepsia, gastric mucosal erosion, GI bleeding, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in all diagnosed stable CAD cases indefinitely. Stop/switch to Clopidogrel (75 mg PO OD) if severe peptic ulceration or aspirin allergy occurs.

#### 2. Statins (HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors)

- **Name:** Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 682; Davidson p. 491). Competitively inhibits HMG-CoA reductase enzyme, blocking hepatic cholesterol synthesis and upregulating LDL receptors. Also stabilizes atheromatous plaques.
- **Dose:** Atorvastatin 20–80 mg PO OD; Rosuvastatin 10–40 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily at night (as hepatic cholesterol synthesis peaks at night).
- **Features:** Prescribed to all CAD patients regardless of baseline cholesterol level.
- **Side Effects:** Myalgia, raised liver transaminases (ALT/AST), rare rhabdomyolysis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately at diagnosis; maintain long-term.

---

### C. Symptom-Relieving Antianginal Drugs

#### 1. Organic Nitrates (Glyceryl Trinitrate - GTN as Prototype)

- **Name:** Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN), Isosorbide Dinitrate, Isosorbide-5-Mononitrate.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 585–590; Davidson p. 491). Enzymatically denitrated in vascular smooth muscle to release **Nitric Oxide (NO)**. NO activates cytosolic soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC) → increases cGMP → activates Protein Kinase G (PKG) → dephosphorylates Myosin Light Chain Kinase (MLCK) → causes smooth muscle relaxation.
    
    ```
    Organic Nitrate → NO → ↑ sGC → ↑ cGMP → PKG activation → MLCK Dephosphorylation → Vasodilation
    ```
    
    - _Venodilation (Preload Reduction):_ Dilates veins more than arteries → decreases venous return and LVEDP → abolishes subendocardial crunch.
    - _Arteriolar Dilatation:_ Reduces afterload.
    - _Coronary Dilatation:_ Dilates epicardial conducting vessels and prevents vasospasm.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Sublingual GTN:_ 0.5 mg tablet or 0.4 mg spray sublingually.
    - _Isosorbide Dinitrate:_ 5–10 mg sublingual; 10–20 mg PO BD/TDS.
    - _Isosorbide-5-Mononitrate:_ 10–40 mg PO BD (or 30–60 mg SR PO OD).
- **Frequency:** Sublingual as-needed ("as and when required") or prophylactic before exercise. Oral once or twice daily.
- **Features:** Sublingual GTN produces relief within 2–3 minutes; drug of choice to terminate acute anginal attacks.
- **Side Effects:** Throbbing headache, flushing, postural hypotension, syncope, reflex tachycardia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Sublingual used at symptom onset or prior to exertion. For oral long-acting nitrates, a **6 to 8-hour nitrate-free interval daily** (eccentric dosing: e.g., 8 a.m. and 2 p.m.) is mandatory to prevent pharmacological tolerance. **Contraindicated** if Systolic BP <90 mmHg or if Sildenafil (PDE-5 inhibitor) was taken within 24 hours.

#### 2. Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers)

- **Name:** Metoprolol, Atenolol, Bisoprolol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 162, 598; Davidson p. 491). Competitively antagonizes β₁-adrenoceptors in myocardium → decreases SA node automaticity, slows heart rate, decreases myocardial contractility, and lowers BP → markedly reduces myocardial O₂ demand. Prolongation of diastole improves subendocardial perfusion.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Metoprolol SR:_ 25–100 mg PO BD.
    - _Atenolol:_ 25–100 mg PO OD.
    - _Bisoprolol:_ 2.5–10 mg PO OD.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line antianginal drug; preferred over CCBs because it reduces re-infarction and mortality post-MI.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, fatigue, cold extremities, bronchospasm, depression.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line therapy in all stable angina patients. **Contraindicated** in severe bronchial asthma, severe bradycardia (HR <50 bpm), 2nd/3rd degree AV block, and vasospastic/Prinzmetal angina.

#### 3. Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)

- **Name:** Amlodipine (Dihydropyridine); Verapamil, Diltiazem (Non-Dihydropyridines).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 592–596; Davidson p. 492). Inhibits L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in vascular smooth muscle and myocardium.
    - _Amlodipine:_ Causes potent arteriolar vasodilation → reduces total peripheral resistance and afterload.
    - _Verapamil / Diltiazem:_ Depresses SA node, slows AV nodal conduction, and decreases contractility → reduces cardiac work.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Amlodipine:_ 2.5–10 mg PO OD.
    - _Diltiazem:_ 30–60 mg PO TDS (or 90–180 mg SR PO OD).
    - _Verapamil:_ 40–80 mg PO TDS.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (Amlodipine) or 3 times daily (Diltiazem/Verapamil).
- **Features:** Drug of choice in **Prinzmetal / Variant Angina** and when β-blockers are contraindicated (e.g., in COPD/asthma).
- **Side Effects:** Ankle edema, flushing, headache (amlodipine); constipation, bradycardia (verapamil).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Second-line or add-on therapy. **Do NOT combine Verapamil or Diltiazem with Beta-blockers** due to risk of severe bradycardia and AV block.

#### 4. Potassium Channel Opener

- **Name:** Nicorandil.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 598; Davidson p. 492). Dual mechanism: Opens ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels (K_ATP) in vascular smooth muscle (causing hyperpolarization and arterial dilatation) AND acts as an NO donor (venodilation). Reduces both preload and afterload. Simulates "ischaemic preconditioning" via mitochondrial K_ATP channels.
- **Dose & Route:** 10–20 mg PO BD.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily.
- **Features:** Does not exhibit the pharmacological tolerance seen with nitrates.
- **Side Effects:** Oral and gastrointestinal ulcerations, headache, flushing, dizziness.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Add-on drug in refractory stable angina.

#### 5. Sinus Node \(I_f\) Channel Inhibitor

- **Name:** Ivabradine.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 600; Davidson p. 492). Selectively inhibits the hyperpolarization-activated "funny" current (\(I_f\)) in sinoatrial nodal pacemaker cells. Slows Phase 4 diastolic depolarization, reducing heart rate without affecting cardiac contractility or BP.
- **Dose & Route:** 5 mg to 7.5 mg PO BD.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily.
- **Features:** Pure heart-rate lowering drug; safe in heart failure and asthma.
- **Side Effects:** Luminous visual phenomena / phosphenes (retinal \(I_f\) channel inhibition), bradycardia, headache.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in stable angina with sinus rhythm (HR >60–70 bpm) when β-blockers are contraindicated or ineffective.

#### 6. Metabolic Modulators & Late Na⁺ Current Inhibitors

- **Trimetazidine (20 mg PO TDS):** (KDT p. 599). Inhibits mitochondrial long-chain 3-ketoacyl-CoA thiolase (LC3-KAT), shifting myocardial substrate utilization from fatty acid oxidation to oxygen-efficient glucose oxidation. Side effects: gastric burning, reversible parkinsonism in elderly.
- **Ranolazine (500–1000 mg PO BD):** (KDT p. 600). Inhibits late inward Na⁺ current (late \(I_{\text{Na}}\)), reducing intracellular Ca²⁺ overload and myocardial wall tension. Prolongs QT interval.

---

### D. Revascularization Therapy

Indicated when medical therapy fails to control symptoms or in high-risk coronary anatomy:

1. **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** Balloon angioplasty with Drug-Eluting Stent (DES) deployment across focal stenoses. Provides excellent symptom control but does not improve survival in chronic stable angina.
2. **Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG):** Indicated for **Left Main Stem Disease** or **Three-Vessel CAD with impaired LV function** / diabetes mellitus, where it confers a proven survival benefit.

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS):** Transition due to plaque rupture into Unstable Angina, NSTEMI, or STEMI.
2. **Ischaemic Heart Failure / Dilated Cardiomyopathy:** Chronic subendocardial ischaemia leads to loss of myocytes, interstitial fibrosis, and impaired LV ejection fraction.
3. **Malignant Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Ventricular ectopic beats, Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), or Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) leading to sudden cardiac death.

---

## 10. Prognosis

Prognosis is dictated by the extent of coronary artery disease, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and effort tolerance on exercise stress testing:

- **Low Risk:** Annual mortality <1% (predictable angina at high workload, single-vessel disease, preserved LVEF).
- **High Risk:** Annual mortality >5% (ischaemia at low workload/Stage 1 Bruce protocol, Left Main Stem disease, three-vessel CAD, or LVEF <35%).

### Memory Palace: "The Narrowing Tunnel Journey"

Visualize walking through a narrow railway tunnel to recall stable angina:

1. **The Narrowed Brick Archway (Atherosclerosis >70%):** A heavy steam train slows down because the tunnel brickwork has narrowed by >70%.
2. **The Overheated Engine (Exertional Mismatch):** When the train speeds up uphill (**Exertion**), the engine overheats (**Ischaemia**) and releases a loud screeching whistle (**Chest Pain**).
3. **The Steam Relief Valve (Nitrates - GTN):** The engineer opens a red steam valve (**Sublingual GTN**), expanding the pipes instantly and cooling the engine within 3 minutes.
4. **The Speed Governor (Beta-Blockers):** A governor limits the train's maximum speed to 60 RPM (**Heart Rate reduction by Metoprolol**), preventing future overheating.
5. **The Maintenance Track Crew (Statin & Aspirin):** Track workers apply anti-rust oil (**Aspirin**) and smooth down the tunnel walls (**Atorvastatin**) to prevent permanent collapse.

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always counsel patients prescribed long-acting oral or transdermal nitrates to maintain a mandatory **6 to 8-hour nitrate-free interval every day** (best scheduled at night when inactive). Continuous exposure to organic nitrates causes rapid depletion of tissue sulfhydryl (-SH) groups, causing profound vascular tolerance and complete loss of antianginal efficacy.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing a Beta-blocker to a patient with chronic stable angina who also has co-existing severe **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) or Asthma**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Non-selective or even high-dose cardioselective β-blockers can trigger life-threatening bronchospasm in asthma/COPD patients. In stable angina with co-existing severe reactive airway disease, **β-blockers are contraindicated**; the preferred first-line antianginal drugs are **Cardioselective Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine)** or **Ivabradine**.

### Diagram 1 : Subendocardial 'Crunch' Mechanism During Angina

- **Type:** Anatomical Cross-Sectional Diagram.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Concentric myocardial wall layers of the Left Ventricle (Epicardium, Myocardium, Subendocardium).

    2. **Perforating Arterioles:** Branching inwards through the myocardium.
    3. **Subendocardial Zone:** Innermost layer adjacent to the LV cavity.
    4. **LVEDP Callout:** Normal 5 mmHg between attacks vs Elevated 25 mmHg during angina.
- **Arrows / Connections:** Compressive arrows directed from the high-pressure LV cavity into the subendocardium, demonstrating mechanical closure of perforating arterioles during diastole.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Subendocardial perfusion occurs exclusively during diastole; elevated LVEDP during ischaemia compresses perforating vessels, aggravating subendocardial hypoxia."_.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Exertional chest pain caused by fixed >70% coronary artery stenosis; relieved within 2–5 minutes by rest or sublingual GTN.
- **First-Line Diagnostic Test:** Exercise Stress ECG (TMT using Bruce Protocol) demonstrating horizontal/downsloping ST depression ≥1 mm.
- **Prognosis-Modifying Drugs:** Aspirin (75–150 mg PO OD) + Statin (Atorvastatin 20–80 mg PO OD).
- **Symptomatic Antianginal Core:** Sublingual GTN (for acute relief) + Beta-blocker (Metoprolol/Bisoprolol for chronic prophylaxis).
- **Nitrate Tolerance Rule:** Mandatory 6–8 hour nitrate-free gap daily to maintain therapeutic responsiveness.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Chronic stable angina is a predictable exertional pain syndrome caused by fixed atheromatous occlusion of epicardial coronary arteries, where myocardial oxygen demand exceeds fixed supply during effort, resulting in reversible subendocardial ischaemia that responds promptly to rest, sublingual nitrates, and beta-blockade.**
