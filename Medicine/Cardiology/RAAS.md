# Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)

## 1. Definition / Introduction [⭐ High-Yield]

The **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)** is a pivotal neurohumoral endocrine cascade that regulates systemic blood pressure, extracellular fluid (ECF) volume, sodium balance, and intrarenal hemodynamics.

It operates as a primary homeostatic feedback loop wherein renal hypoperfusion or sodium depletion triggers the enzymatic secretion of **renin** from the juxtaglomerular (JG) apparatus of the kidney. Renin initiates a proteolytic cascade resulting in the production of **Angiotensin II** (a potent systemic vasoconstrictor) and **Aldosterone** (which promotes renal sodium and water retention).

---

## 2. Components & Physiological Activation Triggers [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary Components of the System

1. **Renin:** An aspartyl protease enzyme synthesized, stored, and secreted by the granular cells of the **Juxtaglomerular (JG) apparatus** located in the tunica media of renal afferent arterioles. Plasma half-life = 15 minutes.
2. **Angiotensinogen:** A high-molecular-weight plasma α₂-globulin synthesized constitutively by the liver.
3. **Angiotensin I (Ang I):** An inactive decapeptide (1-10 amino acids) cleaved from angiotensinogen by renin.
4. **Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE / Kininase II):** A membrane-bound dipeptidyl carboxypeptidase located predominantly on the luminal surface of vascular endothelial cells in the pulmonary capillary bed. (Also degrades the vasodilator bradykinin).
5. **Angiotensin II (Ang II):** The principal biologically active octapeptide (1-8 amino acids) of the cascade. Plasma half-life = 1 minute.
6. **Aldosterone:** A steroid mineralocorticoid hormone synthesized and released by the **zona glomerulosa** of the adrenal cortex in response to Ang II.

### B. Physiological Triggers for Renin Secretion [⭐ High-Yield]

Renin release from JG cells is the rate-limiting step of RAAS activation and is stimulated by three primary pathways:

1. **Intrarenal Baroreceptor Pathway:** Decreased perfusion pressure (hypotension, hypovolemia, renal artery stenosis) lowers stretch/tension in the afferent arteriole, stimulating local prostaglandin (PGE₂) release and renin secretion.
2. **Macula Densa Pathway:** Reduced delivery or concentration of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in the distal convoluted tubule (sensed by the macula densa) upregulates COX-2 and neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS), releasing PGE₂ and PGI₂ to stimulate JG cell renin secretion.
3. **Sympathetic β₁-Adrenoceptor Pathway:** Increased renal sympathetic nerve activity (via baroreceptor reflexes or stress) directly activates β₁-adrenergic receptors on JG cells, elevating intracellular cAMP and triggering renin release.

## 4. Physiological Actions & Effects [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Direct Actions of Angiotensin II [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Systemic Vasoconstriction:** Directly constricts systemic arterioles (potency >40 times that of noradrenaline), elevating Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR) and Systolic/Diastolic Blood Pressure.
- **Renal Hemodynamics:** Selectively constricts the **efferent arteriole** of the glomerulus more than the afferent arteriole, maintaining intraglomerular filtration pressure and preserving GFR during states of low renal blood flow.
- **Renal Tubular Transport:** Directly stimulates the Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter (NHE-3) in the proximal convoluted tubule, increasing Na⁺, Cl⁻, and HCO₃⁻ reabsorption.
- **Central Nervous System:** Acts on the subfornical organ and organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT) to stimulate thirst and trigger Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin) release from the posterior pituitary.
- **Sympathetic Augmentation:** Facilitates noradrenaline release from peripheral sympathetic nerve terminals and adrenal medulla.

### B. Actions of Aldosterone

- Acts on **Principal (P) cells** of the late distal tubule and cortical collecting duct:
    - Binds intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors (MR) to induce synthesis of **Aldosterone-Induced Proteins (AIPs)**.
    - Upregulates and translocates apical **Epithelial Na⁺ Channels (ENaC)** and basolateral **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps**.
    - Increases Na⁺ and water reabsorption while enhancing K⁺ and H⁺ excretion into urine.

### Dosage Rule for Core RAAS Inhibitors

#### 1. Enalapril (ACE Inhibitor Prototype)

- **Name:** Enalapril.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 530–534; Davidson p. 385, 466). Prodrug converted in the liver to active enalaprilat. Inhibits ACE / Kininase II, blocking conversion of Ang I to Ang II. Decreases systemic vasoconstriction, reduces afterload/preload, and prevents aldosterone-mediated Na⁺ retention. Also prevents degradation of bradykinin.
- **Dose:**
    - _Hypertension:_ Initial 2.5–5 mg PO OD; maintenance 10–20 mg PO OD.
    - _Heart Failure:_ Initial 2.5 mg PO BD; target 10 mg PO BD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line drug in hypertension, HFrEF, diabetic nephropathy, and post-MI left ventricular remodeling.
- **Side Effects:** Dry cough (due to pulmonary bradykinin accumulation), first-dose hypotension, hyperkalemia, acute fall in GFR in bilateral renal artery stenosis, angioedema, foetopathy (contraindicated in pregnancy).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at low doses in non-hypovolemic patients. Check renal function and K⁺ within 7–10 days. **Contraindicated in pregnancy, baseline K⁺ >5.5 mmol/L, and bilateral renal artery stenosis**.

#### 2. Losartan (Angiotensin Receptor Blocker - ARB)

- **Name:** Losartan.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 535–537; Davidson p. 467, 533). Highly selective, competitive antagonist at the **AT₁ receptor** subtype. Blocks all actions of Ang II (vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, sympathetic drive) regardless of whether Ang II was generated via ACE or non-ACE (chymase) pathways. Does NOT inhibit ACE and does NOT alter bradykinin levels.
- **Dose:** 25–50 mg PO OD (up to 100 mg PO OD).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line alternative for patients intolerant to ACE inhibitors due to dry cough. Has mild uricosuric property.
- **Side Effects:** Hypotension, hyperkalemia, dizziness, foetopathy. (Dry cough and angioedema are extremely rare).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in hypertension, heart failure, and diabetic nephropathy. **Contraindicated in pregnancy and bilateral renal artery stenosis**.

#### 3. Spironolactone (Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist - MRA)

- **Name:** Spironolactone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 530, 567, 634; Davidson p. 351, 466). Synthetic steroid that competitively blocks intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors in the late distal tubule and cortical collecting duct principal cells. Inhibits synthesis of Aldosterone-Induced Proteins (AIPs), decreasing Na⁺ reabsorption and preventing K⁺ and H⁺ secretion. Also arrests aldosterone-driven myocardial and vascular fibrosis.
- **Dose:** 25–50 mg PO OD (up to 100–400 mg/day in severe ascites/cirrhosis).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Potassium-sparing diuretic; provides significant mortality reduction in moderate-to-severe heart failure (RALES trial).
- **Side Effects:** Hyperkalemia, gynecomastia and erectile dysfunction in men (due to anti-androgenic activity; can switch to Eplerenone), hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Add-on therapy in HFrEF, resistant hypertension, and cirrhotic ascites. **Contraindicated if baseline serum K⁺ >5.0 mmol/L or severe renal failure**.

#### 4. Aliskiren (Direct Renin Inhibitor - DRI)

- **Mechanism:** Binds directly to the catalytic site of renin, blocking cleavage of angiotensinogen to Ang I. Dose: 150–300 mg PO OD.

## 6. Clinical Significance & Pathological Role

1. **Renovascular Hypertension:** Renal artery stenosis (>70% occlusion) causes ischemic renal hypoperfusion, driving chronic hyperreninemia and Ang II-mediated severe secondary hypertension.
2. **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Depressed cardiac output triggers maladaptive chronic RAAS activation. Ang II and Aldosterone drive excessive sodium retention (edema) and accelerate pathological ventricular hypertrophy, myocyte apoptosis, and myocardial fibrosis.
3. **Diabetic & Chronic Kidney Disease:** Intraglomerular hypertension caused by Ang II-mediated efferent arteriolar constriction accelerates proteinuria and progressive glomerulosclerosis. ACEi/ARBs reduce intraglomerular pressure and provide profound renoprotection.
4. **Primary & Secondary Hyperaldosteronism:** Autonomous aldosterone secretion (Conn's syndrome) causes hypokalemic hypertension with suppressed plasma renin activity.

---

## 7. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always check serum creatinine and potassium levels **7 to 14 days after starting or titrating an ACE inhibitor or ARB**! A modest rise in serum creatinine up to **25–30% above baseline** is expected and represents a therapeutic reduction in intraglomerular pressure. Do not stop the drug unless the creatinine rise exceeds 30% or serum potassium rises above 5.5–6.0 mmol/L.

## 8. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing an ACE inhibitor (e.g., Enalapril) together with an ARB (e.g., Losartan) or Direct Renin Inhibitor (Aliskiren) for "dual RAAS blockade" in hypertension or diabetic nephropathy.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Large clinical endpoint trials (ONTARGET, ALTITUDE) demonstrated that combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB or Aliskiren does NOT provide additional cardiovascular or renal survival benefit, but significantly increases the risk of severe **hyperkalemia, acute kidney injury (AKI), and syncope**.
- **Resolution:** Dual RAAS blockade combination therapy is **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**.

## 10. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

### Mnemonic for RAAS Components & Inhibitors: "R-A-A-S"

- **R** — **R**enin released from JG cells (Inhibited by **Aliskiren**)
- **A** — **A**ngiotensin I converted to Ang II by ACE (Inhibited by **Captopril / Enalapril**)
- **A** — **A**ngiotensin II acting on AT₁ receptors (Blocked by **Losartan / Telmisartan**)
- **S** — **S**odium & water retention via Aldosterone (Blocked by **Spironolactone / Eplerenone**)

Visualize walking through a hydroelectric dam facility:

1. **Loci 1 (The Intake Sluice Gate - JG Apparatus):** A low water pressure alarm triggers a security guard (**Renin**) to unlock a heavy chain.
2. **Loci 2 (The Primary Pipeline - Angiotensinogen to Ang I):** Water flows through a thick pipe where a cutter enzyme (**Renin**) slices off a 10-link chain (**Ang I**).
3. **Loci 3 (The Lung Air Vent Filter - ACE Enzyme):** The chain passes through a high-altitude pulmonary ventilation fan (**ACE enzyme**) which trims 2 links to forge an 8-link super-wire (**Ang II**).
4. **Loci 4 (The Pressure Pump & Tower - Ang II Actions):** The super-wire squeezes systemic pipes shut (**Vasoconstriction**) and signals a chemical tower (**Adrenal Zona Glomerulosa**) to discharge yellow oil (**Aldosterone**), driving absorption pumps (**ENaC/Na⁺-K⁺ ATPase**) at the river bank.

## 11. Quick Revision

- **Rate-Limiting Step:** Renin release from renal JG cells.
- **Stimuli for Renin Secretion:** ↓ Afferent arteriole pressure, ↓ NaCl at macula densa, ↑ Sympathetic β₁ stimulation.
- **Angiotensin II Actions:** Potent systemic arteriolar vasoconstriction, efferent arteriolar constriction (maintains GFR), proximal tubule Na⁺ reabsorption, thirst/ADH release, aldosterone secretion.
- **Aldosterone Actions:** Binds mineralocorticoid receptor in cortical collecting duct principal cells → upregulates ENaC and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → ↑ Na⁺/H₂O reabsorption, ↑ K⁺/H⁺ excretion.
- **Core Drug Classes:** Direct Renin Inhibitors (Aliskiren), ACEi (Enalapril), ARBs (Losartan), MRAs (Spironolactone).
