# Mitral Stenosis

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Mitral Stenosis (MS)** is a chronic valvular heart disorder characterized by structural narrowing of the mitral valve orifice, resulting in mechanical obstruction to blood flow from the Left Atrium (LA) to the Left Ventricle (LV) during diastole.

The normal adult mitral valve orifice area is **4 to 6 cm²**. Hemodynamically significant obstruction occurs when the valve area is reduced to **<2 cm²**, and **severe mitral stenosis** is defined by a valve orifice area of **<1 cm²**.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary Etiology

- **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD):** Responsible for **>95% of all clinical cases** of mitral stenosis. It occurs as a late autoimmune delayed sequela of Group A Beta-Hemolytic Streptococcal (GABHS) pharyngitis, taking 10 to 20 years from the initial episode of acute rheumatic fever to manifest as symptomatic stenosis.

### B. Rare Non-Rheumatic Causes

- **Senile Degenerative / Calcific Mitral Stenosis:** Heavy calcification of the mitral valve annulus and leaflets in elderly individuals (>70 years).
- **Congenital Mitral Stenosis:** Parachute mitral valve (single papillary muscle) or congenital mitral ring.
- **Infiltrative / Autoimmune Disorders:** Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (Libman-Sacks endocarditis), Rheumatoid Arthritis, and Fabry Disease.
- **Storage Diseases & Carcinoid:** Mucopolysaccharidoses (Hurler/Hunter syndrome) and Carcinoid heart disease.
- **Functional / Pseudo-Mitral Stenosis:** Left Atrial Myxoma obstructing the valve orifice during diastole, or large left atrial ball-valve thrombus.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Rheumatic Commissural Fusion:** Recurrent streptococcal carditis induces progressive chronic inflammation, fibrotic thickening of leaflet margins, commissural fusion, and fusion/shortening of subvalvular chordae tendineae ("funnel-shaped" or "fish-mouth" valve).
2. **Left Atrial Pressure Elevation:** Narrowing of the orifice (<2 cm²) impedes LV diastolic filling, forcing the LA to generate a persistent pressure gradient across the valve. LA pressure rises from normal (<12 mmHg) to >25–30 mmHg.
3. **Left Atrial Remodeling & Stasis:** Sustained high LA pressure causes progressive left atrial hypertrophy, massive LA dilation, and blood stasis, predisposing to **Atrial Fibrillation (AF)** and mural thrombus formation in the left atrial appendage.
4. **Pulmonary Venous & Arterial Hypertension:** High LA pressure is transmitted backward into pulmonary veins, causing pulmonary venous congestion, interstitial edema, and reactive pulmonary arteriolar vasoconstriction (obliterative vascular remodeling). This leads to **Severe Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)**.
5. **Right Heart Failure:** Increased RV afterload from PAH induces Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH), dilation, functional Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR), elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), and systemic venous congestion.
6. **Effect of Tachycardia:** Any condition that shortens diastolic filling time (exercise, fever, pregnancy, or rapid AF) severely restricts blood passage across the stenotic valve, causing an acute surge in LA pressure and precipitating **Acute Pulmonary Edema**.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Exertional Dyspnea:** The dominant, earliest symptom. Gradually progressive over years, exacerbated by exertion, emotional stress, fever, or pregnancy.
- **Orthopnea & Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND):** Occurs as pulmonary venous congestion worsens.
- **Fatigue & Easy Fatigability:** Due to low fixed cardiac output unable to rise with exertion.
- **Hemoptysis:** Occurs in three patterns:
    1. _Pulmonary Apoplexy:_ Sudden profuse frank bleeding due to rupture of thin-walled dilated bronchial veins.
    2. _Pink Frothy Sputum:_ Associated with acute alveolar pulmonary edema.
    3. _Blood-Stained Sputum:_ Associated with paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea or chronic bronchitis.
- **Palpitations:** Secondary to paroxysmal or permanent Atrial Fibrillation or atrial ectopic beats.
- **Hoarseness of Voice (Ortner's Syndrome):** Compression of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve between the enlarged Left Atrial Appendage and the Pulmonary Artery.
- **Thromboembolic Symptoms:** Sudden focal neurological deficit (stroke), acute limb ischemia, or renal/visceral infarction.

### B. Physical Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. General & Peripheral Examination

- **Mitral Facies:** Characteristic plum-colored malar flush over cheeks with cyanotic lips, secondary to low cardiac output and cutaneous vasoconstriction.
- **Pulse:** Low-volume arterial pulse (due to reduced LV stroke volume). **Irregularly irregular pulse** if Atrial Fibrillation is present.
- **Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP):** Prominent **'a' wave** (due to forceful RA contraction against high RV pressure, lost in AF). Prominent **'v' wave** if functional Tricuspid Regurgitation develops.

#### 2. Precordial Examination

- **Palpation:**
    - _Apex Beat:_ Normal in location (LV is not enlarged), described as a **tapping apex beat** (palpable first heart sound).
    - _Diastolic Thrill:_ Palpable low-pitched thrill at the apex, best felt with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position during held expiration.
    - _Parasternal Heave:_ Left parasternal heave indicating Right Ventricular Hypertrophy.
    - _Palpable P2:_ Palpable pulmonary closure sound at the left 2nd intercostal space.
- **Auscultation (Classic Triad) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    1. **Loud First Heart Sound (S1):** Caused by forceful closure of flexible, non-calcified valve leaflets from high LA pressure. (S1 becomes soft/absent if the valve is heavily calcified).
    2. **Opening Snap (OS):** High-pitched, sharp, early diastolic sound following S2, caused by abrupt tensioning of the stenotic valve leaflets as they bulge into the LV.
        - _A2–OS Interval:_ Shorter A2–OS interval (<0.08 sec) indicates higher LA pressure and **more severe mitral stenosis**.
    3. **Mid-Diastolic Murmur (MDM):** Low-pitched, rough, rumbling diastolic murmur heard best at the apex using the bell of the stethoscope with the patient in the left lateral position during held expiration.
        - _Presystolic Accentuation:_ Increase in murmur intensity just before S1 due to active atrial contraction. **Presystolic accentuation is LOST in Atrial Fibrillation**.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **P Mitrale:** Broad, notched P wave in lead II (>0.12 sec) and biphasic P wave in V1 (negative terminal force), indicating Left Atrial Enlargement (seen if patient is in sinus rhythm).
- **Atrial Fibrillation:** Total absence of P waves with irregular QRS intervals and fibrillatory baseline waves.
- **Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (RVH):** Tall R waves in V1–V3 (R/S ratio >1 in V1), right axis deviation (>90°), and deep S waves in V6, secondary to severe pulmonary hypertension.

### B. Chest X-Ray (PA View)

- **Left Atrial Enlargement Signs:**
    - Straightening of the left cardiac border (due to prominent LA appendage).
    - **Double Density Sign:** Double right cardiac shadow formed by the dilated LA behind the Right Atrium.
    - Widening of the subcarinal angle (>90°) with elevation of the left mainstem bronchus.
- **Pulmonary Venous Congestion Signs:**
    - Cephalization / Antler sign (prominent upper lobe pulmonary veins).
    - **Kerley B Lines:** Short, horizontal, dense septal lines at the costophrenic angles due to subpleural lymphatic edema.
    - Hazy "bat-wing" perihilar shadowing (alveolar pulmonary edema).

### C. Echocardiography & Doppler (Gold Standard Investigation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):**
    - **Doming of Anterior Mitral Leaflet:** Classic "hockey-stick" appearance in diastole.
    - **Thickened, Calcified Leaflets:** Reduced E-F slope on M-mode representing restricted diastolic filling.
    - **Left Atrial Dilation:** Measures LA diameter (>40 mm) and identifies LA appendage thrombus (via Transesophageal Echo - TEE).
- **Doppler Echocardiography:** Calculates mean transvalvular pressure gradient via Bernoulli equation and determines **Mitral Valve Area (MVA)** using pressure half-time (PHT: MVA = 220 ÷ PHT).
- **Wilkins Echocardiographic Score:** Assesses leaflet mobility, thickening, calcification, and subvalvular thickening (score ≤8 predicts success for balloon valvuloplasty).

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Furosemide (Loop Diuretic)

- **Name:** Furosemide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 627, 630). Inhibits Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2) in the thick ascending limb of Henle's loop → promotes renal excretion of Na⁺ and water → decreases intravascular volume → lowers LA pressure and relieves pulmonary venous congestion.
- **Dose:** **20 to 40 mg PO OD/BD** (titrated up to 80–120 mg/day in severe congestion).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral or Intravenous (IV).
- **Features:** First-line drug to relieve dyspnea, orthopnea, and pulmonary edema.
- **Side Effects:** Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, volume depletion, prerenal azotemia, hyperuricemia, ototoxicity.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in patients with symptoms of pulmonary congestion; monitor serum potassium.

#### 2. Metoprolol Succinate (Beta-1 Selective Adrenoceptor Blocker)

- **Name:** Metoprolol Succinate.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 162, 591). Selective β₁-adrenoceptor antagonist. Slows SA node rate and AV nodal conduction → reduces heart rate → **prolongs diastolic filling duration** → lowers LA pressure and transvalvular gradient.
- **Dose:** **25 to 50 mg PO OD** (titrated up to 100 mg OD).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Features:** Essential for rate control in Atrial Fibrillation and sinus rhythm tachycardias.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, bronchospasm (in asthmatics).

#### 3. Digoxin (Cardiac Glycoside)

- **Name:** Digoxin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 556, 561). Direct vagomimetic action on AV node → increases AV nodal refractory period and decreases conduction velocity → controls rapid ventricular response in patients with **Mitral Stenosis with Atrial Fibrillation**.
- **Dose:** **0.125 to 0.25 mg PO OD** (5 days a week schedule).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Features:** Useful for ventricular rate control in AF, especially when combined with heart failure.
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, yellow vision (xanthopsia), PR prolongation, ventricular ectopics, digitalis toxicity (worsened by hypokalemia).

#### 4. Warfarin (Oral Anticoagulant)

- **Name:** Warfarin Sodium.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 670, 672). Inhibits Vitamin K Epoxide Reductase (VKORC1), blocking hepatic synthesis of functional clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X.
- **Dose:** **2 to 5 mg PO OD** (adjusted according to International Normalized Ratio - **INR target 2.0 to 3.0**).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Features:** **MANDATORY** in all patients with Mitral Stenosis who have:
    1. Atrial Fibrillation (paroxysmal or permanent).
    2. Prior history of systemic thromboembolism.
    3. Documented Left Atrial / LA Appendage thrombus.
- **Side Effects:** Bleeding, warfarin-induced skin necrosis.

#### 5. Benzathine Penicillin G (Secondary Rheumatic Fever Prophylaxis)

- **Dose:** **1.2 million units IM every 3 to 4 weeks** (administered up to 40 years of age or lifelong in severe valve disease).

### B. Interventional & Surgical Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Percutaneous Transvenous Mitral Commisurotomy (PTMC / Balloon Valvuloplasty)

- **Procedure:** Transvenous catheter passed via femoral vein → right atrium → transseptal puncture into LA → Inoue balloon inflated across the stenotic mitral valve to split fused commissures.
- **Indications:** Symptomatic moderate-to-severe MS (MVA ≤1.5 cm²) with:
    1. Pliable, non-calcified valve leaflets.
    2. **Wilkins Echocardiographic Score ≤8**.
    3. **Absence of Left Atrial Thrombus** on TEE.
    4. **Absence of Moderate/Severe Mitral Regurgitation**.

#### 2. Surgical Mitral Valve Replacement (MVR)

- **Procedure:** Excision of diseased native valve and surgical insertion of a mechanical (bileaflet tilting disc) or bioprosthetic tissue valve.
- **Indications:**
    1. Failed PTMC or severe restenosis.
    2. Heavy valve calcification and rigid subvalvular fusion (Wilkins Score >8 to 10).
    3. Coexisting moderate/severe Mitral Regurgitation.
    4. Persistent Left Atrial Appendage thrombus despite anticoagulation.

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) [⭐ High-Yield]:** The most common arrhythmia (~40–50% of patients). Driven by LA dilation and fibrosis; causes acute loss of "atrial kick" (reducing cardiac output by 20–30%) and rapid clinical deterioration.
2. **Systemic Thromboembolism & Ischemic Stroke [⭐ High-Yield]:** LA stasis and AF lead to mural thrombus formation. Embolization to cerebral arteries causes ischemic stroke in 20% of untreated patients.
3. **Severe Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) & Cor Pulmonale:** Chronic pulmonary venous hypertension leads to irreversible pulmonary vascular remodeling, right ventricular failure, hepatic congestion, ascites, and peripheral edema.
4. **Acute Pulmonary Edema:** Triggered by sudden surges in heart rate (fever, pregnancy, exercise, emotional stress, or rapid AF) that shorten diastolic filling time.
5. **Ortner's Syndrome:** Hoarseness of voice secondary to left recurrent laryngeal nerve compression.
6. **Infective Endocarditis:** Relatively rare in tight, rigid, calcified MS, but occurs in mild-to-moderate pliable MS.
7. **Recurrent Chest Infections:** Chronic pulmonary venous stasis predisposes to recurrent winter bronchitis and bronchopneumonia.

## 10. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In a patient with Mitral Stenosis, the severity of valve narrowing correlates directly with two key physical findings:
> 
> 1. **Length of the Mid-Diastolic Murmur:** The longer the diastolic murmur persists into late diastole, the higher the persistent LA-LV pressure gradient, indicating **more severe stenosis**.
> 2. **A2–Opening Snap (A2–OS) Interval:** Higher Left Atrial Pressure forces the stenotic valve open earlier in diastole, narrowing the A2–OS interval. An **A2–OS interval <0.08 seconds indicates SEVERE Mitral Stenosis**!

---

## 11. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Expecting to hear **Presystolic Accentuation** of the mid-diastolic murmur in a patient with Mitral Stenosis who has developed **Atrial Fibrillation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Presystolic accentuation is produced specifically by **active atrial contraction** pushing blood through the narrowed orifice at the end of diastole.
- **Resolution:** When Atrial Fibrillation develops, organized atrial contraction ceases completely. Therefore, **Presystolic Accentuation IS LOST in Atrial Fibrillation**, leaving only a mid-diastolic rumbling murmur.

## 12. Diagram Guide

### Diagram : Left Atrial and Left Ventricular Pressure Tracing in Mitral Stenosis

_(Reference in text: See Section 4 - Pathophysiology & Section 5 - Clinical Features)_

- **Type:** Diagnostic Hemodynamic Pressure Waveform Diagram.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** X-axis showing Cardiac Cycle (Systole vs Diastole) vs Y-axis showing Pressure in mmHg (0 to 100 mmHg).
- **Labels in Order:**
    1. **Left Ventricular Pressure Curve:** High systolic peak (~120 mmHg), dropping to near 0 mmHg in diastole.
    2. **Left Atrial Pressure Curve:** Elevated persistent LA pressure (20–30 mmHg) throughout diastole.
    3. **Shaded Area (Diastolic Pressure Gradient):** Area enclosed between LA and LV curves during diastole representing the **Transvalvular Gradient**.
    4. **Auscultation Timing Lines:**
        - **S1:** Loud closure at beginning of systole.
        - **A2/P2:** S2 closure at end of systole.
        - **OS:** Opening snap shortly after A2.
        - **MDM:** Mid-diastolic rumbling murmur filling the shaded gradient area.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"The shaded area between LA and LV pressure curves during diastole represents the diastolic pressure gradient; a narrower A2-OS interval indicates higher LA pressure and severe MS."_.

### Quick Revision

- **Primary Etiology:** Rheumatic Heart Disease (>95% of cases).
- **Diagnostic Area:** Normal MVA = 4–6 cm²; Severe MS = **<1.0 cm²**.
- **Classic Auscultation Triad:** Loud S1 + Opening Snap (OS) + Mid-diastolic rumbling murmur (MDM) at apex.
- **ECG Hallmark:** P mitrale (broad notched P wave in lead II) or Atrial Fibrillation.
- **Echocardiography Choice:** TTE showing "hockey-stick" anterior leaflet doming; MVA calculated via pressure half-time.
- **Treatment of Choice:** Percutaneous Transvenous Mitral Commisurotomy (PTMC) for pliable non-calcified valves (Wilkins score ≤8).
