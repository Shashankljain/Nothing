# Pacemaker in Heart Diseases

## Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **cardiac pacemaker** is an electronic medical device containing a battery, pulse generator, and leads that delivers artificial electrical impulses to the myocardium to initiate tissue depolarization and trigger cardiac contraction when the heart's intrinsic pacemaking or electrical conduction system fails.

---

## Key Clinical Features & Summary (3 Marks Calibration)

- **Types & System Configurations:**
    - **Temporary Pacemakers:** Transvenous electrode inserted via internal jugular or subclavian vein into the right ventricular apex (or transcutaneous gel pads) used for acute, reversible bradycardia (e.g., acute MI) or as a bridge to permanent pacing (maximum duration 7 days).
    - **Permanent Pacemakers:** Subcutaneously implanted in the pectoral region with leads placed transvenously into the right atrium, right ventricle, or coronary sinus.
- **Key Indications [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Sinoatrial Node Dysfunction:** Symptomatic sinus bradycardia, sinus arrest, SA block, or Tachy-Brady syndrome (Sick Sinus Syndrome).
    - **Atrioventricular (AV) Conduction Block:** Symptomatic or asymptomatic Mobitz Type II second-degree AV block, complete (third-degree) heart block, or advanced trifascicular block.
    - **Severe Heart Failure (CRT):** Biventricular pacing (Cardiac Resynchronisation Therapy) indicated in HFrEF (LVEF ≤35%) with Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) to resynchronize uncoordinated ventricular contraction.
    - **Refractory Atrial Fibrillation:** "Pace and ablate" strategy combining permanent AV node catheter ablation with pacemaker implantation.
- **Pacemaker Generic Code (NASPE/BPEG):**
    - Described by a 3 to 4-letter classification code: Position I = **Chamber Paced** (A/V/D), Position II = **Chamber Sensed** (A/V/D/O), Position III = **Response to Sensing** (I-Inhibited/T-Triggered/D-Dual), Position IV = **Rate Response** (R).
    - _Common Modes:_ **VVI** (ventricular demand), **DDD** (fully automatic dual-chamber tracking P waves to maintain AV synchrony), and **AAIR** (atrial rate-responsive for chronotropic incompetence).
- **Complications:**
    - **Early:** Pneumothorax, lead displacement, cardiac tamponade, local hematoma, and exit block (elevated pacing threshold due to endocardial tissue edema).
    - **Late:** Device/lead infection or endocarditis (commonly _Staphylococcus aureus_), lead fracture/fatigue, generator erosion, and **Pacemaker Syndrome** (dizziness and BP drop caused by loss of AV synchrony during single-chamber VVI pacing).

---

### Quick Revision

- **Function:** Electronic device delivering electrical stimuli to maintain cardiac rate and synchrony when intrinsic conduction fails.
- **Top Indications:** Sick Sinus Syndrome, Mobitz II / Complete AV Block, and CRT in HFrEF with LBBB.
- **Key Modes:** VVI (single chamber), DDD (dual chamber preserving AV synchrony), CRT (biventricular pacing).
- **Major Complication to Avoid:** Pacemaker Syndrome in single-chamber VVI pacing (prevented by dual-chamber DDD pacing).

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **A cardiac pacemaker is an implantable electronic pulse generator that delivers timed electrical stimuli to the myocardium, serving as a primary therapeutic intervention for symptomatic bradyarrhythmias, advanced AV conduction blocks, and intraventricular dyssynchrony in heart failure.**
