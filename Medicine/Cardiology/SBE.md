# Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE)** is a form of **Infective Endocarditis (IE)** characterized by an insidious, indolent microbial infection of previously damaged or abnormal cardiac valves or mural endocardium. It is produced by low-virulence microorganisms (most commonly _Viridans streptococci_), creating friable, bulky vegetations composed of thrombotic debris (fibrin and platelets) and bacterial colonies, pursuing a prolonged course over several weeks to months.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Causative Microorganisms [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Viridans Streptococci (50–60% of Subacute Cases):**
    - _Streptococcus mitis_, _Streptococcus sanguis_, _Streptococcus mutans_, and _Streptococcus salivarius_.
    - Commensals of the normal oral cavity flora; enter the bloodstream during tooth-brushing, chewing, or dental procedures to colonize abnormal valves.
2. **Enterococci (8–10% of Cases):**
    - _Enterococcus faecalis_ and _Enterococcus faecium_; enter the blood from the urinary or gastrointestinal tract, especially in elderly males.
3. **Streptococcus gallolyticus subsp. gallolyticus (formerly _Streptococcus bovis_):**
    - Normal intestinal commensal; **strongly associated with colonic carcinoma or adenomatous polyps**.
4. **HACEK Group (3–4% of Cases):**
    - Fastidious Gram-negative oral commensals: _**H**_ _aemophilus aphrophilus_ (now _Aggregatibacter aphrophilus_), _**A**_ _ggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans_, _**C**_ _ardiobacterium hominis_, _**E**_ _ikenella corrodens_, and _**K**_ _ingella kingae_.
5. **Culture-Negative SBE Causes (~5–10%):**
    - Fastidious pathogens including _Coxiella burnetii_ (Q fever - animal exposure), _Bartonella_ species (_B. quintana_, _B. henselae_), _Chlamydia_, or prior antibiotic exposure.

### B. Predisposing Cardiac Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

SBE almost exclusively develops on previously damaged, deformed, or diseased cardiac endothelium:

- **Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD):** Valvular scarring (most commonly affecting the mitral and aortic valves).
- **Congenital Heart Diseases:** Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD), Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA), Tetralogy of Fallot, or Congenital Bicuspid Aortic Valve.
- **Degenerative Valvular Diseases:** Calcific aortic stenosis, floppy/myxomatous Mitral Valve Prolapse (MVP) with regurgitation.
- **Prosthetic Valves & Cardiac Devices:** Bioprosthetic or mechanical heart valves.
- **High-Velocity Jet Damage:** High-pressure hemodynamic blood jets (e.g., VSD, MR, AR) cause mechanical endothelial erosion, predisposing to platelet-fibrin deposition. _(Low-pressure defects like large ASDs carry minimal SBE risk)_.

## 3. Classification (Acute vs Subacute Infective Endocarditis)

|Feature|Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis (SBE)|Acute Infective Endocarditis (AIE)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Microbial Virulence**|Low-virulence organisms (_Viridans streptococci_)|Highly virulent, invasive organisms (_Staphylococcus aureus_)|
|**Substrate Valve**|Previously abnormal / deformed / scarred valves|Normal OR abnormal valves|
|**Clinical Tempo**|Indolent, protracted course over weeks to months|Stormy, rapid, fulminant course over days|
|**Vegetation Character**|Smaller, firm, less destructive; granulation tissue at base|Large, florid, bulky, highly destructive with ring abscesses|
|**Peripheral Stigmata**|Common (Osler nodes, Roth spots, clubbing, splenomegaly)|Absent or rare; embolic events predominate|

## 4. Pathophysiology / Pathogenesis (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Endothelial Damage & Non-Bacterial Thrombotic Endocarditis (NBTE):**
    - Turbulent high-velocity blood flow or jet impacts disrupt the valvular endothelium.
    - Exposed subendothelial collagen triggers platelet adhesion and fibrin deposition, forming a sterile **Non-Bacterial Thrombotic Endocarditis (NBTE)** vegetation.
2. **Transient Bacteremia & Adhesion:**
    - Mucosal trauma (tooth-brushing, dental extraction, mastication) releases oral _Viridans streptococci_ into systemic circulation.
    - Circulating bacteria attach to the sticky platelet-fibrin matrix via dextrans and surface adhesins.
3. **Vegetation Growth & Protection:**
    - Bacteria proliferate rapidly within the matrix, stimulating further platelet aggregation and fibrin deposition.
    - Encasement within the avascular fibrin-platelet meshwork shields bacterial colonies from host neutrophils, macrophages, and circulating antibodies.
4. **Tissue Destruction & Extracardiac Sequelae:**
    - Bacterial enzymes cause gradual valve distortion, cusp perforation, or chordal disruption leading to valvular regurgitation.
    - Friable vegetation fragments detach, causing **systemic arterial embolization** (brain, spleen, kidneys).
    - Persistent low-grade antigenemia triggers **circulating immune-complex (Type III hypersensitivity) deposition** in small blood vessels and renal glomeruli.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Constitutional Symptoms:** Prolonged low-grade pyrexia (fever), rigors, night sweats, persistent fatigue, malaise, anorexia, and unexplained weight loss.
- **Cardiac Symptoms:** Progressive exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, and ankle edema secondary to worsening valvular regurgitation and heart failure.
- **Embolic Symptoms:** Sudden focal weakness / hemiplegia (embolic stroke), sharp left upper quadrant abdominal pain radiating to shoulder (splenic infarction), or flank pain with gross hematuria (renal infarction).

### B. Physical Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Cardiac Signs

- **Changing or New Regurgitant Murmur:** Present in >90% of cases (e.g., new blowing pan-systolic murmur of MR or early diastolic murmur of AR).
- **Signs of Heart Failure:** Tachycardia, elevated JVP, S₃ gallop, bi-basal lung crepitations.

#### 2. Peripheral Vascular & Embolic Stigmata

- **Janeway Lesions:** Small, flat, **non-tender**, erythematous or hemorrhagic macules on the palms and soles caused by septic microemboli.
- **Splinter Hemorrhages:** Linear, dark-red longitudinal subungual streaks beneath fingernails or toenails.
- **Petechiae:** Found on conjunctivae, buccal palatal mucosa, and lower extremities.
- **Systemic Emboli:** Loss of peripheral arterial pulses.

#### 3. Immunological Stigmata

- **Osler Nodes:** Small, **painful and tender**, raised erythematous subcutaneous nodules in the pulp of fingers and toes caused by immune-complex vasculitis.
- **Roth Spots:** Oval retinal hemorrhages with pale, white central centers visible on fundoscopic examination.
- **Glomerulonephritis:** Microscopic or gross hematuria.

#### 4. Chronic Illness Signs

- **Splenomegaly:** Palpable spleen present in 30–40% of long-standing cases.
- **Digital Clubbing:** Present in ~10% of cases with chronic disease.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations (Modified Duke Criteria) [⭐ High-Yield]

A **Definite Clinical Diagnosis** of SBE requires:

- **2 Major Criteria**, OR
- **1 Major + 3 Minor Criteria**, OR
- **5 Minor Criteria**.

#### 1. Major Criteria

1. **Positive Blood Culture for Infective Endocarditis:**
    - Typical microorganism (_Viridans streptococci_, _S. gallolyticus_, HACEK group, or _S. aureus_) isolated from 2 separate blood cultures.
    - Persistently positive blood culture: Organisms grown from blood cultures drawn >12 hours apart, or 3 out of 3 (or majority of ≥4) positive cultures drawn over >1 hour.
2. **Evidence of Endocardial Involvement:**
    - Echocardiogram positive for IE: Oscillating intracardiac mass/vegetation on valve or supporting structures, intracardiac abscess, or new partial dehiscence of prosthetic valve.
    - New valvular regurgitation (worsening of pre-existing murmur insufficient).

#### 2. Minor Criteria

1. **Predisposition:** Known predisposing valvular heart disease or IV drug use.
2. **Fever:** Temperature ≥38.0°C.
3. **Vascular Phenomena:** Major arterial emboli, septic pulmonary infarcts, mycotic aneurysm, intracranial hemorrhage, conjunctival hemorrhages, or Janeway lesions.
4. **Immunological Phenomena:** Glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots, or positive Rheumatoid Factor.
5. **Microbiological Evidence:** Positive blood culture not meeting major criteria or serological evidence of active infection.

---

### B. Laboratory & Diagnostic Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Blood Cultures (Pivotal Initial Test):**
    - Collect **3 to 6 sets of blood cultures** from peripheral venipuncture sites drawn at intervals of ≥6 hours prior to commencing antimicrobial therapy.
    - Aseptic technique is mandatory to avoid skin commensal contamination. Both aerobic and anaerobic bottles are required.
2. **Echocardiography:**
    - **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line test (sensitivity 50–90%).
    - **Transesophageal Echocardiography (TOE):** Gold standard (sensitivity >95%); indicated if TTE is negative/inconclusive, in prosthetic valves, or to detect aortic root ring abscesses.
3. **Hematology & Inflammatory Markers:**
    - Normocytic normochromic anemia of chronic disease.
    - Mild-to-moderate leukocytosis.
    - Markedly elevated C-Reactive Protein (CRP) and Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR).
4. **Urinalysis:** Non-visible microscopic hematuria (present in 60–70%) and proteinuria secondary to immune-complex glomerulonephritis or renal infarction.
5. **Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Development of new PR prolongation or **AV Block** indicates perivalvular extension (aortic root ring abscess).

## 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Management requires high-dose IV bactericidal antimicrobial therapy for **4 to 6 weeks**. Ideally, antimicrobial therapy in SBE is withheld until blood culture results and Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) sensitivities are available.

### A. Empirical Antibiotic Regimen (If Patient Is Unstable Before Culture Results)

- **Subacute Native Valve Endocarditis:**
    - **Amoxicillin** 2 g IV 6-hourly (every 4 hours) ± **Gentamicin** 1 mg/kg IV twice daily.
- **True Penicillin Allergy / MRSA Suspected:**
    - **Vancomycin** 1 g IV 12-hourly + **Gentamicin** 1 mg/kg IV 12-hourly.

---

### B. Targeted Antibiotic Guidelines & Dosage Rules [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Benzylpenicillin (Penicillin G)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 748; Davidson p. 528). β-lactam antibiotic that binds to Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs) on the bacterial cell wall, inhibiting transpeptidation and cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis, exerting a potent bactericidal effect.
    
    ```
    β-Lactam Ring → Binds PBPs → Inhibits Transpeptidation → Bacterial Cell Wall Lysis
    ```
    
- **Dose:** **1.2 g (2 million Units) IV 4-hourly** (6 times daily; total 7.2 g/day).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous.
- **Frequency:** 6 times daily (every 4 hours).
- **Features:** Gold-standard therapy for penicillin-sensitive _Viridans streptococci_ (MIC ≤0.125 mg/L) for 4 weeks (native valve) or 6 weeks (prosthetic valve).
- **Side Effects:** Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, skin rash, Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction, seizures at high doses in renal failure, hyperkalemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiated after blood culture completion. Stop if severe Type I immediate anaphylactic reaction occurs.

#### 2. Gentamicin

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 797, 798; Davidson p. 528). Aminoglycoside antibiotic that binds irreversibly to the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit, inducing mRNA misreading and inhibiting protein synthesis. Exhibits synergistic bactericidal action when combined with cell wall-active agents (penicillins/vancomycin) in endocarditis.
- **Dose:** **1 mg/kg IV 12-hourly** (or 8-hourly in older protocols).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous slow infusion.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (every 12 hours).
- **Features:** Essential synergistic agent in streptococcal and enterococcal SBE.
- **Side Effects:** Nephrotoxicity (acute tubular necrosis) and Ototoxicity (vestibular and auditory impairment).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Monitor trough serum levels (target pre-dose ≤1 mg/L, post-dose 3–5 mg/L). Stop or decrease dose if trough level >1 mg/L or serum creatinine rises.

#### 3. Short-Course 2-Week Regimen Conditions (Fully Sensitive Streptococci) [⭐ High-Yield]

A abbreviated 2-week course of **Benzylpenicillin 1.2 g IV 4-hourly + Gentamicin 1 mg/kg IV 12-hourly** is acceptable ONLY if all criteria are met:

1. Native valve infection.
2. Fully sensitive strain (Penicillin MIC ≤0.125 mg/L).
3. No adverse prognostic factors (no heart failure, severe AR, or AV block).
4. No evidence of thromboembolic disease.
5. No vegetations >5 mm in diameter.
6. Rapid clinical response within 7 days.

---

### C. Indications for Cardiac Surgery in SBE [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Refractory Heart Failure** secondary to acute valve destruction or cusp perforation.
2. **Failure of Antibiotic Therapy** (persistent bacteremia or uncontrolled infection).
3. **Large Vegetations (>10 mm diameter)** on left-sided valves with high embolic risk.
4. **Perivalvular Abscess / Conduction Defect:** Aortic root abscess, fistula, or new AV block.

---

## 9. Prevention / Antibiotic Prophylaxis Before Dental Procedures [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Rationale & High-Risk Indications

Dental procedures involving manipulation of gingival tissue, periapical tooth region, or perforation of oral mucosa induce transient _Viridans streptococci_ bacteremia. Prophylaxis is reserved **strictly for high-risk patients**:

1. Patients with a **prosthetic heart valve** or prosthetic material used for valve repair.
2. Patients with a **previous documented history of infective endocarditis**.
3. Patients with **unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease** or repaired CHD with residual shunts/defects.

---

### B. Prophylactic Regimens Before Dental Procedures

All prophylactic medications are given as a **single dose 30 to 60 minutes BEFORE** the procedure:

## 10. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Cardiac Complications:** Congestive heart failure (40–50%), severe valvular regurgitation, aortic root ring abscess, complete heart block (AV block), and myocarditis.
2. **Embolic Complications:** Ischemic embolic stroke (15%), cerebral mycotic aneurysms, splenic infarction, renal infarction, and mesenteric ischemia.
3. **Renal Complications:** Immune-complex glomerulonephritis, focal renal infarction, and acute tubular necrosis (secondary to aminoglycoside/vancomycin nephrotoxicity).
4. **Neurological Complications:** Brain abscess, purulent bacterial meningitis, and mycotic aneurysm rupture causing subarachnoid hemorrhage.

## 12. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In any patient presenting with SBE due to **_Streptococcus gallolyticus subsp. gallolyticus_(formerly _Streptococcus bovis_)**, ALWAYS schedule a **colonoscopy**! There is an extremely strong clinical association between _S. gallolyticus_ endocarditis and occult gastrointestinal malignancies, specifically colonic adenocarcinoma or adenomatous polyps.

---

## 13. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Confusing **Janeway Lesions** and **Osler Nodes**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:**
    - **Janeway Lesions:** **NON-TENDER**, flat, macular erythematous lesions on palms/soles caused by **septic microemboli**.
    - **Osler Nodes:** **PAINFUL & TENDER**, raised subcutaneous nodular lesions on finger/toe pulps caused by **immune-complex vasculitis**.

### Quick Revision

- **Primary Cause:** Low-virulence _Viridans streptococci_ infecting previously damaged heart valves (RHD, MVP, VSD).
- **Diagnosis:** Modified Duke Criteria (2 Major OR 1 Major + 3 Minor OR 5 Minor).
- **Key Stigmata:** Janeway lesions (non-tender, palms/soles), Osler nodes (tender, digit pulps), Roth spots (retinal hemorrhage), Splinter hemorrhages.
- **Targeted Medical Management:** High-dose IV Benzylpenicillin (1.2 g 4-hourly) + IV Gentamicin (1 mg/kg 12-hourly) for 4–6 weeks.
- **Dental Prophylaxis:** Single oral dose of **Amoxicillin 2.0 g** 30–60 mins before gingival manipulation in high-risk patients.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Subacute bacterial endocarditis is an indolent, low-virulence streptococcal infection of pre-damaged cardiac valves where bacteria colonize platelet-fibrin vegetations, driving progressive valvular regurgitation, systemic thromboembolism, and Type III immune-complex vasculitis.**
