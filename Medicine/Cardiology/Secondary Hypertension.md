# Secondary Hypertension

## 1. Definition & Introduction [⭐ High-Yield]

**Secondary Hypertension** is defined as an elevation in systemic blood pressure (systolic BP ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥90 mmHg) that occurs as a direct physiological consequence of an underlying, identifiable renal, endocrine, vascular, or pharmacological abnormality.

It accounts for approximately **5% to 10% of all hypertensive cases** in the general adult population (with primary/essential hypertension comprising the remaining 90–95%). Identifying secondary hypertension is clinically crucial because, unlike essential hypertension, treating or surgically correcting the root cause can often cure the hypertension or dramatically improve blood pressure control.

---

### A. Renal Causes (Most Common Category ~80%) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Renal Parenchymal Diseases:**
    - Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) of any etiology.
    - Acute and Chronic Glomerulonephritis.
    - Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD).
    - Diabetic Nephropathy.
2. **Renal Vascular Diseases (Renovascular Hypertension):**
    - **Renal Artery Stenosis (RAS):** Narrowing >70% of the renal artery lumen.
        - _Atherosclerosis (~80%):_ Involves the proximal 1–2 cm of the main renal artery; typically seen in older males with generalized atheroma.
        - _Fibromuscular Dysplasia (~15–20%):_ Involves the mid-to-distal renal artery; characteristically affects young females (<40 years).
        - _Other Rare Vascular Causes:_ Takayasu arteritis (common in Asian females), Polyarteritis nodosa, renal artery aneurysm.

### B. Endocrine Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's Syndrome):** Autonomous aldosterone overproduction due to an aldosterone-producing adrenal adenoma (30%) or bilateral adrenal micro/macronodular hyperplasia (60%). Accounts for up to 10% of all hypertensive patients.
2. **Cushing's Syndrome / Hypercortisolism:** Excess glucocorticoids (iatrogenic steroid use, ACTH-secreting pituitary microadenoma, or adrenal adenoma/carcinoma).
3. **Pheochromocytoma / Paraganglioma:** Catecholamine-secreting neuroendocrine tumors of adrenal medullary chromaffin cells (80%) or extra-adrenal sympathetic ganglia (20%).
4. **Thyroid Dysfunction:**
    - _Primary Hypothyroidism:_ Increases total peripheral resistance, causing predominantly **diastolic hypertension**.
    - _Thyrotoxicosis / Hyperthyroidism:_ Increases cardiac output and stroke volume, causing isolated **systolic hypertension**.
5. **Acromegaly:** Excess Growth Hormone (GH) and IGF-1 causing sodium retention and vascular hypertrophy.
6. **Primary Hyperparathyroidism:** Hypercalcemia-induced direct vascular smooth muscle constriction.
7. **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH):** 11β-hydroxylase or 17α-hydroxylase enzyme deficiency causing accumulation of mineralocorticoid precursors (11-deoxycorticosterone).

### C. Cardiovascular & Vascular Causes

- **Coarctation of the Aorta:** Congenital narrowing of the aortic lumen distal to the origin of the left subclavian artery.
- **Rigidity / Reduced Compliance of Aorta:** In elderly individuals with severe calcific arteriosclerosis.

### D. Drug-Induced & Chemical Causes

- **Oral Contraceptive Pills (OCPs):** Estrogen increases hepatic synthesis of angiotensinogen.
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs):** Inhibit renal vasodilatory prostaglandins (PGE₂, PGI₂), causing sodium and water retention.
- **Systemic Glucocorticoids & Mineralocorticoids (Fludrocortisone).**
- **Sympathomimetic Agents:** Nasal decongestants (pseudoephedrine), amphetamines, and cocaine.
- **Calcineurin Inhibitors:** Cyclosporine and Tacrolimus (induce intrarenal vasoconstriction).
- **Excessive Liquorice / Carbenoxolone Ingestion:** Glycyrrhizic acid inhibits 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11β-HSD2), allowing endogenous cortisol to overactivate renal mineralocorticoid receptors (**Apparent Mineralocorticoid Excess**).

### E. Miscellaneous Causes

- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** Recurrent nocturnal hypoxia stimulates chronic sympathetic outflow.
- **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension / Pre-eclampsia.**

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Mechanism (Renovascular Hypertension - Goldblatt Model)

1. **Renal Hypoperfusion:** Stenosis >70% of the renal artery reduces renal blood flow and perfusion pressure at the afferent arteriole of the juxtaglomerular (JG) apparatus.
2. **Renin Secretion:** Granular cells of the JG apparatus release excess **renin**, which cleaves angiotensinogen to Angiotensin I.
3. **Angiotensin II Elevation:** Pulmonary ACE converts Ang I to **Angiotensin II**, causing potent systemic arteriolar vasoconstriction (raising Total Peripheral Resistance).
4. **Aldosterone Secretion:** Angiotensin II stimulates the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex to release **Aldosterone**, which upregulates ENaC channels in the distal nephron, driving avid Na⁺ and water retention and expanding extracellular fluid volume.

## 4. Clinical Features & Diagnostic Clues [⭐ High-Yield]

Clinical suspicion for secondary hypertension should be triggered by specific red-flag features:

### High-Yield Clinical Red Flags

- **Age of Onset:** Onset of severe hypertension at a very young age (**<30 years**) without family history, or sudden onset after **>50 years**.
- **Resistant / Refractory Hypertension:** Blood pressure remaining uncontrolled (≥140/90 mmHg) despite full compliance with optimal doses of **3 antihypertensive drugs from different classes**, one of which is a diuretic.
- **Accelerated / Malignant Hypertension:** Rapid rise in BP accompanied by Grade 3 or 4 hypertensive retinopathy (papilledema, cotton-wool spots) or acute target organ injury.
- **Unexplained Hypokalemia:** Spontaneous or diuretic-induced hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis (strongly points to Primary Hyperaldosteronism).
- **Deterioration of Renal Function on ACEi/ARBs:** An unexpected rise in serum creatinine >25–30% or drop in eGFR after starting an ACE inhibitor or ARB (strongly indicates **Bilateral Renal Artery Stenosis**).
- **Flash Pulmonary Edema:** Recurrent, unprovoked episodes of sudden nocturnal pulmonary edema in the presence of preserved LV systolic function (typical of Bilateral Renovascular Disease).

### Etiology-Specific Physical Findings

- **Coarctation of Aorta:** Radio-femoral delay (delayed and weak femoral pulse relative to radial pulse), lower BP in legs compared to arms, systolic murmur over interscapular back region.
- **Polycystic Kidney Disease:** Bilateral palpable, nodular, enlarged abdominal flank masses.
- **Renal Artery Stenosis:** Systolic-diastolic epigastric or flank abdominal bruits.
- **Cushing's Syndrome:** Moon facies, buffalo hump, centripetal obesity, purple abdominal striae, proximal myopathy, easy bruising.
- **Pheochromocytoma:** Classic paroxysmal triad: **Episodic throbbing headache, palpitations/tachycardia, and profuse sweating** (_perspiration_), associated with marked postural hypotension.

### A. Basic Baseline Screening Investigations (All Hypertensives)

1. **Urinalysis:** Dipstick for blood, protein, and glucose; urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) to screen for renal parenchymal injury.
2. **Renal Function Tests:** Serum urea, creatinine, and eGFR.
3. **Serum Electrolytes:** Serum Na⁺, K⁺ (hypokalemia suggests Conn's), and serum Ca²⁺ (hypercalcemia in hyperparathyroidism).
4. **Metabolic Panel:** Fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, and serum lipid profile (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides).
5. **12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Assesses left ventricular hypertrophy (Sokolow-Lyon voltage criteria) and ischemic heart disease.

---

### B. Specialized Etiology-Specific Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Renal Artery Stenosis (RAS)

- **Screening Test:** **Renal Duplex Doppler Ultrasound** (reveals asymmetric kidney size and high peak systolic velocity in renal arteries).
- **Investigation of Choice (Gold Standard Imaging):** **CT Angiography (CTA)** or **3D Contrast-Enhanced MR Angiography (MRA)** of renal arteries.
- **Functional Test:** Captopril-enhanced renography or Captopril test (shows acute rise in Plasma Renin Activity).

#### 2. Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's Syndrome)

- **Screening Test [⭐ High-Yield]:** Early morning **Plasma Aldosterone Concentration (PAC) to Plasma Renin Activity (PRA) Ratio (ARR)**.
    - _Positive Screen:_ PAC ≥10 ng/dL with suppressed PRA ≤1 ng/mL/hr (ARR >20–30).
    - _Note:_ All antihypertensives that interfere with RAS (β-blockers, ACEi, ARBs, diuretics) must ideally be stopped for 4 weeks prior to testing.
- **Confirmatory Test:** **Saline Infusion Test** (intravenous 2 liters 0.9% saline over 4 hours fails to suppress plasma aldosterone <10 ng/dL) or oral sodium loading test.
- **Localization Imaging:** High-resolution **CT Abdomen** (adrenal protocol) to differentiate single adrenal adenoma from bilateral hyperplasia.

#### 3. Cushing's Syndrome

- **Screening Tests (Require 2 positive results):**
    1. **Overnight 1 mg Dexamethasone Suppression Test (ONDST):** Dexamethasone 1 mg given at 11 PM; 8 AM plasma cortisol >1.8 μg/dL (50 nmol/L) indicates loss of normal feedback inhibition.
    2. **24-Hour Urinary Free Cortisol (UFC):** Elevated >3 times upper limit of normal.
    3. **Late-Night Salivary Cortisol:** Elevated (loss of diurnal rhythm).
- **Etiological Workup:** Plasma ACTH measurement:
    - _Low ACTH (<10 pg/mL):_ ACTH-independent → CT scan of Adrenal glands.
    - _High ACTH (>20 pg/mL):_ ACTH-dependent → MRI Pituitary (gadolinium-enhanced) or CT Thorax/Abdomen for ectopic ACTH.

#### 4. Pheochromocytoma

- **Screening Test of Choice:** Measurement of **Free Plasma Metanephrines** (metanephrine and normetanephrine) or **24-Hour Urinary Fractionated Metanephrines**. High sensitivity (>97%).
- **Tumor Marker:** Serum Chromogranin A.
- **Localization Imaging:** **CT or MRI Abdomen and Pelvis** (adrenal medulla).
- **Nuclear Imaging:** **¹²³I-MIBG (Metaiodobenzylguanidine) Scintigraphy** or ¹⁸F-FDG PET scan for extra-adrenal or metastatic paragangliomas.

#### 5. Coarctation of the Aorta

- **Chest X-Ray:**
    - **Rib Notching:** Erosion of the inferior borders of the 3rd to 8th ribs by dilated, tortuous intercostal collateral arteries.
    - **"3" Sign:** Indentation of the aortic wall at the coarctation site with pre- and post-stenotic dilation on barium swallow or PA view.
- **Echocardiography & Cardiac MRI / CT Aortogram:** Confirms site, length, and pressure gradient across the coarctation.

## 7. Overview of Management Principles

- **Targeted Etiological Treatment:**
    - _Renal Artery Stenosis:_ Antihypertensives (ACEi/ARBs in unilateral; CCBs/Diuretics in bilateral) + Statins/Aspirin; Percutaneous Transluminal Renal Angioplasty (PTRA) with stenting for refractory cases.
    - _Conn's Adenoma:_ Laparoscopic unilateral adrenalectomy. (Bilateral hyperplasia: Spironolactone 25–100 mg OD or Eplerenone).
    - _Cushing's Disease:_ Trans-sphenoidal pituitary microadenomectomy.
    - _Pheochromocytoma:_ Pre-operative **α-blockade (Phenoxybenzamine / Prazosin)** for 10–14 days _before_initiating β-blockers, followed by surgical tumor excision.
    - _Coarctation of Aorta:_ Surgical repair or balloon angioplasty with stenting.

---

## 8. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always check for **Radio-Femoral Delay** and measure blood pressure in **both arms and at least one leg** in any young patient (<30 years) presenting with severe hypertension! A delay or significant pulse amplitude drop in the femoral artery relative to the radial artery, combined with lower blood pressure in the lower extremities, is pathognomonic for **Coarctation of the Aorta**.

---

## 9. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Performing the **Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio (ARR)** screening test for primary hyperaldosteronism while the patient is taking **Beta-Blockers, ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, or Spironolactone**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:**
    - Beta-blockers suppress renin, causing **false-positive ARR**.
    - ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and diuretics stimulate renin, causing **false-negative ARR**.
- **Resolution:** Antihypertensive drugs affecting the RAS must be **discontinued for at least 4 weeks** prior to drawing ARR. During this wash-out period, control blood pressure using drugs with minimal RAS impact, such as **α-blockers (Prazosin / Doxazosin)** and **Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine)**.

## 11. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

### Mnemonic for Secondary Hypertension Causes: "R-E-N-A-L"

- **R** — **R**enal (CKD, Glomerulonephritis, Renal Artery Stenosis)
- **E** — **E**ndocrine (Conn's, Cushing's, Pheochromocytoma, Acromegaly, Thyroid)
- **N** — **N**eoplasms / Neurogenic (Pheochromocytoma, OSA, CNS lesions)
- **A** — **A**ortic Coarctation
- **L** — **L**iquorice & Drugs (OCPs, NSAIDs, Steroids, Cocaine, Cyclosporine)

### Memory Palace: "The Renal Pressure Control Tower"

Visualize walking through 4 stations in a high-pressure municipal pumping station:

1. **Station 1 (The Clogged Water Pipe - Renal Artery Stenosis):** A narrow pipe choked with rust (**Proximal 1-2 cm Atherosclerosis**) causing a pressure sensor to dump red dyes (**Renin release**).
2. **Station 2 (The Yellow Oil Tank - Conn's Adenoma):** A bright canary-yellow storage tank (**Aldosterone-producing adenoma**) leaking salt into the river (**Na⁺ retention and hypokalemia**).
3. **Station 3 (The Sweating Pressure Valve - Pheochromocytoma):** A valve overheating and blowing off bursts of hot steam (**Paroxysmal headache, sweating, and palpitations**).
4. **Station 4 (The Kinked Main Conduit - Coarctation):** A major steel pipe kinked in the middle (**Coarctation distal to subclavian**), creating high pressure upstream in upper pipes and low flow in lower outlets.

## 12. Quick Revision

- **Prevalence:** Accounts for 5–10% of hypertension cases.
- **Most Common Cause Overall:** Renal Parenchymal Disease (~80% of secondary cases).
- **Most Common Curable Vascular Cause:** Renal Artery Stenosis (Atherosclerosis in older males; Fibromuscular dysplasia in young females).
- **Most Common Endocrine Cause:** Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's syndrome; ARR screening test).
- **High-Yield Red Flags:** Age <30 or >50 years, resistant HTN (≥3 drugs), unexplained hypokalemia, >30% creatinine rise on ACEi, flash pulmonary edema.
- **Pheochromocytoma Triad:** Paroxysmal headache, palpitations, profuse sweating.

## 13. One-Line Exam Opener

> **Secondary hypertension refers to blood pressure elevation resulting from a identifiable underlying renal, endocrine, vascular, or drug-induced pathology, where early clinical suspicion and etiology-specific investigations allow targeted medical or surgical intervention.**
