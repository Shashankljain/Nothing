# Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)** is a complex clinical syndrome resulting from any structural or functional cardiac disorder that impairs the ability of the ventricle to fill with or eject blood. It occurs when the cardiac output is insufficient to meet the metabolic demands of peripheral tissues at normal filling pressures, or can do so only at the expense of elevated ventricular filling pressures.

- **Systolic Heart Failure (HFrEF):** Deficient pumping action of the ventricles characterized by reduced myocardial contractility and ejection fraction (LVEF <40%).
- **Diastolic Heart Failure (HFpEF):** Impaired ventricular relaxation and increased wall stiffness during diastole with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF ≥50%).

---

## 2. Precipitating Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

In a patient with pre-existing or compensated heart disease, acute decompensation into overt CHF is commonly triggered by identifiable precipitating factors:

1. **Myocardial Ischaemia / Infarction:** Sudden loss of functioning myocardium.
2. **Intercurrent Infections:** Severe respiratory tract infections (pneumonia) increasing metabolic demand.
3. **Arrhythmias:** Tachyarrhythmias like Atrial Fibrillation (AF) reduce diastolic filling time and loss of atrial kick.
4. **Inappropriate Medication Cessation:** Non-compliance or abrupt stopping of diuretics or ACE inhibitors.
5. **Fluid Overload / Dietary Indiscretion:** Excessive dietary sodium intake or intravenous fluid overload.
6. **Cardiotoxic / Fluid-Retaining Drugs:** Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs), glucocorticoids, or negative inotropic agents (e.g., non-dihydropyridine CCBs, unmonitored β-blockers).
7. **High Cardiac Output States:** Severe anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, pregnancy, or systemic arteriovenous shunts.
8. **Pulmonary Embolism:** Increases right ventricular afterload abruptly.

## 3. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Primary Index Event:** Myocardial injury (MI, hypertension, valvular disease) depresses cardiac contractility or impairs diastolic relaxation.
2. **Haemodynamic Fall & Neurohumoral Activation:** Reduced cardiac output triggers immediate compensatory neurohumoral reflexes:
    - **Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) Stimulation:** Releases noradrenaline to increase heart rate and myocardial contractility.
    - **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Activation:** Angiotensin II causes systemic arteriolar vasoconstriction (increasing afterload), while Aldosterone promotes renal sodium and water retention (increasing preload).
    - **Arginine Vasopressin (ADH) & Endothelin-1 Release:** Further promotes fluid retention and potent vasoconstriction.
3. **Vicious Cycle & Ventricular Remodelling:** While initially maintaining arterial blood pressure, prolonged neurohumoral activation increases wall stress, promotes cardiomyocyte apoptosis, interstitial fibrosis, and progressive ventricular dilation (maladaptive remodelling).
4. **Natriuretic Peptides (ANP & BNP):** Released from stretched atrial and ventricular myocytes to induce counter-regulatory vasodilation and natriuresis, but this defense is eventually overwhelmed in chronic CHF.

## 4. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Left-Sided Heart Failure (Pulmonary Congestion & Low Output)

- **Symptoms:**
    - **Exertional Dyspnoea:** Earliest symptom caused by elevated pulmonary capillary hydraulic pressure.
    - **Orthopnoea:** Dyspnoea when supine due to redistribution of fluid from lower extremities into central circulation.
    - **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnoea (PND):** Severe breathlessness awakening the patient from sleep.
    - **Cough:** Cough with profuse, frothy, pink or blood-streaked sputum.
    - **Low Output Symptoms:** Fatigue, listlessness, cold peripheries, and confusion/irritability due to cerebral hypoperfusion.
- **Signs:**
    - Tachypnoea, cool clammy peripheries.
    - Displaced apex beat (indicating cardiomegaly).
    - **Third Heart Sound (S₃ Gallop):** Caused by rapid ventricular filling during early diastole into a dilated ventricle.
    - Bilateral fine inspiratory crepitations (crackles) at lung bases.

### B. Right-Sided Heart Failure (Systemic Venous Congestion)

- **Symptoms:** Ankle swelling, abdominal distension, right upper quadrant discomfort (hepatic congestion), anorexia, and nausea.
- **Signs:**
    - **Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP):** Prominent 'a' or 'v' waves.
    - **Dependent Pitting Oedema:** Ankle oedema in ambulant patients; sacral/thigh oedema in bed-bound patients.
    - **Tender Hepatomegaly:** Smooth, enlarged liver with positive hepatojugular reflux.
    - **Ascites & Pleural Effusions:** Due to severe venous hypertension.

### A. General & Non-Pharmacological Measures

- **Patient Education:** Self-monitoring of daily weight to detect fluid retention early.
- **Dietary Sodium Restriction:** Limit salt intake (<2–3 g/day) and avoid added salt.
- **Lifestyle:** Smoking cessation, abstinence from alcohol (mandatory in alcoholic cardiomyopathy), and light aerobic exercise within limits.
- **Vaccination:** Annual influenza and pneumococcal vaccines.

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Rules [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors

- **Name:** Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 532–534; Davidson p. 466). Inhibits ACE enzyme → blocks conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II. Suppresses vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, and myocyte apoptosis/fibrosis. Elevates cardioprotective bradykinin and Nitric Oxide (NO) levels.
    
    ```
    Angiotensin I → [ACE Inhibition] ❌ → Angiotensin II ↓ → Vasodilation + Aldosterone ↓ → Remodelling Arrested
    ```
    
- **Receptors / Target:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE).
- **Dose:**
    - _Enalapril:_ Starting dose 2.5 mg PO BD; target dose 10–20 mg PO BD.
    - _Ramipril:_ Starting dose 1.25 mg PO OD; target dose 10 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line disease-modifying drug; reduces mortality by ~20% and prevents ventricular remodelling in all grades of HFrEF.
- **Side Effects:** Dry persistent cough (bradykinin accumulation), symptomatic hypotension, hyperkalaemia, renal function impairment.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in all stable HFrEF cases at low dose. Withhold diuretics for 24 hours prior if hypovolaemic. **Contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis, baseline K⁺ >5.5 mmol/L, and pregnancy**.

#### 2. Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers)

- **Name:** Bisoprolol, Metoprolol Succinate (SR), Carvedilol, Nebivolol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 179, 566; Davidson p. 467). Competitively antagonizes β₁-adrenoceptors → protects myocardium against excess sympathetic drive, lowers heart rate, prevents ventricular arrhythmias, and halts progressive myocyte apoptosis and remodelling.
- **Dose:**
    - _Bisoprolol:_ Starting dose 1.25 mg PO OD; target dose 10 mg PO OD.
    - _Carvedilol:_ Starting dose 3.125 mg PO BD; target dose 25 mg PO BD.
    - _Metoprolol SR:_ Starting dose 12.5–25 mg PO OD; target dose 200 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** Confers a **33% reduction in mortality**. Must be initiated only in stable, compensated patients.
- **Side Effects:** Transient initial worsening of heart failure, bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm.
- **When to Start / Stop:** **NEVER start during acute decompensated heart failure**. Introduce at very low doses in compensated patients and titrate upward slowly every 2–4 weeks. Stop or reduce dose temporarily if acute severe decompensation occurs.

#### 3. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA)

- **Name:** Spironolactone, Eplerenone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 567; Davidson p. 466). Competitively blocks intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors in distal renal tubule and myocardium → inhibits aldosterone-mediated Na⁺ retention, K⁺/Mg²⁺ loss, and myocardial fibroblast proliferation (fibrosis).
- **Dose:** Spironolactone 12.5–25 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Add-on therapy in moderate-to-severe HFrEF (NYHA Class II–IV); provides significant anti-remodelling and mortality benefit.
- **Side Effects:** Hyperkalaemia, gynaecomastia and breast pain (Spironolactone; substitute with Eplerenone).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Add to ACEi + β-blocker regimen. Monitor serum potassium (avoid if K⁺ >5.0 mmol/L or severe renal impairment).

#### 4. Loop Diuretics

- **Name:** Furosemide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 563; Davidson p. 465). Inhibits Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending Limb of Henle → promotes rapid natriuresis and diuresis, reducing circulating blood volume and venous preload.
- **Dose:** 20–80 mg PO OD (or 40–100 mg IV in acute pulmonary oedema).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral or Intravenous.
- **Frequency:** Once daily in the morning (to prevent nocturnal diuresis).
- **Features:** Provides rapid symptomatic relief of pulmonary congestion and peripheral oedema; **does NOT provide long-term mortality benefit**.
- **Side Effects:** Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypovolaemia, prerenal azotemia, hyperuricaemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Use lowest effective dose needed to maintain dry weight and clear congestion.

#### 5. Cardiac Glycosides

- **Name:** Digoxin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 558; Davidson p. 467). Selectively inhibits sarcolemmal Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump → increases intracellular Na⁺ → decreases Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange → increases cytosolic Ca²⁺ availability in sarcoplasmic reticulum, producing a positive inotropic effect. Also increases vagal tone, slowing AV nodal conduction.
    
    ```
    Digoxin → Inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → Intracellular Na⁺ ↑ → Na⁺/Ca²⁺ Exchanger Blocked → Intracellular Ca²⁺ ↑ → Positive Inotropy
    ```
    
- **Dose:** Maintenance dose 0.125–0.25 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (or slow IV).
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Provides symptomatic relief and reduces hospitalizations in persistent HFrEF or CHF associated with **Atrial Fibrillation**; **no effect on overall mortality**.
- **Side Effects:** Narrow therapeutic window (0.5–1.4 ng/mL). Toxicity manifests as anorexia, nausea, yellow/green visual halos (xanthopsia), sinus bradycardia, AV block, and ventricular ectopics/bigeminy.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in patients symptomatic despite ACEi + β-blocker + diuretic, or CHF with rapid AF. **Hypokalaemia markedly increases digoxin toxicity**.

#### 6. Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI)

- **Name:** Sacubitril + Valsartan.
- **Features:** Sacubitril inhibits neprilysin (preventing degradation of beneficial natriuretic peptides ANP/BNP), while Valsartan blocks AT₁ receptors. PARADIGM-HF trial demonstrated 20% superior reduction in mortality and hospitalization compared to Enalapril alone.

## 6. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Renal Failure & Electrolyte Imbalance:** Prerenal azotemia due to low cardiac output; hypokalaemia (loop diuretics) or hyperkalaemia (ACEi + MRA).
2. **Hyponatraemia:** Marker of severe advanced disease due to persistent ADH secretion.
3. **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Atrial Fibrillation (occurs in ~20% of cases) and non-sustained Ventricular Tachycardia.
4. **Thromboembolism:** Deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, or stroke secondary to blood stasis and atrial fibrillation.
5. **Sudden Cardiac Death:** Accounts for up to 50% of deaths in CHF, primarily due to fatal Ventricular Fibrillation.
6. **Cardiac Cachexia:** Severe weight loss and muscle wasting due to intestinal congestion and tissue hypoperfusion.

### Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always check serum potassium and renal function **1 to 2 weeks after initiating or increasing the dose of an ACE inhibitor, ARB, or Spironolactone**! Combination neurohumoral blockade carries a significant risk of severe hyperkalaemia and acute renal impairment. If serum potassium rises above 5.5 mmol/L or serum creatinine rises by >30–50%, reduce the dose or temporarily hold the drug.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing a **Beta-blocker (e.g., Bisoprolol) to a patient in acute decompensated pulmonary oedema**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Beta-blockers possess acute negative inotropic effects. Administering them during an acute decompensated episode can severely depress cardiac output further, precipitating cardiogenic shock.
- **Correct Practice:** β-blockers should be initiated **only after the patient is haemodynamically stable, euvolaemic, and fully compensated** on diuretics and ACE inhibitors, starting at very low doses and titrating upward slowly.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Inability of the heart to output sufficient blood for tissue demands at normal filling pressures.
- **Core Disease-Modifying Drugs (Mortality Reduction):** ACEi / ARNI + Cardioprotective β-blockers (Bisoprolol/Carvedilol) + MRAs (Spironolactone) + SGLT2i.
- **Symptomatic Relief Only:** Loop diuretics (Furosemide) for fluid overload.
- **Digoxin Role:** Used for symptom relief in low EF or rate control in CHF with Atrial Fibrillation; does NOT reduce overall mortality.
- **Key Precaution:** Never start β-blockers during acute decompensated heart failure.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Congestive heart failure is a progressive clinical syndrome initiated by myocardial dysfunction, where chronic compensatory neurohumoral activation drives excessive volume overload, pathological ventricular remodelling, and progressive pump failure.**
