# Unstable Angina

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Unstable Angina (UA)** is an acute, life-threatening form of **Non-ST-Elevation Acute Coronary Syndrome (NSTE-ACS)** characterized by a rapid, unpredictable worsening of anginal status (rest angina, new-onset severe angina, or crescendo angina) caused by severe subtotal coronary artery obstruction, in the **complete absence of biochemical evidence of myocardial necrosis** (normal cardiac troponins I/T and CK-MB levels).

It forms a continuous clinical spectrum with **Non-ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI)**, from which it is distinguished solely by the absence of elevated serum cardiac biomarkers.

---

## 2. Etiology & Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Plaque Disruption:** Physical fissuring, erosion, or rupture of a vulnerable, thin-capped atheromatous plaque in a large epicardial coronary artery.
2. **Thrombosis & Subtotal Occlusion:** Exposure of subendothelial collagen triggers local platelet activation, aggregation, and formation of a transient or incomplete **platelet-rich ("white") thrombus**.
3. **Ischemia without Necrosis:** The thrombus causes severe, dynamic subtotal reduction in coronary blood flow (often aggravated by localized coronary vasospasm), causing acute subendocardial ischemia. However, unlike MI, blood flow is restored before irreversible myocyte necrosis occurs.
4. **Absence of Biomarkers:** Because irreversible myocardial cell membrane disruption does not occur, serum cardiac troponins remain strictly within normal limits (<99th percentile URL).

## 3. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

Unstable angina presents with three classical clinical patterns:

### A. Clinical Presentation Patterns

1. **Rest Angina:** Angina occurring spontaneously at rest, typically prolonged (>20 minutes in duration).
2. **New-Onset Severe Angina:** Angina of new onset (within the preceding 2 months) that is severe, presenting as Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) Class III or IV.
3. **Crescendo Angina:** Previously diagnosed stable angina that has become distinctly more frequent, longer in duration, or provoked by lower thresholds of exertion (increasing by ≥1 CCS class to at least CCS Class III).

### B. Associated Symptoms & Physical Signs

- **Symptoms:** Heavy, crushing, or squeezing retrosternal chest discomfort radiating to the throat, jaw, neck, epigastrium, or left arm; accompanied by profuse diaphoresis, breathlessness, nausea, vomiting, and anxiety (_angor animi_).
- **Physical Signs:** Sympathetic overdrive (pallor, tachycardia, elevated BP) or vagal activation (bradycardia, vomiting in inferior ischemia). Ischemic LV dysfunction may produce a transient **Third Heart Sound (S₃ gallop)**, apical systolic murmur of functional mitral regurgitation, or basal pulmonary crepitations during pain episodes.

## 4. Diagnosis & Risk Stratification

### A. Diagnostic Investigations

1. **12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Transient or persistent **horizontal / downsloping ST-segment depression (≥0.5 mm)** or **T-wave inversion (≥1 mm)** in two contiguous leads during pain. ST elevation is absent.
2. **Serum Cardiac Biomarkers:** Serial **Cardiac Troponin I or T (cTnI / cTnT)** and **CK-MB** are measured on presentation and repeated at 6–12 hours. **Biomarkers remain strictly NORMAL** in Unstable Angina.
3. **Echocardiography:** Evaluates transient regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA) during pain and assesses baseline LV ejection fraction.
4. **Coronary Angiography:** Gold-standard invasive modality to identify severe coronary stenoses and guide revascularization.

### B. Risk Stratification (GRACE & TIMI Scores)

- Clinical risk scoring tools (e.g., **GRACE score** or **TIMI risk score**) evaluate age, heart rate, SBP, creatinine, ST deviation, and elevated biomarkers to categorize patients into Low, Medium, or High risk for in-hospital mortality and recurrent ischemic events, guiding the timing of invasive angiography.

## 6. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Management requires urgent hospital admission to a Coronary Care Unit (CCU), bed rest, continuous cardiac monitoring, intensive antithrombotic therapy, and anti-ischemic medical optimization.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Aspirin (Antiplatelet / COX-1 Inhibitor)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 677; Davidson p. 500). Irreversibly acetylates platelet COX-1 enzyme, suppressing Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) synthesis for the 7–10 day platelet lifespan.
- **Dose:** **300 mg** chewable non-enteric loading dose; **75 to 100 mg PO OD** daily maintenance.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (chewed initially for rapid systemic absorption).
- **Frequency:** Single loading dose, then once daily.
- **Features:** Reduces progression to acute MI and sudden death by 25–50%.
- **Side Effects:** Dyspepsia, gastric mucosal ulceration, GI hemorrhage.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately on presentation; continue indefinitely.

#### 2. Ticagrelor (Reversible P2Y₁₂ Receptor Antagonist)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 678; Davidson p. 500). Reversibly binds and blocks the P2Y₁₂ purinergic receptor on platelets, preventing ADP-mediated platelet activation and aggregation.
- **Dose:** **180 mg** loading dose; **90 mg PO BD** maintenance.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Stat loading dose, then twice daily.
- **Features:** Preferred P2Y₁₂ inhibitor over Clopidogrel in ACS (PLATO trial); exhibits faster onset and greater mortality reduction. (_Alternative:_ Clopidogrel 300–600 mg load, then 75 mg OD).
- **Side Effects:** Dyspnea, minor/major bleeding, ventricular pauses.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at presentation; continue for 12 months in combination with Aspirin (Dual Antiplatelet Therapy - DAPT).

#### 3. Enoxaparin (Low Molecular Weight Heparin - LMWH)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 666, 680; Davidson p. 500). Binds Antithrombin III (AT-III) to selectively accelerate inactivation of Factor Xa > Factor IIa (Thrombin), preventing thrombus expansion.
- **Dose:** **1 mg/kg SC** twice daily (or 30 mg IV bolus followed by 1 mg/kg SC BD). (_Alternative:_ Fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC OD).
- **Dosage / Route:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Frequency:** Every 12 hours (BD).
- **Features:** Preferred over Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) due to higher bioavailability, predictable response, and no need for routine aPTT monitoring.
- **Side Effects:** Bleeding, Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT; rare with LMWH).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate immediately; continue for up to 8 days or until coronary revascularization.

#### 4. Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN / Nitrates)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 586; Davidson p. 499). Releases Nitric Oxide (NO) → activates soluble guanylyl cyclase → ↑ cGMP → venodilation (preload reduction) and epicardial coronary vasodilation.
- **Dose:** Sublingual 0.5 mg tablet / spray every 5 mins (up to 3 doses); followed by continuous IV infusion **10 to 200 μg/min** titrated to pain relief.
- **Dosage / Route:** Sublingual or Intravenous infusion.
- **Features:** Fast-acting relief of ischemic chest pain and pulmonary congestion.
- **Side Effects:** Throbbing headache, flushing, hypotension, reflex tachycardia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start for ongoing ischemic pain if SBP >90 mmHg. **Contraindicated** if SBP <90 mmHg, HR <50 bpm, or PDE-5 inhibitors (Sildenafil) taken in past 24–48 hours.

#### 5. Metoprolol (Beta-1 Selective Adrenoceptor Blocker)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 162, 591; Davidson p. 500). Competitively blocks cardiac β₁-adrenoceptors, decreasing heart rate, contractility, and SBP → reduces myocardial O₂ demand and prolongs diastolic perfusion time.
- **Dose:** **25 to 50 mg** PO twice daily (titrated to maintain resting HR at 55–60 bpm).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (or slow IV 2.5–5 mg over 2 mins if severe hypertension).
- **Frequency:** Twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line anti-ischemic drug; reduces risk of progression to acute MI and sudden death.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, cold peripheries.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start within 24 hours in stable patients. **Contraindicated** in acute heart failure/pulmonary edema, hypotension, bradycardia, or severe asthma.

#### 6. High-Intensity Statin & ACE Inhibitor

- **Atorvastatin 80 mg PO OD** (or Rosuvastatin 40 mg OD) for immediate plaque stabilization.
- **Enalapril 2.5–10 mg PO BD** (or Ramipril 2.5–10 mg OD) started within 24 hours to prevent ventricular remodeling.

---

### B. Revascularization Strategy (Coronary Angiography ± PCI / CABG)

- **Early Invasive Strategy (<24 Hours):** Indicated for medium-to-high risk patients (GRACE score >140, dynamic ST/T changes, elevated TIMI score, or refractory pain).
- **Urgent / Immediate Angiography (<2 Hours):** Indicated for hemodynamically unstable patients, severe refractory chest pain, or life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias.

---

## 7. Complications & Prognosis

1. **Progression to Acute Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI / STEMI):** Highest risk within the first 30 days without treatment.
2. **Malignant Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), Ventricular Fibrillation (VF), or Atrial Fibrillation.
3. **Acute Heart Failure & Cardiogenic Shock:** Due to extensive recurrent ischemia.
4. **Sudden Cardiac Death:** Secondary to fatal ventricular arrhythmias.
5. **Prognosis:** In-hospital mortality is approximately half that of acute MI (~1–3%); long-term mortality is strongly dictated by residual coronary stenoses and LV function.

---

## 8. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In managing Unstable Angina or NSTEMI, **THROMBOLYTIC / FIBRINOLYTIC THERAPY IS STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**! Clinical trials demonstrate that fibrinolytics (e.g., Tenecteplase, Streptokinase) offer NO benefit in subtotal platelet-rich thrombi and actually **increase mortality and bleeding risk**. Thrombolysis is indicated EXCLUSIVELY in acute STEMI when primary PCI is unavailable.

---

## 9. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Confusing **Unstable Angina** with **NSTEMI** on clinical presentation.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Both conditions present identically with rest/crescendo chest pain and ST depression or T-wave inversion on ECG. The **sole distinguishing feature** is that **NSTEMI has ELEVATED serum cardiac troponins** (indicating myocyte necrosis), whereas **Unstable Angina has STRICTLY NORMAL cardiac troponin levels**.
- **Resolution:** Never rule out NSTEMI or diagnose Unstable Angina based on a single baseline normal troponin! Serial troponins must be drawn at presentation and repeated at 6–12 hours.

## 12. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Acute coronary syndrome with rest/crescendo/new-onset angina WITHOUT troponin elevation or ST elevation.
- **Pathophysiology:** Plaque rupture → subtotal, transient platelet-rich thrombus → subendocardial ischemia without necrosis.
- **Core ECG Findings:** ST-segment depression or T-wave inversion; normal cardiac troponin I/T.
- **DAPT Regimen:** Aspirin (300 mg load, then 75–100 mg OD) + Ticagrelor (180 mg load, then 90 mg BD).
- **Anticoagulation:** Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC BD or Fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC OD for up to 8 days.
- **Contraindication:** Thrombolytics/Fibrinolytics are strictly contraindicated in Unstable Angina.
