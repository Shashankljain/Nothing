# Chronic Cor Pulmonale

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Chronic Cor Pulmonale** is defined as right ventricular hypertrophy, dilation, and ultimate right-sided heart failure resulting from primary structural or functional disorders of the lungs, pulmonary vasculature, chest wall, or respiratory control, leading to chronic pulmonary hypertension (mean pulmonary artery pressure ≥25 mmHg at rest). It strictly excludes right ventricular failure secondary to left heart disease or congenital heart disease.

---

## 2. Key Features & Clinical Synopsis [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology:** Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is the dominant cause (>80% of cases). Other causes include Diffuse Parenchymal / Interstitial Lung Diseases (Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis), severe chest wall deformities (kyphoscoliosis), Obstructive Sleep Apnea, and Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH).
- **Pathophysiology:** Chronic alveolar hypoxia triggers reflexive pulmonary arteriolar vasoconstriction + structural capillary bed destruction (emphysema/fibrosis) → sustained elevation of Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR) → chronic pressure overload on the thin-walled right ventricle → right ventricular hypertrophy (RV wall thickness >1.0 cm) and secondary dilation/failure.
- **Clinical Features:**
    - _Symptoms:_ Progressive exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, cough, exertional syncope, and right upper quadrant abdominal discomfort.
    - _Physical Signs:_ Central cyanosis, elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP) with a prominent 'a' wave, Left Parasternal Heave (RV hypertrophy), accentuated pulmonary component of second heart sound (loud P₂), right ventricular S₃/S₄ gallop, pansystolic murmur of functional Tricuspid Regurgitation, tender hepatomegaly ("nutmeg liver"), ascites, and bilateral pitting pedal edema.
- **Investigations:**
    - _Electrocardiogram (ECG):_ Right axis deviation, Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (tall R wave in V₁), and **P pulmonale** (tall, peaked P waves in lead II).
    - _Chest X-Ray:_ Prominent central pulmonary artery trunks with peripheral arterial "pruning" and right ventricular enlargement.
    - _Echocardiography / Doppler:_ Quantifies right ventricular dilation/hypertrophy and estimates pulmonary artery systolic pressure via tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity.
    - _Right Heart Catheterisation (Gold Standard):_ Directly measures mean Pulmonary Artery Pressure (mean PAP ≥25 mmHg at rest).
- **Management Principles:**
    - _Treatment of Underlying Cause:_ Optimal bronchodilators and smoking cessation for COPD; pulmonary rehabilitation.
    - _Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT):_ Controlled continuous low-flow oxygen therapy (target PaO₂ >60 mmHg / SpO₂ >90%) to reverse hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.
    - _Diuretic Therapy:_ Cautious use of loop diuretics (Furosemide) and Spironolactone to manage systemic venous congestion and peripheral edema without compromising RV cardiac output.
    - _Contraindicated Drugs:_ Avoid non-selective β-blockers and nitrates due to risks of bronchospasm and severe hypotension.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Right ventricular hypertrophy and failure secondary to chronic lung disease causing pulmonary hypertension (mean PAP ≥25 mmHg).
- **Most Common Cause:** Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).
- **Hallmark Physical Signs:** Raised JVP with prominent 'a' wave, right parasternal heave, loud P₂, tender hepatomegaly, and peripheral pedal edema.
- **Key Investigations:** ECG (P pulmonale, RAD, RVH), Echo, and Right Heart Catheterisation (gold standard).
- **Core Management:** Long-term controlled oxygen therapy (LTOT) + treating underlying lung disease + cautious diuretics.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Chronic cor pulmonale is right ventricular hypertrophy, dilation, and failure arising as a consequence of chronic pulmonary arterial hypertension induced by intrinsic parenchymal lung disease, chest wall deformities, or pulmonary vascular disorders.**
