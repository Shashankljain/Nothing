# Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)** is a core emergency life-saving procedure comprising sequential chest compressions and artificial ventilation aimed at manually maintaining systemic arterial blood flow and cerebral oxygenation during cardiac arrest until spontaneous cardiac activity and circulation are restored.

**Cardiac Arrest** is defined as the sudden and complete cessation of mechanical cardiac pump function, resulting in absence of systemic perfusion. It is clinically recognized by the triad of:

1. Unconsciousness / Unresponsiveness
2. Absence of a central arterial pulse (carotid or femoral)
3. Apnea or abnormal agonal gasping

---

## 2. Indications & Etiology of Cardiac Arrest

### A. Clinical Indications

- Sudden cardiac arrest from any cause.
- Unconscious patient with absent carotid pulse and absent/agonal breathing.

### B. Precipitating Etiologies

1. **Primary Cardiac Causes (~75–80%):** Acute Coronary Syndromes (Acute MI) triggering fatal ventricular arrhythmias, dilated or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, valvular heart disease, long QT syndrome, and myocarditis.
2. **Non-Cardiac / Secondary Causes:** Severe hypoxaemia/asphyxia, massive pulmonary embolism, hypovolaemic shock, electrocution, drowning, severe electrolyte derangements (hyperkalaemia), drug toxicity, and hypothermia.

---

## 3. The Chain of Survival [⭐ High-Yield]

The probability of surviving a cardiac arrest depends on a continuous, uninterrupted series of timed actions known as **The Chain of Survival**:

```
┌─────────────────┐     ┌─────────────────┐     ┌─────────────────┐     ┌─────────────────┐
│  Early ACCESS   │ ──► │    Early CPR    │ ──► │      Early      │ ──► │    Early ALS    │
│  (Call for Help)│     │  (Buy Time)     │     │  DEFIBRILLATION │     │   (Stabilise)   │
└─────────────────┘     └─────────────────┘     └─────────────────┘     └─────────────────┘
```

1. **Early Access / Early Recognition:** Rapid identification of cardiac arrest and immediate activation of emergency medical services (resuscitation team).
2. **Early CPR:** Immediate high-quality chest compressions administered by bystanders or first responders to maintain marginal coronary and cerebral perfusion.
3. **Early Defibrillation:** Rapid application of an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) or manual defibrillator to terminate shockable tachyarrhythmias within minutes.
4. **Early Advanced Life Support (ALS) & Post-ROSC Care:** Advanced airway management, intravenous pharmacotherapy, and targeted post-resuscitation intensive care.

---

## 4. Basic Life Support (BLS) [⭐ High-Yield]

BLS encompasses non-invasive physical maneuvers performed to maintain airway patency, ventilation, and systemic circulation without specialized medical equipment.

### A. Stepwise BLS Sequence (ABCDE Approach)

1. **Scene Safety & Responsiveness:** Ensure the area is safe. Shake shoulders gently and ask, _"Are you alright?"_.
2. **Shout for Help & Activate Emergency Response:** Call for the resuscitation team and request a defibrillator.
3. **Airway (A):** Open the airway using the **Head-Tilt / Chin-Lift maneuver**. (Use the _Jaw-Thrust maneuver_ if cervical spine injury is suspected).
4. **Breathing (B):** Look, listen, and feel for normal breathing for **no more than 10 seconds**. (Agonal gasps must be treated as cardiac arrest).
5. **Circulation (C):** Palpate the **carotid pulse** in health care settings for no longer than 10 seconds. If no pulse is felt, start chest compressions immediately.

### B. High-Quality Chest Compression Standards

- **Hand Placement:** Heel of one hand placed on the lower half of the sternum, with the second hand interlocked on top.
- **Compression Rate:** **100 to 120 compressions per minute**.
- **Compression Depth:** **5 to 6 cm** (2 to 2.4 inches) in adults, ensuring full chest recoil after each compression without leaning on the chest.
- **Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio:** **30 compressions to 2 rescue breaths** (30:2).
- **Minimizing Interruptions:** Keep interruptions in chest compressions to <10 seconds during rhythm checks or shock delivery.

### A. Shockable Pathway (Ventricular Fibrillation - VF / Pulseless VT)

1. **Initial Shock:** Deliver **1 biphasic shock (150–200 Joules)** using defibrillator pads applied to the upper right sternal edge and left cardiac apex.
2. **Immediate CPR:** Resume CPR 30:2 for **2 minutes** immediately after shock delivery without stopping to check the pulse or rhythm.
3. **Subsequent Cycles:** Re-assess rhythm every 2 minutes. If VF/VT persists, deliver a second shock (150–200 J) and resume CPR.
4. **Drug Timing in Shockable Pathway:**
    - **Adrenaline (Epinephrine):** Administer **1 mg IV** after the **3rd shock**, and repeat every 3–5 minutes (every alternate 2-minute cycle).
    - **Amiodarone:** Administer **300 mg IV bolus** after the **3rd shock**. (An additional 150 mg IV can be given after the 5th shock for refractory VF/VT).

### B. Non-Shockable Pathway (Pulseless Electrical Activity - PEA / Asystole)

1. **Immediate Action:** Resume CPR 30:2 for **2 minutes** immediately.
2. **Early Adrenaline:** Administer **Adrenaline 1 mg IV** as soon as vascular access (IV or Intraosseous - IO) is established. Repeat every 3–5 minutes.
3. **Search for Causes:** Actively identify and correct reversible underlying precipitating factors.

## 6. Reversible Causes of Cardiac Arrest (The 4 Hs & 4 Ts) [⭐ High-Yield]

During continuous cycles of CPR, clinicians must systematically evaluate and treat the **4 Hs and 4 Ts**:

```
                       ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                       │     REVERSIBLE CAUSES OF CARDIAC ARREST  │
                       └────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                            │
             ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                             ▼
┌──────────────────────────┐                                 ┌──────────────────────────┐
│          4 Hs            │                                 │          4 Ts            │
├──────────────────────────┤                                 ├──────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Hypoxia               │                                 │ 1. Thrombosis (Coronary) │
│ 2. Hypovolaemia          │                                 │ 2. Thrombosis (Pulmonary)│
│ 3. Hypo-/Hyperkalaemia   │                                 │ 3. Tension Pneumothorax  │
│ 4. Hypothermia           │                                 │ 4. Tamponade (Cardiac)   │
│    (and Metabolic)       │                                 │ 5. Toxins / Overdose     │
└──────────────────────────┘                                 └──────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Hypoxia:** Administer high-flow 100% oxygen and secure an advanced airway.
2. **Hypovolaemia:** Infuse rapid intravenous fluid challenges (crystalloids/blood).
3. **Hyperkalaemia / Hypokalaemia / Metabolic Acidosis:** Administer IV Calcium Chloride (10%), Sodium Bicarbonate (8.4%), or Glucose-Insulin for severe hyperkalaemia.
4. **Hypothermia:** Initiate active internal core rewarming.
5. **Thrombosis (Coronary or Pulmonary):** Perform emergency Coronary Angiography/PCI or thrombolytic therapy for suspected massive pulmonary embolism.
6. **Tension Pneumothorax:** Perform immediate needle thoracocentesis followed by chest drain insertion.
7. **Tamponade (Cardiac):** Perform immediate pericardiocentesis or thoracotomy.
8. **Toxins / Overdose:** Administer specific antidote agents.

## 7. Pharmacotherapy in CPR (Dosage Rule) [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

- **Name:** Adrenaline (Epinephrine).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 142; Davidson p. 457). Potent direct-acting sympathomimetic agonist:
    - _α₁-Adrenoceptor Activation:_ Produces intense peripheral arteriolar vasoconstriction. This raises systemic vascular resistance and elevates aortic diastolic pressure during chest compressions, significantly improving **coronary and cerebral perfusion pressure**.
    - _β₁-Adrenoceptor Activation:_ Increases SA node automaticity, cardiac contractility, and conduction velocity.
- **Dose:** **1 mg** (10 mL of 1:10,000 solution or 1 mL of 1:1,000 solution diluted in 10 mL saline).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) or Intraosseous (IO) push, followed by a 20 mL saline flush.
- **Frequency:** Every **3 to 5 minutes** during CPR.
- **Features:** Primary vasopressor drug of choice in cardiac arrest.
- **Side Effects:** Severe post-ROSC tachycardia, hypertension, myocardial oxygen demand spike, ventricular arrhythmias.
- **When to Start / Stop:** In PEA/Asystole, start immediately upon access. In VF/pulseless VT, start after the 3rd shock. Stop once ROSC is achieved.

### 2. Amiodarone

- **Name:** Amiodarone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 573–578; Davidson p. 481). Broad-spectrum Class III antiarrhythmic agent:
    - Blocks myocardial delayed-rectifier potassium (K⁺) channels, prolonging action potential duration and effective refractory period.
    - Exhibits additional Class I (Na⁺ channel block), Class II (β-blockade), and Class IV (Ca²⁺ channel block) actions. Terminates re-entrant ventricular tachyarrhythmias.
- **Dose:** Initial bolus **300 mg** (diluted in 20 mL of 5% Dextrose). A secondary dose of **150 mg** can be given for recurrent/refractory VF/VT.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) or Intraosseous (IO) rapid injection.
- **Frequency:** Single 300 mg bolus after 3rd shock; optional 150 mg after 5th shock.
- **Features:** First-line antiarrhythmic for shock-refractory VF and pulseless VT.
- **Side Effects:** Hypotension, bradycardia upon ROSC, phlebitis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start after the 3rd shock in shockable VF/VT.

## 8. Post-Resuscitation Care (Post-ROSC) [⭐ High-Yield]

Following Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC), structured post-cardiac arrest management is critical to prevent secondary organ injury:

1. **Airway & Ventilation:** Secure airway via endotracheal intubation. Titrate inspired oxygen to maintain arterial oxygen saturation (**SaO₂**) between **94% and 98%**. Maintain normal arterial PaCO₂ (4.5–6.0 kPa or 33–45 mmHg).
2. **Hemodynamic Optimization:** Maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (**MAP**) **≥70 mmHg** (or Systolic BP >120 mmHg) using IV fluid challenges and vasopressor infusions (Noradrenaline / Dobutamine) to ensure organ perfusion.
3. **Coronary Revascularization:** Perform immediate 12-lead ECG; if ST-elevation MI is present, proceed directly to emergency Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI).
4. **Targeted Temperature Management (TTM):** Maintain constant core body temperature at **32°C to 36°C for at least 24 hours** in comatose patients to reduce ischemic brain damage.
5. **Glycemic & Metabolic Control:** Avoid hypoglycemia; maintain capillary blood glucose between 6 and 10 mmol/L.

---

## 9. Complications of CPR

- **Rib Fractures & Sternum Fracture:** Most common structural complication of chest compressions.
- **Pulmonary:** Pulmonary contusion, hemothorax, pneumothorax, aspiration pneumonia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial contusion, aortic tear, pericardial effusion/tamponade.
- **Abdominal:** Gastric inflation/rupture, hepatic laceration, splenic hematoma.

### Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Never interrupt chest compressions for more than 10 seconds during rhythm evaluations or advanced airway placement! Interruptions cause an immediate collapse in coronary perfusion pressure. It requires up to 60 seconds of continuous chest compressions to rebuild adequate myocardial perfusion pressure; frequent pauses drastically diminish ROSC and neurological survival rates.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Pausing chest compressions immediately after delivering a defibrillation shock to check for a carotid pulse or evaluate the ECG monitor.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Mechanical cardiac pump activity rarely resumes instantly after electrical defibrillation. Pausing compressions creates critical myocardial ischemia.
- **Correct Practice:** **Immediately resume CPR (30:2) for 2 full minutes after shock delivery** before re-evaluating the cardiac rhythm or checking for a pulse.

---

### Quick Revision

- **BLS Compression Metrics:** 100–120 compressions/min, depth 5–6 cm, 30:2 compression-to-ventilation ratio.
- **Shockable Rhythms:** VF and Pulseless VT → Deliver 1 biphasic shock (150–200 J) + immediate 2-minute CPR.
- **Non-Shockable Rhythms:** PEA and Asystole → Immediate CPR + early Adrenaline 1 mg IV.
- **Core ALS Drugs:** Adrenaline 1 mg IV every 3–5 mins; Amiodarone 300 mg IV after 3rd shock.
- **Post-ROSC Targets:** MAP ≥70 mmHg, SaO₂ 94–98%, Targeted Temperature Management at 32–36°C.
