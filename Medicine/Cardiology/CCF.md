# Case Analysis & Long Answer: Congestive Cardiac Failure (CCF) / Chronic Heart Failure

## Primary Diagnosis

**Most Probable Diagnosis:** **Congestive Cardiac Failure (CCF) / Chronic Heart Failure** (specifically Left Ventricular Failure with secondary Right Heart Failure / Biventricular Failure secondary to Coronary Artery Disease / Ischaemic Heart Disease, exacerbated by chronic cigarette smoking).

### Clinical Case Correlation

- **Age & Chronic Smoking History:** A 58-year-old male chronic smoker is at exceptionally high risk for accelerated coronary atherosclerosis, myocardial ischaemia, and prior silent or overt myocardial infarction (MI).
- **Progressive Exertional Breathlessness, Orthopnea, and Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnea (PND):** Classic cardinal symptoms of Left Ventricular Failure (LVF) resulting from elevated left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) and passive pulmonary venous congestion.
- **Bilateral Pedal Edema:** Indicates systemic venous congestion and Na⁺/H₂O retention secondary to right-sided heart failure and secondary hyperaldosteronism.
- **Tachycardia:** Reflex compensatory activation of the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) to preserve cardiac output in the setting of reduced stroke volume.
- **Gallop Rhythm (S₃ Gallop):** Pathognomonic clinical feature of left ventricular volume overload and severe systolic dysfunction, caused by rapid early diastolic filling into a non-compliant, dilated ventricle.
- **Fine Basal Crackles (Crepitations):** Caused by transudation of fluid into the pulmonary alveoli (pulmonary congestion/oedema) secondary to elevated pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure.

---

## Four Important Conditions Leading to Congestive Heart Failure

1. **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) / Myocardial Infarction (MI):** Represents the single most common cause (~60–70% of cases). Ischaemic injury and fibrous scarring lead to irreversible loss of functioning myocardium, segmental wall motion abnormalities, and depressed systolic contractility.
2. **Systemic Hypertension:** Chronic arterial pressure overload increases left ventricular afterload. This forces the heart to undergo concentric left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), eventually progressing to myocyte apoptosis, interstitial fibrosis, chamber dilatation, and combined systolic/diastolic failure.
3. **Valvular Heart Diseases:** Conditions such as Mitral Regurgitation, Aortic Stenosis, Aortic Regurgitation, or Rheumatic Mitral Stenosis cause chronic volume or pressure overload, inducing progressive ventricular remodeling and congestive failure.
4. **Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) & Myocarditis:** Primary disease of the myocardium caused by genetic mutations (e.g., titin truncation), viral infections (e.g., Coxsackie B, HIV), or cardiotoxins (alcohol, doxorubicin chemotherapy), producing global four-chamber cardiac enlargement and severe pump failure.

---

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Heart Failure (HF)**, or **Congestive Cardiac Failure (CCF)**, is a complex clinical syndrome resulting from any structural or functional cardiac disorder that impairs the ability of the ventricle to fill with or eject blood. It is characterized by the inability of the heart to pump blood at a rate commensurate with the metabolic requirements of peripheral tissues, or doing so only at an elevated filling pressure.

- **Systolic Heart Failure (HFrEF - Reduced Ejection Fraction):** Impaired myocardial contractility (Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction - LVEF ≤40%) where the ventricle fails to generate sufficient wall tension to eject adequate stroke volume.
- **Diastolic Heart Failure (HFpEF - Preserved Ejection Fraction):** Impaired ventricular relaxation and compliance during diastole (LVEF ≥50%), resulting in elevated ventricular filling pressures despite normal contractile function.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary Underlying Causes

- **Ischaemic Heart Disease (CAD/MI):** Most frequent cause globally.
- **Pressure Overload:** Systemic Hypertension, Aortic Stenosis.
- **Volume Overload:** Mitral Regurgitation, Aortic Regurgitation, High-output states (severe anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, AV fistulae).
- **Myocardial Disease:** Dilated Cardiomyopathy, Viral Myocarditis, Infiltrative disorders (Amyloidosis, Haemochromatosis).
- **Arrhythmias:** Persistent Atrial Fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, Complete Heart Block.

### B. Precipitating / Decompensating Factors (Mnemonic: "HEART FAILURES")

- **H** — Hypertension (Uncontrolled spike in afterload)
- **E** — Endocrine / Metabolic stress (Thyrotoxicosis, anaemia, fever)
- **A** — Arrhythmia (Acute Atrial Fibrillation)
- **R** — Reduction of therapy / Non-adherence
- **T** — Treatment with NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, or negative inotropes
- **F** — Fluid overload / High dietary salt intake
- **A** — Acute Myocardial Ischaemia or Infarction
- **I** — Intercurrent Infection (Pneumonia, UTI)
- **L** — Lung pathology (Pulmonary Embolism)
- **U** — Uraemia / Acute Kidney Injury
- **R** — Restlessness / Physical exertion
- **E** — Electrolyte imbalance (Hypokalemia)
- **S** — Silent Valvular dysfunction (Papillary muscle rupture)

## 3. Classification

### A. NYHA Functional Classification (Based on Symptoms)

- **Class I:** No limitation of physical activity. Ordinary physical activity does not cause undue fatigue, palpitation, or dyspnoea.
- **Class II:** Slight limitation of physical activity. Comfortable at rest, but ordinary activity causes fatigue, palpitation, or dyspnoea.
- **Class III:** Marked limitation of physical activity. Comfortable at rest, but less than ordinary activity causes symptoms.
- **Class IV:** Inability to carry out any physical activity without discomfort. Symptoms present even at rest.

### B. Pathophysiologic Subtypes

- **HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%):** Primary systolic pump failure.
- **HFmrEF (LVEF 41–49%):** Mildly reduced ejection fraction.
- **HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%):** Diastolic dysfunction due to stiff, non-compliant LV wall.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence & Compensatory Neurohumoral Mechanisms

1. **Initial Myocardial Injury:** Ischaemia, hypertension, or toxic insult reduces stroke volume and cardiac output.
2. **Laplace Law & Wall Tension:** \[\text{Wall Tension} = \frac{\text{Intraventricular Pressure} \times \text{Ventricular Radius}}{2 \times \text{Wall Thickness}}\] As the ventricle dilates to maintain end-diastolic volume, radius increases, raising wall tension and oxygen demand, setting up a vicious cycle.
3. **Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS) Activation:** Decreased systemic perfusion triggers baroreceptors → ↑ Noradrenaline release → ↑ Heart rate and contractility, but α₁-mediated vasoconstriction increases afterload and myocardial work.
4. **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Hyperactivation:**
    - Reduced renal perfusion stimulates juxtaglomerular renin release → converts Angiotensinogen to Angiotensin I → converted by ACE to **Angiotensin II**.
    - Angiotensin II causes potent arteriolar vasoconstriction (↑ afterload) and drives pathological myocyte apoptosis, hypertrophy, and interstitial fibrosis.
    - **Aldosterone:** Promotes distal renal Na⁺ and H₂O reabsorption (↑ preload) and myocardial fibroblast proliferation.
5. **Vasopressin (ADH) & Endothelin-1:** Promote water retention, hyponatraemia, and intense vasoconstriction.
6. **Counter-Regulatory Peptides (ANP & BNP):** Released from stretched atria and ventricles to promote natriuresis and vasodilation; ultimately overwhelmed by chronic RAAS/SNS activity.

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Symptoms

- **Exertional Dyspnoea:** Earliest symptom due to elevated pulmonary capillary pressure during activity.
- **Orthopnoea:** Breathlessness when lying flat, relieved by sitting upright (due to venous blood redistribution from legs to thorax).
- **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Dyspnoea (PND):** Sudden awakening from sleep gasping for air, forcing patient to stand or go to an open window.
- **Fatigue & Weakness:** Low cardiac output resulting in skeletal muscle hypoperfusion.
- **Anorexia & Abdominal Distension:** Secondary to hepatic congestion and mucosal gastrointestinal edema.

### B. Signs

- **General Examination:** Tachypnoea, central cyanosis, cool clammy extremities, low pulse volume.
- **Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP):** Elevated JVP with prominent waves.
- **Cardiovascular System:** Displaced hyperdynamic apex beat (cardiomegaly), **Third Heart Sound (S₃ gallop)**.
- **Respiratory System:** Bi-basal fine inspiratory crackles (crepitations), expiratory wheezing ("cardiac asthma"), dullness at lung bases (pleural effusion).
- **Abdomen & Extremities:** Tender congestive hepatomegaly, ascites, **bilateral dependent pitting pedal edema**(pretibial/sacral).

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Laboratory Tests

- **Serum Biomarkers (BNP / NT-proBNP) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - Brain Natriuretic Peptide is released by ventricular myocytes under wall stretch.
    - High sensitivity: NT-proBNP <125 pg/mL or BNP <35 pg/mL excludes heart failure (high negative predictive value).
- **Renal Function Tests & Electrolytes:** Elevated serum urea and creatinine (prerenal azotemia/cardiorenal syndrome); Serum K⁺ (detects hypokalemia or hyperkalemia); Serum Na⁺ (hyponatraemia is a marker of severe, end-stage HF).
- **Full Blood Count (FBC):** Identifies anaemia or infection triggering decompensation.
- **Liver Function Tests:** Elevated transaminases, bilirubin, and prolonged prothrombin time secondary to hepatic congestion.
- **Cardiac Troponins (cTnI / cTnT):** Elevated in acute ischaemia or active myocyte injury.

### B. Imaging & Electro-Diagnostics

1. **12-Lead ECG:** Detects prior MI (Q waves), LVH (Sokolow-Lyon criteria), bundle branch blocks (LBBB), and arrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation).
2. **Chest X-Ray (PA View) [Mnemonic: "ABCDE"]:**
    - **A** — **A**lveolar oedema ("Bat-wing" hilar opacities)
    - **B** — **B**-lines (Kerley B septal lines at lung bases)
    - **C** — **C**ardiomegaly (Cardiothoracic ratio >0.50)
    - **D** — **D**ilated upper lobe vessels (Cephalization/Stag-antler sign)
    - **E** — **E**ffusions (Pleural effusions, right > left)
3. **Echocardiography (Gold Standard Bedside Test):** Accurately determines LVEF, chamber dimensions, wall thickness, regional wall motion abnormalities, and valvular morphology.
4. **Cardiac MRI:** Gold standard for precise ventricular volume quantification and tissue characterisation (myocardial fibrosis/viability).

### C. Diagnostic Criteria (Framingham Criteria for Heart Failure)

Requires **2 Major Criteria** OR **1 Major + 2 Minor Criteria**:

|Major Criteria|Minor Criteria|
|:--|:--|
|• Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea• Neck vein distension (Elevated JVP)• Pulmonary crackles (crepitations)• Cardiomegaly on CXR• Acute pulmonary oedema• S₃ gallop rhythm• Hepatojugular reflux|• Bilateral ankle / pedal edema• Nocturnal cough• Exertional dyspnoea• Hepatomegaly• Pleural effusion• Tachycardia (Heart rate >120 bpm)• Weight loss >4.5 kg in 5 days in response to treatment|

## 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. General & Lifestyle Measures

- **Dietary Sodium Restriction:** Limit salt intake to <2.0 g/day (<5.0 g NaCl/day).
- **Fluid Restriction:** Limit fluid intake to 1.5–2.0 L/day in severe hyponatraemia.
- **Weight Monitoring:** Daily weighing; sudden weight gain (>1.5–2 kg in 2 days) indicates fluid retention.
- **Lifestyle:** Mandatory smoking cessation, complete alcohol abstinence, pneumococcal and influenza vaccination.

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy Guidelines & Dosage Protocols

#### 1. Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors

- **Name:** Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 534, 564, 608). Inhibits ACE enzyme → prevents conversion of Angiotensin I to Angiotensin II. Produces arteriolar/venodilation (↓ afterload and preload), suppresses aldosterone secretion, inhibits Ang II-driven myocyte apoptosis and cardiac fibrosis. Inhibits kininase II, raising vasodilatory bradykinin levels.
    
    ```
    Angiotensinogen → (Renin) → Angiotensin I → (ACE) ✖ → Angiotensin II (Blocked)
    ```
    
- **Dose:**
    - _Enalapril:_ Starting dose 2.5 mg PO BD; target maintenance dose 10 mg PO BD.
    - _Ramipril:_ Starting dose 1.25–2.5 mg PO OD; target maintenance dose 10 mg PO OD.
    - _Lisinopril:_ Starting dose 2.5 mg PO OD; target maintenance dose 20 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line disease-modifying drug in all grades of HFrEF. Reduces mortality by ~20% and decreases hospitalisation.
- **Side Effects:** Dry persistent cough (due to bradykinin accumulation), first-dose hypotension, hyperkalemia, acute drop in GFR, angioedema.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in all stable HFrEF patients with Systolic BP >100 mmHg. Monitor serum K⁺ and creatinine at baseline and 1–2 weeks post-initiation. Hold/stop if baseline K⁺ >5.5 mmol/L or if serum creatinine rises by >30%. Contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis and pregnancy.

#### 2. Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI)

- **Name:** Sacubitril + Valsartan (Entresto).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 555, 563). Dual action: Sacubitril inhibits Neprilysin enzyme (preventing breakdown of ANP/BNP, bradykinin) → ↑ natriuresis, diuresis, vasodilation. Valsartan selectively blocks AT₁ receptors.
- **Dose:** 24/26 mg, 49/51 mg, or 97/103 mg PO BD (available as 50 mg, 100 mg, 200 mg combination tablets).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Twice daily.
- **Features:** PARADIGM-HF trial showed a 20% additional reduction in CV mortality/hospitalisation compared to Enalapril alone. Replaces ACEI/ARB in symptomatic HFrEF.
- **Side Effects:** Symptomatic hypotension, hyperkalemia, renal impairment, angioedema.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in stable HFrEF remaining symptomatic on ACEI/ARB. **Mandatory 36-hour washout period** after stopping ACE inhibitor before starting Sacubitril/Valsartan to prevent severe angioedema.

#### 3. Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers)

- **Name:** Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol Succinate SR.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 145, 566, 567). Antagonises chronic sympathetic overdrive → lowers heart rate, reduces oxygen demand, inhibits myocyte apoptosis, prevents pathological remodeling, and suppresses malignant ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Dose:**
    - _Bisoprolol:_ Starting dose 1.25 mg PO OD; target dose 10 mg PO OD.
    - _Carvedilol:_ Starting dose 3.125 mg PO BD; target dose 25–50 mg PO BD.
    - _Metoprolol SR:_ Starting dose 12.5–25 mg PO OD; target dose 200 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** **Reduces all-cause mortality by ~33%** (superior to ACEI alone).
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, fatigue, transient fluid retention.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start **ONLY** in clinically stable, compensated patients without fluid overload ("start low, go slow"). **Strictly contraindicated in acute decompensated heart failure** or cardiogenic shock.

#### 4. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRAs)

- **Name:** Spironolactone, Eplerenone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 567, 634). Competitive antagonist of aldosterone at late DT/collecting duct → blocks Na⁺/H₂O retention, prevents myocardial/vascular fibrosis (anti-remodeling), and prevents K⁺/Mg²⁺ loss.
- **Dose:**
    - _Spironolactone:_ 12.5–25 mg PO OD (maximum 50 mg/day).
    - _Eplerenone:_ 25–50 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Mortality reduction in moderate-to-severe HFrEF (RALES and EPHESUS trials).
- **Side Effects:** Hyperkalemia, gynaecomastia, impotence (spironolactone; avoided by selective eplerenone).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Add to ACEI/ARB + β-blocker in symptomatic HFrEF. Avoid if baseline K⁺ >5.0 mmol/L or eGFR <30 mL/min.

#### 5. Loop Diuretics

- **Name:** Furosemide, Torsemide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 563, 625). Inhibits Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in thick ascending limb of Henle's loop → produces rapid natriuresis and diuresis → reduces circulating blood volume, lowers preload, and relieves pulmonary/systemic congestion.
- **Dose:** Furosemide 20–80 mg PO OD/BD (or 40–100 mg IV in acute pulmonary oedema).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral or Intravenous.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** Provides rapid, dramatic symptomatic relief of dyspnoea and edema. **Does NOT improve long-term survival**.
- **Side Effects:** Hypokalemia, hyponatraemia, hypovolaemia, hypotension, prerenal azotemia, hyperuricaemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Use in all symptomatic HF with fluid retention. Titrate to lowest maintenance dose once dry weight is reached to avoid excessive volume depletion.

#### 6. SGLT2 Inhibitors

- **Name:** Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter 2 in renal proximal tubule → causes osmotic diuresis/natriuresis, reduces preload/afterload, improves cardiac energetics, and reduces intraglomerular pressure.
- **Dose:** 10 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Reduces CV mortality and HF hospitalisations in HFrEF regardless of diabetic status (DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced trials).
- **Side Effects:** Mycotic genital infections, urinary tract infections, volume depletion, rare euglycemic ketoacidosis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line core disease-modifying therapy for all HFrEF patients.

#### 7. Positive Inotropes / Cardiac Glycosides

- **Name:** Digoxin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 556, 561). Inhibits myocardial sarcolemmal Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Na⁺ → decreases Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ availability → **positive inotropic effect**. Increases vagal tone → slows AV nodal conduction.
- **Dose:** Maintenance 0.125–0.25 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Symptomatic relief in severe HFrEF or HF co-existing with Atrial Fibrillation. **No survival benefit** (DIG trial).
- **Side Effects:** Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, xanthopsia (yellow-green vision), AV block, ventricular ectopic beats/arrhythmias.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Reserved for patients symptomatic despite optimal quad-therapy or with AF. Reduce dose in renal impairment and elderly.

### C. Device & Advanced Interventions

- **Cardiac Resynchronisation Therapy (CRT / CRT-D):** Biventricular pacing indicated in symptomatic HFrEF (LVEF ≤35%) with Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB and QRS >130–150 ms) to resynchronize uncoordinated ventricular contraction.
- **Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD):** Recommended for primary or secondary prevention of sudden cardiac death from ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation in patients with LVEF ≤35%.
- **Left Ventricular Assist Device (LVAD) & Cardiac Transplantation:** Indicated in end-stage refractory Class IV heart failure.

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Acute Decompensated Heart Failure & Acute Pulmonary Oedema:** Massive fluid transudation into alveoli causing acute respiratory failure and severe hypoxia.
2. **Cardiorenal Syndrome / Acute Kidney Injury:** Low cardiac output impairs renal perfusion, worsened by aggressive diuresis or ACEI therapy.
3. **Electrolyte Disturbances:** Hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia (loop diuretics) or hyperkalemia (ACEI + MRA combination); hyponatraemia indicates advanced end-stage disease.
4. **Cardiac Arrhythmias & Sudden Death:** Atrial Fibrillation occurs in ~20% of cases. Ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation cause sudden cardiac death in up to 50% of patients.
5. **Thromboembolism:** Stasis in dilated chambers or AF predisposing to DVT, Pulmonary Embolism, or embolic stroke.
6. **Cardiac Cachexia:** Severe loss of muscle and fat mass due to anorexia, intestinal mucosal congestion/malabsorption, and elevated inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α).

---

## 10. Prognosis

Untreated or advanced heart failure carries a poor outlook: approximately **50% of patients with severe left ventricular dysfunction die within 2 years** of diagnosis, primarily from progressive pump failure or sudden arrhythmic death.

## 12. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In chronic heart failure, ACE inhibitors (or ARNIs) and Beta-blockers form the mandatory disease-modifying foundation. While ACE inhibitors are started early in stable patients, **Beta-blockers must NEVER be initiated during acute fluid overload or decompensation**. Always achieve a dry, compensated state using loop diuretics first, then introduce Bisoprolol or Carvedilol at a tiny dose, titrating upward every 2–4 weeks.

---

## 13. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Confusing the role of Beta-blockers in _Chronic Stable Heart Failure_ versus _Acute Decompensated Heart Failure / Acute Pulmonary Oedema_.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In **Chronic Stable Heart Failure**, β-blockers (Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol SR) are life-saving drugs that reduce mortality by 33%. In **Acute Decompensated Heart Failure**, β-blockers are **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** because their acute negative inotropic effect depresses cardiac contractility further, precipitating cardiogenic shock. Hold β-blockers during acute crises, restore compensation with IV furosemide and vasodilators, and re-institute β-blockers only when the patient is dry and stable.

## 14. Diagram Guides

### Diagram 1 : Starling's Law Curves in Normal and Failing Heart

_(Reference in text: See Section 4 & Section 8 - Treatment)_

- **Type:** Ventricular Performance Curve Plot (Cardiac Output vs Preload / LVEDP).
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Standard rectangular plot axes (Y-axis: Stroke Volume / Cardiac Output; X-axis: Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Pressure / Preload).
- **Labels in Order:**
    1. **Curve A (Normal Heart):** Steep, rising curve showing increasing cardiac output with increasing filling pressure.
    2. **Curve B (Heart Failure):** Flattened, depressed curve operating at lower stroke volume for any given preload.
    3. **Congestion Threshold Line:** Horizontal line at LVEDP = 18–20 mmHg, above which pulmonary edema and congestive symptoms develop.
    4. **Arrow 1 (Diuretic Effect):** Moves point Leftward along the failing curve, dropping filling pressure below the congestion threshold without sacrificing cardiac output.
    5. **Arrow 2 (ACE-I / Inotrope Effect):** Shifts the entire performance curve **Upwards and to the Left**, improving cardiac output while lowering congestion.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Heart failure operates on a flattened Starling curve; diuretics reduce congestive filling pressures without causing a major drop in cardiac output."_.

  Stroke Volume

_(Reference in text: See Section 6 - Diagnostic Investigations)_

- **Type:** Radiological Line Diagram of Chest PA View.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Thoracic cage outline showing cardiac shadow and lung fields.
- **Labels in Order:**

    2. **Cephalization (Stag-Antler Sign):** Dilated, prominent upper lobe pulmonary vessels.
    3. **Kerley B Lines:** Short, horizontal, dense septal lines perpendicular to the pleural surface at the lung bases.
    4. **Bat-Wing / Butterfly Opacities:** Hazy bilateral alveolar opacities radiating outward from both hilar regions.
    5. **Costophrenic Angle Blunting:** Bilateral pleural effusions.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Kerley B lines represent interlobular septal edema secondary to chronically elevated left atrial and pulmonary capillary venous pressures."_.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Diagnosis:** Congestive Heart Failure (CCF) secondary to CAD/hypertension; confirmed by elevated NT-proBNP/BNP and Echocardiography (LVEF).
- **Top 4 Etiologies:** Coronary Artery Disease/MI, Systemic Hypertension, Valvular Heart Disease, and Dilated Cardiomyopathy/Myocarditis.
- **Pathophysiology:** Myocardial injury triggers SNS and RAAS hyperactivation, driving myocyte apoptosis, fibrosis, and progressive ventricular remodeling.
- **Quadruple Disease-Modifying Therapy:** ACE-I/ARNI + Beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i (all four improve long-term survival in HFrEF).
- **Symptomatic Relief:** Loop diuretics (Furosemide) rapidly clear edema and pulmonary congestion but do NOT confer mortality benefit.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Congestive heart failure in a patient with ischaemic risk factors represents a progressive neurohumoral clinical syndrome where compensatory sympathetic and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system hyperactivation drives maladaptive ventricular remodeling, congestive pulmonary and systemic fluid retention, and elevated long-term mortality.**
