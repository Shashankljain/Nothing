# Accelerated Hypertension

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Accelerated Hypertension** (often termed **Accelerated-Malignant Hypertension**) is a life-threatening medical emergency characterized by a rapid, marked elevation in systemic blood pressure—typically Systolic BP >200–220 mmHg and Diastolic BP >120–130 mmHg—accompanied by evidence of acute, rapidly progressive microvascular damage and acute target organ dysfunction.

The pathognomonic hallmark of accelerated hypertension is the presence of **Grade 3 or Grade 4 Hypertensive Retinopathy** (flame-shaped hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, and/or papilledema), acute hypertensive nephrosclerosis (rapidly progressive renal failure with proteinuria and hematuria), and/or hypertensive encephalopathy. Untreated, it leads to death within months to 1–2 years.

1. **Essential Hypertension (50–60% of cases):** Superimposed on pre-existing essential ("benign") hypertension, often triggered by medication non-compliance or sudden withdrawal of short-acting antihypertensives (e.g., clonidine or beta-blockers).
2. **Renal Parenchymal & Vascular Diseases (~30–40%):**
    - Acute and Chronic Glomerulonephritis (DPGN, RPGN).
    - Renal Artery Stenosis (Atherosclerotic or Fibromuscular Dysplasia).
    - Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD).
    - Scleroderma Renal Crisis (mediated by intense intrarenal Vasoconstriction and Angiotensin II surge).
3. **Endocrine Disorders:** Pheochromocytoma, Cushing's syndrome, Primary Hyperaldosteronism.
4. **Pregnancy-Related:** Severe Pre-eclampsia and Eclampsia.
5. **Drugs & Sympathomimetic Toxins:** Cocaine, amphetamines, MAO inhibitors taken with tyramine-containing foods ("cheese reaction"), and calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine).

## 3. Classification & Clinical Spectrum

Accelerated hypertension belongs to the clinical spectrum of **Hypertensive Crises**:

|Feature|Hypertensive Emergency (Accelerated MI)|Hypertensive Urgency|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Blood Pressure Level**|Systolic BP >220 mmHg / Diastolic BP >120–130 mmHg|Systolic BP >180–220 mmHg / Diastolic BP >110–120 mmHg|
|**Acute Target Organ Damage**|**PRESENT** (Retinopathy Grade 3/4, Encephalopathy, Acute Renal Failure, Acute LVF, Aortic Dissection)|**ABSENT** (Patient may have severe headache or anxiety, but no acute end-organ damage)|
|**Parenteral vs Oral Rx**|Requires immediate **Intravenous (IV) Antihypertensives** in HDU/ICU|Managed with **Oral Antihypertensives** over 24–48 hours|
|**Target BP Reduction Rate**|Lower Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by **no more than 20–25%** in first 1–2 hours|Lower BP gradually over 24–48 hours|

_Historical Distinction:_

- **Accelerated Phase:** Severe BP elevation + Grade 3 Retinopathy (flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots) without papilledema.
- **Malignant Phase:** Severe BP elevation + Grade 4 Retinopathy WITH Papilledema (optic disc swelling) + Acute Nephrosclerosis / Encephalopathy. _(Modern consensus groups both together as Accelerated-Malignant Hypertension / Hypertensive Emergency)._

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Myogenic Autoregulatory Breakdown:** A sudden, critical rise in systemic blood pressure overcomes the physiological myogenic autoregulatory tone of small resistance arterioles.
2. **Endothelial Shearing & Plasma Leakage:** Hydrostatic pressure causes mechanical shearing of endothelial cells, leading to extravasation of plasma proteins, thrombin, and fibrin into the subendothelial arteriolar wall.
3. **Transmural Fibrinoid Necrosis:** Plasma protein accumulation and cellular necrosis result in **Fibrinoid Necrosis**of small arteries and afferent arterioles (Robbins Pathology p. 200, 500).
4. **Hyperplastic Arteriolosclerosis ("Onion-Skinning"):** In response to vascular injury, smooth muscle cells proliferate and synthesize basement membrane matrix, forming concentric, laminated luminal narrowing (**onion-skin arteriolitis**).
5. **Microthrombosis & Ischemia:** Endothelial loss exposes collagen, triggering platelet aggregation and fibrin microthrombi formation, causing microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA with fragmentation of RBCs/schistocytes) and severe downstream tissue ischemia.
6. **Vicious Cycle of Renin Release:** Intrarenal ischemia stimulates massive juxtaglomerular **Renin secretion**, generating high circulating levels of Angiotensin II and Aldosterone, which further elevates blood pressure and worsens systemic necrotizing arteriolitis.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Neurological:** Severe, throbbing, generalized headache (worse in the morning), visual blurring, scotomas, diplopia, confusion, somnolence, disorientation, seizures, or focal weakness (Hypertensive Encephalopathy / Stroke).
- **Cardiovascular:** Severe shortness of breath, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (Acute Left Ventricular Failure / Pulmonary Edema), or tearing retrosternal chest pain radiating to the back (Aortic Dissection).
- **Renal & Systemic:** Oliguria, gross hematuria (cola-colored urine), nausea, persistent vomiting, and weakness.

### B. Physical Signs

- **Blood Pressure:** Systolic BP >200–220 mmHg, Diastolic BP >120–130 mmHg.
- **Ophthalmoscopic Signs (Fundoscopy - Gold Standard Bedside Sign) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - _Grade 3 Changes:_ Arteriolar narrowing, silver-wiring, AV nipping, flame-shaped retinal hemorrhages, and cotton-wool spots (nerve fiber layer microinfarcts).
    - _Grade 4 Changes:_ **Papilledema** (optic disc swelling with blurred disc margins and loss of venous pulsations) and macular star formation.
- **Cardiovascular Signs:** Accentuated aortic second sound (loud A₂), S₄ gallop, basal pulmonary crepitations/crackles, and elevated JVP.
- **Neurological Signs:** Altered mental status, confusion, hyperreflexia, extensor plantar response, and focal neurological deficits.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Immediate Bedside & Laboratory Panel

1. **Fundoscopy (Direct Ophthalmoscopy):** Mandatory bedside examination to confirm Grade 3 or Grade 4 hypertensive retinopathy.
2. **Urinalysis:** Dipstick reveals significant **proteinuria (1+ to 3+)**, microhematuria, and dysmorphic RBCs or granular casts (reflecting necrotizing glomerulitis).
3. **Renal Function Tests:** Rapidly rising serum urea and creatinine with falling eGFR.
4. **Peripheral Blood Smear & Hemogram:** Assesses for **Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA)**—fragmented RBCs (schistocytes, helmet cells), reticulocytosis, elevated serum LDH, reduced haptoglobin, and mild thrombocytopenia.
5. **Serum Electrolytes:** May reveal hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis secondary to severe secondary hyperaldosteronism.

### B. Imaging & Special Workup

1. **12-Lead ECG:** Demonstrates Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH - Sokolow-Lyon voltage S in V₁ + R in V₅/V₆ >35 mm), ST-segment depression, and T-wave inversion (LV strain pattern).
2. **Chest X-Ray:** Assesses cardiac enlargement, hilar vascular congestion, "bat-wing" alveolar pulmonary edema, or mediastinal widening (suggesting aortic dissection).
3. **Non-Contrast Head CT / Brain MRI:** Indicated in patients with altered consciousness or focal signs to rule out intracerebral hemorrhage, acute ischemic stroke, or **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**(white matter edema in parieto-occipital regions).
4. **Renal Duplex Ultrasound / CT Angiography:** Evaluates kidney size and excludes underlying renal artery stenosis or polycystic kidney disease once acute crisis is stabilized.

Accelerated hypertension is an absolute medical emergency requiring immediate admission to an Intensive Care Unit (ICU) or High Dependency Unit (HDU), bed rest, and continuous intra-arterial blood pressure monitoring.

### A. Fundamental BP Lowering Rate Rule [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Target:** Lower Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP = Diastolic BP + 1/3 Pulse Pressure) by **no more than 20% to 25% within the first 1 to 2 hours**, and then toward **160/100 mmHg over the next 2 to 6 hours** (KD Tripathi p. 618, Davidson p. 511).
- **Rationale:** Chronic severe hypertension shifts the cerebral and renal vascular autoregulatory curves to the right. Dropping blood pressure too quickly or drastically compromises organ perfusion, triggering **ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, or acute tubular necrosis**.
- **Exceptions to 20% Rule:**
    1. _Acute Aortic Dissection:_ Rapidly lower Systolic BP to **<120 mmHg and HR <60 bpm within 20 minutes**.
    2. _Acute Ischemic Stroke:_ Do not lower BP unless >220/120 mmHg (or >185/110 mmHg if candidate for thrombolysis).

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Labetalol (Combined α + β Adrenoceptor Blocker) [First-Line Choice]

- **Name:** Labetalol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 165, 619). Competitive antagonist at both **α₁ and β (β₁ + β₂)**adrenoceptors (ratio of β:α blockade is ~3:1 to 5:1).
    
    - _α₁-Blockade:_ Produces arteriolar vasodilation, rapidly lowering total peripheral resistance.
    - _β₁-Blockade:_ Prevents reflex tachycardia and reduces myocardial oxygen demand.
    
    ```
    Labetalol → α₁ Blockade (Arteriolar Vasodilation) + β₁ Blockade (Prevents Reflex Tachycardia) → Controlled BP Drop
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Vascular α₁-adrenoceptors, cardiac β₁ and vascular/bronchial β₂-adrenoceptors.
- **Dose:**
    - _Bolus Injection:_ **20 mg IV** slow injection over 2 minutes; repeat **40 to 80 mg IV** every 10 minutes (up to a maximum cumulative dose of 300 mg).
    - _Continuous IV Infusion:_ **1 to 2 mg/min** (20–120 mg/hr), titrated according to BP response.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous bolus injection or continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** Repeat boluses q10min or continuous infusion.
- **Features:** Rapid onset (2–5 minutes), highly predictable, does not cause sedation, and **does not increase intracranial pressure** (ideal for hypertensive encephalopathy and stroke).
- **Side Effects:** Postural hypotension, severe bradycardia, bronchospasm, heart block, scalp tingling.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon confirming hypertensive emergency. **Contraindicated** in severe bronchial asthma, 2nd/3rd-degree AV block, and acute decompensated heart failure.

#### 2. Nicardipine (Parenteral Dihydropyridine CCB)

- **Name:** Nicardipine.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 618). Highly vasoselective short-acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. Inhibits L-type Ca²⁺ channels in vascular smooth muscle cells, producing potent arteriolar dilation and reducing total peripheral resistance without depressing cardiac contractility.
    
    ```
    Nicardipine → Inhibits L-Type Ca²⁺ Channels in Vascular Smooth Muscle → Arteriolar Dilation → ↓ Peripheral Resistance
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** L-type Voltage-Gated Calcium Channels.
- **Dose:** Initial IV infusion at **5 mg/hr**; increase by 2.5 mg/hr every 15 minutes up to a maximum of **15 mg/hr** until target BP is achieved, then decrease maintenance to **3–5 mg/hr**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Continuous Intravenous infusion.
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion.
- **Features:** Extremely fast onset and offset (elimination t½ ~45 mins); widely preferred in neurosurgical emergencies, stroke, and pre-eclampsia.
- **Side Effects:** Reflex tachycardia (combine with esmolol/labetalol in ACS/MI), flushing, headache, pedal edema.
- **When to Start / Stop:** First-line alternative to nitroprusside; stop once oral antihypertensive therapy is established.

#### 3. Sodium Nitroprusside (Direct Arteriovenous Vasodilator)

- **Name:** Sodium Nitroprusside.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 613, 619). Non-enzymatically converted by glutathione/RBCs to release **Nitric Oxide (NO)** in vascular smooth muscle → activates soluble guanylyl cyclase → increases cyclic GMP (cGMP) → produces immediate, powerful relaxation of both resistance arterioles (↓ afterload) and capacitance veins (↓ preload).
    
    ```
    Sodium Nitroprusside → Non-enzymatic NO Release → ↑ cGMP → Balanced Arteriolar & Venous Dilation → Rapid BP Drop
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase (sGC).
- **Dose:** Continuous IV infusion starting at **0.25 to 0.3 μg/kg/min**, titrated upward every 5 minutes up to a maximum of **5 to 10 μg/kg/min**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Continuous IV infusion (requires an infusion pump and intra-arterial BP line; infusion bottle must be wrapped in black paper to prevent light decomposition).
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion.
- **Features:** Instantaneous onset (seconds) and short duration of action (2–5 mins).
- **Side Effects:** Cyanide and thiocyanate toxicity (manifesting as weakness, disorientation, psychosis, and severe metabolic lactic acidosis if infused for >48–72 hours or at high rates), coronary steal phenomenon, increased intracranial pressure.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in severe aortic dissection (combined with esmolol). Avoid in ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, eclampsia, and severe renal failure.

#### 4. Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN / Nitroglycerin)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 586, 619). Predominant venodilator; releases NO → ↑ cGMP → reduces venous return (preload) and myocardial O₂ demand.
- **Dose:** **5 to 20 μg/min** IV infusion, titrated up by 5–10 μg/min every 5–10 minutes (up to 200 μg/min).
- **Features:** Drug of choice when accelerated hypertension is complicated by **Acute Coronary Syndrome (MI/Unstable Angina)** or **Acute Left Ventricular Failure / Pulmonary Edema**.

#### 5. Adjunctive Loop Diuretic: Furosemide

- **Dose:** **20 to 80 mg slow IV** bolus. Indicated when hypertensive emergency is accompanied by volume overload, acute LVF, or pulmonary edema (KDT p. 620).

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Cerebrovascular Disasters:** Hypertensive encephalopathy, fatal intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or acute ischemic stroke.
2. **Cardiovascular Collapse:** Acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema, acute myocardial infarction, or acute Type A aortic dissection.
3. **Renal Failure:** Malignant nephrosclerosis with rapidly progressive acute kidney injury (AKI) requiring renal replacement therapy / hemodialysis.
4. **Hematological:** Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) and severe Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA).
5. **Ophthalmic:** Permanent visual loss / blindness secondary to optic atrophy, macular star formation, or central retinal artery occlusion.

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **Untreated Natural History:** Historical 1-year mortality was >80–90%, with patients dying within 1 to 2 years due to uremia/renal failure, stroke, or heart failure (Robbins p. 200).
- **Modern Survival:** Early ICU recognition, controlled titratable parenteral antihypertensives, and subsequent oral maintenance therapy have transformed prognosis, achieving **5-year survival rates >80–90%**.

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In managing **Accelerated / Malignant Hypertension**, NEVER drop the mean arterial blood pressure (MAP) by more than 20% to 25% within the first 1 to 2 hours! The autoregulatory curve of cerebral and renal blood flow is shifted to the right in chronic hypertensives. A precipitous or drastic drop in blood pressure impairs organ perfusion, unmasking catastrophic **ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, or acute tubular necrosis**.

---

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Oral / Sublingual Nifedipine (Immediate Release Capsules)** to rapidly lower blood pressure in severe hypertension or hypertensive crises.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Immediate-release sublingual or oral nifedipine causes a rapid, uncontrolled, unpredictable fall in blood pressure, triggering severe reflex sympathetic surge, cerebral ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, and death.
- **Resolution:** Sublingual/immediate-release nifedipine in hypertensive crises is **STRICTLY ABANDONED AND CONTRAINDICATED**! Always use titratable IV infusions (Labetalol, Nicardipine, Sodium Nitroprusside) for hypertensive emergencies and slow oral drugs (Amlodipine, Labetalol) for urgencies.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Severe BP elevation (>200–220 / >120–130 mmHg) + acute progressive target organ damage (Grade 3/4 Retinopathy, Acute Renal Failure, Encephalopathy).
- **Histopathological Hallmark:** Arteriolar **Fibrinoid Necrosis** and **Concentric Hyperplastic Arteriolosclerosis ("onion-skinning")**.
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Sign:** Fundoscopy showing Grade 3 (flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots) or Grade 4 Retinopathy (papilledema).
- **Core Treatment Rule:** Lower Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) by **no more than 20–25%** over the first 1–2 hours using IV Labetalol or IV Nicardipine.
- **Absolute Contraindication:** Sublingual immediate-release Nifedipine is strictly abandoned due to stroke/MI risk.
