# Ischaemic Heart Disease (IHD)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Ischaemic Heart Disease (IHD)**, also termed **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)** or **Coronary Heart Disease (CHD)**, is defined as a spectrum of acute and chronic cardiac disorders characterized by an imbalance between myocardial oxygen supply (coronary perfusion) and myocardial oxygen demand.

Ischaemia not only deprives the myocardium of oxygen (limiting ATP generation via oxidative phosphorylation) but also reduces nutrient availability and impairs the removal of toxic metabolic waste products (such as lactic acid and H⁺ ions). Consequently, myocardial ischaemia is far less tolerated by cardiac tissue than arterial hypoxaemia alone.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

In >90% of cases, myocardial ischaemia results from reduced coronary blood flow secondary to progressive **atherosclerotic obstruction** of the epicardial coronary arteries.

### A. Etiological Causes

1. **Coronary Atherosclerosis (Dominant Cause):** Fixed structural occlusion or acute plaque disruption with superimposed thrombosis.
2. **Non-Atherosclerotic Coronary Pathology:**
    - **Coronary Artery Spasm (Variant / Prinzmetal Angina):** Abnormally hyperreactive smooth muscle vasospasm.
    - **Coronary Embolism:** Emboli originating from left atrial appendages in atrial fibrillation, infective endocarditis vegetations, or prosthetic valves.
    - **Coronary Vasculitis:** Polyarteritis nodosa, Kawasaki disease, Takayasu arteritis.
    - **Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection (SCAD).**
3. **Severe Hemodynamic / Systemic Mismatch:**
    - Severe hypotension / Shock (↓ coronary perfusion pressure).
    - Severe anaemia or cyanotic congenital heart disease (↓ arterial O₂ content).
    - Marked left ventricular hypertrophy secondary to Aortic Stenosis or Systemic Hypertension (↑ myocardial oxygen demand outstripping supply).

### B. Risk Factors for Coronary Atherosclerosis [⭐ High-Yield]

Risk factors are categorized into **non-modifiable** and **modifiable** cardiovascular factors:

#### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors

- **Age:** Risk increases progressively after age 45 in men and age 55 in women.
- **Male Gender:** Men have a significantly higher incidence through middle age; post-menopausal females lose estrogenic protection and reach equal risk.
- **Family History / Genetics:** First-degree male relative with premature CAD (<55 years) or female relative (<65 years).

#### Modifiable Risk Factors (Major Targets for Primary & Secondary Prevention)

- **Dyslipidaemia:** Elevated total serum cholesterol, elevated Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL-C >130 mg/dL), elevated triglycerides, and reduced High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL-C <40 mg/dL).
- **Cigarette Smoking:** Accelerates endothelial injury, promotes platelet aggregation, induces vasospasm, and lowers HDL-C levels.
- **Systemic Hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg):** Promotes shear-stress endothelial dysfunction, smooth muscle proliferation, and increases myocardial oxygen demand.
- **Diabetes Mellitus & Insulin Resistance:** Glycation of arterial wall proteins, endothelial dysfunction, and diffuse, multi-vessel CAD.
- **Obesity & Metabolic Syndrome (BMI ≥30 kg/m² or abdominal waist circumference >102 cm in men, >88 cm in women).**
- **Physical Inactivity & Psychosocial Stress.**
- **Elevated Inflammatory Biomarkers:** High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein (hs-CRP >3 mg/L) indicates chronic vascular inflammation.

1. **Stable Angina Pectoris:** Predictable, transient exertional chest pain caused by fixed atherosclerotic plaque obstructing >70% of luminal cross-sectional area ("critical stenosis"), subsiding with rest or sublingual nitroglycerin.
2. **Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS):** Acute worsening resulting from plaque disruption, thrombosis, or vasospasm:
    - **Unstable Angina (UA):** Ischaemic pain occurring at rest, of new onset, or increasing in frequency/severity, without elevation of cardiac necrosis biomarkers.
    - **Non-ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (NSTEMI):** Ischaemic chest pain with elevated cardiac biomarkers (Troponin I/T) but without ST-segment elevation on 12-lead ECG.
    - **ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):** Transmural myocardial necrosis caused by complete occlusive thrombus, characterized by persistent ST-segment elevation or new LBBB.
3. **Variant / Prinzmetal / Vasospastic Angina:** Anginal attacks occurring at rest secondary to focal coronary artery vasospasm.
4. **Ischaemic Cardiomyopathy (Chronic IHD with Heart Failure):** Progressive congestive heart failure secondary to accumulated ischaemic myocardial loss or chronic hibernation.
5. **Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD):** Unexpected fatal cardiac arrest within 1 hour of symptom onset, usually driven by ischaemia-induced fatal ventricular fibrillation.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Endothelial Injury & Atheroma Formation:**
    - Chronic endothelial trauma (smoking, HTN, oxidized LDL) permits lipid accumulation in the intima, recruiting monocyte-macrophages which ingest lipids to become **foam cells**.
    - Fibrofatty plaque formation progressively encroaches on the vessel lumen.
2. **Critical Stenosis & Dynamic Ischaemia:**
    - Occlusion of **>70% cross-sectional area** ("critical stenosis") prevents compensatory autoregulatory vasodilation during exertion, precipitating subendocardial ischaemia (stable angina).
    - Occlusion of **>90% luminal area** produces inadequate blood flow even at rest.
3. **Subendocardial "Crunch" Mechanism:**
    - During exertion, ischaemic left ventricle fails to relax, raising Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Pressure (LVEDP) from 5 mmHg up to 25 mmHg.
    - High LVEDP compresses intramyocardial perforating arterioles during diastole ("subendocardial crunch"), worsening subendocardial ischaemia. (See **Diagram 1**).
4. **Acute Plaque Disruption & Thrombosis (ACS Transition):**
    - Physical disruption (rupture, erosion, or ulceration) of a vulnerable thin-cap fibroatheroma exposes thrombogenic subendothelial collagen and von Willebrand factor.
    - Rapid platelet activation, aggregation, and coagulation cascade activation generate an intracoronary thrombus:
        - _Incomplete / Transient Occlusion:_ Unstable Angina or NSTEMI.
        - _Complete Occlusive Thrombus:_ Transmural STEMI.
5. **Cardiomyocyte Necrosis & Time-Course:**
    - Loss of contractility occurs within 1–2 minutes of total ischaemia.
    - Irreversible necrotic cell death initiates within **20–30 minutes** of severe ischaemia, progressing from the vulnerable subendocardium outward toward the epicardium, becoming transmural by 6–12 hours ("time is myocardium").

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Symptoms

- **Chest Pain / Discomfort (Cardio-Specific Features):**
    - **Site:** Retrosternal, precordial.
    - **Character:** Constricting, crushing, heavy pressure, squeezing, or burning sensation ("an elephant sitting on the chest").
    - **Radiation:** Radiates to the **left shoulder, inner aspect of left arm**, neck, jaw, epigastrium, or interscapular region.
    - **Precipitating Factors (The 4 E's):** **E**xertion, **E**motional stress, **E**xposure to cold weather, **E**ating a heavy meal.
    - **Duration & Relief:**
        - _Stable Angina:_ Lasts **2–15 minutes**; relieved within 2–5 minutes by rest or sublingual Glyceryl Trinitrate.
        - _Unstable Angina / MI:_ Prolonged pain lasting **>20–30 minutes**; NOT relieved by rest or nitrates.
- **Associated "Angina Equivalents":**
    - Profuse sweating / Diaphoresis (sympathetic activation).
    - Exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, lightheadedness, nausea, or vomiting (vagal discharge in inferior MI).
    - _Silent MI:_ Up to 25% of MIs are clinically silent, particularly in elderly patients and diabetic patients with autonomic neuropathy.

### B. Physical Signs

- **General Examination:** Patient is anxious, pale, diaphoresis present, lying still (in angina) or tossing restlessly (in MI).
- **Cardiovascular System:**
    - **Pulse & BP:** Tachycardia/bradycardia; BP may be elevated (sympathetic discharge) or hypotensive (cardiogenic shock).
    - **Auscultation:** Soft S₁, **Fourth Heart Sound (S₄)** due to reduced LV compliance, **Third Heart Sound (S₃ gallop)** indicating acute heart failure, transient apical pansystolic murmur of Mitral Regurgitation secondary to papillary muscle dysfunction.
- **Respiratory System:** Bi-basal fine crackles (crepitations) indicate acute pulmonary congestion (Killip Class II–III).

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Laboratory Biomarkers of Myocardial Necrosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cardiac Troponins (cTnI and cTnT) [Gold Standard]:**
    - Highly sensitive and specific markers of myocardial injury.
    - **Rise:** Begins within **2–4 hours** after myocardial injury.
    - **Peak:** 24–48 hours.
    - **Duration:** Remains elevated for **7–14 days**.
    - _Diagnostic Threshold:_ Value >99th percentile upper reference limit (URL) with rise/fall pattern.
- **Creatine Kinase-MB (CK-MB):**
    - Rises within 4–6 hours, peaks at 12–24 hours, normalizes within 48–72 hours.
    - Useful for detecting **early re-infarction** due to its rapid clearance.
- **Routine Blood Tests:** Full Blood Count (leukocytosis peak on day 1), Lipid Profile (fasting lipid profile within 24 hours), Fasting Blood Glucose & HbA1c, Serum Creatinine and Electrolytes.

### B. 12-Lead Electrocardiogram (ECG) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Ischaemia (Angina / NSTEMI):**
    - Horizontal or downsloping **ST-segment depression ≥1 mm** (0.1 mV).
    - T-wave inversion or hyperacute symmetrical T waves.
2. **Acute STEMI (Evolutionary Sequence):**
    - _Hyperacute Phase:_ Tall, peaked, symmetrical T waves.
    - _Acute Phase:_ Convex-upward **ST-segment elevation ≥1 mm** in limb leads or ≥2 mm in chest leads, with reciprocal ST depression in opposite leads. (See **Diagram 2**).
    - _Subacute / Late Phase:_ Development of **pathological Q waves** (width ≥0.04s and depth >25% of R wave) representing transmural scar, with T-wave inversion.
3. **Anatomical ECG Localization:**
    - **Anteroseptal:** V₁–V₄ (Left Anterior Descending - LAD).
    - **Anterolateral:** V₅–V₆, I, aVL (Left Circumflex / LAD).
    - **Inferior:** II, III, aVF (Right Coronary Artery - RCA).
    - **Posterior:** Dominant tall R wave and ST depression in V₁–V₂.

### C. Non-Invasive Cardiac Imaging

- **Echocardiography:** Bedside evaluation of Regional Wall Motion Abnormalities (RWMA), Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF), and detection of complications (mural thrombus, papillary muscle rupture, VSD).
- **Treadmill Exercise Stress ECG (Bruce Protocol):**
    - Diagnostic for stable CAD.
    - _Positive Test:_ Planar or downsloping ST-segment depression ≥1 mm occurring at low workload.
- **Myocardial Perfusion Scintigraphy (SPECT / PET) & Stress Echo:** Demonstrates reversible perfusion defects during stress (ischaemia) vs fixed defects (infarction/scar).
- **CT Coronary Angiography (CTCA):** Non-invasive anatomical visualization of epicardial plaque burden; ideal for low-to-intermediate risk stable chest pain.

### D. Invasive Diagnostic Test

- **Invasive Coronary Angiography (CAG) [Gold Standard]:** Direct fluoroscopic visualization of coronary stenosis anatomy, identifying lesions requiring Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) or Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG).

## 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Management is divided into **A. Acute Management of ACS / MI** and **B. Chronic Stable Angina & Secondary Prevention**.

---

### A. Management of Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS) & STEMI

#### 1. Immediate Initial Measures (Emergency Room / CCU)

- **High-Flow Oxygen:** Administered only if SpO₂ <90% or patient is in respiratory distress.
- **Sublingual Nitrates:** Glyceryl trinitrate 0.5 mg sublingual spray/tablet every 5 mins (up to 3 doses).
- **Analgesia:** Morphine sulfate 2–4 mg IV slow injection for relief of severe pain and anxiety.
- **Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT) Loading:**
    - **Aspirin:** 325 mg non-enteric chewable tablet immediately.
    - **P2Y₁₂ Inhibitor:** Clopidogrel 300–600 mg, Ticagrelor 180 mg, or Prasugrel 60 mg PO.
- **High-Intensity Statin:** Atorvastatin 80 mg or Rosuvastatin 40 mg PO immediately.

#### 2. Reperfusion Strategy in STEMI (Time-Critical) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) [Gold Standard]:**
    - Recommended if door-to-balloon time is **<120 minutes**.
    - Restores vessel patency using balloon angioplasty and drug-eluting stent (DES) placement.
- **Fibrinolytic / Thrombolytic Therapy (If PCI unavailable within 120 mins):**
    - Recommended within **12 hours** of symptom onset (door-to-needle time <30 mins).
    - **Tenecteplase (TNK-tPA):** 0.5 mg/kg IV single bolus (maximum 50 mg).
    - **Alteplase (tPA):** 15 mg IV bolus, then 0.75 mg/kg over 30 mins, then 0.5 mg/kg over 60 mins.
    - **Streptokinase:** 1.5 million Units IV in 100 mL Normal Saline over 1 hour (non-fibrin specific, avoid if used in prior 6 months).

#### 1. Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN) / Organic Nitrates

- **Name:** Glyceryl Trinitrate (Nitroglycerine), Isosorbide Dinitrate, Isosorbide Mononitrate.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 585–589). Organic nitrates are denitrated in vascular smooth muscle cells by mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase to release free **Nitric Oxide (NO)**. NO activates cytosolic soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC), raising intracellular cGMP levels. Elevated cGMP activates protein kinase G (PKG), causing dephosphorylation of Myosin Light Chain Kinase (MLCK), reducing intracellular Ca²⁺, and relaxing vascular smooth muscle.
    
    ```
    Organic Nitrate → NO → ↑ sGC → ↑ cGMP → PKG activation → MLCK Dephosphorylation → Vasodilation
    ```
    
    - _Venodilation (Preload Reduction):_ Dominant action at clinical doses; dilates systemic veins, reducing venous return, LVEDP, and myocardial wall tension.
    - _Arteriolar Dilitation (Afterload Reduction):_ Reduces systemic vascular resistance at higher doses.
    - _Coronary Dilatation:_ Dilates epicardial conducting vessels and counteracts vasospasm.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Sublingual GTN:_ 0.5 mg sublingual tablet or 0.4 mg per spray under the tongue.
    - _Oral Isosorbide Mononitrate:_ 20–40 mg PO OD/BD.
    - _IV GTN Infusion:_ 5–20 μg/min IV infusion, titrated upward by 5 μg/min every 5 mins.
- **Frequency:** Sublingual as-needed for acute attacks; Oral once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line agent for acute anginal attacks.
- **Side Effects:** Throbbing headache, facial flushing, postural hypotension, reflex tachycardia, methemoglobinemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start sublingually at onset of chest pain. **Mandatory 8–10 hour nitrate-free interval daily**(at night) to prevent pharmacological tolerance. **Contraindicated** if Systolic BP <90 mmHg, Right Ventricular Infarction, or if Sildenafil (PDE-5 inhibitor) was taken within 24 hours.

#### 2. Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers (β-Blockers)

- **Name:** Atenolol, Metoprolol SR, Bisoprolol, Carvedilol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 158–164, 585). Competitively antagonizes β₁-adrenoceptors in the myocardium. Decreases SA node automaticity, slows heart rate, decreases myocardial contractility, and lowers systemic blood pressure. By lowering HR and contractility, β-blockers markedly reduce myocardial oxygen consumption. Prolonged diastole increases subendocardial perfusion time .
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Metoprolol SR:_ 25–100 mg PO OD/BD.
    - _Atenolol:_ 25–100 mg PO OD.
    - _Bisoprolol:_ 2.5–10 mg PO OD.
    - _Carvedilol:_ 3.125–25 mg PO BD.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** **Only anti-anginal drug proven to improve survival and lower mortality by ~25%** post-MI.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, fatigue, cold peripheries, masking of hypoglycaemic symptoms.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start within 24 hours of ACS admission in stable patients. Continue indefinitely. **Contraindicated** in acute heart failure / pulmonary oedema, severe bradycardia (HR <50 bpm), AV block, or severe bronchial asthma.

#### 3. Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs)

- **Name:** Amlodipine, Diltiazem, Verapamil.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 592–596). Inhibits L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in vascular smooth muscle and cardiac myocytes.
    - _Dihydropyridines (Amlodipine):_ Potent arteriolar vasodilation → decreases Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR) and afterload.
    - _Non-Dihydropyridines (Verapamil / Diltiazem):_ Directly depresses SA node automaticity, slows AV conduction, and decreases myocardial contractility.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Amlodipine:_ 2.5–10 mg PO OD.
    - _Diltiazem:_ 30–60 mg PO TDS.
    - _Verapamil:_ 40–80 mg PO TDS.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (Amlodipine) or 3 times daily (Verapamil/Diltiazem).
- **Features:** Drug of choice in **Vasospastic / Prinzmetal Angina** and as add-on therapy in stable angina when β-blockers are contraindicated.
- **Side Effects:** Ankle edema (amlodipine), flushing, headache, constipation (verapamil), bradycardia, heart block.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start when β-blockers are poorly tolerated or in variant angina. Avoid short-acting nifedipine alone (causes reflex tachycardia and higher MI mortality). Avoid Verapamil/Diltiazem in Left Ventricular Failure.

#### 4. Antiplatelet Agents

- **Name:** Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid), Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 590, 680).
    - _Aspirin:_ Irreversibly inhibits Cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), blocking Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) synthesis in platelets.
    - _Clopidogrel / Ticagrelor:_ Inhibits P2Y₁₂ ADP receptors on platelet membranes, preventing GPIIb/IIIa receptor activation.
- **Dose & Route:**
    - _Aspirin:_ 75–150 mg PO OD maintenance (325 mg acute loading).
    - _Clopidogrel:_ 75 mg PO OD maintenance (300–600 mg acute loading).
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Mandatory secondary prevention therapy; reduces recurrent MI and overall mortality.
- **Side Effects:** Gastric ulceration, GI bleeding, bruising, dyspnoea (ticagrelor).
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiated immediately at diagnosis and continued indefinitely.

#### 5. Lipid-Lowering Drugs (HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors)

- **Name:** Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase (rate-limiting enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis). Upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, clearing LDL-C from blood. Pleiotropic effects: stabilizes atherosclerotic plaques, improves endothelial function, anti-inflammatory.
- **Dose & Route:** Atorvastatin 20–80 mg PO OD; Rosuvastatin 10–40 mg PO OD.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (at night).
- **Features:** Mandatory in all CAD patients regardless of baseline cholesterol. Target LDL-C <55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L).
- **Side Effects:** Myalgia, myopathy, elevated liver transaminases (ALT/AST), rare rhabdomyolysis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start high-dose statin immediately during ACS and continue long-term.

#### 6. Novel Antianginal Agents

- **Ivabradine (5–7.5 mg PO BD):** Selectively inhibits hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (\(I_f\)) "funny" current in SA node, slowing HR without negative inotropy .
- **Trimetazidine (20 mg PO TDS):** Inhibits mitochondrial long-chain 3-ketoacyl-CoA thiolase (LC3-KAT), shifting myocardial substrate utilization from fatty acid oxidation to oxygen-efficient glucose oxidation.
- **Ranolazine (500–1000 mg PO BD):** Inhibits late inward sodium current (\(I_{\text{Na}}\)), reducing intracellular Ca²⁺ overload and LV wall tension.
- **Nicorandil (5–20 mg PO BD):** Dual ATP-sensitive K⁺ channel opener and NO donor.

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Cardiac Arrhythmias (Leading Cause of Early Death):** Ventricular Fibrillation (VF), Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), Atrial Fibrillation, or Complete Heart Block occurring within the first 24 hours.
2. **Cardiogenic Shock & Acute Heart Failure:** Occurs when >40% of left ventricular mass undergoes acute infarction.
3. **Mechanical Rupture Complications (Typically Days 3–7):**
    - **Free Wall Rupture:** Leads to acute cardiac tamponade and electromechanical dissociation.
    - **Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) Rupture:** Produces acute biventricular failure and harsh pansystolic murmur.
    - **Papillary Muscle Rupture:** Causes acute severe Mitral Regurgitation and sudden pulmonary oedema.
4. **Mural Thrombus & Systemic Thromboembolism:** Akinesis of infarcted wall causes blood stasis and mural thrombus formation, risking embolic stroke.
5. **Ventricular Aneurysm:** Late complication characterized by persistent ST-segment elevation >6 weeks post-MI and paradoxical systolic wall bulging.
6. **Post-MI Pericarditis:**
    - _Early Pericarditis (Days 2–3):_ Fibrinous inflammation over transmural infarct.
    - _Dressler Syndrome (Weeks 2–10):_ Autoimmune pericarditis, pleuritis, fever, and elevated ESR, treated with high-dose aspirin or steroids.

---

## 10. Prognosis

Prognosis depends on the extent of left ventricular impairment, residual myocardial ischaemia, and presence of severe ventricular arrhythmias.

- **Early Mortality:** ~25% of MI victims die within minutes before reaching medical care (due to VF).
- **In-Hospital Survival:** With modern Primary PCI and cardiac care unit monitoring, 28-day survival exceeds 85–90%.
- **Long-Term Outlook:** 80% survive 1 year, 75% survive 5 years, and 50% survive 10 years. Anterior MIs have a worse prognosis than inferior MIs.

### Memory Palace: "The Fire Station Emergency Response"

Visualize walking through a city fire station to recall IHD presentation and drugs:

1. **The Alarm Bell & Burning Engine (Angina Attack):** The station alarm rings violently as a heavy red fire truck presses against a fireman's chest (**Crushing retrosternal chest pain**).
2. **The Fire Hose Sprayer (Nitrate Vasodilation):** A fireman sprays a wide, high-pressure water hose (**GTN Spray**), causing all narrow pipe valves to expand instantly (**Venodilation & Preload reduction**).
3. **The Slowing Engine Brake (Beta-Blocker):** The driver pulls a heavy emergency handbrake (**Beta-Blocker**), bringing the roaring engine RPM down to a calm 60 bpm (**HR & O₂ demand reduction**).
4. **The Sticky Wall Scraping Tool (Antiplatelets):** A crew member uses a scraper to stop yellow oil plaque from sticking to the pipeline walls (**Aspirin & Clopidogrel**).
5. **The High-Pressure Water Jet Cleaner (Thrombolysis / PCI):** A specialized jet pipe blasts through a total pipe blockage within **120 minutes** (**Tenecteplase / Primary PCI reopening vessel**).

---

### Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In any patient presenting with acute inferior wall STEMI (ST elevation in II, III, aVF), **ALWAYS obtain right-sided ECG leads (V₃R and V₄R) before administering Nitrates or Morphine!**Inferior MIs frequently involve the Right Ventricle (RV Infarction). RV infarction creates strict preload dependency; giving GTN or Morphine drops preload abruptly, precipitating severe, life-threatening hypotension and cardiogenic shock. RV infarction requires aggressive IV Normal Saline volume loading instead.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering Nitrates to a patient with chest pain who has taken a Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitor like **Sildenafil** within the past 24 hours (or Tadalafil within 48 hours).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Nitrates stimulate cGMP production, while Sildenafil inhibits cGMP degradation. Combining them causes massive, uncontrolled cGMP accumulation, resulting in catastrophic, refractory hypotension, severe MI, and fatal cardiac collapse. Nitrates are **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** within 24 hours of Sildenafil use.

### Diagram 1 : Coronary Hemodynamics & Subendocardial Crunch

_(Reference in text: See Section 4 - Pathophysiology)_

- **Type:** Cross-sectional Anatomical & Pathophysiological Diagram.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Concentric circles representing left ventricular epicardium, myocardium, and endocardium, with perforating coronary arterioles.
- **Labels in Order (Outward to Inward):**
    1. **Epicardial Conducting Coronary Vessel:** Site of atherosclerotic plaque obstruction (>70% stenosis).
    2. **Perforating Arterioles:** Branching vertically into deep myocardium.
    3. **Subendocardial Zone:** Innermost layer adjacent to LV cavity.
    4. **LV Cavity Pressure Callout:** Elevated LVEDP (25 mmHg during ischaemia) exerting outward compressive pressure.
- **Arrows / Connections:** Red arrows showing normal diastolic perfusion vs black block arrows showing subendocardial compression during elevated LVEDP ("Subendocardial Crunch").
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Subendocardial myocardium is most vulnerable to ischaemia because intramyocardial blood flow occurs almost exclusively during diastole and is compromised by high LV end-diastolic pressure."_.

### Diagram 2 : Evolutionary ECG Changes in Acute STEMI

_(Reference in text: See Section 6 - Diagnosis / ECG)_

- **Type:** Sequential Waveform Progression Diagram.
- **Outline Shape to Draw:** Five sequential single-complex ECG waveforms labeled A through E.
- **Labels & Waveforms in Order:**
    - **A (Normal Baseline):** Isoelectric ST segment, normal T wave.
    - **B (Hyperacute - Minutes):** Tall, broad, peaked T wave.
    - **C (Acute Phase - Hours):** Convex-upward **ST-segment elevation ("tombstone" appearance)** merging with T wave.
    - **D (Late Phase - 24–48 Hours):** ST segment returning to baseline, **deep T-wave inversion**, and onset of pathological **Q wave**.
    - **E (Healed Infarct - Months):** Persistent pathological Q wave with upright T wave and normal ST segment.
- **Extra Mark Annotation:** _"Pathological Q waves (width ≥0.04s, depth >25% R-wave height) indicate irreversible transmural myocardial necrosis and scar tissue formation."_.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Myocardial ischaemia caused by imbalance between oxygen supply and demand; >90% due to coronary atherosclerosis.
- **Diagnosis of ACS:** Requires 2 of 3: Ischaemic chest pain (>20 mins), ECG changes (ST elevation/depression), and elevated serum Cardiac Troponin I/T.
- **STEMI Reperfusion Target:** Primary PCI within 120 minutes (door-to-balloon) OR Thrombolysis with Tenecteplase within 12 hours (door-to-needle <30 mins).
- **Disease-Modifying Drug Pillars:** Dual Antiplatelets (Aspirin + Clopidogrel), High-Intensity Statin (Atorvastatin 80 mg), Beta-Blocker, and ACE Inhibitor/ARB.
- **Nitrate Rule:** GTN relieves acute angina via venodilation (preload reduction); strictly contraindicated with PDE-5 inhibitors or RV infarction.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Ischaemic heart disease is a spectrum of acute and chronic cardiovascular disorders resulting from atherosclerotic epicardial coronary occlusion, where acute plaque disruption triggers intra-coronary thrombosis, culminating in myocardial ischaemia, transmural necrosis, and potential fatal ventricular arrhythmias.**
