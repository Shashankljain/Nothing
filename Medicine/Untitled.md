## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Bell's Palsy** is an acute, isolated, lower motor neuron (LMN) facial nerve (Cranial Nerve VII) paralysis of unknown cause (idiopathic), presumed to be caused by acute inflammation, edema, and entrapment of the facial nerve within the narrow bony facial (fallopian) canal.

---

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Etiology & Trigger Factors

- **Viral Reactivation:** Presumed reactivation of latent **Herpes Simplex Virus Type 1 (HSV-1)** or **Varicella Zoster Virus (VZV)** in the geniculate ganglion, triggering acute viral neuritis, localized demyelination, and nerve edema.
- **Cold Exposure:** Classic history of acute exposure to cold draughts or a preceding upper respiratory viral prodrome.

### B. High-Risk Populations

- **Pregnancy:** Particularly during the third trimester or early postpartum state.
- **Systemic Conditions:** Diabetes mellitus, systemic hypertension, and immunocompromised states.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Viral Reactivation & Acute Neuritis:** Latent HSV-1 or VZV reactivates within the geniculate ganglion of the facial nerve.
2. **Nerve Swelling & Entrapment:** Acute cell-mediated inflammation causes edema and swelling of CN VII. Because the facial nerve passes through the tight, rigid, non-distensible bony **fallopian canal**, swelling causes mechanical nerve compression.
3. **Ischemia & Conduction Block:** Microvascular compression causes nerve ischemia, neuropraxia (conduction block), and variable degrees of axonal degeneration.
4. **Facial Muscle Denervation:** Loss of lower motor neuron signals results in acute flaccid paralysis of all ipsilateral facial expression muscles.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Onset & Pain

- **Rapid Onset:** Sudden development of facial weakness, reaching peak severity within **24 to 72 hours**.
- **Retroauricular Pain:** Mild to moderate pain behind the ear (mastoid region), often preceding facial paralysis by 1 to 2 days.

---

### B. Facial Muscle Paralysis Signs

1. **Loss of Forehead Wrinkling:** Smooth forehead on the affected side due to frontalis muscle paralysis.
2. **Lagophthalmos:** Inability to close the eye tightly due to orbicularis oculi weakness, predisposing to eye dryness and exposure keratitis.
3. **Bell's Phenomenon [Pathognomonic Sign]:** Upon attempted forced eye closure, the eyeball reflexively rolls **upward and outward**, exposing the white sclera beneath the unclosed eyelid.
4. **Flattening of Nasolabial Fold & Mouth Drooping:** Asymmetry of the face; corner of the mouth sags on the affected side.
5. **Loss of Facial Expressions:** Inability to smile, frown, whistle, or puff out cheeks. Food accumulates in the gingivobuccal sulcus during mastication, and saliva or liquids dribble from the paralyzed mouth corner.

---

### C. Associated Parasympathetic & Special Sensory Signs (Topographic Localization)

- **Hyperacusis:** Uncomfortable loudness of sounds in the ipsilateral ear due to paralysis of the **stapedius muscle**.
- **Loss of Taste (Ageusia):** Impaired taste sensation over the **anterior 2/3 of the tongue** due to involvement of the **chorda tympani nerve**.
- **Altered Lacrimation:** Dry eye or epiphora (overflow of tears caused by eversion of the lower eyelid lacrimal punctum).

---

## 6. Diagnosis & Laboratory Investigations

- **Clinical Diagnosis:** Diagnosis is primarily clinical, established by finding an isolated, acute unilateral LMN facial nerve palsy without other cranial neuropathies or long-tract signs.
- **Electrodiagnostic Testing (NCS / EMG):** Performed after 10 to 14 days; nerve conduction amplitude drops <10% of normal indicating severe neurotmesis/axonal degeneration.
- **Neuroimaging (MRI / CT Brain):** Indicated if onset is insidious (>3 weeks), recurrent, or if atypical features (sparing of forehead, hearing loss, or other cranial nerve deficits) suggest a stroke, acoustic neuroma, or parotid tumor.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Prednisolone [⭐ First-Line Gold Standard Drug]

- **Name:** Prednisolone [Kdt 2 p. 317, 99].
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 317, 99). Potent synthetic glucocorticoid. Binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors, translocates to the nucleus, and inhibits NF-κB transcription. Suppresses synthesis of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1), attenuating facial nerve inflammation, tissue edema, and ischemic compression within the fallopian canal.
    
    ```
    Prednisolone → Inhibits NF-kB → Suppresses Pro-inflammatory Cytokines → Reduces CN VII Edema in Fallopian Canal → Promotes Motor Recovery
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Intracellular Glucocorticoid Receptors (GR).
- **Dose:** **60 mg PO once daily** (or 1 mg/kg/day) for 5 days, followed by a 5-day taper by 10 mg daily (total 10-day treatment course).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO) tablets.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (OD) in the morning after breakfast.
- **Features:** Gold standard first-line treatment; **must be initiated within 72 hours of symptom onset** to significantly hasten motor recovery and reduce permanent facial paralysis/synkinesis.
- **Side Effects:** Gastric irritation, transient hyperglycemia, fluid retention, insomnia, hypertension.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start within 72 hours of onset; complete 10-day course and taper off.

#### 2. Valaciclovir / Acyclovir (Adjuvant Antiviral)

- **Name:** Valaciclovir / Acyclovir [Kdt 3 p. 841, 17, 23].
- **Mechanism of Action:** Phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase to active triphosphate form; selectively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates HSV-1/VZV DNA chain synthesis.
- **Dose:** **Valaciclovir 1000 mg PO thrice daily (TDS) for 7 days** OR **Acyclovir 400 mg PO 5 times daily for 7 days**.
- **Features:** Prescribed as an adjuvant to oral Prednisolone in complete facial paralysis or suspected Ramsay Hunt syndrome.

---

### B. Mandatory Ocular Protection Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

Lagophthalmos carries a severe risk of **exposure keratitis, corneal drying, and permanent corneal ulceration**:

1. **Daytime:** Instill **Isopto-Tears / Methylcellulose eye drops** (1 to 2 drops every 1 to 2 hours).
2. **Nighttime:** Apply **Lubricating Eye Ointment** (e.g., Paraffin ointment) into the lower conjunctival sac, and manually tape the eyelid shut or apply a protective eye patch during sleep.

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Exposure Keratitis & Corneal Ulceration:** Inability to close the eyelid (lagophthalmos) causes corneal drying, ulceration, and potential visual impairment.
2. **Aberrant Nerve Regeneration (Misdirection of Sprouting Axons):**
    - **Crocodile Tears Syndrome (Bogorad's Syndrome):** Misdirection of regenerating parasympathetic salivary fibers to the lacrimal gland, causing inappropriate **lacrimation (tearing) while eating or smelling food**.
    - **Motor Synkinesis:** Involuntary contraction of one facial muscle group during voluntary movement of another (e.g., eye winking or narrowing when smiling).
3. **Facial Hemispasm & Contractures:** Permanent facial asymmetry, muscle tightness, and involuntary twitching.

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **Overall Prognosis:** Excellent; ~80% to 85% of patients recover completely within 3 to 6 months without residual deficits.
- **Favorable Prognostic Markers:** Incomplete paralysis within the first week, early onset of recovery within 3 weeks, and early initiation of oral Prednisolone.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always check the **forehead wrinkles** to rapidly differentiate between an **Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) stroke** and a **Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Bell's palsy**! In UMN lesions (stroke), the upper forehead muscles are **SPARED** (wrinkling intact) due to bilateral cortical representation of the upper facial nucleus; in LMN lesions (Bell's palsy), the **ENTIRE half of the face is paralyzed, including the forehead**!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing **Acyclovir antiviral monotherapy without Prednisolone**, or delaying oral Prednisolone beyond 72 hours.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Antiviral monotherapy is ineffective for Bell's palsy. Oral Prednisolone is the single most crucial evidence-based drug that reduces facial nerve edema and prevents long-term axonal degeneration.
- **Resolution:** **Initiate Oral Prednisolone (60 mg daily for 5 days, then taper) strictly within 72 hours of symptom onset!** Antivirals (Valaciclovir) are only added as an adjuvant to steroids in severe or Ramsay Hunt cases!

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Acute, isolated idiopathic lower motor neuron (LMN) 7th cranial nerve paralysis.
- **Pathognomonic Physical Sign:** **Bell's Phenomenon** (upward/outward eye rotation on attempted lid closure).
- **UMN vs LMN Key Difference:** LMN paralyses the **entire ipsilateral face including forehead**; UMN spares the forehead.
- **Gold Standard Drug:** **Oral Prednisolone 60 mg daily for 5 days, then 5-day taper** (started <72 hours).
- **Essential Non-Pharmacological Care:** **Eye protection** (artificial tears + night eye patch) to prevent exposure keratitis.
