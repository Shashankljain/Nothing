# Hepatorenal Syndrome

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)** is a severe, potentially reversible form of functional acute kidney injury (AKI) that occurs in patients with advanced liver cirrhosis, severe alcoholic hepatitis, or acute fulminant hepatic failure in the absence of pre-existing intrinsic renal parenchymal disease or structural tract abnormalities [Robbins 3 p. 243, Kdt 1 p. 79, Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].

It is characterized by intense renal afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction and profound renal hypoperfusion driven by extreme splanchnic arterial vasodilation [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes & Precipitating Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### Underlying Etiology

- Advanced Liver Cirrhosis with Portal Hypertension and Ascites [Robbins 3 p. 243, Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
- Severe Acute Alcoholic Hepatitis [Robbins 3 p. 243].
- Acute Fulminant Hepatic Failure [Robbins 3 p. 236].

### Common Precipitating Factors

1. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) & Sepsis (~30% of cases):** Most common precipitant; bacterial endotoxins trigger systemic cytokine release.
2. **Large-Volume Therapeutic Paracentesis:** Paracentesis performed without concurrent IV Human Albumin replacement.
3. **Overzealous / Unmonitored Diuretic Therapy:** Aggressive loop diuresis causing intravascular volume depletion [Kdt 1 p. 79, Kdt 3 p. 627].
4. **Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage:** Variceal rupture reducing effective circulating volume [Robbins 2 p. 201].
5. **Gastrointestinal Fluid Losses:** Diarrhea, vomiting, or excessive lactulose-induced purging.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Portal Hypertension & Splanchnic Vasodilation:** Severe portal hypertension triggers massive endothelial release of potent vasodilators—predominantly **Nitric Oxide (NO)**, carbon monoxide, and prostacyclins—into the splanchnic arterial bed [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
2. **Splanchnic Pooling & Reduced EABV:** Splanchic arteriolar vasodilation causes massive venous pooling of blood, resulting in a marked drop in **Effective Arterial Blood Volume (EABV)** despite systemic fluid overload (ascites/edema) [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
3. **Compensatory Neurohumoral Activation:** The arterial baroreceptors sense intravascular underfilling, triggering maximal activation of systemic vasoconstrictor systems:
    - **Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)**
    - **Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS)**
    - **Arginine Vasopressin (ADH)**
4. **Intense Renal Vasoconstriction:** While splanchnic vessels remain refractory to vasoconstrictors due to local NO, renal afferent arterioles undergo severe, profound vasoconstriction, leading to a drastic decline in **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)** while renal tubular architecture remains structurally intact [Robbins 3 p. 243, Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].

## 5. Clinical Features

- **Renal Manifestations:** Marked oliguria (urine output <400 mL/day) or anuria, rapidly rising serum creatinine and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) [Robbins 3 p. 243].
- **Hepatic Decompensation Signs:** Deep jaundice, gross refractory ascites, caput medusae, spider angiomas, palmar erythema, and hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis) [Robbins 3 p. 243].
- **Hemodynamic Features:** Systemic arterial hypotension (systolic BP often <90 to 100 mmHg), hyperdynamic circulation with tachycardia and wide pulse pressure.
- **Electrolyte Abnormalities:** Severe dilutional hyponatremia (serum Na⁺ <130 mEq/L) driven by non-osmotic vasopressin release [Davidson 1 p. 358].

---

## 6. Diagnostic Criteria (International Club of Ascites - ICA) [⭐ High-Yield]

Diagnosis requires fulfilling all **6 ICA Criteria**:

1. **Cirrhosis with ascites.**
2. **Diagnosis of AKI:** Rise in serum creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or ≥50% increase from baseline [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
3. **No response after 48 hours of diuretic withdrawal and volume expansion:** Serum creatinine does not improve after at least **2 consecutive days of IV Albumin (1 g/kg/day, up to max 100 g/day)** [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
4. **Absence of shock.**
5. **No current or recent exposure to nephrotoxic drugs** (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, iodinated contrast) [Davidson 1 p. 402].
6. **Absence of structural kidney disease**, defined by:
    - Proteinuria <500 mg/day
    - Microhematuria <50 RBCs per high-power field
    - Normal renal ultrasonography (normal kidney size and echogenicity).

### A. General & Initial Measures

1. **Withdraw All Diuretics:** Immediately stop spironolactone, furosemide, and antihypertensives [Kdt 3 p. 627, Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
2. **Discontinue Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Avoid NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, and ACE inhibitors [Davidson 1 p. 402].
3. **Screen & Treat Infection:** Perform diagnostic paracentesis to rule out SBP; start empirical IV Ceftriaxone (1–2 g IV daily) if infection is suspected.
4. **Volume Expansion Trial:** Administer **IV Human Albumin 20% (1 g/kg/day)** for 48 hours to confirm lack of response before diagnosing HRS.

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Terlipressin + IV Albumin 20% [First-Line Gold Standard] [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Terlipressin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 643). Synthetic vasopressin prodrug. Cleaved in vivo to release active vasopressin, which selectively stimulates **splanchnic vascular V₁ receptors**. This produces intense splanchnic arteriolar vasoconstriction, reversing splanchnic pooling, increasing Effective Arterial Blood Volume (EABV), and restoring renal perfusion pressure.
    
    ```
    Terlipressin → Splanchnic V1 Receptor Activation → Splanchnic Vasoconstriction → ↑ EABV → Renal Re-perfusion & GFR Restoration
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Vasopressin V₁ receptors.
- **Dose:** **0.5 mg to 1.0 mg IV bolus every 4 to 6 hours**. If serum creatinine does not drop by at least 25% after 3 days, double the dose step-wise up to **2.0 mg IV every 4 hours** (max 12 mg/day). (Alternatively, administered as continuous IV infusion: **2 mg/day**, titrated up to 12 mg/day).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous bolus or continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** 4-hourly or 6-hourly.
- **Features:** Reverses HRS in 40% to 60% of cases; acts as a critical bridge to liver transplantation [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Side Effects:** Abdominal cramps, diarrhea, peripheral vascular ischemia, ischemic bowel, hypertension, pulmonary edema.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon meeting ICA criteria for HRS-AKI; stop when serum creatinine falls to within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline or after a maximum of **14 days**.

#### 2. Human Albumin (20% Solution) [Mandatory Co-Therapy] [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Human Albumin (20%).
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 643). Plasma volume expander. Increases oncotic pressure, expands intravascular volume, binds circulating endotoxins/cytokines, and improves cardiac output.
- **Dose:** **1 g/kg IV loading dose on Day 1 (max 100 g)**, followed by **20 to 40 g/day IV** maintained concurrently with Terlipressin.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Side Effects:** Volume overload, pulmonary edema, allergic reactions.

#### 3. Alternative Vasopressor Regimens (When Terlipressin is Unavailable)

- **Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine):** **0.5 to 3.0 mg/hr continuous IV infusion** (titrated to raise Mean Arterial Pressure [MAP] by ≥10 mmHg) combined with IV Albumin (requires ICU monitoring).
- **Midodrine + Octreotide:** Midodrine (7.5 to 12.5 mg PO TID, oral α₁ agonist) + Octreotide (100 to 200 μg SC TID, somatostatin analogue) combined with IV Albumin [Kdt 3 p. 643].

---

### C. Definitive & Rescue Interventions

1. **Liver Transplantation [⭐ Only Curative Treatment]:** Reverses renal failure completely because kidney parenchyma is intrinsically normal.
2. **Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS):** Decompresses portal pressure; considered in select non-transplant candidates.
3. **Renal Replacement Therapy (Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy - CRRT):** Used as a temporary bridge to liver transplantation in patients with severe refractory hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, or volume overload [Davidson 1 p. 424].

---

## 9. Complications

- Severe, life-threatening hyperkalemia (K⁺ >6.5 mmol/L) [Davidson 1 p. 363].
- Refractory metabolic acidosis (pH <7.20).
- Pulmonary edema secondary to aggressive IV Albumin infusion.
- Ischemic complications from vasoactive therapy (myocardial or bowel infarction).
- Uremic encephalopathy and multiorgan failure [Robbins 3 p. 243].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **HRS-AKI (Type 1):** Dismal outcome without treatment; median survival is **<2 weeks**, with a 30-day mortality >80%.
- **HRS-NAKI (Type 2):** Median survival is **~6 months**.
- Reversal of HRS with Terlipressin and Albumin significantly improves pre-transplant survival.

---

## 11. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Urinary sodium **<10 mEq/L** and Fractional Excretion of Sodium (FENa) **<1%** in a patient with decompensated cirrhosis presenting with acute azotemia that **FAILS to resolve after 48 hours of diuretic withdrawal and IV Albumin expansion (1 g/kg/day)** is pathognomonic for Hepatorenal Syndrome, distinguishing it from pre-renal azotemia!

---

## 12. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **aggressive 0.9% Normal Saline infusions or high-dose Loop Diuretics** to treat oliguria in a cirrhotic patient with Hepatorenal Syndrome.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Normal saline pools in the dilated splanchnic bed and worsens ascites/edema without expanding effective arterial blood volume; loop diuretics exacerbate hypovolemia and worsen renal vasoconstriction [Kdt 1 p. 79, Kdt 3 p. 627].
- **Resolution:** **Loop diuretics are STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in HRS!** The correct management requires **IV Albumin 20% combined with Terlipressin** to constrict splanchnic vessels and restore renal blood flow!

## 14. Mnemonic / Memory Palace

### Mnemonic for ICA Diagnostic Criteria for HRS: "C-A-R-N-O-S"

- **C** — **C**irrhosis with ascites
- **A** — **A**KI diagnosis (creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL)
- **R** — **R**esponse negative (no improvement after 48h diuretic withdrawal + Albumin)
- **N** — **N**o shock
- **O** — **O**ffending nephrotoxic drugs absent
- **S** — **S**tructural kidney disease absent (proteinuria <500 mg/day, normal USG)

### Memory Palace: "The Dried-Up Water Mill"

Visualize walking through 3 stations in a dried-up mill beside a swollen river:

1. **Station 1 (The Swollen River - Splanchnic Dilation):** A massive, wide, muddy river (**Splanchnic vasodilation**) holding all the water, leaving main city pipes empty (**Low EABV**).
2. **Station 2 (The Clamped Mill Gate - Renal Vasoconstriction):** A technician pulling a heavy emergency lever (**RAAS & SNS activation**) that tightly clamps the intake gate (**Renal afferent arteriolar constriction**), stopping the mill wheel (**Zero GFR**).
3. **Station 3 (The Blue V₁ Pressure Pump - Terlipressin & Albumin):** A worker attaching a blue V₁ pressure pump (**Terlipressin**) and pouring thick white protein canisters (**IV Albumin 20%**) into the intake pipe to force water back to the mill wheel (**Renal re-perfusion**).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Functional, reversible acute kidney injury in advanced liver disease driven by intense renal vasoconstriction [Robbins 3 p. 243, Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
- **Core Pathology:** Splanchnic vasodilation → ↓ EABV → RAAS/SNS activation → Renal afferent arteriolar constriction [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
- **Diagnostic Feature:** U_Na <10 mEq/L, FENa <1%, bland urine sediment, **NO response to 48h diuretic withdrawal + IV Albumin** [Marrow Vol 3 p. 796].
- **First-Line Drug Treatment:** **Terlipressin** (0.5–2 mg IV q4–6h) + **IV Albumin 20%** (20–40 g/day) [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Definitive Curative Treatment:** **Liver Transplantation**.
