# Cardiomyopathies

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Cardiomyopathy** is defined as a heterogeneous group of intrinsic diseases of the myocardium associated with mechanical and/or electrical dysfunction, usually exhibiting inappropriate ventricular hypertrophy or dilatation. These conditions are caused by a variety of genetic, infectious, toxic, or metabolic etiologies, and are strictly distinct from myocardial dysfunction secondary to coronary artery disease (ischaemic heart disease), systemic hypertension, valvular heart disease, or congenital cardiac anomalies.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Cardiomyopathies are divided into **Primary Cardiomyopathies** (solely or predominantly involving the heart muscle) and **Secondary Cardiomyopathies** (myocardial involvement as part of a systemic or multiorgan disorder).

### A. Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) Etiology (~90% of all cases)

- **Genetic Causes (20–50%):** Predominantly autosomal dominant mutations in genes encoding cytoskeletal, nuclear, or sarcomeric structural proteins. Common mutations involve **Titin (TTN truncation mutations account for up to 20% of DCM)**, Lamin A/C (_LMNA_), Dystrophin, Desmin, and Emerin.
- **Infectious / Post-Myocarditis:** Viral myocarditis (Coxsackie B, Parvovirus B19, Adenovirus, HIV), Chagas disease (_Trypanosoma cruzi_ in South America).
- **Toxic / Drug-Induced:** Chronic alcohol abuse ("alcoholic cardiomyopathy"), chemotherapeutic agents (Anthracyclines like Doxorubicin), Cocaine, Cobalt, and Cyclophosphamide.
- **Metabolic & Nutritional:** Thiamine deficiency ("wet beriberi"), Hypothyroidism, Thyrotoxicosis, and Hemochromatosis.
- **Peripartum Cardiomyopathy:** Occurs in late pregnancy or up to 5 months postpartum due to microvascular angiogenic imbalance (elevated antiangiogenic prolactin cleavage products).
- **Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy ("Broken Heart Syndrome"):** Transient left ventricular apical ballooning secondary to intense sympathetic/catecholamine surge following extreme psychological or physical stress.

### B. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM) Etiology

- **100% Genetic Transmission:** Autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expressivity and high penetrance.
- **Sarcomeric Protein Mutations:** Over 400 mutations identified across 9 genes encoding sarcomeric contractile proteins.
    - **β-Myosin Heavy Chain (_MYH7_) & Myosin-Binding Protein C (_MYBP-C_):** Account for 70–80% of all cases.
    - **Cardiac Troponin T/I & α-Tropomyosin:** Associated with marked myofiber disarray and high risk of sudden cardiac death despite minimal physical hypertrophy.

### C. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM) Etiology

- **Infiltrative Disorders:** **Cardiac Amyloidosis** (most common cause in developed nations; light chain AL or transthyretin ATTR), Sarcoidosis, Hemochromatosis, and Glycogen storage diseases.
- **Endomyocardial Disease / Scarring:** **Endomyocardial Fibrosis (EMF)** (common in tropical Africa/South Asia; linked to eosinophilia and nutritional factors) and **Löffler Endocarditis / Obliterative Cardiomyopathy**(hypereosinophilic syndrome).
- **Non-Infiltrative / Fibrotic:** Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma), Radiation-induced chest wall fibrosis.

### D. Arrhythmogenic Right Ventricular Cardiomyopathy (ARVC) Etiology

- **Genetic Desmosomal Defect:** Autosomal dominant mutations in genes encoding desmosomal cell-adhesion proteins, most commonly **Plakophilin-2 (_PKP-2_)**.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Pathogenetic Sequence by Type

#### 1. Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)

- **Contractile Defect:** Cytoskeletal or structural protein defects impair force generation and force transmission within cardiomyocytes.
- **Ventricular Remodeling:** Ventricles undergo progressive four-chamber dilation. According to **Laplace's Law** (\(\text{Wall Tension} = \text{Pressure} \times \text{Radius} \div 2 \times \text{Wall Thickness}\)), an increased ventricular radius elevates wall tension and oxygen demand, setting up a vicious cycle of progressive myocyte loss, subendocardial ischemia, and systolic pump failure (LVEF <40%).

#### 2. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM / HOCM)

- **Diastolic Dysfunction:** Sarcomeric point mutations cause abnormal energy transfer from mitochondria to sarcomeres, leading to massive non-uniform hypertrophy and impaired diastolic relaxation.
- **Dynamic LVOT Obstruction:** In ~25–30% of cases, disproportionate asymmetric septal hypertrophy (ASH) narrows the subaortic left ventricular outflow tract.
- **Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM):** Ventricular ejection through the narrowed outflow tract creates a high-velocity Venturi effect, pulling the anterior leaflet of the mitral valve forward into the septum during systole, producing dynamic LVOT obstruction and secondary Mitral Regurgitation. (See **Diagram 2**).

#### 3. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)

- **Diastolic Stiffness:** Infiltration of proteinaceous deposits (amyloid) or interstitial fibrosis renders the ventricular myocardium rigid and non-compliant.
- **Bi-Atrial Dilation:** High ventricular end-diastolic pressures impede diastolic filling, forcing atria to hypertrophy and dilate, producing severe pulmonary and systemic venous congestion.

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)

- **Symptoms:** Progressive exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND), easy fatigability, and peripheral edema.
- **Signs:**
    - **Pulse & BP:** Low-volume pulse, narrow pulse pressure, sinus tachycardia.
    - **Precordium:** Leftward displaced, diffuse, hypodynamic apex beat.
    - **Auscultation:** **Third Heart Sound (S₃ gallop)** reflecting volume overload, functional soft pansystolic murmur of Mitral or Tricuspid Regurgitation.
    - **Systemic Stigmata:** Elevated JVP, hepatomegaly, ascites, bi-basal lung crackles, and bilateral pitting pedal edema.

### B. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM / HOCM)

- **Symptoms:** Effort-related angina pectoris, exertional dyspnea, exertional presyncope/syncope, palpitations, and **Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)**.
- **Signs:**
    - **Arterial Pulse:** **Jerky / Bisferiens pulse** (rapid upstroke followed by secondary wave due to mid-systolic outflow obstruction).
    - **Precordium:** Double or triple apical impulse (palpable S₄ heart sound due to forceful atrial contraction against stiff LV).
    - **Auscultation:** Harsh mid-systolic ejection murmur best heard at left sternal edge (radiates to base, **NOT carotids**), plus apical pansystolic murmur of MR.
    - **Dynamic Maneuvers [⭐ High-Yield]:**
        - _Increases Murmur (↑ Obstruction):_ Standing up, Valsalva strain phase, Sublingual GTN (reduces preload/LV volume).
        - _Decreases Murmur (↓ Obstruction):_ Squatting, handgrip (increases afterload/preload).

### C. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)

- **Symptoms:** Severe right-sided heart failure symptoms out of proportion to left-sided symptoms: abdominal distension, painful hepatic swelling, peripheral edema, fatigue.
- **Signs:** Markedly elevated JVP with prominent 'a' and 'v' waves, **Kussmaul's Sign** (paradoxical rise in JVP during inspiration), S₄/S₃ gallop, hepatomegaly with ascites, and marked dependent pedal edema.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **DCM:** Sinus tachycardia, non-specific ST-T wave changes, Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB), atrial fibrillation, and low QRS voltage.
- **HCM:** Bizarre Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) voltage criteria, deep symmetrical T-wave inversions (precordial leads V₃–V₆), and "dagger-like" Q waves in inferior/lateral leads.
- **RCM:** Low voltage QRS complexes (despite thick ventricular walls on echo in amyloidosis), atrial fibrillation, conduction blocks.
- **ARVC:** T-wave inversion in V₁–V₃, broadened QRS, and pathognomonic **Epsilon waves** (notch at the end of QRS complex).

### B. Echocardiography (Investigation of Choice) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **DCM:** Demonstrates four-chamber cardiac enlargement, thinned ventricular walls, global hypokinesia with severely depressed LVEF (<40%), functional mitral/tricuspid regurgitation, and apical mural thrombi. (See **Diagram 1**).
- **HCM:** Demonstrates Asymmetric Septal Hypertrophy (interventricular septum to LV free wall ratio >1.3:1), subaortic narrow outflow tract, **Systolic Anterior Motion (SAM)** of anterior mitral leaflet, and dynamic gradient on Doppler.
- **RCM:** Demonstrates non-dilated, rigid ventricles with normal wall thickness (or granular sparkling appearance in amyloidosis), preserved LVEF (50–70%), and **massive bi-atrial enlargement**.

### C. Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (cMRI)

- **Gold Standard for Tissue Characterization:**
    - **DCM:** Quantifies precise volumes and late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) showing mid-wall interstitial fibrosis.
    - **HCM:** Identifies apical hypertrophy and patchy LGE at septal insertion points.
    - **ARVC:** Excellent visualization of RV fatty infiltration and focal wall motion aneurysms.
    - **RCM / Amyloidosis:** Subendocardial diffuse late gadolinium enhancement pathognomonic for amyloid infiltration.

### D. Endomyocardial Biopsy

- Indicated when specific infiltrative or inflammatory etiologies are suspected (e.g., Amyloidosis showing Congo Red stain positive apple-green birefringence under polarized light, Sarcoidosis non-caseating granulomas, or Myocarditis lymphocytic infiltrate).

## 8. Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) Management

Management is aligned with standard HFrEF guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT):

1. **Quadruple Disease-Modifying Therapy:**
    - **ACE Inhibitors / ARNI:** Enalapril / Sacubitril-Valsartan (reduces mortality and halts remodeling).
    - **Beta-Blockers:** Bisoprolol, Metoprolol SR, or Carvedilol (reduces mortality by 35% in DCM; start low, titrate slowly in stable compensated states).
    - **Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA):** Spironolactone or Eplerenone.
    - **SGLT2 Inhibitors:** Dapagliflozin or Empagliflozin 10 mg PO OD.
2. **Symptomatic Relief:** Loop diuretics (Furosemide 20–80 mg PO OD) for volume overload.
3. **Anticoagulation:** Warfarin or DOACs indicated if atrial fibrillation or documented intracardiac mural thrombus is present.
4. **Device & Advanced Interventions:**
    - **Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator (ICD):** Primary prevention of sudden cardiac death in LVEF ≤35% despite 3 months of optimal GDMT.
    - **Cardiac Resynchronisation Therapy (CRT-P / CRT-D):** Indicated for patients with LVEF ≤35%, sinus rhythm, and LBBB with QRS duration >130–150 ms.
    - **Cardiac Transplantation / LVAD:** End-stage refractory Class IV heart failure.

---

### B. Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM / HOCM) Management

1. **First-Line Medical Therapy:**
    - **Beta-Blockers (Bisoprolol / Metoprolol SR):** Drug of choice; slows heart rate, prolongs diastole, decreases contractility, and reduces dynamic LVOT gradient.
    - **Rate-Limiting CCBs (Verapamil / Diltiazem):** Alternative if beta-blockers are poorly tolerated or contraindicated.
    - **Disopyramide (Class Ia Antiarrhythmic):** Added for refractory symptoms due to its potent negative inotropic action.
    - **Amiodarone:** Used for atrial or ventricular arrhythmias.
2. **Septal Reduction Therapy (For Refractory LVOT Gradient ≥50 mmHg):**
    - **Surgical Myectomy (Morrow Procedure):** Excision of subaortic hypertrophied septal muscle.
    - **Alcohol Septal Ablation:** Catheter-based infusion of absolute alcohol into first septal perforator artery to induce localized therapeutic septal infarction.
3. **Primary Prevention of Sudden Death:** ICD placement in high-risk patients (prior cardiac arrest, family history of sudden death, unexplained syncope, septal thickness ≥30 mm).
4. **STRICT CONTRAINDICATIONS IN HOCM [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Digoxin:** Positive inotropic action worsens LVOT obstruction.
    - **Organic Nitrates & Vasodilators:** Venodilation decreases LV volume, aggravating obstruction.
    - **Diuretics (in high doses):** Hypovolemia collapses LV chamber, worsening obstruction.

---

### C. Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM) Management

1. **Targeted Etiological Therapy:**
    - **Tafamidis:** Transthyretin stabilizer for ATTR cardiac amyloidosis.
    - **Corticosteroids / Immunosuppressants:** For cardiac sarcoidosis.
    - **Phlebotomy / Chelation (Deferoxamine):** For cardiac hemochromatosis.
2. **Symptomatic Relief:** Cautious use of loop diuretics (Furosemide) and spironolactone to control systemic edema without causing severe hypotension.
3. **Anticoagulation:** Mandatory if atrial fibrillation is present.

---

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Refractory Congestive Heart Failure:** End-stage pump failure requiring heart transplantation.
2. **Malignant Ventricular Arrhythmias & Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD):** Leading cause of sudden death in young competitive athletes with HCM.
3. **Thromboembolism & Stroke:** Stasis of blood in dilated or restrictive chambers and AF leads to mural thrombi and embolic stroke.
4. **Infective Endocarditis:** Risk elevated in HOCM due to turbulent subaortic blood flow.

---

## 10. Prognosis

- **DCM:** 5-year mortality is ~50% without modern GDMT; dramatically improved by ARNI, beta-blockers, CRT, and ICD therapy.
- **HCM:** Overall annual mortality is 1–2% in adults; high-risk individuals require primary ICD protection.
- **RCM:** Generally poor prognosis (50% 2-year mortality in endomyocardial fibrosis and advanced amyloidosis).

## 11. Dosage Rule (Core Drugs in Cardiomyopathy)

### 1. Bisoprolol (Beta-1 Selective Blocker)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 179, 198; Davidson p. 22). Selective competitive antagonist of β₁-adrenoceptors in myocardium. Reduces heart rate, decreases myocardial contractility, prolongs diastolic filling time, and suppresses sympathetic overdrive-induced myocyte apoptosis and remodeling.
    
    ```
    β₁-Adrenoceptor Blockade → ↓ SA Node Rate + ↓ Contractility → ↑ Diastolic Perfusion Time + ↓ Remodeling
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Cardiac β₁-adrenoceptors.
- **Dose:**
    - _DCM (Heart Failure):_ Initial 1.25 mg PO OD; slowly titrated every 2–4 weeks to target 10 mg PO OD.
    - _HCM:_ 2.5–10 mg PO OD.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Features:** Mortality-reducing drug in DCM; first-line antianginal/antiarrhythmic in HCM.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, bronchospasm, cold peripheries.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in stable, compensated DCM. **Contraindicated in acute decompensated heart failure, severe bradycardia (HR <50 bpm), and 2nd/3rd-degree AV block**.

### 2. Verapamil (Non-Dihydropyridine CCB)

- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 205, 206). Inhibits L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in vascular smooth muscle and cardiac myocytes. Decreases SA node automaticity, slows AV conduction, and decreases contractility.
- **Receptors / Target:** L-type Calcium Channels.
- **Dose:** 40–80 mg PO TDS (or 120–240 mg SR PO OD).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** 3 times daily (or once daily for SR).
- **Features:** First-line drug in HCM when beta-blockers are contraindicated.
- **Side Effects:** Constipation, bradycardia, AV block, ankle edema, severe negative inotropy.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Indicated in HCM. **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in severe systolic DCM / HFrEF**(precipitates severe pulmonary edema).

## 14. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In any young patient or athlete presenting with an unexplained **harsh mid-systolic ejection murmur at the left sternal border**, perform simple bedside dynamic maneuvers before ordering tests! Have the patient **squat** (increases venous return/preload and afterload), which widens the LV cavity and **causes the HOCM murmur to dramatically soften or disappear**. Then have the patient **stand up or perform a Valsalva maneuver**, which reduces preload and **causes the HOCM murmur to become intensely louder**!

## 15. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Prescribing **Digoxin, Sublingual GTN / Nitrates, or High-Dose Diuretics** to a patient with Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In HOCM, dynamic outflow obstruction depends on LV cavity volume and contractility.
    - **Digoxin** increases contractility, worsening septal impingement.
    - **Nitrates / Vasodilators** decrease afterload/preload, collapsing the LV chamber and worsening obstruction.
    - **Diuretics** decrease intravascular volume, exacerbating LVOT gradient.
    - **Resolution:** Digoxin, Nitrates, and Diuretics are **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED** in HOCM. The correct first-line drugs are **Beta-Blockers (Bisoprolol/Metoprolol)** or **Verapamil**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **DCM:** 90% of cases; four-chamber dilation, systolic failure (LVEF <40%), titin/dystrophin gene mutations, S₃ gallop; treated with GDMT (ARNI + Beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i) + ICD/CRT.
- **HCM:** 100% genetic (sarcomeric MYH7/MYBP-C mutations), asymmetric septal hypertrophy, diastolic failure, SAM of mitral valve, jerky pulse, S₄; treated with Beta-blockers/Verapamil, septal myectomy; **Nitrates & Digoxin Contraindicated**.
- **RCM:** Least common; rigid myocardium, severe diastolic failure, preserved LVEF (50–70%), bi-atrial enlargement, Kussmaul sign; commonest cause Amyloidosis.
- **ARVC:** Fibrofatty replacement of RV, desmosomal PKP-2 mutation, Epsilon wave on ECG, VT risk.

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **Cardiomyopathies are intrinsic myocardial disorders classified functionally into dilated, hypertrophic, and restrictive patterns, where specific structural, sarcomeric, or cytoskeletal gene mutations drive distinct systolic or diastolic heart failure syndromes and sudden arrhythmic death.**
