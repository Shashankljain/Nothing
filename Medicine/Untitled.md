# Medical Management of Acute Variceal Bleeding

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Acute Variceal Bleeding** is a catastrophic, life-threatening upper gastrointestinal medical emergency resulting from the spontaneous rupture of dilated submucosal/subepithelial esophagogastric varices, driven by severe portal hypertension with a **Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) ≥12 mmHg** (normal physiological HVPG: 1 to 5 mmHg) [Robbins 3 p. 298, 299, Kdt 3 p. 643].

---

## 2. Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### Underlying Etiology (Causes of Portal Hypertension)

1. **Intra-Hepatic Sinusoidal Causes (~90% of cases):** **Cirrhosis of the liver** (Alcoholic, Chronic Hepatitis B/C, NASH/NAFLD, Wilson's disease, Hemochromatosis) [Robbins 3 p. 298].
2. **Pre-Hepatic Causes:** Portal vein thrombosis (PVT), splenic vein thrombosis (secondary to acute/chronic pancreatitis or pancreatic cancer), massive splenomegaly.
3. **Post-Hepatic Causes:** Budd-Chiari syndrome, IVC web/obstruction, constrictive pericarditis.

### High-Yield Risk Factors for Variceal Rupture [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Variceal Size & Appearance:** Large diameter (>5 mm), tortuous varices with endoscopic **Red wale marks**, cherry-red spots, or hematocystic spots [Robbins 3 p. 299].
- **Hemodynamic Threshold:** HVPG ≥12 mmHg [Robbins 3 p. 298, 299].
- **Hepatic Decompensation:** Advanced Child-Pugh Class B or C disease (elevated bilirubin, low albumin, severe ascites) [Robbins 3 p. 298, 299].

---

## 3. Classification

Esophagogastric varices are classified anatomically by endoscopic appearance:

|Category|Anatomical Location|Bleeding Frequency|Management Approach|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Esophageal Varices (Grade I to IV)**|Submucosa of distal 1/3rd of esophagus|**Most Common (~85%)**|Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL) + Vasoactive drugs|
|**Gastroesophageal Varices Type 1 (GOV1)**|Extension of esophageal varices along lesser curve|Common|Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL)|
|**Gastroesophageal Varices Type 2 (GOV2)**|Extension of esophageal varices toward fundus|High severity|Cyanoacrylate Tissue Adhesive Glue Injection|
|**Isolated Gastric Varices (IGV1/IGV2)**|Isolated fundic varices|Less common, severe hemorrhage|Cyanoacrylate Glue / BRTO / TIPS|

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Portal Hypertension & Shunting:** Increased intrahepatic vascular resistance (fibrous scarring and sinusoidal endothelial dysfunction) forces portal blood into low-resistance collateral channels connecting the left gastric (coronary) vein to azygos and intercostal veins at the gastroesophageal junction [Robbins 3 p. 298].
2. **Submucosal Venous Congestion:** High pressure distends thin-walled esophageal submucosal veins, causing progressive variceal dilatation [Robbins 3 p. 298].
3. **Critical Wall Tension & Rupture:** When **HVPG reaches ≥12 mmHg**, vessel wall tension exceeds bursting threshold (Laplace Law: Tension = Pressure × Radius ÷ Wall Thickness), triggering explosive, painless bleeding into the esophageal lumen [Robbins 3 p. 298, 299].

## 5. Clinical Features

### A. Symptoms

- **Massive Hematemesis:** Sudden vomiting of dark red blood or "coffee-ground" material without preceding pain or trauma [Robbins 3 p. 298].
- **Melena:** Passage of sticky, jet-black, tarry stools secondary to blood digestion by intestinal bacteria.
- **Hemodynamic Symptoms:** Postural dizziness, extreme weakness, thirst, confusion, diaphoresis, and syncope [Davidson 1 p. 7, 8].

### B. Physical Signs

- **Hypovolemic Shock:** Tachycardia (pulse >100 beats/min), hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg), cold clammy extremities, prolonged capillary refill (>2 seconds), and oliguria [Davidson 1 p. 7, 8, 20].
- **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease:** Jaundice, spider angiomas, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, ascites, congestive splenomegaly, caput medusae, and flapping tremor (asterixis) [Robbins 3 p. 298].

---

## 6. Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Emergent Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (UGIE) [⭐ Gold Standard]:**
    - Performed within 12 hours of admission once hemodynamically stabilized.
    - Confirms active variceal spurting/oozing, presence of an adherent blood clot, or red wale marks over varices, while ruling out non-variceal causes (peptic ulcer, Mallory-Weiss tear) [Davidson 1 p. 91, Robbins 3 p. 298].
2. **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Serial hemoglobin and hematocrit; **Thrombocytopenia (<100,000/μL)** signals hypersplenism [Robbins 3 p. 298].
3. **Liver Function Tests (LFTs) & Coagulation Profile:** Decreased serum albumin, elevated serum bilirubin, and prolonged Prothrombin Time / INR (>1.5) reflecting liver failure [Robbins 3 p. 298].
4. **Blood Grouping & Cross-Matching:** Mandatory for packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusions.
5. **Renal Function Tests & Serum Electrolytes:** Monitors for acute kidney injury and Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS).

### A. Immediate Resuscitation & Stabilization Protocols

1. **Airway Management:** Secure airway with endotracheal intubation in obtunded patients or those with severe hematemesis to prevent aspiration pneumonia [Davidson 1 p. 15].
2. **Hemodynamic Resuscitation:** Establish two wide-bore (16G or 18G) peripheral IV lines; infuse isotonic crystalloids (0.9% Normal Saline) to restore intravascular volume [Davidson 1 p. 20, 23].
3. **Restrictive Blood Transfusion Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]:** Transfuse packed RBCs strictly targeting a **restrictive Hemoglobin threshold of 7.0 to 8.0 g/dL**. _Liberal transfusion (Hb >9–10 g/dL) increases portal venous pressure and directly precipitates variceal rebleeding!_
4. **Prophylactic Antibiotic Therapy:** Administer **IV Ceftriaxone 1 g once daily** for 5 to 7 days. Reduces bacterial translocation, risk of SBP, rebleeding rates, and overall mortality [Davidson 1 p. 2].

---

### B. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Terlipressin [⭐ First-Line Gold Standard]

- **Name:** Terlipressin.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 643). Synthetic prodrug of vasopressin (8-lysine vasopressin). Slowly converted in vivo by endothelial peptidases to lysine vasopressin. Stimulates **vascular V₁ receptors** on splanchnic vascular smooth muscle, causing selective, potent splanchnic arteriolar vasoconstriction. This reduces splanchnic blood inflow into the portal system, lowers portal venous pressure, and promotes hemostasis.
    
    ```
    Terlipressin → Cleaved to Vasopressin → Splanchnic V1 Activation → Splanchnic Vasoconstriction → ↓ Portal Inflow & Pressure
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Vasopressin V₁ receptors.
- **Dose:** **2 mg IV bolus every 4 hours** initially for 24 to 48 hours; once bleeding is controlled, reduce dose to **1 mg IV every 4 hours** for a total duration of 2 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) bolus.
- **Frequency:** Every 4 hours.
- **Features:** First-line vasoactive agent; **the only drug proven to reduce overall mortality** in acute variceal bleeding [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Side Effects:** Abdominal cramps, diarrhea, hyponatremia, peripheral tissue ischemia, hypertension, bradycardia, myocardial ischemia [Kdt 3 p. 644].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon suspicion of acute variceal bleed; stop 24 to 48 hours after complete hemostasis (max 5 days).

#### 2. Octreotide

- **Name:** Octreotide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 260, 643, 644). Synthetic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin. Binds to **somatostatin SSTR2 and SSTR5 receptors** on splanchnic vessels, inhibiting the release of endogenous vasodilatory peptides (such as glucagon and VIP). Induces selective splanchnic vasoconstriction, reducing portal vein pressure.
    
    ```
    Octreotide → Somatostatin SSTR2/SSTR5 Binding → Inhibits Glucagon & VIP → Selective Splanchnic Vasoconstriction → ↓ Portal Pressure
    ```
    
- **Receptors:** Somatostatin SSTR2 and SSTR5 receptors.
- **Dose:** **50 μg IV rapid bolus**, followed immediately by a **continuous IV infusion of 25 to 50 μg/hour** for 2 to 5 days.
- **Dosage / Route:** IV bolus followed by continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency:** Continuous infusion over 48 to 120 hours.
- **Features:** Excellent safety profile; preferred alternative when Terlipressin is unavailable or contraindicated (e.g., ischemic heart disease) [Kdt 3 p. 260, 644].
- **Side Effects:** Nausea, abdominal cramps, transient hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia, gallstones (on long-term use) [Kdt 3 p. 260].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start at presentation; maintain for 2 to 5 days.

#### 3. Propranolol (Secondary Prophylaxis — Start AFTER Recovery) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Propranolol.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 157, Kdt 3 p. 585). Non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker. Dual mechanism: **β₁-blockade** reduces cardiac output; **β₂-blockade** blocks splanchnic vasodilation, allowing **unopposed α₁-adrenergic vasoconstriction** of splanchnic arterioles, reducing portal inflow and keeping HVPG <10 mmHg.
- **Dose:** **20 mg to 40 mg PO BD**, titrated every 3 to 5 days to achieve a resting heart rate of **55 to 60 beats/min**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral (PO).
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (BD).
- **Features:** Gold-standard oral drug for long-term secondary prophylaxis to prevent rebleeding.
- **Side Effects:** Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, bronchospasm, cold extremities.
- **When to Start / Stop:** **DO NOT start during acute active bleeding!** Initiate 5 to 7 days after acute bleeding has completely resolved; continue **indefinitely**.

---

### C. Endoscopic & Rescue Interventions

1. **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL / Banding) [⭐ Gold Standard]:**
    - Performed during UGIE within 12 hours. Elastic rubber bands are deployed around variceal trunks, inducing ischemic thrombosis, sloughing, and fibrous obliteration [Davidson 1 p. 91, Robbins 3 p. 298, 299].
2. **Balloon Tamponade (Bridge Therapy):**
    - **Sengstaken-Blakemore (S-B) Tube:** Double-balloon catheter (gastric balloon inflated with 200–250 mL air; esophageal balloon inflated to 30–45 mmHg pressure).
    - Provides direct mechanical compression over bleeding varices.
    - _Precaution:_ Temporary bridge therapy only; **do not leave inflated >24 hours** to avoid esophageal necrosis and perforation [Robbins 3 p. 298].
3. **Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt (TIPS) [Rescue Therapy]:**
    - Percutaneous expandable metal stent placed between hepatic vein and intrahepatic portal vein branch, decompressing portal pressure [Robbins 3 p. 298]. Indicated for refractory bleeding despite pharmacotherapy + EVL.

## 9. Complications

1. **Hypovolemic Shock & Cardiac Arrest:** From rapid blood loss [Davidson 1 p. 7, 20].
2. **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** Digestion of blood proteins in the gut surges systemic ammonia (NH₃) levels [Robbins 3 p. 298].
3. **Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) & Sepsis:** Triggered by bacterial translocation.
4. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS):** Acute oliguric renal failure secondary to extreme splanchnic vasodilation.
5. **Aspiration Pneumonia:** Secondary to vomiting blood with altered sensorium [Davidson 1 p. 15].

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Acute variceal hemorrhage carries a **15% to 20% 6-week mortality rate** [Robbins 3 p. 299].
- Over **50% of untreated survivors experience recurrent hemorrhage within 1 year** [Robbins 3 p. 299].

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** When resuscitating a patient with acute variceal bleeding, **NEVER over-transfuse to achieve a normal hemoglobin level (>9–10 g/dL)**! Over-transfusing with PRBCs or aggressive crystalloids expands intravascular volume, sharply raises central venous and portal pressures, and directly breaks fresh hemostatic clots—precipitating catastrophic, fatal **rebleeding of esophageal varices** [Robbins 3 p. 299]! Always enforce a **restrictive transfusion strategy targeting a Hemoglobin of 7.0 to 8.0 g/dL**!

---

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Administering **Propranolol** or other Beta-Blockers during the **ACUTE active bleeding episode** of esophageal varices.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Beta-blockers reduce cardiac output and blood pressure. Administering them during acute ongoing hemorrhage worsens hypovolemic shock and blunts compensatory tachycardia.
- **Resolution:** **Propranolol is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED during acute active bleeding!** Management during acute bleeding requires **Vasoactive Vasoconstrictors (Terlipressin / Octreotide)** and **EVL**. Propranolol is initiated ONLY for secondary prophylaxis **5 to 7 days AFTER acute bleeding has completely resolved** [Kdt 1 p. 157, Kdt 3 p. 585].

## 15. Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Life-threatening upper GI hemorrhage from ruptured submucosal esophageal varices (HVPG ≥12 mmHg) [Robbins 3 p. 298, 299].
- **First-Line Vasoactive Drug:** **Terlipressin** (2 mg IV q4h) — only drug proven to reduce mortality in variceal bleeding [Kdt 3 p. 643].
- **Gold Standard Endoscopic Treatment:** **Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL)** within 12 hours [Davidson 1 p. 91, Robbins 3 p. 298].
- **Transfusion Threshold:** Restrictive target Hb of **7.0 to 8.0 g/dL** to prevent portal pressure spikes [Robbins 3 p. 299].
- **Prophylaxis Rule:** Propranolol is for **secondary prophylaxis AFTER recovery**, never during acute bleeding [Kdt 1 p. 157, Kdt 3 p. 585].
