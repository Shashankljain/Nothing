# Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)** is an acute, non-surgical bacterial infection of pre-existing ascitic fluid occurring in patients with advanced liver cirrhosis or nephrotic syndrome, developing in the absence of any identifiable intra-abdominal, visceral, or localized surgical source of infection (such as hollow organ perforation, appendicitis, or intra-abdominal abscess) [Robbins 3 p. 308, Davidson 1 p. 38, Marrow Vol 3 p. 758].

- **Diagnostic Cut-off:** Defined by an ascitic fluid **Absolute Polymorphonuclear (PMN) Leukocyte Count ≥250 cells/μL** (0.25 × 10⁹/L), typically accompanied by a monomicrobial bacterial culture [Robbins 3 p. 308].

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Causative Microorganisms [⭐ High-Yield]

Over 70% to 80% of SBP episodes are caused by monomicrobial Gram-negative enteric bacilli originating from the gut lumen [Robbins 3 p. 308]:

1. **Escherichia coli (40% to 50% of cases):** Most common pathogen [Robbins 3 p. 308].
2. **Klebsiella pneumoniae (15% to 20% of cases)** [Robbins 3 p. 308].
3. **Streptococcus pneumoniae & Enterococcus faecalis (10% to 20%):** Gram-positive cocci [Robbins 3 p. 38, 308].
4. **Other Enterobacteriaceae:** _Enterobacter_, _Proteus_ species, and _Pseudomonas_ species [Robbins 3 p. 308].

### B. High-Yield Risk Factors

- **Low Ascitic Fluid Total Protein (<1.0 to 1.5 g/dL):** Reduced complement levels (C3/C4) and impaired local ascitic opsonic activity [Robbins 3 p. 310].
- **Advanced Liver Cirrhosis (Child-Pugh Class C / High MELD Score):** Severe hepatic synthetic failure [Robbins 3 p. 308].
- **Acute Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage:** Promotes bacterial translocation from ischemic gut mucosa.
- **Prior Episode of SBP:** Recurrence rate reaches ~70% within 1 year without continuous antibiotic prophylaxis.
- **Hyperbilirubinemia (Serum Bilirubin >3 mg/dL) & Malnutrition.**

---

## 3. Classification of Infected Ascites [⭐ High-Yield]

|Clinical Variant|Ascitic PMN Count|Ascitic Culture Result|Clinical Significance & Management|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (Classic SBP)**|**≥250 cells/μL**|**Positive (Monomicrobial)**|Treat immediately with IV 3rd Gen Cephalosporin + IV Albumin|
|**Culture-Negative Neutrocytic Ascites (CNNA)**|**≥250 cells/μL**|**Negative**|**Treat identically to SBP** (30–40% of cases; high mortality if untreated)|
|**Monomicrobial Non-neutrocytic Bacterascites (MNB)**|**<250 cells/μL**|**Positive (Monomicrobial)**|Early bacterial colonization; re-tap or treat if symptomatic|
|**Secondary Bacterial Peritonitis**|**≥250 cells/μL**|**Polymicrobial (Multiple species)**|Surgical emergency (perforated viscus); high protein >1 g/dL & low glucose <50 mg/dL|

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth & Altered Permeability:** Splanchnic venous congestion, intestinal dysbiosis, and mucosal edema in portal hypertension promote excessive proliferation of enteric bacteria [Robbins 3 p. 310].
2. **Bacterial Translocation:** Viable enteric bacteria pass across the damaged intestinal mucosal barrier into mesenteric lymph nodes [Robbins 3 p. 308, 310].
3. **Impaired Systemic Clearance & Sustained Bacteremia:** Reticuloendothelial (Kupffer cell) phagocytic dysfunction and portosystemic shunting allow translocated bacteria to enter systemic circulation, escaping hepatic clearance [Robbins 3 p. 310].
4. **Ascitic Fluid Seeding:** Circulating bacteria seed the nutrient-rich peritoneal cavity.
5. **Deficient Ascitic Opsonization & Neutrophil Influx:** Low ascitic protein (<1.0 g/dL) provides insufficient C3/C4 complement opsonins, preventing effective macrophage phagocytosis. Bacteria multiply, attracting a massive influx of neutrophils (**PMN ≥250 cells/μL**) [Robbins 3 p. 308, 310].

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Fever & Chills (80% of cases):** Sudden or insidious onset of low-grade to high-grade fever with rigors [Robbins 3 p. 308].
- **Abdominal Pain & Discomfort (70%):** Continuous, diffuse abdominal ache or worsening abdominal distension.
- **Acute Decompensation Signs:** Rapid onset of confusion, lethargy, or altered mental status secondary to **Worsening Hepatic Encephalopathy** [Robbins 3 p. 310].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, paralytic ileus, or anorexia.
- _Note:_ Up to **10% to 30% of patients are completely asymptomatic**, presenting solely with unexplained renal deterioration or encephalopathy.

---

### B. Physical Signs

1. **Abdominal Tenderness:** Diffuse, mild-to-moderate abdominal tenderness to palpation.
2. **Rebound Tenderness & Guarding:** Often mild or absent because underlying ascitic fluid separates the parietal and visceral peritoneal surfaces.
3. **Fever (>37.8 °C) & Tachycardia.**
4. **Hypotension & Septic Shock:** Cold clammy extremities and low BP (<90/60 mmHg).
5. **Signs of Hepatic Failure & Encephalopathy:** Jaundice, asterixis ("flapping tremor"), oliguria, and ascites.

### A. Diagnostic Abdominal Paracentesis [⭐ Gold Standard]

Must be performed in **ALL cirrhotic patients admitted with ascites**, or experiencing fever, abdominal pain, encephalopathy, or renal deterioration before starting antibiotics [Robbins 3 p. 308].

1. **Ascitic Fluid Absolute PMN Count [⭐ Diagnostic Threshold]:** \[\text{Absolute PMN Count} = \text{Total Ascitic WBC Count} \times \frac{\text{% Neutrophils}}{100}\]
    - **PMN ≥250 cells/μL (0.25 × 10⁹/L):** Diagnostic of SBP (or CNNA) regardless of symptoms.
2. **Bedside Inoculation into Blood Culture Bottles:** Injecting 10 mL ascitic fluid directly into aerobic and anaerobic blood culture bottles at the bedside increases culture yield from 40% to **>80–90%** [Davidson 1 p. 225].
3. **Ascitic Biochemical Profile (Runyon's Criteria for Differentiating Secondary Peritonitis):**
    - _Ascitic Total Protein:_ Usually <1.0 to 1.5 g/dL in SBP.
    - _Ascitic Glucose:_ >50 mg/dL in SBP (markedly reduced <50 mg/dL in secondary peritonitis).
    - _Ascitic LDH:_ Normal in SBP (elevated above serum upper limit in secondary peritonitis).
    - _SAAG:_ ≥1.1 g/dL (underlying portal hypertension) [Robbins 3 p. 310].

---

### B. Systemic & Supporting Investigations

- **Blood Cultures:** Positive in 30% to 50% of SBP cases (confirming systemic bacteremia).
- **Renal Function Tests (BUN & Creatinine):** Essential to screen for impending Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI).
- **Leukocytosis:** Elevated peripheral WBC count with left shift.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Ceftriaxone (First-Line Empirical Antibiotic) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Name:** Ceftriaxone.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 778, 781). Third-generation cephalosporin. Binds to **Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs)** on bacterial cell membranes, inhibiting transpeptidation during peptidoglycan cell wall synthesis. Leads to bacterial cell wall autolysis and rapid bactericidal destruction of Gram-negative enteric bacilli (_E. coli_, _Klebsiella_).
    
    ```
    Ceftriaxone → Inhibits Transpeptidase PBP Binding → Blocked Peptidoglycan Cross-linking → Cell Wall Lysis & Bactericidal Death
    ```
    
- **Target / Receptors:** Bacterial Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs).
- **Dose:** **2 g IV once daily (OD)** (or 1 to 2 g IV 12-hourly for severe sepsis).
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous (IV) slow injection or infusion.
- **Frequency:** Once daily (OD) or 12-hourly.
- **Features:** Drug of choice for empirical treatment of SBP; long plasma half-life (8 hours); excellent peritoneal fluid and biliary penetration [Kdt 3 p. 778].
- **Side Effects:** Hypersensitivity rashes, diarrhea, _C. difficile_ colitis, pseudolithiasis/biliary sludging, transient eosinophilia [Kdt 3 p. 780].
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start immediately upon obtaining ascitic fluid with PMN ≥250 cells/μL; continue for **5 to 7 days** until fever and PMN count normalize.

#### 2. Cefotaxime (Alternative 3rd Generation Cephalosporin)

- **Name:** Cefotaxime.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 777, 778). Third-generation cephalosporin; inhibits bacterial peptidoglycan wall synthesis via PBP binding.
- **Dose:** **2 g IV every 8 hours (TID)**.
- **Dosage / Route:** Intravenous.
- **Frequency:** Every 8 hours.
- **Features:** High peritoneal penetration; well-studied classic regimen for SBP [Kdt 3 p. 777].
- **Side Effects:** Rashes, diarrhea, elevated transaminases, thrombophlebitis.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Given for 5 to 7 days.

#### 3. Human Albumin 20% (Co-Therapy for Prevention of HRS) [⭐ Critical Co-Therapy]

- **Name:** Human Albumin (20% Solution).
- **Mechanism of Action:** Expands effective arterial blood volume, scavenges circulating nitric oxide and cytokines, and maintains renal perfusion.
- **Dose Protocol:**
    - **Day 1:** **1.5 g/kg IV** administered within 6 hours of SBP diagnosis.
    - **Day 3:** **1.0 g/kg IV**.
- **Features:** Reduces the incidence of **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)** from 30% to 10% and overall hospital mortality from 30% to 10% (especially beneficial if serum bilirubin >3 mg/dL or serum creatinine >1.0 mg/dL).

---

### B. Prophylactic Regimens (Primary & Secondary Prophylaxis) [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Secondary Prophylaxis (Post-SBP Recovery):**
    - **Norfloxacin:** **400 mg PO once daily (OD)** OR **Ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO OD** administered **indefinitely** (or until liver transplantation).
2. **Primary Prophylaxis Indications:**
    - _Acute GI Hemorrhage in Cirrhosis:_ **IV Ceftriaxone 1 g daily for 7 days** (or oral Norfloxacin 400 mg BD).
    - _Low Ascitic Protein (<1.5 g/dL) + Advanced Liver Failure (Child-Pugh ≥9 or Serum Creatinine ≥1.2 mg/dL)._

## 9. Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS-AKI):** Severe oliguric functional renal failure triggered by systemic sepsis and renal vasoconstriction (occurs in ~30% of SBP cases) [Robbins 3 p. 308].
2. **Septic Shock & Multiorgan Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS):** Refractory hypotension and lactic acidosis [Robbins 3 p. 308].
3. **Worsening Hepatic Encephalopathy & Coma:** Nitrogenous load from infection precipitates brain edema [Robbins 3 p. 308].
4. **High Recurrence Rate:** ~70% 1-year recurrence rate without daily fluoroquinolone prophylaxis.

---

## 10. Prognosis

- Hospital mortality for acute SBP ranges from **20% to 30%** per episode despite appropriate antibiotic therapy [Robbins 3 p. 308].
- Development of SBP signifies end-stage decompensated liver disease; 1-year mortality exceeds **50% to 70%**, making SBP a major indication for **Liver Transplantation evaluation**.

## 13. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** In patients diagnosed with Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis, **always administer Intravenous Human Albumin 20% (1.5 g/kg on Day 1 and 1.0 g/kg on Day 3)** alongside IV Ceftriaxone! IV Albumin expands effective arterial blood volume and scavenges circulating nitric oxide, reducing the incidence of **Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)** from 30% down to 10% and cutting overall hospital mortality by more than half!

## 14. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Withholding empirical IV antibiotics in a jaundiced patient whose ascitic fluid PMN count is **≥250 cells/μL**, simply because the initial ascitic Gram stain or culture is negative.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** Ascitic fluid Gram stain is positive in <30% of cases, and initial cultures take 48 to 72 hours to yield growth. **Culture-Negative Neutrocytic Ascites (CNNA)** carries the same mortality rate as classic SBP.
- **Resolution:** **Never wait for culture results!** An ascitic fluid **PMN count ≥250 cells/μL is an absolute indication to initiate empirical IV Ceftriaxone immediately** [Robbins 3 p. 308]!

---

### Quick Revision

- **Diagnostic Threshold:** Ascitic fluid **PMN count ≥250 cells/μL** (0.25 × 10⁹/L) [Robbins 3 p. 308].
- **Most Common Organism:** **Escherichia coli** (Gram-negative enteric bacillus) [Robbins 3 p. 308].
- **First-Line Empirical Antibiotic:** **Ceftriaxone 2 g IV once daily** for 5 to 7 days [Kdt 3 p. 778].
- **HRS Prevention:** **IV Albumin 20%** (1.5 g/kg on Day 1 + 1.0 g/kg on Day 3).
- **Secondary Prophylaxis:** **Norfloxacin 400 mg PO OD** (or Ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO OD) continuous long-term.
