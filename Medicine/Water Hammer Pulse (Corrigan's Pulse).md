# Water Hammer Pulse (Corrigan's Pulse)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **Water Hammer Pulse** (also known as _Corrigan's pulse_ or _collapsing pulse_) is a bounding arterial pulse characterized by a rapid, forceful upstroke followed by an immediate, precipitous diastolic collapse. It is classically demonstrated by palpating the patient's radial or brachial artery with their arm elevated vertically above heart level.

---

## 2. Key High-Yield Features & Mechanisms [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemodynamic Mechanism:** Results from a large stroke volume ejected rapidly into the arterial system during systole, followed by rapid diastolic run-off of blood back into the Left Ventricle or into low-resistance peripheral beds.
- **Associated Blood Pressure Finding:** Characteristically accompanied by a **widened pulse pressure** (elevated systolic blood pressure with a markedly decreased or near-zero diastolic blood pressure).
- **Bedside Examination Technique:** Palpate the radial pulse with the flat of the palm/fingers and elevate the patient's arm above the heart; gravity accelerates diastolic drainage toward the heart, exaggerating the collapsing sensation.
- **Primary Valvular Cause:** **Chronic Severe Aortic Regurgitation** (the classic and most common structural cause).
- **Hyperdynamic Non-Valvular Causes:** High cardiac output and low peripheral resistance states, including **Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)**, severe **anaemia**, **thyrotoxicosis**, **fever**, **sepsis**, **arteriovenous fistulae**, and **pregnancy**.

---

## 3. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Elevating the patient's arm amplifies the water hammer quality because gravity increases blood run-off from the forearm vessels back toward the heart during diastole, making the rapid pulse collapse far more prominent to palpation!

---

## 4. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Expecting to find a water hammer pulse in **Acute Severe Aortic Regurgitation**.
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In acute AR, the left ventricle is non-compliant and cannot dilate quickly, causing left ventricular end-diastolic pressure to rise abruptly and cut off diastolic regurgitation early.
- **Resolution:** In acute AR, pulse pressure is normal or near-normal and the water hammer pulse is **ABSENT**; it is a feature of **Chronic Aortic Regurgitation**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Bounding pulse with rapid upstroke and steep diastolic collapse.
- **Elicitation:** Radial pulse felt with arm raised above heart level.
- **Key Valvular Cause:** Chronic Severe Aortic Regurgitation.
- **Hyperdynamic Causes:** Anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, PDA, AV fistula, sepsis, pregnancy.
- **Hemodynamic Requirement:** High stroke volume + rapid diastolic arterial run-off.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

> **A water hammer pulse is a bounding arterial pulse characterized by a rapid upstroke and immediate diastolic collapse caused by an increased stroke volume and rapid diastolic run-off, serving as a classic peripheral sign of chronic severe aortic regurgitation and hyperdynamic circulatory states.**
