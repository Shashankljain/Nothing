# Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)** is a congenital cardiac malformation characterized by an incomplete closure or defect in the interventricular septum, permitting free communication and shunting of blood between the left and right ventricles.

It is the **most common congenital heart defect (CHD)**, accounting for **30% to 42%** of all cardiac malformations and occurring in approximately **1 in 500 live births**.

---

## 2. Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Primary / Congenital Etiologies

- **Defective Embryogenesis:** Incomplete septation during 3 to 8 weeks of gestation due to failure of fusion between the embryonic membranous septum and muscular septum.
- **Chromosomal / Genetic Abnormalities:**
    - **Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21):** VSD accounts for ~32% of CHD in Down syndrome.
    - Other genetic syndromes: DiGeorge syndrome (22q11 deletion), Holt-Oram syndrome (TBX5 mutation), CHARGE syndrome, Trisomy 13, and Trisomy 18.
- **Maternal Environmental Factors:** Maternal alcohol misuse, gestational diabetes, intrauterine rubella infection, and exposure to teratogenic drugs (e.g., antiepileptic agents).

### B. Acquired Etiology

- **Post-Myocardial Infarction Septal Rupture:** Acute ischemic necrosis and rupture of the interventricular septum occurring 3 to 5 days post-MI (typically anteroseptal or inferior MI), leading to sudden hemodynamic deterioration and acute right heart failure.

### A. Anatomic Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Perimembranous VSD (~80% to 90%):** Located at the junction of the membranous and muscular septum near the septal leaflet of the tricuspid valve. Most common type.
2. **Muscular VSD (~10%):** Located within the trabecular muscular septum; may be single or multiple ("Swiss-cheese" septum). Exhibits the highest rate of spontaneous closure.
3. **Infundibular / Subarterial / Supracristal VSD (~5%):** Located in the right ventricular outflow tract beneath the pulmonary valve; associated with aortic valve prolapse and aortic regurgitation.
4. **Inflow / Atrioventricular Canal VSD:** Located posterior to the septal leaflet of the tricuspid valve, frequently associated with endocardial cushion defects.

### B. Hemodynamic Classification

- **Small VSD (_Maladie de Roger_):** Defect area <0.5 cm² (restrictive); produces a high pressure gradient with a loud murmur, but negligible hemodynamic disturbance and no pulmonary hypertension.
- **Moderate VSD:** Defect area 0.5 to 1.0 cm²; moderate left-to-right shunt with mild-to-moderate elevation of pulmonary artery pressure.
- **Large VSD:** Defect area >1.0 cm² (non-restrictive, approaching aortic orifice diameter); equalizes LV and RV pressures, producing a massive left-to-right shunt and high risk of early heart failure and Eisenmenger syndrome.

## 4. Pathophysiology (with Flowchart) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Pathogenetic Sequence

1. **Left-to-Right Shunting:** During ventricular systole, blood flows down the pressure gradient from the high-pressure Left Ventricle (120 mmHg) into the low-pressure Right Ventricle (25 mmHg).
2. **Pulmonary Overcirculation & Volume Overload:** The shunted blood increases total pulmonary blood flow (Qp:Qs >1.5:1), returning to the Left Atrium and Left Ventricle. This produces **Left Atrial and Left Ventricular volume overload and dilation**.
3. **Pulmonary Vascular Remodeling:** Prolonged exposure of the pulmonary circulation to high volume and high pressure stimulates medial hypertrophy, vasoconstriction, and intimal proliferation in small pulmonary arterioles (<1 mm).
4. **Eisenmenger Syndrome (Shunt Reversal):** As pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) rises to equal or exceed systemic vascular resistance (SVR), right-sided pressures surpass left-sided pressures. The left-to-right shunt **reverses to a right-to-left shunt**, delivering deoxygenated blood into the systemic circulation and causing severe central cyanosis.

## 5. Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Symptoms

- **Small VSD (_Maladie de Roger_):** Completely asymptomatic; detected incidentally during routine clinical examination as an isolated loud murmur.
- **Large VSD (Infantile Presentation):** Symptoms typically appear at **4 to 6 weeks of life** as pulmonary vascular resistance drops postnatally:
    - Poor feeding, prolonged feeding times, and profuse diaphoresis (sweating) over the forehead during feeding.
    - Tachypnoea, intercostal/subcostal retractions (indrawing of lower ribs on inspiration).
    - Failure to thrive and poor weight gain.
    - Recurrent lower respiratory tract infections (bronchopneumonia) due to pulmonary congestion.
- **Eisenmenger VSD (Adolescent / Adult Presentation):** Progressive exertional dyspnea, fatigue, central cyanosis, syncope, chest pain, and hemoptysis.

### B. Physical Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Precordial Inspection & Palpation:**
    - **Apex Beat:** Displaced laterally, hyperdynamic in character (LV volume overload).
    - **Parasternal Heave:** Left parasternal lift indicating Right Ventricular Hypertrophy.
    - **Systolic Thrill:** Palpable high-frequency thrill best felt at the 3rd or 4th left intercostal space at the lower left sternal border.
2. **Auscultation (Classic Murmur) [⭐ High-Yield]:**
    - **Pansystolic (Holosystolic) Murmur:** Loud, harsh, high-pitched pansystolic murmur heard best at the lower left sternal border (3rd/4th intercostal space), radiating widely over the entire precordium.
    - **Auscultatory Paradox:** Small VSDs produce a **very loud, harsh murmur** due to a high pressure gradient (_Maladie de Roger_); conversely, large VSDs with high PVR or Eisenmenger syndrome produce a **soft, short, or absent murmur** as pressures between ventricles equalize.
    - **Pulmonary Component (P2):** Accentuated, loud P2 sound indicating pulmonary hypertension.
    - **Apical Mid-Diastolic Flow Murmur:** Soft, low-pitched rumble heard at the apex in large left-to-right shunts (Qp:Qs >2:1) due to hyperdynamic blood flow across the mitral valve during early diastole.

## 6. Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

### A. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

- **Small VSD:** Completely normal ECG.
- **Moderate VSD:** Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (tall R waves in V5–V6) and Left Atrial Enlargement (P mitrale).
- **Large VSD:** Biventricular Hypertrophy; characterized by the **Katz-Wachtel Phenomenon** (large equiphasic/biphasic QRS complexes in mid-precordial leads V2–V4).
- **Eisenmenger VSD:** Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (tall R wave in V1, right axis deviation >110°).

### B. Chest X-Ray (PA View)

- **Cardiomegaly:** Enlargement of LA, LV, and RV.
- **Pulmonary Plethora:** Increased pulmonary vascular markings extending to the outer third of the lung fields, with a dilated main pulmonary artery segment.
- **Eisenmenger Stage:** "Pruning" of peripheral pulmonary vasculature with marked central hilar pulmonary artery enlargement.

### C. Echocardiography & Color Doppler (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **2D Echocardiography:** Directly visualizes the exact anatomical location (perimembranous, muscular, or subarterial), size, and number of septal defects.
- **Color Flow & Spectral Doppler:** Demonstrates high-velocity left-to-right turbulent jet across the defect; measures peak jet velocity to estimate RV systolic pressure via the modified Bernoulli equation (\(PG = 4v²\)).

### D. Cardiac Catheterization

- Indicated when non-invasive imaging is inconclusive or when pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) must be precisely calculated to assess surgical reversibility in borderline pulmonary hypertension.

### A. Pharmacotherapy & Dosage Protocols (Dosage Rules) [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Furosemide (Loop Diuretic)

- **Name:** Furosemide.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 627, 630). Inhibits Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2) in the thick ascending limb of Henle's loop → promotes diuresis → reduces intravascular volume and pulmonary congestion.
- **Dose:** **1 to 2 mg/kg/day PO/IV** divided in 1 to 2 doses.
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral or Intravenous.
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily.
- **Features:** First-line medical therapy for infants presenting with heart failure and tachypnea.
- **Side Effects:** Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, volume depletion, prerenal azotemia.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Initiate upon onset of congestive heart failure symptoms; titrate based on respiratory rate and liver size.

#### 2. Captopril (ACE Inhibitor)

- **Name:** Captopril.
- **Mechanism of Action:** (KDT Textbook p. 562). Inhibits Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE), reducing Angiotensin II synthesis → decreases systemic vascular resistance (afterload) → reduces left-to-right shunting and increases forward cardiac output.
- **Dose:** **0.1 to 0.5 mg/kg/dose PO** (titrated up to 1 mg/kg/dose).
- **Dosage / Route:** Oral.
- **Frequency:** Three times daily (TID).
- **Features:** Synergistic afterload reduction paired with loop diuretics in symptomatic infants.
- **Side Effects:** Hypotension, hyperkalemia, renal dysfunction, dry cough.
- **When to Start / Stop:** Start in infants with moderate-to-large shunts and heart failure prior to elective surgery.

---

### B. Surgical & Transcatheter Intervention [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Conservative Management:** Small asymptomatic VSDs require no surgery, as **~50% close spontaneously** during early childhood.
- **Indications for Surgical / Transcatheter Closure:**
    1. Persistent congestive heart failure or failure to thrive despite maximal medical therapy.
    2. Hemodynamically significant shunt with Qp:Qs ratio ≥1.5:1.
    3. Evidence of progressive pulmonary vascular disease / rising PVR before irreversible changes set in.
    4. Subarterial / Infundibular VSD (due to risk of aortic valve prolapse and insufficiency).
- **Timing of Surgery:** Usually performed within the **first 3 to 12 months of life** for large defects.
- **Absolute Contraindication [⭐ High-Yield]:** Fully established **Eisenmenger Syndrome** with fixed high PVR (>8 Wood units) and net right-to-left shunting (closing the defect in this setting causes immediate right ventricular collapse and death).

---

## 9. Complications

1. **Infantile Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Severe LV volume overload causing pulmonary congestion.
2. **Eisenmenger Syndrome:** Irreversible pulmonary vascular obstructive disease with shunt reversal and cyanosis.
3. **Infective Endocarditis:** High-velocity jet damages the right ventricular endocardium opposite the defect, predisposing to bacterial seeding.
4. **Aortic Regurgitation:** Venturi effect of subarterial VSD pulls the right coronary cusp of the aortic valve into the defect.
5. **Recurrent Bronchopneumonia:** Chronic pulmonary congestion compromises local lung defenses.
6. **Failure to Thrive / Stunted Growth:** Secondary to chronic hypoxia and increased metabolic workload of breathing.

---

## 10. Clinical Pearl

> **Clinical Pearl:** Always remember the **VSD Auscultatory Paradox** during bedside examination!
> 
> - A **tiny, hemodynamically benign VSD (_Maladie de Roger_) produces a VERY LOUD, harsh pansystolic murmur** with a thrill because of a huge pressure gradient between the LV and RV.
> - A **massive, life-threatening VSD with Eisenmenger syndrome produces a VERY SOFT or ABSENT murmur** because ventricular pressures are equalized and shunting velocity drops!

---

## 11. Exam Trap

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-Up:** Attempting surgical patch closure of a VSD in an older child or adult who has developed **Eisenmenger Syndrome** (central cyanosis, clubbing, and right-to-left shunt).
- **Distinguishing Fact:** In Eisenmenger syndrome, the fixed high pulmonary vascular resistance makes the VSD act as a crucial "safety valve" allowing the right ventricle to decompress.
- **Resolution:** Surgical or transcatheter VSD closure is **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in Eisenmenger Syndrome**! Closing the defect causes acute, fatal right heart failure. Treatment is limited to medical management or combined heart-lung transplantation.

1. **Loci 1 (The Cracked Partition Wall - Septal Defect):** A high-pressure steel partition cracking open (**Interventricular septal defect**) letting high-pressure water spray from the left tank into the right tank (**Left-to-right shunt**).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition:** Most common CHD (30–42% of all CHD; 1 in 500 births); incomplete closure of interventricular septum.
- **Most Common Site:** Perimembranous septum (~80–90%).
- **Classic Murmur:** Harsh pansystolic murmur at lower left sternal border with a thrill.
- **Classic Presentation:** Asymptomatic if small (_Maladie de Roger_); heart failure at 4–6 weeks of life if large.
- **Gold Standard Diagnostic Tool:** Transthoracic Echocardiography with Color Flow Doppler.
- **Core Contraindication:** Surgical closure is strictly contraindicated once Eisenmenger syndrome develops.
