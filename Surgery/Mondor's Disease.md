# Mondor's Disease

### Definition

**Mondor's disease** is a benign, self-limiting superficial thrombophlebitis affecting the subcutaneous veins of the breast and anterior chest wall, most commonly involving the **lateral thoracic vein**, thoracoepigastric vein, or superior epigastric vein.

- **Etiology & Risk Factors**: Usually obscure/idiopathic in the absence of direct local injury or infection; risk is increased in **cigarette smokers**. It is a cause of non-cyclical mastalgia and is strictly **not a component of ANDI**(Aberrations of Normal Development and Involution).
- **Clinical Presentation**: Patients present with localized non-cyclical breast pain and a palpable, tender, firm subcutaneous cord-like swelling attached to the overlying skin.
- **Pathognomonic Sign**: Stretching the skin over the breast by elevating/raising the ipsilateral arm reveals a **narrow, shallow subcutaneous groove** running alongside the thrombosed cord (**Mondor's cord sign**).
- **Differential Diagnosis & Cancer Risk**: Must be differentiated from lymphatic permeation caused by an underlying occult breast carcinoma; Mondor's disease carries **no increased risk of cancer**.
- **Management & Prognosis**: Treated conservatively with rest/restriction of arm movements, oral analgesics (painkillers/NSAIDs), and reassurance. The condition resolves spontaneously within a few weeks to months without recurrence, permanent deformity, or complications.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Superficial thrombophlebitis of the breast/chest wall veins (most commonly the lateral thoracic vein).
- **Pathognomonic Sign**: Tender subcutaneous cord with a shallow skin groove apparent on arm elevation.
- **Clinical Context**: Cause of non-cyclical mastalgia; not part of ANDI and carries no risk of malignancy.
- **Management**: Self-limiting condition managed conservatively with analgesics and arm rest.
