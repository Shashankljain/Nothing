# Richter's Hernia

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Richter's hernia** is a specialized variant of a strangulated hernia in which only a portion of the circumference of the bowel wall (typically the antimesenteric border of the small intestine) becomes trapped and constricted within a narrow, rigid hernia defect.

- **Anatomical Mechanism & Pathophysiology**: Tight constriction by a narrow fibrous ring selectively impedes low-pressure venous return of the trapped bowel wall portion, rapidly raising intramural capillary pressure and producing localized arterial occlusion, hemorrhagic infarction, gangrene, and perforation.
- **Common Anatomical Sites**: Occurs most frequently in hernias bounded by small, unyielding rigid rings—predominantly **femoral hernias**, obturator hernias, umbilical defects, and laparoscopic port-site hernias.
- **Classic Diagnostic Trap**: Complete mechanical bowel obstruction is frequently **absent or incomplete** because the main intestinal lumen remains partially patent; absolute constipation is often lacking, causing dangerous diagnostic delay and late presentation.
- **Complications & Surgical Management**: Carries an exceptionally high risk of localized bowel wall necrosis and toxic perforation; requires **immediate emergency surgical exploration**, release of the constricting ring, and segmental bowel resection if the bowel wall is non-viable.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Strangulation involving only a part of the circumference of the bowel wall (antimesenteric border).
- **Common Sites**: Femoral hernia, obturator hernia, and laparoscopic port-site defects.
- **Clinical Trap**: Presents without complete intestinal obstruction or absolute constipation, leading to diagnostic delay.
- **Surgical Principle**: High incidence of rapid localized gangrene and perforation requiring emergency surgical repair and bowel resection if non-viable.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Richter's hernia is a dangerous form of strangulated hernia in which only a portion of the bowel wall circumference is trapped within a narrow defect, frequently presenting without complete intestinal obstruction in femoral or laparoscopic port sites and rapidly progressing to localized gangrene and perforation._
