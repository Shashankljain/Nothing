# Obstructed Hernia (Clinical Features & Management)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Obstructed Hernia**: An irreducible hernia in which the lumen of the herniated intestinal segment is mechanically occluded, leading to mechanical bowel obstruction without initial interference to its mesenteric blood supply (no vascular compromise).
- **Key Distinction**: It differs from a **strangulated hernia**, where there is coexisting vascular occlusion, tissue ischemia, and impending gangrene.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Narrow Rigid Sac Neck** [⭐ High-Yield]: Hernias with small, rigid, fibrous defect rings (e.g., femoral hernia, paraumbilical hernia, indirect inguinal hernia) trap intestinal loops.
- **Adhesions within the Sac**: Fibrous adhesions between the peritoneal sac wall and the herniated bowel loops prevent spontaneous or manual reduction.
- **Impacted Intraluminal Contents**: Passage of solid, undigested food residue, fecalith, or gas into the herniated bowel loop causing luminal occlusion.
- **Precipitating Factors**: Sudden spikes in intra-abdominal pressure (coughing, straining, heavy lifting) forcing additional bowel loops through the narrow defect.

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Simple Irreducible Hernia**: Swelling cannot be replaced back into the abdomen, but intestinal transit and blood supply remain normal.
- **Obstructed Hernia**: Luminal obstruction of trapped bowel loop; intestinal transit blocked; blood supply intact.
- **Strangulated Hernia**: Luminal obstruction AND vascular supply compromise; ischemic necrosis and gangrene.
- **Richter's Hernia**: Only a portion of the circumference of the bowel wall is trapped in the narrow neck; can produce gangrene or partial obstruction.

- **Pathological Cascade**: Entrapment of an intestinal loop inside the narrow neck leads to mechanical luminal occlusion. Proximal bowel undergoes hyperperistalsis initially, followed by progressive gaseous overgrowth (90% nitrogen) and digestive fluid sequestration (saliva, gastric juice, bile, pancreatic juice). Increased intraluminal pressure causes bowel wall distension; if unrelieved, intramural pressure rises, eventually compromising venous outflow and converting an obstructed hernia into a **strangulated hernia**.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

The clinical presentation of an obstructed hernia represents the combination of **local hernia sac findings** and the **classic cardinal quartet of mechanical intestinal obstruction**:

#### 1. Symptoms [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Local Pain & Heaviness**: Aching, dragging pain or severe discomfort over the hernia site.
- **Colicky Abdominal Pain** [⭐ High-Yield]: First cardinal symptom of intestinal obstruction; sudden onset of severe, crampy pain localized around the umbilicus (small bowel obstruction) or lower abdomen (large bowel obstruction), coinciding with hyperperistaltic waves.
- **Vomiting** [⭐ High-Yield]: Second cardinal symptom; occurs early and is profuse in high small bowel obstruction (digested food → bilious). Occurs later in low small bowel or large bowel obstruction, becoming **feculent** due to enteric bacterial overgrowth.
- **Abdominal Distension** [⭐ High-Yield]: Third cardinal symptom; progressive central abdominal distension, more pronounced in low small bowel and large bowel obstruction.
- **Absolute Constipation** [⭐ High-Yield]: Fourth cardinal symptom; failure to pass both flatus and feces. (May be relative or absent initially if the distal bowel evacuations continue or in a Richter's hernia).

#### 2. Signs [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Local Examination of Hernia Sac**:
    - _Site & Inspection_: Swelling present over a known hernial orifice (inguinal, femoral, umbilical, incisional). Overlying skin color is **normal** (absence of dark purplish discoloration or erythema seen in strangulation).
    - _Irreducibility_: Swelling is firm, tense, and **completely irreducible** on manual manipulation.
    - _Expansile Cough Impulse_: **Significantly REDUCED or ABSENT** because the tight neck of the sac occludes the communication with the peritoneal cavity.
    - _Local Tenderness_: Moderate local tenderness due to tissue tension, but **lacks the exquisite tenderness, guarding, or brawny induration** characteristic of strangulation/gangrene.
- **Systemic & Abdominal Examination**:
    - _Abdominal Distension & Visible Peristalsis_: Abdominal contour is rounded/distended; visible peristaltic waves may be seen in thin patients.
    - _Bowel Sounds_: Characteristically **high-pitched, exaggerated, and metallic / hyperactive** bowel sounds coinciding with episodes of colicky pain.
    - _Dehydration Signs_: Dry tongue, reduced skin turgor, tachycardia, and oliguria secondary to fluid sequestration and vomiting.
    - _Systemic Hemodynamics_: Normal blood pressure and absence of septic shock or high fever (fever or hypotension indicates progression to strangulation/peritonitis).

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Examination [Primary Diagnostic Basis]**: Meticulous physical examination of all hernial orifices (especially femoral ring in elderly/obese females) in any patient presenting with acute intestinal obstruction.
- **Plain Abdominal Radiography (X-Ray - Supine View)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Demonstrates central dilated small bowel loops (>3 cm diameter) with step-ladder air-fluid levels.
    - Absence of gas in the colon/rectum confirms complete mechanical obstruction.
- **Contrast-Enhanced Computed Tomography (CECT Abdomen & Pelvis) [Gold Standard]** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Identifies the exact site, type, and neck diameter of the obstructed hernia.
    - Visualizes the **transition point** (dilated proximal bowel loops abruptly transitioning to collapsed distal bowel loops at the hernia ring).
    - Confirms adequate bowel wall contrast enhancement (rules out coexisting vascular ischemia/strangulation).
- **Laboratory Tests**:
    - _Complete Blood Count_: Hemoconcentration (elevated hematocrit); normal WBC count (marked leukocytosis indicates strangulation).
    - _Serum Electrolytes & Renal Function_: Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, and elevated blood urea / serum creatinine secondary to fluid sequestration.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

```

                                    |
     +------------------------------+------------------------------+
     |                                                             |
Emergency Resuscitation & Decompression                   Emergency Surgical Intervention
* High-flow O₂ + 2 Large-bore IV lines                    * Open / Laparoscopic Surgical Exploration
* IV Isotonic Crystalloids (Ringer's Lactate)             * Manually hold bowel loop BEFORE dividing neck
* Nasogastric (Ryle's) Tube Aspiration                    * Incise constricting neck & deliver bowel
* IV Prophylactic Antibiotics (Cefuroxime)                * Inspect Bowel Viability (Pink, Peristalsis, Pulsation)
                                                                         |
                                                +------------------------+------------------------+
                                                |                                                 |
                                         Viable Bowel                                      Non-Viable Bowel
                                         * Reduce bowel into abdomen                       * Segmental Bowel Resection
                                         * Perform Herniotomy                              * End-to-End Anastomosis
                                         * Repair Defect (Mesh or Suture)                  * Tissue Suture Repair (Avoid Mesh if Sepsis)
```

#### 1. Emergency Preoperative Resuscitation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid IV infusion of isotonic crystalloids (Ringer's lactate or 0.9% normal saline) to correct dehydration and electrolyte depletion.
- **Nasogastric (Ryle's) Tube Decompression**: Insertion of an NG tube on continuous aspiration to decompress the stomach, prevent feculent aspiration during anesthesia, and reduce intraluminal bowel distension.
- **Urinary Catheterization**: Foley catheter insertion to monitor hourly urine output (target >0.5 mL/kg/h).
- **Intravenous Antibiotics**: Single dose of IV broad-spectrum antibiotic (Cefuroxime 1.5 g IV) at induction.

#### 2. Emergency Surgical Decompression [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Surgical Access**: Groin incision over the hernia sac or midline laparotomy if abdominal distension is massive.
- **Golden Rule of Hernia Surgery** [⭐ High-Yield]: **Manually grasp and anchor the trapped bowel loop BEFORE incising the constricting neck of the sac** to prevent an uninspected loop from slipping back into the abdominal cavity.
- **Release of Constriction Ring**: Carefully incise the fibrous neck under direct vision.
- **Bowel Viability Assessment**: Confirm that the bowel is **viable** (pink/red color, active peristaltic waves, shiny serosa, visible mesenteric arterial pulsations).
- **Reduction & Defect Repair**: Reduce the viable bowel into the peritoneal cavity, perform high ligation of the sac (herniotomy), and repair the abdominal wall defect using tension-free mesh or tissue repair.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Progression to Strangulation & Gangrene**: Elevated intramural pressure leading to arterial occlusion, bowel wall necrosis, and perforation.
- **Fecal Peritonitis & Septic Shock**: Rupture of gangrenous bowel releasing toxic intestinal contents into the peritoneal cavity.
- **Severe Electrolyte Imbalance & Dehydration**: Hypokalemia, prerenal acute kidney injury, and hypovolemic shock.
- **Postoperative Wound Infection & Seroma**: Increased in emergency procedures.

---

### Prognosis

- Excellent prognosis (>95% success) when operated promptly in the obstructed stage prior to the onset of vascular strangulation; mortality rises significantly (5–15%) if strangulation or bowel perforation supervenes.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In an elderly female presenting with **unexplained small bowel obstruction (vomiting, distension, colicky pain) without any previous surgical abdominal scar**, **always perform a meticulous palpation of the groin below the inguinal ligament for a small, occult obstructed femoral hernia**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing an **Obstructed Hernia** with a **Strangulated Hernia**.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: An obstructed hernia has **luminal blockage only with an INTACT vascular supply**(normal skin color, colicky pain, moderate tenderness, no fever/shock). A strangulated hernia has **VASCULAR ISCHEMIA** (continuous pain, purplish skin discoloration, exquisite tenderness, fever, and septic shock).

---

### Dosage Rule (Surgical Antibiotic Prophylaxis)

#### 1. Cefuroxime

- **Name**: Cefuroxime Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Second-generation cephalosporin; binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) to inhibit bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis.
- **Dose**: 1.5 g.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Frequency**: Single dose at anesthesia induction (within 30 minutes prior to skin incision).
- **Features**: First-line prophylactic coverage against surgical site pathogens in emergency hernia repairs.
- **Side Effects**: Hypersensitivity reactions, skin rash, gastrointestinal upset.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Give single IV dose at induction; discontinue postoperatively in uncomplicated cases without spillage.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: O-B-S-T-R-U-C-T

- **O** – **O**ccluded intestinal lumen with intact mesenteric vascular supply.
- **B** – **B**owel sounds are high-pitched, exaggerated, and metallic.
- **S** – **S**tep-ladder air-fluid levels on plain supine abdominal radiograph.
- **T** – **T**ense, firm, irreducible hernia sac.
- **R** – **R**educed or completely absent expansile cough impulse.
- **U** – **U**nexplained small bowel obstruction in females requires examining the femoral ring.
- **C** – **C**ardinal quartet: Colicky pain, Vomiting, Distension, Absolute constipation.
- **T** – **T**ime-critical surgical decompression to prevent conversion to strangulation.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Irreducible hernia with mechanical luminal occlusion of trapped bowel and intact blood supply.
- **Cardinal Quartet**: Colicky abdominal pain + Vomiting (bilious/feculent) + Abdominal distension + Absolute constipation.
- **Local Sac Signs**: Irreducible, tense, normal skin color, **absent/reduced cough impulse**, moderate tenderness.
- **Gold-Standard Investigation**: CECT Abdomen showing transition point and bowel wall enhancement.
- **Surgical Rule**: Hold the bowel loop manually BEFORE incising the constricting neck.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_An obstructed hernia is an acute surgical condition characterized by the mechanical occlusion of the lumen of an entrapped intestinal loop within a narrow-necked hernia sac while maintaining intact mesenteric vascular perfusion, presenting with the cardinal quartet of intestinal obstruction (colicky pain, vomiting, distension, absolute constipation) alongside an irreducible, tense hernia mass lacking an expansile cough impulse._
