# Femoral Hernia

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **femoral hernia** is defined as an abnormal protrusion of a peritoneal sac containing abdominal viscera (bowel or omentum) or extraperitoneal fat through the **femoral ring** into the **femoral canal**, entering below the inguinal ligament and lying medial to the femoral vein.

---

### Anatomical Boundaries of the Femoral Ring & Canal [⭐ High-Yield]

The femoral canal is the rigid medial compartment of the femoral sheath (normally containing fat and the **node of Cloquet**) bounded by:

- **Anteriorly**: Inguinal ligament (Poupart's).
- **Posteriorly**: Pectineal ligament (Astley Cooper's) covering the pectineal line of the pubic bone.
- **Medially**: Sharp, crescentic free edge of the **lacunar ligament (Gimbernat's)**.
- **Laterally**: Femoral vein (separated by a fibrous septum).

---

### Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Female Predisposition**: Femoral hernias account for approximately **20% of all groin hernias in women**, compared to **<1% in men**. This female preponderance is attributed to a wider female bony pelvis, larger femoral ring diameter, and structural changes during pregnancy.
- **Age-Related Structural Degradation**: Progressive widening of the femoral ring occurs with advancing age and tissue atrophy in thin, elderly individuals.
- **Raised Intra-Abdominal Pressure**: Chronic cough, constipation, urinary straining, ascites, or multiple pregnancies forcing peritoneal contents through the femoral septum.

- **Pathological Trajectory**: The hernia sac descends vertically through the femoral ring into the femoral canal. At the saphenous opening (fossa ovalis), further downward descent into the leg is prevented by the attachment of cribriform fascia; the sac turns anteriorly and **curves superiorly over the inguinal ligament** into subcutaneous tissue.
- **High Rate of Strangulation**: Because the femoral ring is enclosed by rigid, unyielding structures—especially the sharp medial edge of **Gimbernat's lacunar ligament**—femoral hernias carry the highest rate of incarceration and strangulation (~50% present as surgical emergencies).
- **Richter's Hernia Variant**: Entrapment of only a portion of the bowel wall circumference within the narrow neck; can rapidly progress to ischemia, gangrene, and perforation without complete mechanical bowel obstruction.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- Painful or painless small bulge in the upper inner thigh/groin below the inguinal crease.
- Nausea, vomiting, and colicky pain indicating acute intestinal obstruction.

#### Signs

- **Anatomical Site**: Globular swelling situated **below and lateral to the pubic tubercle**.
- **Expansile Cough Impulse**: **Frequently ABSENT** because the tight neck grips the sac.
- **Reducibility**: Highly irreducible; often small (1–2 cm) and easily misdiagnosed as a lymph node.
- **Strangulation Signs**: Severe localized tenderness and dark purplish skin discoloration over the mass indicate gangrenous bowel.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Examination [Gold Standard]**: Primary diagnostic method; differentiated from an inguinal hernia by its position **below and lateral to the pubic tubercle** (inguinal hernias lie above and medial).
- **Ultrasonography (USG Groin)**: Helpful in obese patients to visualize the defect relative to the femoral vein.
- **CT Abdomen & Pelvis**: Investigation of choice in patients with unexplained small bowel obstruction to identify an occult, strangulated femoral hernia.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

- **General Rule**: Mandatory surgical repair upon diagnosis; trusses and watchful waiting are **strictly contraindicated**due to the high risk of strangulation.

#### Open Surgical Approaches

1. **Low Approach (Lockwood's Operation)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Technique_: Transverse incision below inguinal ligament; sac dissected, reduced, and ligated. Defect closed by suturing the **inguinal ligament above to the pectineal ligament below**.
    - _Indication_: Simple, elective hernias where there is **zero risk of bowel resection**.
2. **Inguinal Approach (Lotheissen's Operation)**:
    - _Technique_: Inguinal canal opened; transversalis fascia incised; sac reduced by traction from above and pressure from below.
3. **High Preperitoneal Approach (McEvedy's Operation / Nyhus Modification)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Technique_: Incision above inguinal canal over rectus sheath; enters preperitoneal space.
    - _Indication_: **Gold-standard operation for emergency strangulated femoral hernias**. Allows generous peritoneal incision for inspection and formal bowel resection with end-to-end anastomosis.
    - _Crucial Advantage_: Bowel resection **cannot be performed via Lockwood's low approach** because the completed bowel anastomosis cannot pass back through the narrow femoral canal into the abdomen.

#### Laparoscopic Repair (TEP & TAPP) [⭐ High-Yield]

- Recommended as first-line repair for **elective female groin hernias** to eliminate diagnostic confusion between inguinal and femoral hernias and reduce early recurrence.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Mechanical small bowel obstruction, Richter's strangulation, and gangrene.
- **Corona Mortis Injury**: Severe arterial hemorrhage caused by lacerating an aberrant obturator artery while dividing the lacunar ligament.
- Femoral vein suture transfixion or narrowing.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In an elderly female presenting with **unexplained small bowel obstruction without any abdominal scar**, **always perform a meticulous examination of the groin below the inguinal ligament for a small, hidden femoral hernia**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Attempting to perform a formal small bowel resection and anastomosis through a **Low (Lockwood) approach** in a strangulated femoral hernia.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: The rigid boundaries of the femoral canal make it physically impossible to return a completed bowel anastomosis back into the abdominal cavity from below. **Always use a High Preperitoneal (McEvedy) approach when bowel strangulation or resection is suspected**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Surgical Antibiotic Prophylaxis)

#### 1. Cefuroxime

- **Name**: Cefuroxime Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Second-generation cephalosporin; binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) to inhibit bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis.
- **Dose**: 1.5 g IV.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous injection.
- **Frequency**: Single dose at anesthesia induction (within 30 minutes prior to skin incision).
- **Features**: First-line surgical prophylaxis for emergency hernia repair or mesh insertion.
- **Side Effects**: Hypersensitivity reactions, skin rash, diarrhea.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Give single IV dose at induction; discontinue postoperatively unless active clinical infection or peritonitis is present.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: F-E-M-O-R-A-L

- **F** – **F**emoral ring bounded by Inguinal (ant), Pectineal (post), Lacunar (med), and Femoral vein (lat).
- **E** – **E**mergency operation indicated for all cases due to high risk of strangulation.
- **M** – **M**cEvedy high approach is preferred for strangulated/obstructed hernias requiring bowel resection.
- **O** – **O**bturator artery anomaly (corona mortis) crosses the lacunar ligament.
- **R** – **R**ichter's type strangulation is common (nipping of partial bowel wall).
- **A** – **A**lways present **below and lateral** to the pubic tubercle.
- **L** – **L**ockwood low approach used for simple elective reducible hernias.

### Quick Revision

- **Groin Hernia Ratio**: Accounts for ~20% of groin hernias in women vs <1% in men.
- **Pubic Tubercle Rule**: Femoral hernia = **below & lateral**; Inguinal hernia = **above & medial**.
- **Anatomical Boundaries**: Anterior = Inguinal lig; Posterior = Pectineal lig; Medial = Lacunar lig; Lateral = Femoral vein.
- **Surgical Approaches**: Low (Lockwood) for simple elective; High (McEvedy) for emergency strangulated; Laparoscopic (TEP/TAPP) for elective females.
- **Surgical Hazard**: Watch for **corona mortis** (aberrant obturator artery) when dividing the lacunar ligament.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A femoral hernia is a groin protrusion arising through the femoral ring into the femoral canal below the inguinal ligament, occurring predominantly in females, carrying a high risk of strangulation due to the rigid lacunar ligament, and requiring mandatory surgical repair via low (Lockwood), high (McEvedy), or laparoscopic (TEP/TAPP) approaches._
