# Complications of Hernia Surgery

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Complications of hernia surgery** refer to adverse intraoperative or postoperative clinical events—categorized into immediate, early (medium-term), and late morbidities—arising from tissue dissection, nerve injury, fluid accumulation, foreign-body mesh reaction, or structural repair breakdown following open or laparoscopic hernia repair.

- **Immediate & Vascular Complications**: Wound hematoma or active hemorrhage (commonly from subcutaneous vessels or accidental laceration of inferior epigastric or iliac vessels) and acute urinary retention requiring catheterization.
- **Early / Medium-Term Complications**: Seroma formation (accumulation of inflammatory fluid within the residual sac space, especially if left in situ, which can mimic early recurrence) and surgical site infection.
- **Chronic Groin Neuralgia (Late)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Burning neuralgic pain persisting >3 months postoperatively (affecting up to 20% of open repairs), caused by direct nerve entrapment, transection, or chronic irritation of the ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric, or genitofemoral nerves by sutures or mesh.
- **Testicular Ischemia & Atrophy (Late)**: Testicular infarction resulting from accidental damage or thrombosis of the testicular artery and pampiniform venous plexus during deep cord dissection.
- **Hernia Recurrence (Late)**: Structural repair failure or progressive collagen contraction around the mesh ("mesh contracture") leading to recurrence (<5% in specialist centers).
