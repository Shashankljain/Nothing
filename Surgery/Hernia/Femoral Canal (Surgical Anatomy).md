# Femoral Canal (Surgical Anatomy)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

The **femoral canal** is the short, conical medial compartment of the femoral sheath (measuring approximately 1.2–2 cm in length) situated in the groin below the inguinal ligament, serving as a physiological conduit for deep lymphatics and forming the anatomical site of femoral herniation.

---

### Key Anatomical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Femoral Ring (Superior Orifice Boundaries)** [⭐ High-Yield]: The rigid anatomical orifice of the canal bounded by:
    - **Anteriorly**: Inguinal ligament (Poupart's).
    - **Posteriorly**: Pectineal ligament (Astley Cooper's) covering the pectineal line of the pubic bone.
    - **Medially**: Sharp, unyielding crescentic edge of the **lacunar ligament (Gimbernat's)**.
    - **Laterally**: Femoral vein (separated by a fibrous fascial septum).
- **Canal Contents** [⭐ High-Yield]: Contains extraperitoneal fat, lymphatic vessels, and the **node of Cloquet**(rosenmüller/deep inguinal lymph node draining the glans penis/clitoris and deep perineal structures).
- **Surgical Path of Herniation**: A hernia enters the femoral ring, descends vertically down the canal to the saphenous opening (fossa ovalis), and then turns anteriorly and superiorly over the inguinal ligament within the subcutaneous tissue.
- **Mechanism of Strangulation**: The unyielding, sharp medial border formed by **Gimbernat's lacunar ligament**causes early constriction of the hernia neck, resulting in high rates of bowel incarceration and strangulation.
- **Vascular Hazard (Corona Mortis / Aberrant Obturator Artery)** [⭐ High-Yield]: In ~20% of cases, an abnormal/aberrant obturator artery crosses the lacunar ligament; incising this ligament to release a strangulated neck risks severe, hard-to-control hemorrhage.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Boundaries**: Anterior = Inguinal lig; Posterior = Pectineal lig; Medial = Lacunar lig; Lateral = Femoral vein.
- **Key Content**: Extraperitoneal fat and the **Node of Cloquet**.
- **Strangulation Site**: Junction of the falciform margin of the saphenous opening with the unyielding **lacunar ligament**.
- **Surgical Hazard**: Laceration of an aberrant obturator artery (_corona mortis_) when dividing the lacunar ligament.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_The femoral canal is the medial compartment of the femoral sheath containing retroperitoneal fat and Cloquet's node, bounded by the inguinal ligament anteriorly, pectineal ligament posteriorly, lacunar ligament medially, and femoral vein laterally, serving as the anatomical site for femoral hernias which carry a high strangulation risk due to the rigid lacunar border._
