# Maydl's Hernia (Retrograde Strangulation)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Maydl's hernia** (also known as a **hernia-in-W** or **retrograde strangulated hernia**) is a rare, life-threatening variant of a strangulated hernia in which two loops of small bowel lie within the hernia sac, while the intervening/central loop remains inside the peritoneal cavity and selectively undergoes acute ischemia, gangrene, and necrosis.

---

### Etiology / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Large Hernial Defect & Spacious Sac** [⭐ High-Yield]: Typically occurs in long-standing, large indirect inguinal, scrotal, or femoral hernias with a wide abdominal wall ring and a voluminous sac that permits multiple bowel loops to enter and exit.
- **Hypermotility & Increased Intra-Abdominal Pressure**: Spikes in intra-abdominal pressure (straining, coughing, heavy lifting) force two adjacent loops of small intestine (usually ileum) into the hernia sac.
- **Tight Constriction of Central Mesentery**: Constriction at the narrow neck of the sac selectively impedes the mesenteric arterial and venous blood supply supplying the intra-abdominal central loop, while the loops inside the sac remain relatively uncompressed.

- **W-Shaped Anatomical Configuration**:
    - **Limb 1 (In Sac)**: Proximal entering bowel loop.
    - **Limb 2 (In Abdomen)**: Intervening central loop lying within the main peritoneal cavity.
    - **Limb 3 (In Sac)**: Distal exiting bowel loop.
- **Mechanism of Retrograde Ischemia**: The constriction ring at the neck compresses the mesentery supplying the intra-abdominal central loop (Limb 2). Venous congestion leads to rapid intramural edema, arterial occlusion, hemorrhagic infarction, and gangrene **inside the abdominal cavity**, even though the bowel loops visible inside the hernia sac may appear pink and viable.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Abdominal Pain Out of Proportion to Sac Findings** [⭐ High-Yield]: Severe, continuous, agonizing central abdominal pain due to intra-abdominal ischemic bowel necrosis, despite the hernia swelling being relatively non-tender initially.
- **Acute Mechanical Intestinal Obstruction**: Profuse bilious or feculent vomiting, crampy abdominal colic, abdominal distension, and absolute constipation (failure to pass flatus and feces).
- **Systemic Toxemia & Septic Shock**: High fever, rigors, tachycardia, hypotension, and marked prostration resulting from intra-abdominal bacterial translocation.

#### Signs

- **Hernia Sac Inspection & Palpation**: Irreducible, moderately tense groin or scrotal mass; may show surprisingly mild local tenderness compared to the patient's severe systemic distress.
- **Abdominal Examination [Key Finding]**: **Exquisite, widespread tenderness, muscle guarding, and rebound rigidity over the lower abdomen** (indicating intra-abdominal peritonitis from the gangrenous central loop).
- **Expansile Cough Impulse**: Completely **ABSENT** over the hernia sac.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: High index of suspicion when a patient with an irreducible hernia presents with severe abdominal peritonitis and toxic shock disproportionate to local hernia sac tenderness.
- **Plain Abdominal Radiography (X-Ray)**: Shows multiple dilated small bowel loops with central air-fluid levels and absence of colonic gas.
- **Contrast-Enhanced Computed Tomography (CECT Abdomen & Pelvis) [Gold Standard]**:
    - Demonstrates the pathognomonic **W-shaped configuration** of small bowel loops.
    - Identifies non-enhancing, thick-walled, gangrenous central bowel loop inside the abdominal cavity alongside mesenteric stranding and ascites.
- **Laboratory Workup**: Marked neutrophilic leukocytosis (>15,000/mm³), elevated blood lactate, and metabolic acidosis on arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis.

```
                                                          |                   |
                                                   Viable Central Loop   Gangrenous Central Loop

```

#### 1. Emergency Preoperative Resuscitation

- **Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid IV isotonic crystalloid administration to treat dehydration and septic shock.
- **Decompression & Antibiotics**: Nasogastric tube suction and immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics covering Gram-negative and anaerobic organisms (e.g., Cefuroxime 1.5 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV).

#### 2. Surgical Technique & Mandatory Rule [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Surgical Access**: Standard groin incision over hernia sac or midline laparotomy if peritonitis is advanced.
- **The Non-Negotiable Maydl's Surgical Rule** [⭐ High-Yield]: **Never declare the bowel viable or reduce the hernia sac without pulling out and inspecting the intermediate/central intra-abdominal loop in its entirety**.
- **Procedure**:
    1. Open hernia sac and identify both entering and exiting bowel loops.
    2. Gently apply traction to deliver the **intervening intra-abdominal loop** into the surgical field.
    3. Assess central loop viability using the **10-minute warm pack test** with 100% oxygen.
    4. If the central loop is non-viable/gangrenous: Perform **segmental small bowel resection** with double-layer end-to-end anastomosis.
    5. Reduce viable bowel into the abdomen and perform high ligation of sac (herniotomy) with primary suture repair of the abdominal wall defect (**avoid synthetic mesh if gross fecal peritonitis is present**).

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Intra-abdominal bowel perforation, fecal peritonitis, severe septic shock, acute kidney injury, enterocutaneous fistula, and high operative mortality (10–25%) if diagnosis is delayed.

---

### Clinical Pearl

When operating on a large strangulated hernia where the bowel inside the sac appears surprisingly pink or only mildly edematous despite the patient having severe abdominal peritonitis, **always pull down and inspect the central intervening loop (Maydl's hernia)**. Failure to inspect the intra-abdominal loop will result in reducing a gangrenous bowel segment back into the peritoneal cavity, leading to fatal postoperative peritonitis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Inspecting only the two bowel loops present inside the hernia sac, declaring them viable, and reducing them into the abdomen without examining the central loop.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: In Maydl's hernia, the **intra-abdominal central loop undergoes primary strangulation (retrograde strangulation)** while the loops inside the sac remain viable. **Always pull down and inspect the central loop before completing the operation**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Emergency Perioperative Antibiotics)

#### 1. Cefuroxime

- **Name**: Cefuroxime Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Second-generation cephalosporin; binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) to inhibit bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis.
- **Dose**: 1.5 g.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS).
- **Side Effects**: Hypersensitivity reactions, rash, diarrhea.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start during emergency resuscitation; continue for 5–7 days postoperatively following bowel resection.

#### 2. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Nitroimidazole derivative; causes DNA strand breakage in anaerobic bacteria (_Bacteroides fragilis_).
- **Dose**: 500 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Slow IV infusion over 20 minutes.
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS).

### Quick Revision

- **Anatomical Hallmark**: W-shaped hernia containing 2 loops in the sac and 1 central loop in the peritoneal cavity.
- **Strangulation Pattern**: **Retrograde strangulation**—the intra-abdominal central loop becomes gangrenous while sac loops remain falsely viable.
- **Clinical Clue**: Severe generalized abdominal peritonitis out of proportion to mild hernia sac tenderness.
- **Golden Surgical Rule**: Always pull down and inspect the central loop during emergency repair.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Resection of gangrenous central loop + end-to-end anastomosis + hernia repair.
