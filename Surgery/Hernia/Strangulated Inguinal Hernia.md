# Strangulated Inguinal Hernia

_(Marks Weightage: 5 Marks)_

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **strangulated inguinal hernia** is a surgical emergency in which the rigid fibrous neck of an irreducible inguinal hernia sac acts as a constriction ring, severely compromising the vascular supply of the herniated contents (most commonly small intestine or omentum). This leads sequentially to venous congestion, tissue ischemia, hemorrhagic infarction, gangrene, and bowel perforation.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rigid Constricting Neck** [⭐ High-Yield]: Hernias with a small, narrow, rigid fibrous defect (neck) relative to the volume of the sac are at the highest risk of strangulation.
- **Indirect Inguinal Hernias**: Higher risk than direct hernias because indirect hernias emerge through the narrow deep ring, whereas direct hernias have a broad-based neck in Hesselbach's triangle.
- **Precipitating Factors**: Sudden increases in intra-abdominal pressure (straining, coughing, heavy lifting) that force a larger volume of viscera through the narrow defect.
- **Richter's Hernia Variant**: Trapping of only a portion of the circumference of the bowel wall within the narrow neck, leading to rapid strangulation and gangrene without complete mechanical intestinal obstruction.

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Reducible Hernia**: Swelling appears on standing/coughing and returns freely into the abdominal cavity.
- **Irreducible / Incarcerated Hernia**: Viscera trapped in the sac due to a narrow neck or adhesions; at immediate risk of strangulation.
- **Strangulated Hernia**: Irreducible hernia with acute tissue ischemia and severe pain.
- **Infarcted / Gangrenous Hernia**: Irreversible bowel necrosis and gangrene following unrelieved strangulation.

- **Vascular Compromise Sequence**: The narrow neck selectively obstructs low-pressure venous return while arterial inflow continues. Capillary pressure rises rapidly, causing fluid transudation, marked bowel wall edema, and increased intramural tension. When intramural pressure exceeds systemic arterial pressure, arterial inflow ceases, producing **hemorrhagic infarction** and gangrene.
- **Bacterial Translocation & Perforation**: Ischemic mucosal breakdown allows translocation of anaerobic bacteria and endotoxins into the circulation, followed by full-thickness bowel perforation into the sac and peritoneal cavity.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Severe Continuous Pain** [⭐ High-Yield]: Sudden onset of excruciating, relentless pain localized over the hernia sac, which does not settle with simple analgesia or position changes.
- **Mechanical Bowel Obstruction**: Nausea, bilious or feculent vomiting, crampy abdominal pain, and absolute constipation (failure to pass flatus and feces).
- **Systemic Toxicity**: High fever, rigors, malaise, and progressive restlessness.

#### Physical Signs

- **Inspection**: Tense, irreducible groin swelling. Overlying skin shows **erythema, warmth, edema, or purplish/bluish discoloration** indicating underlying tissue infarction.
- **Palpation**: **Exquisite local tenderness** and marked rigidity over the mass.
- **Expansile Cough Impulse**: **Completely ABSENT** because the tight neck occludes the sac.
- **Systemic Signs of Septic Shock**: Tachycardia (>90 beats/min), hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg), tachypnea (>20 breaths/min), and oliguria.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Diagnosis [Primary Modality]**: Diagnosis is made primarily on physical examination. Urgent surgery must never be delayed for imaging if clinical signs of strangulation are present.
- **Laboratory Tests**:
    - _Full Blood Count_: Marked neutrophilic leukocytosis (>12,000/mm³).
    - _Arterial Blood Gas & Serum Lactate_: Elevated blood lactate and metabolic acidosis confirm tissue ischemia.
    - _Serum Electrolytes & Urea_: Elevated urea and creatinine secondary to dehydration and fluid sequestration.
- **Imaging**:
    - _Plain Abdominal X-Ray_: Demonstrates central dilated small bowel loops with multiple fluid levels and absence of colonic gas.
    - _Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) Abdomen & Pelvis_: Demonstrates bowel wall thickening, lack of contrast enhancement, fluid in the hernia sac, and pneumatosis intestinalis.

#### 1. Emergency Resuscitation [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid intravenous administration of isotonic crystalloids (Ringer's lactate or 0.9% normal saline) to correct hypovolemia and shock.
- **Nasogastric Decompression**: Insertion of a Ryle's tube on continuous aspiration to decompress the stomach and prevent pulmonary aspiration during anesthesia induction.
- **Intravenous Antibiotics**: Immediate administration of broad-spectrum IV antibiotics covering Gram-negative aerobes and anaerobes.

#### 2. Emergency Surgical Principles [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Surgical Access**: Groin incision over the hernia sac or a midline laparotomy if generalized peritonitis is present.
- **Securing the Bowel Loop** [⭐ High-Yield]: The surgeon **must manually grasp and secure the trapped bowel loop BEFORE incising the constricting neck**, preventing an uninspected ischemic loop from retracting back into the peritoneal cavity.
- **Releasing the Constriction Ring**: The tight neck is incised under direct vision to restore perfusion.

#### 3. Assessment of Bowel Viability ("10-Minute Warm Pack Test") [⭐ High-Yield]

- Wrap questionable bowel in warm saline sponges for **10 minutes** and administer **100% oxygen**.
- **Viability Criteria** [⭐ High-Yield]:

|Parameter|Viable Bowel (Reduce)|Non-Viable Bowel (Resect)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Color**|Dark color lightens to **pink/red**|Dark purple/black color persists|
|**Mesenteric Pulsations**|**Visible arterial pulsations** in arcades|Absent arterial pulsations|
|**Serosal Sheen**|**Shiny and smooth**|Dull, lustreless, and friable|
|**Peristalsis**|**Active peristalsis** observed|Absent peristalsis|

#### 4. Definite Resection & Mesh Guidelines [⭐ High-Yield]

- **If Viable**: Reduce bowel, perform herniotomy, and repair the defect.
- **If Non-Viable**: Perform segmental bowel resection with end-to-end anastomosis.
- **Mesh Placement Rule** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Clean-Contaminated Field (No bowel spillage)_: Synthetic polypropylene mesh **may be safely placed** under antibiotic coverage.
    - _Gross Fecal Contamination / Bowel Perforation_: Synthetic mesh is **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED**due to high risk of mesh infection; perform primary tissue suture repair (Shouldice, Bassini, or Maloney darn) instead.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Preoperative**: Bowel wall gangrene, perforation, fecal peritonitis, septic shock, acute kidney injury, and multi-organ failure.
- **Postoperative**: Wound infection, mesh sepsis, enterocutaneous fistula, ischemic orchitis, and elevated operative mortality (5–15%).

---

### Clinical Pearl

During emergency repair of a strangulated hernia, **never cut the constricting neck of the sac until you have manually anchored the herniated bowel loop**. If the neck is divided first, sudden loss of tension causes the ischemic, potentially gangrenous bowel to slip back into the abdomen, forcing an unnecessary laparotomy.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Inserting a **synthetic Polypropylene mesh** during emergency surgery for a strangulated hernia with **gross fecal spillage from bowel perforation**.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Synthetic mesh in a grossly contaminated field leads to chronic mesh infection, sinus formation, and enterocutaneous fistulae. **Use primary tissue suture repair (Shouldice/Bassini/Darn) in gross fecal contamination; reserve mesh for clean-contaminated fields without spillage**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Emergency Antimicrobial Therapy)

#### 1. Cefuroxime

- **Name**: Cefuroxime Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Second-generation cephalosporin; inhibits bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins.
- **Dose**: 1.5 g.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS).
- **Features**: First-line coverage against Gram-positive and Gram-negative surgical pathogens.
- **Side Effects**: Hypersensitivity reactions, rash, diarrhea.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start preoperatively during resuscitation; continue for 24–48 hours postoperatively (or 7 days if bowel resection is performed).

#### 2. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Nitroimidazole derivative; causes DNA strand breakage in anaerobic bacteria (_Bacteroides fragilis_).
- **Dose**: 500 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Slow IV infusion over 20 minutes.
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS).
- **Features**: Essential anaerobic coverage for strangulated or perforated bowel.
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, nausea, disulfiram-like reaction.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Vascular compromise of hernia contents caused by a rigid constricting neck.
- **Classic Signs**: Tense, irreducible mass, severe continuous pain, **ABSENT cough impulse**, and purplish skin discoloration.
- **Operative Rule**: Secure the bowel loop manually BEFORE incising the neck.
- **Bowel Viability Test**: 10-minute warm saline pack test with 100% oxygen; check color, sheen, peristalsis, and arterial pulsations.
- **Mesh Contradiction**: Synthetic mesh is acceptable in clean-contaminated fields, but **STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED in gross fecal spillage/perforation**.
