# Chronic Duodenal Ulcer

### Definition

A **chronic duodenal ulcer** is a focal, chronic mucosal breach extending through the muscularis mucosae into the submucosa or muscularis propria layer of the duodenum, occurring predominantly in the first part (D₁ / duodenal cap) as a result of acid-peptic digestion paired with mucosal defense failure.

---

### Basic Surgical Anatomy

- **Anatomical Location**: The vast majority (>95%) of duodenal ulcers occur in the **first part of the duodenum (D₁)**, within 2–3 cm of the pylorus.
- **Arterial Relations**:
    - The posterior wall of D₁ is directly related to the **gastroduodenal artery (GDA)**, gastroduodenal vein, and common bile duct. Deep erosion of posterior ulcers into the GDA leads to catastrophic upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage.
    - The anterior wall of D₁ is covered only by peritoneum and abuts the gallbladder and liver base; anterior ulcers freely perforate into the greater peritoneal sac.
- **Innervation**: Parasympathetic motor and secretory innervation is supplied by the anterior and posterior vagal trunks via the **nerves of Latarjet**, terminating in the **crow's foot** supplying the antrum and pyloric sphincter.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **_Helicobacter pylori_ Infection**: The single most critical primary causative agent, identified in **90%–95% of patients with duodenal ulcers**.
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)**: Second most common cause; inhibits cyclo-oxygenase-1 (COX-1) enzymes, suppressing cytoprotective mucosal prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Gastric Acid Hypersecretion**: Increased parietal cell mass, elevated basal acid output, and loss of normal G-cell negative feedback.
- **Cigarette Smoking**: Directly impairs mucosal blood flow, inhibits pancreatic bicarbonate secretion, accelerates gastric emptying of acid into the duodenum, and significantly increases relapse rates.
- **Genetic & Blood Group Predisposition**: Increased incidence in individuals with **Blood Group O** and non-secretor status.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (Gastrinoma)**: Neuroendocrine tumor causing extreme hypergastrinemia, producing severe, multiple, or atypical post-bulbar duodenal ulcers refractory to standard therapy.

---

### Pathology & Anatomical Sites

- **Primary Site**: First part of the duodenum (D₁ / duodenal cap).
- **Kissing Ulcers**: Co-existing anterior and posterior duodenal ulcers facing each other across the lumen.
- **Giant Duodenal Ulcer**: Ulcer diameter >2 cm; usually benign despite extensive induration.
- **Histopathology**: Microscopic examination shows four classic layers from surface to depth:
    1. Surface fibrinopurulent exudate containing neutrophils.
    2. Zone of eosinophilic fibrinoid necrosis.
    3. Active granulation tissue rich in capillaries.
    4. Deep fibrous scar tissue exhibiting **endarteritis obliterans** in blood vessels.

- **_H. pylori_ Urease Cascade**: _H. pylori_ colonizes the gastric antrum and produces urease, converting urea to ammonia. Local ammonia raises pH, removing normal acid-mediated inhibition of G-cells, causing **hypergastrinemia and acid hypersecretion**.
- **Duodenal Gastric Metaplasia**: Chronic acid overload forces the duodenal mucosa to undergo metaplastic transformation into gastric-type columnar epithelium to protect itself.
- **Metaplastic Colonization**: _H. pylori_ colonizes this metaplastic duodenal tissue, initiating active mucosal inflammation (**duodenitis**), leading directly to mucosal breakdown and chronic ulceration.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Epigastric Pain (Hunger Pain)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Gnawing, burning epigastric pain occurring **2–3 hours after meals** or in the middle of the night (between 1:00 AM and 3:00 AM when basal acid output peaks).
- **Food Relief**: Characteristically **relieved by eating food, drinking milk, or taking antacids** (food buffers gastric acid temporarily).
- **Periodicity**: Symptoms occur in distinct episodes lasting weeks or months, followed by symptom-free intervals corresponding to spontaneous ulcer healing and relapse.
- **Weight Changes**: Patient often experiences **weight gain** because frequent eating relieves epigastric discomfort.
- **Vomiting**: Absent in uncomplicated cases; indicates developing **pyloric stenosis / gastric outlet obstruction** if persistent and non-bilious.

#### Signs

- **Pointed Epigastric Tenderness ("Moynihan's Point")**: Localized tenderness on deep palpation in the epigastrium just to the right of the midline.
- **Succussion Splash**: Auscultated over the epigastrium >3–4 hours after a meal in patients with secondary gastric outlet obstruction.

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

```

                                       |
        +------------------------------+------------------------------+
        |                                                             |
  Flexible OGD (Gold Standard)                               Non-Invasive H. pylori Tests
  - Visualizes D1 ulcer crater                               - 13C/14C Urea Breath Test (IOC for eradication)
  - Evaluates pyloric channel deformity                      - Stool Antigen Assay
  - Antral biopsy for CLO / Rapid Urease Test                - Serology (IgG antibodies)
```

#### 1. Endoscopic Evaluation

- **Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (OGD) [Investigation of Choice - IOC]**:
    - Direct visualization of the ulcer crater in D₁, evaluating shape, depth, and surrounding mucosal edema or pyloric scarring.
    - _Note_: Duodenal ulcers are virtually always benign; routine biopsy of the duodenal ulcer edge is unnecessary unless malignancy or Crohn's disease is suspected.
- **Rapid Urease Test (CLO Test)**: Antral mucosal biopsy placed in urea-containing agar to confirm active _H. pylori_infection.

#### 2. Non-Invasive _H. pylori_ Testing

- **Urea Breath Test (¹³C or ¹⁴C UBT) [Gold Standard Non-Invasive Test]**: Sensitivity and specificity >95%; **test of choice to verify eradication** 4–8 weeks post-treatment.
- **Stool Antigen Assay**: Highly accurate alternative for primary diagnosis and post-treatment monitoring.

#### 3. Laboratory & Special Tests

- **Fast Serum Gastrin**: Indicated in refractory, recurrent, or multiple post-bulbar ulcers to rule out **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome** (gastrin level >1000 pg/mL).
- **Serum Calcium & Parathyroid Hormone**: Checked if ZES / MEN-1 syndrome is suspected.

Medical eradication therapy is the absolute mainstay of treatment; elective surgery is now rarely performed.

#### 1. Medical Management [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Lifestyle Modifications**: Absolute cessation of cigarette smoking and complete withdrawal of offending NSAIDs.
- **First-Line _H. pylori_ Eradication Protocol (14-Day Triple Therapy)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)**: Omeprazole 20 mg PO BD (or Pantoprazole 40 mg PO BD).
    - **Amoxicillin**: 1000 mg PO BD.
    - **Clarithromycin**: 500 mg PO BD for **14 days**.
    - _(In penicillin-allergic patients, substitute Metronidazole 400 mg PO BD for Amoxicillin)_.
- **Second-Line Bismuth Quadruple Therapy** (for persistent infection or macrolide resistance):
    - PPI BD + Bismuth Subsalicylate 525 mg QID + Metronidazole 400 mg TDS + Tetracycline 500 mg QID for 14 days.

#### 2. Surgical Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Elective surgery for uncomplicated duodenal ulcer disease has been virtually eliminated by PPIs and eradication therapy. Surgery is indicated strictly for **complications** (Perforation, Bleeding, Obstruction) or rare refractory cases.

##### Historical & Emergency Surgical Options

1. **Highly Selective Vagotomy (HSV / Parietal Cell Vagotomy)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Denervates only the parietal cell-bearing mass (body and fundus) while preserving the "crow's foot" innervation to the antrum and pylorus.
    - _Advantages_: **Lowest operative mortality (<0.2%)** and **lowest side effects (<5% dumping/diarrhea)**; **does NOT require a drainage procedure**.
    - _Disadvantage_: Highest ulcer recurrence rate (2%–10%) if the criminal nerve of Grassi is missed.
2. **Truncal Vagotomy + Drainage (TV + Pyloroplasty / Gastrojejunostomy)**:
    - Divides main vagal trunks at the lower esophagus; requires Heineke-Mikulicz pyloroplasty to prevent gastric stasis.
3. **Truncal Vagotomy + Antrectomy (or Billroth II / Pólya Gastrectomy)**:
    - Maximum acid reduction with lowest recurrence (1%), but carries highest complication rates (20%–40%).

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Perforation (10%–15%)**: Occurs predominantly in **anterior D₁ ulcers**; presents with sudden explosive epigastric pain, board-like rigidity, and subdiaphragmatic free gas on erect CXR.
- **Hemorrhage (15%–20%)**: Occurs predominantly in **posterior D₁ ulcers** eroding into the **gastroduodenal artery**; presents with hematemesis and melena.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction / Pyloric Stenosis (5%)**: Fibrous cicatrization of the duodenal cap; presents with intractable non-bilious vomiting containing undigested food, succussion splash, and hypochloraemic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria.

---

### Prognosis

Medical eradication therapy achieves cure rates >90% with recurrence rates <0.5% per year once _H. pylori_ is successfully eliminated.

---

### Dosage Rule (Eradication & Antisecretory Pharmacotherapy)

#### 1. Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Pantoprazole Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits H⁺/K⁺ ATPase proton pumps in parietal cells, suppressing gastric acid production.
- **Dose**: 40 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet / Intravenous injection
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Features**: First-line acid suppressant in eradication protocols and acute ulcer bleeding.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, long-term hypomagnesemia.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start Day 1 of eradication therapy for 14 days; extend for 4–8 weeks in deep ulcers.

#### 2. Amoxicillin

- **Name**: Amoxicillin Trihydrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Beta-lactam antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis in _H. pylori_.
- **Dose**: 1000 mg (1 g)
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Side Effects**: Rash, diarrhea, anaphylaxis in penicillin allergy.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Take for **14 days**.

#### 3. Clarithromycin

- **Name**: Clarithromycin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Macrolide antibiotic that binds the 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis.
- **Dose**: 500 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, nausea, QT prolongation.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Take for **14 days**.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Duodenal ulcers are **virtually never malignant**. Unlike gastric ulcers (which carry a 1%–5% risk of underlying adenocarcinoma and mandate multiple biopsies), routine endoscopic biopsies of a duodenal ulcer crater are unnecessary unless the ulcer is giant (>2 cm), refractory, or associated with atypical features.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Re-testing for _H. pylori_ eradication using serology immediately after completing 14-day triple therapy.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Circulating anti-_H. pylori_ IgG antibodies persist in the blood for up to 6 months post-eradication, producing false-positive serology. **Post-treatment eradication MUST be verified at 4–8 weeks using ¹³C/¹⁴C Urea Breath Test or Stool Antigen Assay after stopping PPIs for at least 2 weeks**.

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: _H. pylori_ infection (90%–95%) + NSAIDs; acid hypersecretion over metaplastic duodenal mucosa.
- **Classic Pain Pattern**: Burning epigastric hunger pain 2–3 hours post-meal and at night, relieved by food/milk.
- **Complications**: Anterior wall perforation (free air on CXR), posterior wall bleeding (gastroduodenal artery erosion), pyloric stenosis.
- **Medical Gold Standard**: 14-day Triple Eradication Therapy (PPI BD + Amoxicillin 1 g BD + Clarithromycin 500 mg BD).
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: Emergency omental patch repair for perforation; dual endoscopic hemostasis or vessel under-running for bleeding; Highly Selective Vagotomy for rare refractory cases.

### One-Line Exam Opener
