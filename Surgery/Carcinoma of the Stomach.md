# Carcinoma of the Stomach (Gastric Cancer)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Carcinoma of the stomach** (gastric cancer) is a primary malignant epithelial neoplasm arising from the mucosal lining of the stomach, overwhelmingly dominated by **adenocarcinoma** (>95% of cases). It is one of the leading causes of cancer mortality worldwide, characterized by marked geographic variation, late clinical presentation, and aggressive local, lymphatic, hematogenous, and transcoelomic dissemination.

---

### Premalignant Conditions & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Gastric carcinogenesis is a complex, multifactorial process involving environmental, infectious, mucosal-pathological, and genetic risk factors.

```
                          RISK FACTORS FOR GASTRIC CANCER
                                         |
     +-------------------+---------------+---------------+-------------------+
     |                   |                               |                   |
Infectious &             Environmental &                 Premalignant        Genetic &
Inflammatory             Dietary Factors                 Conditions          Familial Factors
* H. pylori (distal)     * Smoked/salted food            * Atrophic gastritis* Blood Group A
* Epstein-Barr Virus     * N-nitroso compounds           * Pernicious anemia * CDH1 gene (diffuse)
  (EBV)                  * Antioxidant deficiency        * Gastric adenoma   * Lynch syndrome
                         * Smoking & Alcohol             * Billroth II stoma * FAP / Li-Fraumeni
```

#### 1. Infectious & Inflammatory Drivers

- **_Helicobacter pylori_ Infection**: Classified by the WHO as a Class 1 human carcinogen. Chronic _H. pylori_ antral gastritis triggers a cascade: active inflammation → chronic atrophic gastritis → intestinal metaplasia → dysplasia → adenocarcinoma.
    - _Note_: _H. pylori_ is strongly associated with **distal gastric cancer** (body and antrum), but **NOT** with proximal gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) cancer.
- **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)**: Associated with ~10% of gastric carcinomas (defines a distinct molecular subtype).

#### 2. Environmental & Dietary Factors

- **Preserved & Salted Foods**: High intake of smoked fish, salted meats, pickled vegetables, and foods rich in **N-nitroso compounds** and nitrites.
- **Lack of Refrigeration & Fresh Produce**: Diets deficient in fresh fruits, vegetables, and antioxidants (Vitamin C, Vitamin E, selenium). Domestic refrigeration dramatically lowers incidence by preventing bacterial conversion of nitrates to carcinogenic nitrites.
- **Tobacco & Alcohol**: Cigarette smoking increases risk by 1.5 to 2-fold; heavy alcohol consumption exerts a synergistic carcinogenic effect.
- **Occupational / Industrial Dust**: Increased risk among workers exposed to rubber, coal, and asbestos dust.
- **Socioeconomic Status**: Distal/body gastric cancer is significantly more prevalent in **lower socioeconomic groups**, whereas proximal GEJ adenocarcinoma predominantly affects higher socioeconomic groups.

#### 3. Premalignant Conditions & Previous Gastric Pathology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Chronic Autoimmune Atrophic Gastritis (Type A Gastritis) & Pernicious Anemia**: Achlorhydria leads to bacterial overgrowth and endogenously synthesized N-nitroso compounds, carrying a 3 to 6-fold increased risk.
- **Gastric Adenomatous Polyps**: True adenomatous polyps are premalignant precursors and mandate complete endoscopic excision (in contrast, fundic gland and metaplastic polyps have no proven malignant potential).
- **Previous Gastric Resection / Peptic Ulcer Surgery**: Partial gastrectomy (e.g., Billroth II, Pólya) or gastroenterostomy carries a **4-fold increased risk** of **gastric remnant/stump carcinoma**.
    - _Mechanism_: Persistent alkaline bile reflux causes chronic reflux gastritis and intestinal metaplasia.
    - _Lag Period_: Typically requires a latency period of **≥10 years** post-surgery.
- **Ménétrier's Disease**: Giant hypertrophic gastropathy with protein-losing enteropathy (up to 10% risk of malignant transformation).

#### 4. Genetic & Familial Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Blood Group A**: Classically associated with the **diffuse type** of gastric cancer.
- **Hereditary Diffuse Gastric Cancer (HDGC)**: Autosomal dominant germline mutation in the **CDH1 gene**(encoding the cell adhesion protein **E-cadherin**), conferring an 80% lifetime risk of diffuse gastric adenocarcinoma.
- **Inherited Cancer Syndromes**: Lynch syndrome (HNPCC), Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP / APC gene), Li-Fraumeni syndrome (p53 mutation), and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome.

---

### Etiology & Pathogenesis

Gastric adenocarcinoma develops via two distinct etiologic pathways depending on anatomical location and histologic type:

1. **Distal / Non-cardia Gastric Cancer**: Geographically declining; driven primarily by environmental factors, _H. pylori_ infection, dietary salt, and chronic mucosal inflammation leading to the multistep **correa cascade** (Normal Mucosa → Chronic Gastritis → Atrophic Gastritis → Intestinal Metaplasia → Dysplasia → Adenocarcinoma).
2. **Proximal / Cardia / GEJ Adenocarcinoma**: Rapidly rising in incidence in Western countries; driven by central obesity, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and Barrett's esophagus, independent of _H. pylori_.

### Classification & Pathology [⭐ High-Yield]

Gastric cancer is categorized using four major pathological and morphological classification systems:

```
                            CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS
                                       |
    +------------------+---------------+---------------+------------------+
    |                  |                               |                  |
Laurén Classification  Borrmann Classification         Japanese EGC       TCGA Molecular
(Intestinal vs Diffuse) (Advanced Gastric Cancer)       Classification     Classification
```

#### 1. Laurén Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

The Laurén classification divides gastric adenocarcinoma into two primary histological subtypes with distinct clinical and prognostic behaviors:

|Pathological Feature|Intestinal Type|Diffuse Type|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Epidemiology**|Environmental origin; high-incidence areas|Familial / Genetic origin; uniform incidence|
|**Demographics**|Males > Females; older age group (>60 years)|Females > Males; younger age group (<50 years)|
|**Precursor Lesions**|Arises from gastric atrophy & intestinal metaplasia|Arises directly from native gastric mucosa|
|**Histopathology**|Forms distinct tubular/papillary glands|Poorly differentiated; cohesive cells lost; **Signet-ring cells (+)**|
|**Growth Pattern**|Cohesive, polypoid, or well-demarcated ulcers|Infiltrative, transmural spreading; **Linitis Plastica**|
|**Primary Spread**|**Hematogenous spread** (to liver)|**Transmural / Lymphatic / Transcoelomic spread**|
|**Key Mutations**|Microsatellite Instability (MSI), APC, p53, p16|**Decreased E-cadherin (CDH1)**, p53, p16|
|**Prognosis**|Relatively better prognosis|**Worse prognosis**|

#### 2. Japanese Classification of Early Gastric Cancer (EGC) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Definition**: **Early Gastric Cancer (EGC)** is defined as adenocarcinoma confined strictly to the **mucosa (T1a)** or **submucosa (T1b)**, **regardless of regional lymph node status (T1, any N)**.
- **Morphological Types**:
    - **Type I**: Protruded (exophytic lesion projecting into lumen).
    - **Type IIa**: Elevated (slightly raised above surrounding mucosa).
    - **Type IIb**: Flat (level with surrounding mucosa).
    - **Type IIc**: Depressed (slightly erosion below mucosa; **most common variant**).
    - **Type III**: Excavated (deep ulceration breaching muscularis mucosae).

#### 3. Borrmann Classification of Advanced Gastric Cancer [⭐ High-Yield]

Applies to advanced cancers invading the muscularis propria or deeper:

- **Type I**: Polypoid or fungating mass with clear borders.
- **Type II**: Ulcerating lesion with elevated, sharply demarcated borders.
- **Type III**: Ulcerating lesion with diffuse, ill-defined infiltrating borders into surrounding gastric wall.
- **Type IV**: Diffuse, rigid, circumferential infiltration involving the entire stomach (**Linitis Plastica** or "Leather Bottle Stomach"; **worst prognosis**).
- **Type V**: Unclassified advanced cancer.

#### 4. TCGA Molecular Classification

Modern next-generation sequencing divides gastric cancer into 4 molecular subtypes:

1. **Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) positive**: High PIK3CA mutations, extreme DNA hypermethylation; best response to immunotherapy.
2. **Microsatellite Instable (MSI-High)**: Hypermutated; associated with older age and intestinal histology.
3. **Genomically Stable (GS)**: Enriched for diffuse histology, CDH1 and RHOA mutations; worst prognosis.
4. **Chromosomal Instability (CIN)**: Marked receptor tyrosine kinase amplifications (HER2, EGFR, VEGFA); responsive to targeted therapies.

- **Direct Extension**: Tumors breach the serosa to directly invade the pancreas, transverse mesocolon, liver, diaphragm, anterior abdominal wall, or lower esophagus.
- **Lymphatic Dissemination**: Follows predictable tiers (Japanese Nodal Stations 1 to 16):
    - **Virchow's Node (Troisier's Sign)**: Left supraclavicular lymph node enlargement due to tumor emboli tracking via the thoracic duct.
    - **Irish Node**: Left anterior axillary lymph node enlargement.
- **Transcoelomic (Peritoneal) Seeding**: Occurs once the tumor breaches the serosa:
    - **Krukenberg Tumor**: Bilateral ovarian metastases containing mucin-secreting signet-ring cells (spread via retrograde lymphatics > transcoelomic drop metastases).
    - **Sister Mary Joseph's Nodule**: Indurated periumbilical metastatic deposit via the falciform/round ligament.
    - **Blumer's Shelf**: Rigid tumor deposit in the rectovesical or rectouterine (pouch of Douglas) pouch felt on digital rectal exam.
- **Hematogenous Spread**: Portal venous embolization to the **liver** (most common site of distant metastases), followed by lungs, bones, and brain.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Clinical Mnemonic: L-O-A-D-S

- **L** – **L**ump (palpable epigastric mass).
- **O** – **O**utlet obstruction (gastric outlet obstruction with non-bilious vomiting).
- **A** – **A**nemia, Anorexia, & weight loss.
- **D** – **D**yspepsia / Neodyspepsia (new-onset refractory GERD/dyspepsia in patient >55 years).
- **S** – **S**ilent presentation in early stages.

#### Symptoms

- **Early Stages**: Usually asymptomatic or presents with non-specific "neodyspepsia," epigastric discomfort, or indigestion.
- **Advanced Stages**:
    - **Profound Weight Loss & Anorexia**: Most common presenting symptom in advanced disease.
    - **Persistent Epigastric Pain**: Constant, gnawing pain radiating to the back (indicates retroperitoneal pancreatic infiltration).
    - **Early Satiety & Postprandial Fullness**: Caused by loss of gastric compliance (especially in linitis plastica).
    - **Nausea & Vomiting**: Antral/pyloric lesions cause **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO)** with vomiting of undigested food eaten hours earlier.
    - **Dysphagia**: Proximal cardia / GEJ lesions present with progressive solid-to-liquid dysphagia.
    - **Hematemesis & Melena**: Chronic occult bleeding producing microcytic anemia, or frank hematemesis.

#### Signs

- **General Inspection**: Cachexia, pallor, temporal wasting, and jaundice (liver metastases or biliary tree obstruction).
- **Abdominal Examination**: Palpable, firm, irregular, non-tender epigastric mass; visible gastric peristalsis; ascites (peritoneal carcinomatosis).
- **Metastatic Physical Signposts [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. _Virchow's Node (Troisier's Sign)_: Left supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.
    2. _Irish Node_: Left axillary lymphadenopathy.
    3. _Sister Mary Joseph's Nodule_: Periumbilical nodular induration.
    4. _Krukenberg Tumor_: Palpable pelvic/adnexal masses.
    5. _Blumer's Shelf_: Palpable shelf-like mass on digital rectal examination.
    6. _Trousseau's Sign (Migratory Thrombophlebitis)_: Recurrent venous thrombosis secondary to tumor-induced hypercoagulability.
    7. _Paraneoplastic Dermatological Signs_: **Leser-Trélat sign** (sudden eruptive seborrheic keratoses), Acanthosis nigricans, and Tripe palms.

### Staging (AJCC / UICC 8th Edition TNM) [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                             TNM STAGING SYSTEM
                                     |
        +----------------------------+----------------------------+
        |                            |                            |
  Primary Tumor (T)          Regional Nodes (N)          Distant Metastasis (M)
  Tis: In situ               N0: 0 nodes                 M0: No distant mets
  T1a: Lamina propria/MM     N1: 1–2 nodes               M1: Distant mets
  T1b: Submucosa             N2: 3–6 nodes                   (Liver, peritoneum,
  T2:  Muscularis propria    N3a: 7–15 nodes                  extra-abdominal)
  T3:  Subserosa             N3b: ≥16 nodes
  T4a: Perforates serosa
  T4b: Adjacent structures
```

#### 1. Primary Tumor (T)

- **Tis**: Carcinoma in situ (intraepithelial tumor without invasion of lamina propria).
- **T1**: Tumor involves lamina propria, muscularis mucosae, or submucosa.
    - **T1a**: Tumor invades lamina propria or muscularis mucosae (**Intramucosal**).
    - **T1b**: Tumor invades submucosa (**Submucosal**).
- **T2**: Tumor invades muscularis propria.
- **T3**: Tumor invades subserosal connective tissue without invading serosa or adjacent structures.
- **T4**: Tumor involves serosa or adjacent structures.
    - **T4a**: Tumor perforates visceral peritoneum (serosa).
    - **T4b**: Tumor directly invades adjacent structures (pancreas, liver, colon, spleen, abdominal wall).

#### 2. Regional Lymph Nodes (N)

_(Note: Pathological N-staging requires a minimum retrieval of **≥16 lymph nodes**)_.

- **N0**: No regional lymph node metastasis.
- **N1**: Metastasis in **1 or 2** regional lymph nodes.
- **N2**: Metastasis in **3 to 6** regional lymph nodes.
- **N3**: Metastasis in **7 or more** regional lymph nodes.
    - **N3a**: Metastasis in **7 to 15** regional lymph nodes.
    - **N3b**: Metastasis in **16 or more** regional lymph nodes.

#### 3. Distant Metastasis (M)

- **M0**: No distant metastasis.
- **M1**: Distant metastasis present (includes hepatic, lung, bone, peritoneal seeding, or non-regional lymph node involvement such as para-aortic or retropancreatic nodes).

### Investigations (Diagnostic & Surgical Staging Panel) [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                       INVESTIGATION WORK-UP
                                 |
     +---------------------------+---------------------------+
     |                                                       |
Primary Diagnosis                                   Staging & Operability
* Upper GI Endoscopy + Biopsy                       * CECT Abdomen & Pelvis (Overall)
  (Gold Standard: 6–10 cores)                       * Endoscopic Ultrasound (T & N)
                                                    * PET-CT (Distant / Occult Mets)
                                                    * Diagnostic Laparoscopy (Peritoneum)
```

#### 1. Primary Diagnostic Modality

- **Flexible Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy + Biopsy [Gold Standard Diagnostic Test]**:
    - Allows direct visualization of the tumor, evaluation of location/extent, and mucosal sampling.
    - **Gastric Ulcer Biopsy Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]**: **Multiple targeted biopsies (6 to 10 cores)** taken from the raised, rolled edges and base of any gastric ulcer are mandatory to exclude malignancy.

#### 2. Staging & Preoperative Assessment Modalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS) [Investigation of Choice for T and N Staging]**:
    - Highest resolution modality for evaluating **depth of wall invasion (T-stage, 90% accuracy)** by depicting the 5-layered structure of the gastric wall, and distinguishing early (T1a vs T1b) from advanced disease.
    - Guides Fine Needle Aspiration (EUS-FNA) of perigastric lymph nodes.
- **Contrast-Enhanced Computed Tomography (CECT Abdomen, Pelvis & Thorax) [Investigation of Choice for Overall Staging]**:
    - Evaluates gastric wall thickening, local organ invasion, regional lymphadenopathy, and distant hematogenous metastases (liver, lungs).
- **18F-FDG PET-CT Scan**:
    - Highly sensitive for detecting occult distant nodal and visceral metastases in ~10% of patients considered for curative surgery.
    - Evaluates metabolic response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
- **Diagnostic Laparoscopy ± Peritoneal Washing Cytology [Mandatory Preoperative Step]**:
    - Most sensitive modality for detecting **occult peritoneal carcinomatosis and subcentimeter liver surface deposits** missed on CT/PET imaging, preventing unnecessary open laparotomy in non-resectable Stage IV disease.

#### 1. Organ-Preserving Endoscopic Resection (For Early Gastric Cancer) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Endoscopic Mucosal Resection (EMR) / Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection (ESD)**:
    - **Indications**: Intramucosal carcinoma (**T1a**), well-differentiated, lesion size ≤2 cm, non-ulcerated, without lymphovascular invasion.
    - _Rationale_: T1a intramucosal cancers carry a negligible risk of lymph node metastasis (<1%).

#### 2. Surgical Resection (Primary Treatment for Resectable Disease) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Surgical Goal**: **R0 Resection** (complete macroscopic and microscopic clearance with negative resection margins).
- **Resection Margins**: **Proximal margin ≥5 cm** from gross tumor edge; distal margin includes the pylorus and first part of duodenum.
- **Surgical Options by Anatomical Site**:
    1. **Distal / Antral Cancer**: **Subtotal Gastrectomy** (60%–70% of stomach removed) with gastrojejunostomy (Roux-en-Y or Billroth II) reconstruction.
    2. **Body, Proximal, or Diffuse Cancer / Linitis Plastica**: **Radical Total Gastrectomy** with **Roux-en-Y Esophagojejunostomy** reconstruction.

```
                  GASTRIC CANCER SURGICAL SELECTION
                                  |
        +-------------------------+-------------------------+
        |                                                   |
  Antral / Distal Cancer                            Body / Diffuse / Proximal Cancer
  (Subtotal Gastrectomy)                            (Total Gastrectomy)
  - Removes 60–70% stomach                          - En-bloc removal of stomach & omenta
  - Proximal margin ≥5 cm                           - Roux-en-Y Esophagojejunostomy
  - Billroth II / Roux-en-Y                           reconstruction
```

#### 3. Surgical Lymph Node Clearance (D1 vs D2 Lymphadenectomy) [⭐ High-Yield]

Japanese Research Society / JGCA Nodal Stations (Stations 1 to 11):

- **D1 Lymphadenectomy**: Removal of perigastric lymph nodes only (**Stations 1 to 6**).
- **D2 Lymphadenectomy [Standard Gold Standard]**: Complete clearance of **N1 perigastric nodes (Stations 1 to 6) PLUS N2 extra-perigastric nodes along major arterial trunks (Stations 7 to 11)**:
    - _Station 7_: Left gastric artery nodes.
    - _Station 8a_: Common hepatic artery nodes.
    - _Station 9_: Coeliac axis nodes.
    - _Station 10_: Splenic hilar nodes.
    - _Station 11_: Splenic artery nodes.
- _Modern Paradigm_: Standard D2 dissection is performed as a **spleen-preserving and pancreas-preserving procedure** to minimize operative morbidity.

#### 4. Systemic Chemotherapy Protocols [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Perioperative Chemotherapy (Gold Standard - FLOT Regimen)**:
    - Administered as **4 cycles preoperatively (neoadjuvant)** followed by **4 cycles postoperatively (adjuvant)**.
    - **FLOT Regimen**: 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) + Leucovorin + Oxaliplatin + Docetaxel.
    - _(Replaced the older ECF regimen: Epirubicin + Cisplatin + 5-FU based on FLOT4 trial evidence demonstrating superior overall survival)_.
- **Targeted & Immunotherapy Modalities**:
    - **HER2-Positive Cancers (<20% of cases)**: Addition of **Trastuzumab (Herceptin)** to first-line chemotherapy.
    - **PD-L1 Positive / MSI-High Cancers**: Addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors (**Pembrolizumab** or **Nivolumab**).

#### 5. Palliative Interventions (For Incurable / Stage IV Disease)

- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction**: Palliative **Gastrojejunostomy** bypass or endoscopic deployment of Through-The-Scope (TTS) **Self-Expanding Metal Stents (SEMS)**.
- **Palliative Radiotherapy**: Indicated for controlling persistent tumor bleeding or painful bone metastases.

---

### Complications

- **Preoperative / Tumor-Related**: Gastric outlet obstruction, massive upper GI hemorrhage, gastric perforation, and severe malnutrition/cachexia.
- **Postoperative Complications**: Anastomotic leak (esophagojejunostomy leak), duodenal stump blowout, intra-abdominal abscess, pancreatitis, and wound infection.
- **Long-Term Sequelae**: **Vitamin B₁₂ deficiency** (requires lifelong parenterally administered B₁₂ replacement due to total loss of intrinsic factor), iron-deficiency anemia, dumping syndrome, and weight loss.

---

### Prognosis

- **Overall Survival**: Poor in Western countries (5-year survival ~25%–30%) due to late presentation; significantly higher in Japan (5-year survival 50%–70%) due to nationwide endoscopic screening programs detecting early gastric cancer.
- **Most Important Prognostic Factors [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. **Overall Depth of Invasion (T-stage)**: Most critical overall prognostic factor.
    2. **Lymph Node Status (N-stage)**: Single most important prognostic factor in **operable/resected disease**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Chemotherapy & Targeted Agents)

#### 1. FLOT Regimen (Perioperative Chemotherapy)

- **Components**:
    - **Docetaxel**: 50 mg/m² IV infusion on Day 1.
    - **Oxaliplatin**: 85 mg/m² IV infusion on Day 1.
    - **Leucovorin (Folinic Acid)**: 200 mg/m² IV infusion on Day 1.
    - **5-Fluorouracil (5-FU)**: 2600 mg/m² continuous 24-hour IV infusion on Day 1.
- **Frequency**: Administered every 2 weeks (14-day cycle) for **4 cycles preoperatively** and **4 cycles postoperatively**.
- **Features**: Standard perioperative regimen demonstrating superior survival compared to ECF.
- **Side Effects**: Neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, peripheral neuropathy, mucositis, diarrhea.

#### 2. Trastuzumab (Herceptin)

- **Name**: Trastuzumab
- **Mechanism of Action**: Recombinant humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting the extracellular domain of the Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 (HER2/neu).
- **Dose**: Loading dose **8 mg/kg IV**, followed by maintenance dose **6 mg/kg IV**.
- **Frequency**: Administered every 3 weeks in combination with chemotherapy.
- **Indication**: HER2-positive metastatic or advanced gastric adenocarcinoma.
- **Side Effects**: Cardiotoxicity (left ventricular ejection fraction decline, heart failure), infusion-related reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start concurrently with chemotherapy; continue until disease progression or cardiotoxicity.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In patients presenting with suspected gastric carcinoma, **never accept a single negative biopsy report to rule out malignancy in an ulcerated lesion**. A malignant gastric ulcer can partially heal when treated with Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs), creating a false impression of a benign peptic ulcer. **All gastric ulcers MUST undergo 6 to 10 targeted biopsies from the edges and base, and must be followed endoscopically to complete healing with repeat biopsies**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing a splenectomy or pancreatic tail resection routinely during D2 lymphadenectomy for gastric cancer.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Historically, splenectomy was routinely performed to clear Station 10 (splenic hilum) nodes. Modern evidence demonstrates that routine splenectomy and distal pancreatectomy significantly increase postoperative morbidity and mortality without improving survival. **Standard D2 lymphadenectomy is strictly a spleen-preserving and pancreas-preserving procedure**.

    3. _Roux-en-Y Anastomosis_ — Esophagojejunostomy loop with jejunojejunostomy 40–50 cm distally.

### Quick Revision

- **Primary Pathology**: Adenocarcinoma (>95%); Laurén classification divides into Intestinal (gland-forming, hematogenous) and Diffuse (signet-ring, linitis plastica, worse prognosis).
- **Early Gastric Cancer (EGC)**: Confined to mucosa (T1a) or submucosa (T1b) regardless of LN status; high 5-year survival (>90%).
- **Metastatic Signposts**: Virchow's node (supraclavicular), Irish node (axillary), Sister Mary Joseph's nodule (umbilical), Krukenberg tumor (ovarian), Blumer's shelf (pelvic pouch).
- **Diagnostic Gold Standard**: Upper GI endoscopy + 6–10 biopsies; EUS for T/N staging; CECT + Laparoscopy for overall staging.
- **Surgical Management**: R0 resection (5-cm proximal margin); Subtotal gastrectomy for distal cancer; Total gastrectomy for proximal/diffuse cancer; **Spleen-preserving D2 lymphadenectomy (Stations 1–11)**.
- **Systemic Therapy**: Perioperative **FLOT regimen** (4 cycles pre-op + 4 cycles post-op); Trastuzumab for HER2-positive cases.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Gastric carcinoma is a highly aggressive primary malignant epithelial neoplasm dominated by adenocarcinoma, classified pathologically by the Laurén and Borrmann systems, staged using endoscopic ultrasound and cross-sectional CT, and managed definitively through R0 radical gastrectomy with spleen-preserving D2 lymphadenectomy combined with perioperative FLOT chemotherapy._
