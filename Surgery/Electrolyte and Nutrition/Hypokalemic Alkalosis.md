# Hypokalemic Alkalosis

### Definition

**Hypokalemic alkalosis** (hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis) is a systemic acid-base and electrolyte derangement characterized by a plasma pH >7.45, serum potassium <3.5 mmol/L, and serum chloride <95 mmol/L, typically resulting from persistent loss of gastric hydrochloric acid (HCl) or renal mineralocorticoid excess.

---

- **Etiology in Surgery**: Classically caused by severe vomiting in **gastric outlet obstruction** (e.g., infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis, cicatricial peptic ulcer disease) or prolonged nasogastric suction, leading to massive loss of gastric HCl, water, and KCl.
- **Mechanism of Paradoxical Aciduria [⭐ High-Yield]**: Loss of Cl⁻ and volume depletes extracellular fluid, stimulating aldosterone release to reabsorb Na⁺. To retain Na⁺, distal renal tubules initially excrete K⁺; as hypokalemia worsens, the kidney excretes H⁺ in exchange for Na⁺, producing **paradoxical aciduria**.
- **Clinical Manifestations**: Severe dehydration, muscle weakness, lethargy, carpopedal spasms/tetany (due to reduced circulating ionized Ca²⁺ in an alkalotic environment), hypoventilation, and paralytic ileus.
- **Management Protocol**: Resuscitate with **0.9% Normal Saline + KCl** supplementation. Chloride replacement allows the kidney to excrete excess HCO₃⁻, while K⁺ replacement stops renal H⁺ wasting and restores normal urine pH.
