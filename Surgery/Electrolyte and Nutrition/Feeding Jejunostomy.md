# Feeding Jejunostomy

### Definition

A **feeding jejunostomy** is a surgically or endoscopically created opening directly into the proximal jejunum (typically 15–20 cm distal to the ligament of Treitz) through the anterior abdominal wall to deliver enteral nutrition, bypassing the upper gastrointestinal tract (stomach and duodenum).

---

- **Indications [⭐ High-Yield]**: Indicated for long-term enteral nutrition when feeding via the stomach is impossible, unsafe, or contraindicated:
    - _Gastric / Duodenal Pathology_: Gastric cancer, total/subtotal gastrectomy, gastric outlet obstruction, or severe corrosive gastric burns.
    - _Severe Gastric Motility Disorders_: Intractable gastroparesis or severe gastroesophageal reflux with recurrent aspiration risk.
    - _Major Upper GI Surgery_: Done routinely as an adjunctive procedure during major resections (e.g., Whipple's pancreaticoduodenectomy, total gastrectomy, oesophagectomy) for early postoperative enteral feeding.
- **Surgical / Procedural Techniques**:
    1. **Witzel Jejunostomy** _(Open Surgical - Most Common)_: A catheter is inserted into the jejunum and buried in a serosal tunnel (5–6 cm) formed by lamberts sutures along the antimesenteric border to prevent leakage.
    2. **Needle Catheter Jejunostomy (NCJ)**: Micro-catheter inserted via a needle track into the jejunum using a Seldinger-like technique.
    3. **Roux-en-Y Jejunostomy**: Permanent jejunal limb fashioned to prevent peristomal regurgitation.
    4. **Direct Percutaneous Endoscopic Jejunostomy (DPEJ)**: Endoscopic placement directly into the proximal jejunum.
- **Complications**: Mechanical occlusion or dislodgement of the tube, peristomal leakage, bowel obstruction (due to volvulus/kinking around the jejunostomy site), bowel ischemia/necrosis, and **diarrhoea/cramping** (due to rapid infusion of hyperosmolar feeds directly into the jejunum without gastric reservoir function).

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Artificial enteral access into proximal jejunum (15–20 cm distal to ligament of Treitz).
- **Key Indication**: Post-gastrectomy, upper GI cancer, or as part of major upper GI surgery (Oesophagectomy/Whipple's).
- **Classic Technique**: Witzel Jejunostomy (serosal tunnel technique).
- **Key Complication**: Feed-induced osmotic diarrhoea and catheter dislodgement/kinking.

_A feeding jejunostomy is an artificial enteral access tube surgically or endoscopically placed into the proximal jejunum distal to the ligament of Treitz to provide nutritional support while completely bypassing the upper gastrointestinal tract._
