# Feeding Gastrostomy

### Definition

A **feeding gastrostomy** is a surgically or endoscopically created artificial opening (stoma) directly into the anterior wall of the stomach through the anterior abdominal wall to deliver enteral nutrition, fluids, and medications, completely bypassing the oral cavity, pharynx, and esophagus.

---

- **Indications**: Indicated for long-term enteral nutritional support (>4 to 6 weeks) in patients with a functional gastrointestinal tract who cannot swallow due to:
    - _Esophageal Obstruction_: Inoperable or obstructing carcinoma of the esophagus or hypopharynx, corrosive esophageal strictures.
    - _Neurological Dysphagia_: Bulbar palsy, cerebrovascular accident (stroke), motor neuron disease, traumatic brain injury, or severe head and neck trauma.
- **Surgical / Procedural Types [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. **Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG)** _(Most Common / Method of Choice)_: Performed endoscopically using the "pull" or "push" technique under local anesthesia and conscious sedation.
    2. **Stamm Gastrostomy** _(Open Surgical)_: Temporary gastrostomy using two concentric pursetring sutures placed on the anterior gastric wall with an invaginated Foley or Malecot catheter, brought out through a separate stab incision.
    3. **Janeway Gastrostomy** _(Open Surgical)_: Permanent, mucosally lined gastric tube flap fashioned from the anterior gastric wall and sutured to the skin as a mature stoma.
    4. **Laparoscopic Gastrostomy**: Performed under direct laparoscopic visualization when endoscopy is technically impossible.
- **Complications**: Peristomal wound infection, leakage of gastric contents causing chemical peritonitis/excoriation, tube displacement/dislodgement, **Buried Bumper Syndrome** (internal bumper migrates into the gastric wall/abdominal wall due to excessive tension), and accidental colonic perforation.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Artificial stoma into the stomach for long-term (>4–6 weeks) enteral nutrition.
- **Method of Choice**: Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG).
- **Open Surgical Types**: Stamm (temporary, pursestring sutures) vs. Janeway (permanent, mucosally lined tube).
- **Key Complication**: Buried Bumper Syndrome (migration of internal retainer into gastric wall).

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A feeding gastrostomy is an artificial enteral access tube surgically or endoscopically placed through the anterior abdominal wall into the stomach to provide long-term (>4 weeks) nutritional support in patients with severe dysphagia or proximal gastrointestinal obstruction._
