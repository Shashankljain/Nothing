# Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)** is the intravenous administration of complete daily nutritional requirements—comprising carbohydrates, amino acids, lipid emulsions, essential electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements, and water—directly into the central venous circulation, completely bypassing the gastrointestinal tract. It is indicated when enteral feeding is impossible, inadequate, or contraindicated.

---

### Etiology / Indications for TPN [⭐ High-Yield]

TPN is indicated when the gastrointestinal tract cannot be safely or effectively utilized for nutrient absorption for >5 to 7 days:

1. **Short Bowel Syndrome (Intestinal Failure)**: Following massive small bowel resection where remnant bowel is <100–200 cm (especially with an end-stoma), resulting in intractable malabsorption, dehydration, and malnutrition.
2. **High-Output Enterocutaneous / Gastrointestinal Fistulae**: Proximal or high-output fistulae (>500 mL/24 hours) where enteral nutrition increases fistula discharge and prevents spontaneous closure.
3. **Prolonged Paralytic Ileus & Mechanical Bowel Obstruction**: Ileus lasting >72 hours postoperatively or mechanical obstruction where transit and absorption are absent.
4. **Severe Acute Pancreatitis**: Initial phase of severe necrotizing pancreatitis with paralytic ileus, gastric outlet obstruction, or enteral feed intolerance.
5. **Severe Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**: Acute severe exacerbations of Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis with severe malabsorption, deep fistulation, or strictures requiring complete bowel rest.
6. **Complicated Postoperative State**: Anastomotic leaks (e.g., post-oesophagectomy or bariatric staple line leaks), chylous ascites/chylothorax (>500–1000 mL/day), or severe intra-abdominal sepsis.

---

### Classification, Routes & Composition [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Routes of TPN Administration

- **Central Venous Route (Standard / Best Option) [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Highly hyperosmolar TPN mixtures (>850–1000 mOsm/L) must be delivered into a high-flow, large-calibre central vein (tip positioned in the lower ⅓ of the Superior Vena Cava [SVC] or at the atriocaval junction) to prevent rapid venous thrombosis and thrombophlebitis.
    - _Access Vessels_:
        - **Subclavian Vein**: Lowest risk of thrombosis and catheter infection, but highest risk of insertion pneumothorax.
        - **Internal Jugular Vein**: Easiest insertion under ultrasound guidance; moderate infection risk.
        - **Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter (PICC)**: Inserted via basilic, cephalic, or brachial veins of the arm; suitable for medium to long-term TPN (2–3 months).
        - **Tunnelled Line (Hickman Line) / Implantable Port**: Preferred for long-term or Home Parenteral Nutrition (HPN).
        - **Femoral Vein**: Strictly avoided due to maximum risk of line infection and deep vein thrombosis.
- **Peripheral Venous Route (Peripheral Parenteral Nutrition - PPN)**:
    - Restricted to low-osmolarity feeds (<800 mOsm/L), short durations (<14 days), and neutral pH using soft polyurethane cannulae changed every few days; carries a high rate of superficial thrombophlebitis.

#### 2. Chemical Composition of TPN Solution

- **Delivery System**: Provided as a "3-in-1" total nutrient admixture (a 3-litre light-protected bag containing amino acids, glucose, and lipid emulsion) or "2-in-1" (amino acids + glucose, with separate lipid infusion).
- **Caloric Ratio & Distribution [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Energy ratio: 150 to 250 kcal per gram of protein nitrogen.
    - Proportional composition: **20% Protein : 30% Fat : 50% Carbohydrate**.
- **Macronutrient Components**:
    - _Carbohydrates_: Glucose (dextrose); provides 4 kcal/g. Minimum 100–200 g/day to meet CNS requirements and prevent muscle catabolism.
    - _Lipids_: Long-chain or mixed-chain triglyceride emulsions (soybean, olive, fish oil, MCT); provides 9 kcal/g. Prevents essential fatty acid deficiency (linoleic/linolenic acid).
    - _Proteins / Nitrogen_: Crystalline L-amino acid solutions; provides 4 kcal/g. Nitrogen intake = 0.15 to 0.25 g N/kg/day (equivalent to 1.0–1.5 g protein/kg/day).
- **Micronutrients & Electrolytes**:
    - _Electrolytes_: Daily requirements include Sodium (0.9–1.2 mmol/kg/day), Potassium (1 mmol/kg/day), Calcium (5 mmol/day), Magnesium (1 mmol/day), and Phosphate (20–30 mmol/day, crucial for glucose phosphorylation).
    - _Vitamins & Trace Elements_: Daily water-soluble (B, C) and fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) vitamins. Folic acid (15 mg 1–2 times/week) and periodic Vitamin B₁₂. Trace elements include Zinc, Copper, Selenium, Chromium, and Manganese.

- **Substrate Utilization**: TPN provides energy and amino acids directly to systemic circulation, halting the auto-cannibalistic breakdown of skeletal muscle protein (which loses 10–20 g N/day in surgical sepsis) and preserving hepatic visceral protein synthesis.
- **Refeeding Mechanism**: In starvation/malnutrition, cells are depleted of electrolytes. Introducing high-carbohydrate TPN stimulates a sudden insulin surge, driving phosphate, potassium, and magnesium into cells, precipitating life-threatening **hypophosphatemia** and fluid overload.

### Clinical Features & Monitoring [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Daily Clinical & Laboratory Schedule

- **Vital Signs & Weight**: Daily pulse, BP, temperature, and body weight. _Clinical Pearl_: A body weight gain of **>1 kg/day** is the earliest sign of fluid retention and overfeeding.
- **Fluid Balance**: Strict hourly input and output charting (measuring urine, stoma, fistula, and NG aspirate losses).
- **Daily Biochemistry**: Serum Na⁺, K⁺, urea, creatinine, blood glucose, serum phosphate, and magnesium.

#### Weekly to Fortnightly Schedule

- Full blood count, liver function tests (bilirubin, ALP, AST, ALT), serum calcium, albumin, triglycerides, and C-reactive protein.
- Micronutrient panel (Zinc, Copper, Selenium, Vitamin D, Vitamin B₁₂, Ferritin) every 1 to 3 months in long-term TPN.

### Treatment / Management / Administration

1. **Catheter Insertion & Verification**:
    - Insert central catheter under strict aseptic conditions.
    - Obtain an immediate **post-insertion Chest Radiograph (CXR)** before starting TPN to confirm line tip position in the lower SVC/atriocaval junction and to exclude pneumothorax.
2. **Infusion Protocols**:
    - Infuse continuously over 24 hours (or 12–16 hours cyclic HPN) via a dedicated central line lumen.
    - Keep the TPN bag enclosed in an **opaque protective cover** at all times to prevent light-induced degradation of vitamins.
3. **Prevention of Refeeding Syndrome**:
    - In high-risk malnourished patients (BMI <16 kg/m², weight loss >15% in 3–6 months, or no food intake for >10 days), start TPN at **max 10 kcal/kg/day** and slowly advance to full target needs over 4 to 7 days. Supplement with IV Thiamine (Vitamin B₁) and multivitamins prior to feeding.

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Complications are classified into three major categories:

#### 1. Central Line / Mechanical Complications

- **Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection (CRBSI / Line Sepsis) [⭐ High-Yield]**: Most common major line complication (up to 15%). Confirmed by paired blood cultures taken from the central line and a peripheral vein showing the same organism, or tip culture >15 CFU. Requires line removal and broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- **Pneumothorax**: Occurs in 0.5–1% of insertions (highest with subclavian vein approach); requires chest drain insertion if large.
- **Line Misplacement & Migration**: Tip in internal jugular or right atrium (causing cardiac ectopics/arrhythmias).
- **Central Venous Thrombosis**: Occlusion of the SVC or subclavian vein; managed with systemic anticoagulation.

#### 2. Metabolic & Biochemical Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Refeeding Syndrome [⭐ High-Yield]**: Characterized by severe **hypophosphatemia** (the primary driver of mortality), hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and fluid overload, leading to cardiac arrhythmias, heart failure, seizures, and death.
- **Blood Sugar Derangements**: **Hyperglycemia** is the most common metabolic complication (requires concurrent regular insulin infusion). Rebound hypoglycemia occurs if TPN is abruptly stopped.
- **Intestinal Failure-Associated Liver Disease (IFALD) [⭐ High-Yield]**: Occurs in ~25% of patients on long-term TPN. Features hepatic steatosis (fatty liver), cholestasis, jaundice, and progressive liver fibrosis/cirrhosis. Managed by introducing lipid-free solutions, cyclic feeding, or enteral weaning.
- **Metabolic Bone Disease**: Long-term TPN causes osteoporosis, osteomalacia, hypercalciuria, and pathological fractures.
- **Micronutrient Deficiencies**: Most commonly **Zinc deficiency** (perioral/acral dermatitis, alopecia, impaired wound healing), Copper deficiency (anemia, leukopenia), or Selenium deficiency.

#### 3. Gastrointestinal Complications

- **Gut Atrophy & Bacterial Translocation**: Lack of enteral luminal stimulation causes villous atrophy, breakdown of gut mucosal barrier, and translocation of aerobic Gram-negative bacteria to mesenteric lymph nodes, triggering SIRS/sepsis.

### Dosage Rule (TPN Requirements & Electrolyte Prescription)

- **Daily Energy Target**: 25 to 35 kcal/kg ideal body weight per day (up to 40 kcal/kg/day in severe burns/sepsis).
- **Daily Protein / Nitrogen Target**: 1.0 to 1.5 g/kg/day of protein (0.15–0.25 g Nitrogen/kg/day).
- **Daily Carbohydrate (Glucose) Target**: 2 to 3 g/kg/day (minimum 100–200 g/day).
- **Daily Lipid Target**: 1 to 2 g/kg/day.
- **Daily Electrolyte Requirements**:
    - **Sodium**: 0.9 to 1.2 mmol/kg/day (50–90 mmol/day in adults).
    - **Potassium**: 1.0 mmol/kg/day (50 mmol/day).
    - **Phosphate**: 20 to 30 mmol/day.
    - **Calcium**: 5.0 mmol/day.
    - **Magnesium**: 1.0 mmol/day.
- **Refeeding Syndrome Initial Dose**: Start at **10 kcal/kg/day** for the first 48 hours, then gradually increase to full requirements over 4 to 7 days.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Before starting a TPN infusion through a newly placed central venous line or PICC, **always obtain an erect chest X-ray to confirm that the catheter tip is positioned in the lower ⅓ of the SVC / atriocaval junction and to rule out a pneumothorax**. Never infuse hyperosmolar TPN solutions into a peripheral line or an unverified central line.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing the management of Refeeding Syndrome with standard high-calorie nutritional replenishment.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Aggressively infusing high-calorie TPN in a severely malnourished patient triggers a rapid insulin surge that drives phosphate, potassium, and magnesium into cells, causing **fatal hypophosphatemic cardiac arrest and pulmonary edema**. **In malnourished patients, TPN must be started at a maximum rate of 10 kcal/kg/day with daily electrolyte monitoring and thiamine supplementation**.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: T-P-N-F-E-E-D (Complications and Management)

- **T** – **T**hrombophlebitis / Line Thrombosis (avoid peripheral lines for >14 days).
- **P** – **P**neumothorax (0.5–1% risk during subclavian insertion).
- **N** – **N**itrogen Balance & Non-protein Calorie Ratio (150–250 kcal/g N).
- **F** – **F**atty Liver & IFALD (deranged LFTs in 25% of chronic TPN users).
- **E** – **E**lectrolyte Shifts in Refeeding Syndrome (hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia).
- **E** – **E**xcess Blood Sugar / Hyperglycemia (most common metabolic complication).
- **D** – **D**edicated Line Sepsis (CRBSI; paired blood cultures required).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Complete intravenous nutritional delivery via a central venous line, bypassing the GI tract.
- **Key Indications**: Short bowel syndrome (<100–200 cm remnant bowel), high-output enterocutaneous fistulae (>500 mL/day), prolonged paralytic ileus, and severe acute pancreatitis.
- **Gold Standard Route**: Subclavian or Internal Jugular central line with tip in the lower ⅓ of SVC / atriocaval junction.
- **Macronutrient Ratio**: 20% Protein : 30% Fat : 50% Carbohydrate; 25–35 kcal/kg/day.
- **Major Complications**: Catheter-related sepsis (CRBSI), Pneumothorax, Refeeding Syndrome (hypophosphatemia), Hyperglycemia, and IFALD.

_Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) is the complete intravenous administration of hyperosmolar macronutrients, micronutrients, and electrolytes directly into the central venous system, indicated in surgical patients with intestinal failure or unusable gastrointestinal tracts, and requiring meticulous clinical monitoring to prevent catheter-related sepsis and refeeding syndrome._
