# Hyponatremia

### Definition

**Hyponatremia** is defined as a fall in the serum sodium concentration below **135 mmol/L** (or 135 mEq/L). Severe hyponatremia is defined as a serum sodium level of **<120 to 125 mmol/L**, which creates an osmotic gradient driving water into cells and placing the patient at high risk of cerebral edema and encephalopathy.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Inappropriate Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) Secretion (SIADH)

- Unregulated ADH release occurs in response to **surgical stress, trauma, chest infections, and traumatic brain injury (TBI)**.
- Elevated ADH increases aquaporin insertion in distal renal tubules, causing water retention despite systemic normovolemia.

#### 2. TURP Syndrome / Intraoperative Water Intoxication [⭐ High-Yield]

- Occurs during Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP) due to systemic absorption of **hypotonic irrigating fluids** (distilled water, 5% dextrose, or 1.5% glycine) through severed prostatic venous sinuses.
- Absorption of approximately 1 liter or more of hypotonic irrigant directly into the bloodstream produces rapid **dilutional hyponatremia**.

#### 3. Cerebral Salt Wasting (CSW)

- Seen in traumatic brain injury and subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH); dysregulated renal sodium excretion leads to **primary sodium depletion and extracellular volume loss**.

#### 4. Excessive Administration of Hypotonic Fluids

- Postoperative administration of hypotonic intravenous fluids (e.g., 0.18% saline in 4% dextrose or 0.45% saline) or oral water without salt in burn and trauma patients.
- Failure to restrict postoperative maintenance fluids despite the physiological oliguria and fluid retention induced by surgical stress.

#### 5. Endocrine Deficiencies & Gastrointestinal Losses

- **Adrenal Insufficiency (Addisonian Crisis)**: Lack of mineralocorticoids (aldosterone) leads to uninhibited renal sodium excretion.
- **High-Output Gastrointestinal Losses**: Short bowel syndrome, enterocutaneous fistulae, severe vomiting, or excessive stoma losses replaced with salt-free fluids.

- **Osmotic Fluid Shift**: A reduction in extracellular sodium concentration lowers extracellular fluid osmolality relative to the intracellular compartment.
- **Cerebral Edema**: Water moves rapidly by osmosis across the blood-brain barrier into brain parenchyma, causing cellular swelling, raised intracranial pressure, and encephalopathy.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Mild to Moderate Hyponatremia (Na⁺ 120–130 mmol/L)**:
    - Nausea, vomiting, and anorexia.
    - Dull headache, fatigue, lethargy, and muscle weakness.
- **Severe Hyponatremia (Na⁺ <120 mmol/L)**:
    - Acute altered sensorium, disorientation, and delirium.
    - Restlessness, agitation, and slurred speech.

#### Signs

- **Neurological Signs**: Confusion, drowsiness, hyperreflexia, muscle twitching, and focal neurological deficits.
- **Severe Decompensation**: Generalized tonic-clonic seizures, coma, and signs of raised intracranial pressure (e.g., bradycardia, respiratory depression).
- **TURP Syndrome Specific Signs**: Onset 4–6 hours postoperatively with nausea, vomiting, confusion, visual disturbances, and hypertension followed by hypotension.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Laboratory Evaluation

- **Serum Electrolytes**: Confirms serum Na⁺ <135 mmol/L (severe if <120–125 mmol/L).
- **Serum Osmolality**: Decreased in true hypotonic hyponatremia (<275 mOsm/kg).
- **Urine Sodium & Urine Osmolality**:
    - _SIADH_: High urine sodium (>30 mEq/L) with concentrated urine (osmolality >100 mOsm/kg) in a normovolemic patient.
    - _Cerebral Salt Wasting_: High urine sodium with clinical hypovolemia.
- **Renal Function Tests**: Serum urea and creatinine to evaluate underlying acute kidney injury or fluid overload.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Mild to Moderate Asymptomatic Hyponatremia (Na⁺ >120 mmol/L)

- **Fluid Restriction**: Mainstay of conservative therapy. Restrict maintenance IV fluids to **50–70%** of daily requirements postoperatively.
- **Discontinue Hypotonic Solutions**: Stop all IV 0.18% or 0.45% saline and 5% dextrose solutions.

#### 2. Severe or Symptomatic Hyponatremia (Na⁺ <120 mmol/L or Seizures / Confusion)

- **Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl)**: Indicated in patients presenting with altered sensorium, seizures, or Na⁺ <125 mmol/L.
- **Rate of Correction Rule [⭐ High-Yield / Exam Trap]**:
    - Serum sodium correction **MUST NOT exceed ≤8–10 mEq/L per 24 hours** (8–10 mmol/L/day).
    - Rapid overcorrection causes irreversible **Central Pontine Demyelinosis (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome)**.

#### 3. Prevention Strategies in Surgery

- **Isotonic Irrigating Fluids**: Use **1.5% isotonic glycine** or normal saline (in bipolar TURP) during endoscopic urological procedures.
- **Surgical Timing**: Limit resection time during TURP to avoid massive irrigant absorption.
- **Avoid Hypotonic Resuscitation**: Use balanced crystalloids (Hartmann's solution) rather than hypotonic solutions for perioperative resuscitation.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acute Cerebral Edema**: Seizures, brain herniation, and death if rapid drops in sodium occur.
- **Central Pontine Demyelinosis (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome)**: Caused by excessively rapid sodium correction (>8–10 mEq/L/day); leads to spastic quadriparesis, pseudobulbar palsy, and locked-in syndrome.

---

### Dosage Rule (Hypertonic Saline 3% NaCl)

- **Name**: 3% Hypertonic Saline (NaCl)
- **Mechanism of Action**: Hypertonic crystalloid solution; raises extracellular osmolality to draw fluid out of edematous cerebral cells back into the vascular space.
- **Dose**: 100 mL IV bolus over 10–15 minutes; repeatable up to 2–3 times if severe neurological symptoms (seizures/coma) persist.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion via central line or large peripheral vein.
- **Frequency**: Bolus or slow continuous infusion titrated against serum sodium levels monitored every 2–4 hours.
- **Features**: Rapidly reverses life-threatening acute cerebral edema.
- **Side Effects**: Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, fluid overload, heart failure, and central pontine demyelinosis if overcorrected.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately in symptomatic patients with Na⁺ <120–125 mmol/L; stop or slow down once serum sodium increases by 4–6 mEq/L or symptoms resolve, ensuring total 24-hour correction remains ≤8–10 mEq/L.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In postoperative patients who develop acute altered sensorium, nausea, or seizures, **always check serum sodium levels before ordering a CT head**. Acute dilutional hyponatremia (e.g., post-TURP syndrome or SIADH) is a rapidly reversible medical emergency that can be detected instantly with a point-of-care electrolyte panel.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Rapidly correcting a low serum sodium level back to normal (135 mmol/L) within 12–24 hours to quickly relieve cerebral symptoms.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Over-rapid correction of hyponatremia (>8–10 mEq/L per day) dehydrates brain cells abruptly, precipitating irreversible **Central Pontine Demyelinosis (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome)**. **Always limit sodium correction to ≤8–10 mEq/L in any 24-hour period**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Serum sodium <135 mmol/L; severe if <120–125 mmol/L.
- **Key Surgical Causes**: SIADH (surgical stress/TBI), TURP syndrome (glycine/water absorption), and excess hypotonic IV fluids.
- **Clinical Picture**: Nausea, vomiting, lethargy, progressing to confusion, seizures, and coma due to cerebral edema.
- **Mild Management**: Fluid restriction (50–70% maintenance).
- **Severe Management**: IV 3% Hypertonic Saline.
- **Golden Rule**: Limit correction to **≤8–10 mEq/L per 24 hours** to prevent central pontine demyelinosis.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Hyponatremia is a common perioperative fluid and electrolyte derangement defined as a serum sodium level <135 mmol/L, caused primarily by SIADH, excessive hypotonic fluid administration, or TURP syndrome, presenting with features of cerebral edema, and managed by fluid restriction in mild cases or judicious 3% hypertonic saline infusion in severe cases with a strict correction limit of ≤8–10 mEq/L/day._
