# Indications for Artificial Nutritional Support and Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Artificial Nutritional Support**: The delivery of essential macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats) and micronutrients (vitamins, trace elements, electrolytes, water) via non-volitional routes—either enterally (via tube feeding into the GI tract) or parenterally (intravenously into the systemic circulation)—to maintain lean body mass and visceral protein status in patients unable to meet their metabolic requirements through normal oral intake.
- **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)**: The complete intravenous administration of hyperosmolar macronutrients, micronutrients, electrolytes, and water directly into a high-flow central vein, completely bypassing the gastrointestinal tract.

---

### Indications for Artificial Nutritional Support [⭐ High-Yield]

Surgical stress, major trauma, and severe sepsis induce a hypermetabolic catabolic response characterized by elevated counter-regulatory hormones, severe insulin resistance, and accelerated skeletal muscle breakdown. Without intervention, urinary nitrogen losses reach 14 to 20 g/day (equivalent to 500 g of wet lean skeletal muscle loss per day). Loss of 30% to 40% of total body protein mass is incompatible with survival.

#### 1. General Screening & Assessment Criteria (MUST Tool & Clinical Indicators) [⭐ High-Yield]

Artificial nutritional support is formally indicated when any of the following criteria are met:

- Inadequate oral intake for **≥5 continuous days**, or when a patient is anticipated to eat little or nothing for >5 days in the perioperative period.
- **Body Mass Index (BMI) <18.5 kg/m²** (BMI <15 kg/m² is associated with significant hospital mortality).
- **Unintentional weight loss >10%** of premorbid body weight within the preceding 3 to 6 months.
- High risk score on the **Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST)** (incorporating BMI score + weight loss score + acute disease effect score).

#### 2. Disease-Specific Clinical Indications

Artificial nutritional support is categorized into **Enteral Nutrition (EN)** (preferred whenever a functional gut is available) and **Parenteral Nutrition (PN)**:

- **Indications for Enteral Nutrition (EN)**:
    
    - _Oropharyngeal / Esophageal Obstruction_: Inoperable head and neck or esophageal carcinomas, corrosive strictures.
    - _Neurological Dysphagia_: Cerebrovascular accidents (stroke), bulbar palsy, motor neuron disease, traumatic brain injury.
    - _Major Upper GI Resections_: Perioperative placement of fine-bore nasojejunal tubes or surgical feeding jejunostomies during oesophagectomy, gastrectomy, or Whipple's pancreaticoduodenectomy.
    - _Severe Burns / Trauma_: Major burns (>15-20% TBSA) requiring early enteral tube feeding within 6 hours to prevent gut mucosal atrophy.
- **Indications for Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)**:
    
    - **Short Bowel Syndrome (Intestinal Failure)**: Remnant small bowel <100 to 200 cm (especially ending in a stoma), producing intractable malabsorption, dehydration, and high intestinal fluid losses (>4 L/day).
    - **High-Output Enterocutaneous / Gastrointestinal Fistulae**: Proximal or high-output fistulae (**>500 mL/24 hours**) where enteral feeding increases discharge and prevents spontaneous tract closure.
    - **Prolonged Paralytic Ileus & Mechanical Bowel Obstruction**: Postoperative ileus lasting **>72 hours** or mechanical obstruction where intestinal transit and absorption are absent.
    - **Severe Acute Pancreatitis**: Initial phase of severe necrotizing pancreatitis complicated by paralytic ileus, duodenal obstruction, or enteral feed intolerance.
    - **Severe Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**: Acute severe exacerbations of Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis with severe malabsorption, deep fistulation, or strictures requiring complete bowel rest.

### Enteral vs. Parenteral Nutrition (Comparison) [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Enteral Nutrition (EN)|Parenteral Nutrition (PN / TPN)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Route of Delivery**|GI tract (oral, NG, NJ, PEG, Jejunostomy)|Central venous catheter (SVC / Atriocaval junction)|
|**Physiological Status**|Highly physiological|Non-physiological (bypasses portal liver filter)|
|**Gut Mucosal Barrier**|Preserves villous height & mucosal immunity|Causes villous atrophy & gut mucosal breakdown|
|**Bacterial Translocation**|Prevents translocation of aerobic Gram-negative bacilli|High risk of bacterial translocation & SIRS|
|**Infection Risk**|Low (mainly tube position / aspiration risks)|High (catheter-related bloodstream infection / CRBSI up to 15%)|
|**Cost & Complexity**|Inexpensive, simple ward administration|Expensive, requires complex pharmacy mixing & line care|

### Detailed Surgical Anatomy & Routes of TPN Administration [⭐ High-Yield]

High-osmolar TPN admixtures (>850 to 1000 mOsm/L) cause rapid severe thrombophlebitis in small peripheral veins and **must be infused into a high-flow, large-calibre central vein**.

#### 1. Central Venous Routes (Standard Care) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Subclavian Vein Access**: Lowest risk of line infection and venous thrombosis; carries the highest risk of insertion pneumothorax (0.5–1%).
- **Internal Jugular Vein Access**: Easiest placement under ultrasound guidance; widely used in acute care.
- **Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter (PICC)**: Inserted via basilic, cephalic, or brachial veins in the arm under ultrasound guidance; catheter tip is advanced into the lower SVC; ideal for medium-term TPN (2–3 months).
- **Tunnelled Line (Hickman Line) / Implantable Port**: Preferred for long-term Home Parenteral Nutrition (HPN).
- **Femoral Vein Access**: **Strictly avoided** for TPN due to maximum rates of catheter-related sepsis and deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
- **Verification Rule [⭐ High-Yield]**: An erect Chest Radiograph (CXR) is **mandatory** before starting TPN to confirm catheter tip position in the lower ⅓ of the Superior Vena Cava (SVC) or at the atriocaval junction, and to exclude a pneumothorax.

#### 2. Peripheral Venous Route (Peripheral Parenteral Nutrition - PPN)

- Restricted to low-osmolarity solutions (<800 mOsm/L) and short durations (**<14 days**) using soft polyurethane cannulae changed every few days; carries a high incidence of superficial thrombophlebitis.

### Chemical Composition of TPN [⭐ High-Yield]

TPN is typically supplied as a **"3-in-1" Total Nutrient Admixture** in a 3-litre light-protected bag containing amino acids, glucose, lipid emulsion, electrolytes, trace elements, and vitamins:

```
                     TPN MACRONUTRIENT RATIO
                                 |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
        |                        |                        |
   Carbohydrate                 Fat                    Protein
       50%                      30%                      20%
  (Glucose 4 kcal/g)     (Lipids 9 kcal/g)      (Amino Acids 4 kcal/g)
```

#### 1. Caloric & Macronutrient Distribution

- **Energy-to-Nitrogen Ratio**: **150 to 250 kcal per gram of protein nitrogen**.
- **Proportional Composition**: **20% Protein : 30% Fat : 50% Carbohydrate**.
- **Total Daily Caloric Target**: **20 to 30 kcal/kg ideal body weight/day** (increases up to 30–40 kcal/kg/day in severe catabolic stress/burns).
- **Carbohydrates (Glucose/Dextrose)**: Provides 4 kcal/g; forms 45–65% of total calories. Minimum **100 to 200 g/day** required to supply the CNS and prevent muscle catabolism.
- **Lipids (Triglyceride Emulsions)**: Provides 9 kcal/g; forms 30–50% of total calories. Uses long-chain or MCT emulsions (100–200 g/week) to supply essential fatty acids (linoleic and linolenic acid).
- **Proteins / Nitrogen**: Provided as crystalline L-amino acid solutions (4 kcal/g). Nitrogen intake = **0.15 to 0.25 g N/kg ideal body weight/day** (equivalent to 1.0–1.5 g protein/kg/day).

#### 2. Micronutrients & Electrolytes [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Phosphate**: **20 to 30 mmol/day** mandatory in TPN solutions to ensure glucose phosphorylation and prevent fatal hypophosphatemia.
- **Sodium**: 0.9 to 1.2 mmol/kg/day (50–90 mmol/day).
- **Potassium**: 1.0 mmol/kg/day (~50 mmol/day).
- **Calcium & Magnesium**: Ca²⁺ 5.0 mmol/day; Mg²⁺ 1.0 mmol/day.
- **Vitamins & Trace Elements**: Daily B, C, A, D, E, K; Folic acid 15 mg 1–2 times/week; periodic Vitamin B₁₂. Trace elements include Zinc, Copper, Selenium, Chromium, and Manganese.

- **Protein Preservation**: Direct intravenous substrate delivery halts "auto-cannibalism" of skeletal muscle protein and supports hepatic synthesis of visceral proteins.
- **Mechanism of Refeeding Syndrome [⭐ High-Yield]**: In malnourished patients, total body stores of phosphate, potassium, and magnesium are depleted. Re-introducing carbohydrate-rich TPN stimulates a large insulin release. Insulin drives phosphate, potassium, and magnesium out of extracellular plasma into cells for glycolysis and protein synthesis.
- **Primary Cause of Mortality**: **Severe acute hypophosphatemia** impairs ATP production and tissue oxygenation, leading to cardiac arrhythmias, acute heart failure, muscle paralysis, respiratory depression, and death.

### Patient Assessment & Monitoring Schedule [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                  TPN MONITORING TIMELINE
                             |
        +--------------------+--------------------+
        |                                         |
  Daily Monitoring                      Weekly to Monthly
  - Pulse, BP, Temperature               - LFTs & Bilirubin (IFALD)
  - Weight (Gain >1 kg/day = Overfeeding) - FBC & Coagulation
  - Fluid Balance (In/Out)                - Plasma Proteins & Albumin
  - Serum Na⁺, K⁺, Urea, Creatinine       - Serum Zinc, Copper, Selenium
  - Blood Glucose (Hyperglycemia)        - Vitamin D, B₁₂, Ferritin
  - Serum PO₄³⁻, Mg²⁺ (Refeeding)
```

- **Daily Clinical & Laboratory Schedule**:
    - _Vital Signs & Body Weight_: Daily pulse, BP, temperature, and weight. _Clinical Pearl_: Weight gain **>1 kg/day**is the earliest sign of fluid retention and overfeeding.
    - _Fluid Balance_: Strict hourly input-output charting.
    - _Biochemistry_: Serum Na⁺, K⁺, urea, creatinine, blood glucose, serum phosphate, and magnesium.
- **Weekly to Monthly Schedule**:
    - Full blood count, liver function tests (bilirubin, ALP, AST, ALT), serum calcium, albumin, triglycerides, and CRP.
    - Micronutrients (Zinc, Copper, Selenium, Vitamin D, Vitamin B₁₂, Ferritin) checked after 28 days and every 3 months in long-term TPN.

### Treatment Protocol & TPN Administration

1. **Central Line Insertion & Verification**: Insert central catheter under strict aseptic conditions. Obtain an immediate erect Chest Radiograph to verify line tip position in the lower SVC/atriocaval junction and rule out pneumothorax.
2. **Infusion Management**:
    - Infuse continuously over 24 hours (or 12–16 hours cyclic HPN) via a dedicated central line lumen.
    - Enclose the TPN bag in an **opaque protective cover** to prevent light-induced vitamin degradation.
3. **Refeeding Syndrome Prevention Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - In high-risk malnourished patients (BMI <16 kg/m², unintentional weight loss >15% in 3–6 months, or no food intake for >10 days), initiate TPN at **maximum 10 kcal/kg/day**.
    - Administer IV Thiamine (Vitamin B₁) and multivitamins prior to initiating feed, and advance calories slowly over 4 to 7 days to full target requirements while monitoring electrolytes daily.

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Complications are divided into three primary categories:

#### 1. Central Line & Mechanical Complications

- **Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection (CRBSI / Line Sepsis) [⭐ High-Yield]**: Most common major line complication (occurs in up to 15% of patients). Confirmed by paired blood cultures taken from the central line and a peripheral vein showing the same organism, endoluminal brush, or tip culture >15 CFU. Requires central line removal and systemic antibiotic therapy.
- **Pneumothorax**: Occurs in 0.5–1% of central line insertions (highest with subclavian vein access).
- **Line Misplacement & Migration**: Tip displaced into internal jugular vein or right atrium (causing cardiac ectopics/arrhythmias).
- **Central Venous Thrombosis**: Occlusion of the SVC or subclavian vein; managed with systemic anticoagulation.

#### 2. Metabolic & Biochemical Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Refeeding Syndrome**: Characterized by severe **hypophosphatemia**, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and fluid overload, leading to fatal cardiac arrest and pulmonary edema.
- **Blood Sugar Derangements**: **Hyperglycemia** is the most common metabolic complication (requires concurrent regular insulin infusion). Rebound hypoglycemia occurs if TPN is abruptly stopped.
- **Intestinal Failure-Associated Liver Disease (IFALD) [⭐ High-Yield]**: Occurs in ~25% of patients on long-term TPN. Manifests as hepatic steatosis (fatty liver), cholestasis, jaundice, and progressive liver fibrosis/cirrhosis. Managed by introducing lipid-free days/solutions, cyclic feeding, or enteral weaning.
- **Micronutrient Deficiencies**: Most commonly **Zinc deficiency** (manifests with perioral/acral dermatitis, alopecia, and impaired wound healing).

#### 3. Gastrointestinal Complications

- **Gut Atrophy & Bacterial Translocation**: Absence of luminal nutrition leads to villous atrophy and mucosal barrier failure, allowing aerobic Gram-negative bacilli to translocate across the gut wall into mesenteric lymph nodes, triggering SIRS and MODS.

### Dosage Rule (TPN Requirements & Prescription)

- **Daily Energy Target**: 20 to 30 kcal/kg ideal body weight/day (up to 35–40 kcal/kg/day in severe catabolism/burns).
- **Daily Protein / Nitrogen Target**: 1.0 to 1.5 g protein/kg/day (0.15–0.25 g Nitrogen/kg/day).
- **Daily Carbohydrate Target**: 2 to 3 g glucose/kg/day (minimum 100–200 g/day).
- **Daily Lipid Target**: 1 to 2 g/kg/day.
- **Daily Electrolyte Requirements**:
    - **Phosphate**: 20 to 30 mmol/day.
    - **Sodium**: 0.9 to 1.2 mmol/kg/day.
    - **Potassium**: 1.0 mmol/kg/day.
    - **Calcium**: 5.0 mmol/day.
    - **Magnesium**: 1.0 mmol/day.
- **Refeeding Syndrome Initial Dose**: Start at **10 kcal/kg/day** for the first 48 hours, then gradually increase to full needs over 4 to 7 days.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In patients receiving Total Parenteral Nutrition, **a body weight gain of >1 kg/day is the earliest clinical indicator of fluid overload and overfeeding**. Furthermore, before starting TPN infusion through a newly placed central line, **always verify that the catheter tip lies in the lower ⅓ of the SVC on an erect Chest X-ray**.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Rapidly infusing high-calorie TPN solutions in a severely malnourished patient to quickly correct a nutritional deficit.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: In severely malnourished patients, high-calorie carbohydrate infusions trigger a massive insulin surge that drives phosphate, potassium, and magnesium into cells, causing **fatal hypophosphatemic cardiac arrest and respiratory failure (Refeeding Syndrome)**. **Always start TPN at a maximum rate of 10 kcal/kg/day with daily electrolyte monitoring and thiamine supplementation in at-risk patients**.

### Quick Revision

- **Indications for Artificial Nutrition**: Inadequate oral intake for ≥5 days, weight loss >10%, BMI <18.5 kg/m², or high MUST score.
- **Indications for TPN**: Short bowel syndrome (<100–200 cm remnant bowel), high-output enterocutaneous fistulae (>500 mL/day), prolonged paralytic ileus (>72h), and severe acute pancreatitis.
- **Gold Standard Access Route**: Central line or PICC with tip positioned at the lower ⅓ of SVC / atriocaval junction (verified by erect CXR).
- **Macronutrient Ratio**: 20% Protein : 30% Fat : 50% Carbohydrate; 150–250 kcal/g N; 20–30 kcal/kg/day.
- **Major Complications**: Line sepsis / CRBSI (15%), Pneumothorax (0.5–1%), Refeeding Syndrome (hypophosphatemia), Hyperglycemia, and IFALD.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Artificial nutritional support is indicated when oral intake is inadequate for ≥5 days or in chronic severe malnutrition, with Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) serving as the complete central intravenous delivery of hyperosmolar nutrients when the gastrointestinal tract is unusable or unsafe, requiring strict clinical monitoring to prevent catheter-related sepsis and refeeding syndrome._
