# Hyperkalemia

### Definition

**Hyperkalemia** (hyperkalaemia) is defined as an elevation in serum potassium concentration above **>5.3 mmol/L** (normal range = 3.5–5.3 mmol/L). Severe hyperkalemia is defined as a serum potassium level of **>6.5 mmol/L**, which constitutes a medical and surgical emergency due to the risk of imminent, lethal cardiac dysrhythmias and cardiac arrest in diastole.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Decreased Renal Excretion

- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) & Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**: Most common surgical cause; failure of glomerular filtration and tubular secretion.
- **Potassium-Sparing Drugs**: ACE inhibitors, Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs), Spironolactone, NSAIDs, and Heparin.
- **Adrenal Insufficiency (Addisonian Crisis)**: Deficient aldosterone secretion impairs renal distal tubular sodium reabsorption and potassium excretion.

#### 2. Excessive Intracellular-to-Extracellular Shift

- **Tissue Necrosis & Crush Syndrome**: Massive cell destruction in crush injuries, major burns, compartment syndrome, and extensive surgical trauma releases intracellular potassium (~150 mmol/L intracellularly) into systemic circulation.
- **Ischemic Cell Death & Reperfusion**: Intestinal ischemia, acute limb ischemia, and shock lead to ischemic cell death and rapid potassium release.
- **Metabolic Acidosis**: Systemic accumulation of H⁺ ions drives H⁺ into cells in exchange for extracellular K⁺ movement.
- **Hypercatabolic States**: Sepsis, severe hyperpyrexia, and malignant hyperthermia.

#### 3. Iatrogenic & Transfusion Factors

- **Massive Blood Transfusion**: Transfusion of stored, aged red blood cells (>10 units) where RBC lysis releases accumulated extracellular potassium.
- **Cardioplegia Solutions**: Intracoronary infusion of high-potassium cardioplegic solutions during cardiopulmonary bypass to deliberately induce diastolic cardiac arrest.
- **Exogenous Overdose**: Inappropriate IV potassium replacement or rapid KCl administration.

---

### Classification

- **Mild Hyperkalemia**: Serum K⁺ = 5.4 to 6.0 mmol/L.
- **Moderate Hyperkalemia**: Serum K⁺ = 6.1 to 6.5 mmol/L.
- **Severe Hyperkalemia**: Serum K⁺ >6.5 mmol/L (or any level with toxic ECG changes).

- **Membrane Depolarization**: Elevated extracellular K⁺ reduces the transmembrane concentration gradient, driving the resting membrane potential closer to zero (partial depolarization).
- **Inactivation of Fast Sodium Channels**: Sustained partial depolarization inactivates voltage-gated fast Na⁺ channels, slowing cardiac action potential conduction velocity and prolonging repolarization.
- **Diastolic Cardiac Arrest**: At extreme concentrations (>8.0 mmol/L), complete loss of cardiac excitability stops the heart in diastole.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Neuromuscular**: Muscle weakness starting in lower extremities and ascending proximally, fatigue, paresthesias, and flaccid quadriplegia.
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea, vomiting, intestinal colic, and diarrhoea.
- **Cardiovascular**: Palpitations, chest discomfort, and syncope.

#### Signs

- **Cardiovascular Signs**: Bradycardia, irregular pulse, hypotension, and sudden cardiovascular collapse.
- **Neuromuscular Signs**: Hyporeflexia, hypotonia, ascending flaccid paralysis, and respiratory depression due to diaphragmatic weakness.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Laboratory Evaluation

- **Serum Electrolytes**: Confirms serum K⁺ >5.3 mmol/L. (Rule out pseudohyperkalemia due to hemolysis during venipuncture).
- **Renal Function Tests**: Elevated serum urea (>20 mmol/L) and serum creatinine (>260 μmol/L) indicating acute kidney injury.
- **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG)**: Demonstrates metabolic acidosis (pH <7.35, low HCO₃⁻).
- **Serum Calcium & Magnesium**: Hypocalcemia worsens hyperkalemic cardiac toxicity.

#### 2. Electrocardiogram (ECG) Changes [⭐ High-Yield]

Characteristic sequential ECG manifestations:

1. **Tall, Peaked T Waves**: Narrow base ("tent-shaped") in precordial leads (K⁺ 5.5–6.5 mmol/L).
2. **PR Interval Prolongation & P-Wave Flattening**: P wave broadens and eventually disappears (K⁺ 6.5–7.0 mmol/L).
3. **QRS Complex Widening**: Merges with T wave (K⁺ 7.0–8.0 mmol/L).
4. **Sine Wave Pattern**: Precedes terminal Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) or Asystole in diastole (K⁺ >8.0 mmol/L).

#### 1. Emergency Resuscitation Protocol

- **Step 1: Cardiac Membrane Stabilization (Immediate Action)**:
    - **10% Calcium Gluconate**: 10 mL IV over 5–10 minutes. Reverses membrane antagonism within 1–3 minutes (duration 30–60 minutes). _Note: Does NOT lower serum K⁺ levels; acts purely as a cardioprotectant._
- **Step 2: Intracellular Redistribution of Potassium (Medium Action)**:
    - **Insulin-Dextrose Regimen**: 10 units Regular Insulin in 50 mL 50% Dextrose (or 500 mL 10% Dextrose) IV over 15–30 minutes. Insulin activates Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, shifting K⁺ into cells (lowers serum K⁺ by 0.5–1.2 mmol/L within 15 minutes).
    - **Nebulized β₂-Agonist (Salbutamol)**: 10–20 mg nebulized over 10 minutes (synergistic with insulin).
    - **Sodium Bicarbonate**: 50–100 mmol IV infusion if concomitant metabolic acidosis is present.
- **Step 3: Definitive Elimination of Potassium (Long-Term Action)**:
    - **Loop Diuretics (Furosemide)**: 40–80 mg IV to promote renal K⁺ excretion if kidney function and urine output are preserved.
    - **Cation Exchange Resins**: Calcium Polystyrene Sulfonate (Kayexalate) 15–30 g orally or rectally.
    - **Hemodialysis**: Gold standard and definitive treatment for severe hyperkalemia secondary to acute kidney injury or failed medical therapy.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Lethal Arrhythmias**: Ventricular Fibrillation, Ventricular Tachycardia, and Asystole in diastole.
- **Sudden Cardiac Death**.
- **Flaccid Quadriplegia & Respiratory Arrest**: Due to severe intercostal and diaphragmatic muscle paralysis.

---

### Dosage Rule (Emergency Antihyperkalemic Drugs)

#### 1. Calcium Gluconate

- **Name**: 10% Calcium Gluconate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Antagonizes hyperkalemia-induced membrane threshold depolarization, stabilizing cardiac myocyte excitability.
- **Dose**: 10 mL of 10% solution (1 gram)
- **Dosage / Route**: Slow IV injection over 5 to 10 minutes
- **Frequency**: Single dose; repeatable after 5–10 minutes if ECG abnormalities persist
- **Features**: Onset of action in 1–3 minutes; does NOT lower plasma K⁺ concentration.
- **Side Effects**: Bradycardia, peripheral vasodilatation, flushing, arrhythmias if pushed rapidly.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately upon identifying toxic ECG changes or K⁺ >6.5 mmol/L; stop when ECG normalizes.

#### 2. Regular Insulin with Dextrose

- **Name**: Regular (Soluble) Insulin + 50% Dextrose
- **Mechanism of Action**: Stimulates cell membrane Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, driving K⁺ from extracellular fluid into skeletal muscle and hepatic cells.
- **Dose**: 10 units Regular Insulin + 50 mL of 50% Dextrose (25 g glucose)
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion over 15 to 30 minutes
- **Frequency**: Single infusion; repeatable after 4–6 hours as needed
- **Features**: Onset within 15–30 minutes; lowers serum K⁺ by 0.5 to 1.2 mmol/L.
- **Side Effects**: Severe hypoglycemia (mandatory capillary blood glucose monitoring), hyperosmolality.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start concurrently with Calcium Gluconate in acute hyperkalemia; monitor blood glucose hourly for 4–6 hours post-infusion.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In an emergency setting of severe hyperkalemia with toxic ECG changes, **10% Calcium Gluconate is the first drug to administer**. It protects the myocardium within 1 to 3 minutes by stabilizing cell membranes, buying crucial time for Insulin-Dextrose and diuretics to actually remove or redistribute potassium.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Believing that IV Calcium Gluconate lowers serum potassium levels.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Calcium Gluconate is purely a **cardioprotective membrane stabilizer** and has ZERO effect on serum potassium concentrations. **Always follow Calcium Gluconate immediately with Regular Insulin + Dextrose and diuretics/resins to shift and eliminate potassium.**

- **C** – **C**alcium Gluconate 10% (stabilizes cardiac membrane immediately)
- **B** – **B**icarbonate IV (if metabolic acidosis is present)
- **I** – **I**nsulin (Regular 10 units)

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Serum K⁺ >5.3 mmol/L; severe if >6.5 mmol/L.
- **Key Surgical Causes**: Acute kidney injury, crush syndrome/tissue necrosis, shock, and massive stored blood transfusion.
- **ECG Hallmark**: Tall peaked T waves → loss of P wave → widened QRS → **sine wave pattern**.
- **First Action in Emergency**: IV 10% Calcium Gluconate (10 mL over 5–10 mins) for cardioprotection.
- **K⁺ Shifting & Elimination**: Regular Insulin + 50% Dextrose IV followed by Furosemide or emergency hemodialysis.

_Hyperkalemia is a life-threatening fluid and electrolyte emergency defined as a serum potassium concentration >5.3 mmol/L, caused in surgical practice by renal impairment, crush injuries, tissue necrosis, or massive blood transfusion, characterized by progressive ECG abnormalities culminating in a sine wave pattern, and managed urgently with IV calcium gluconate, insulin-dextrose infusion, and hemodialysis._
