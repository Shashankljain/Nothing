# Linitis Plastica

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Linitis plastica** (also known as "leather bottle stomach") is an advanced, diffusely infiltrating form of gastric adenocarcinoma (**Borrmann Type IV**) characterized by transmural cancerous infiltration and severe fibrous stroma, causing the gastric wall to become rigid, thickened, and contracted with total loss of luminal distensibility.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Histopathology & Genetics**: Corresponds to the **diffuse type** in the Laurén classification, featuring poorly differentiated, mucin-secreting **signet-ring cells** and loss of cellular cohesion secondary to decreased **E-cadherin expression (CDH1 gene mutation)**.
- **Macroscopic Appearance**: Converts the normal pliable gastric wall into a rigid, non-distensible, thickened "leather bottle" structure with flattened, rigid rugal folds.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Characterized by prominent **early satiety**, postprandial fullness, vomiting, and profound weight loss resulting from loss of gastric reservoir capacity and wall compliance.
- **Biological Behavior**: Demonstrates rapid, widespread submucosal/subserosal transmural and lymphatic dissemination, representing the most aggressive macroscopic variant with the **worst prognosis** among advanced gastric cancers.
- **Management**: Diagnosed via upper GI endoscopy with deep mucosal biopsies and EUS; managed by **radical total gastrectomy** with Roux-en-Y esophagojejunostomy and perioperative chemotherapy when resectable.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Classification**: Borrmann Type IV advanced gastric adenocarcinoma ("leather bottle stomach").
- **Histology**: Diffuse Laurén subtype featuring signet-ring cells and E-cadherin / CDH1 gene loss.
- **Clinical Hallmark**: Early satiety and vomiting due to a rigid, non-compliant gastric wall.
- **Prognosis**: Most aggressive macroscopic form carrying the worst overall survival.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Linitis plastica is a highly aggressive, diffusely infiltrating gastric adenocarcinoma (Borrmann Type IV) characterized histologically by signet-ring cells and E-cadherin loss, producing a rigid, non-distensible "leather bottle" gastric wall with early satiety and a grave prognosis._
