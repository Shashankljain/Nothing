# Carcinoma of the Upper 1/3rd Rectum (Above Peritoneal Reflection)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Carcinoma of the upper 1/3rd rectum** refers to a primary malignant epithelial neoplasm (overwhelmingly adenocarcinoma) arising between **12 cm and 15 cm from the anal verge** (or sacral promontory to the peritoneal reflection). Anatomically, the upper third of the rectum is mobile and covered by visceral peritoneum on its anterior and lateral surfaces, placing it **entirely above the peritoneal reflection**. Oncologically and surgically, tumors in this region behave similarly to sigmoid colon cancers, characterized by a predominantly upward lymphatic drainage pattern and low local recurrence rates following resection.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Genetic & Molecular Pathways

- **Adenoma-Carcinoma Sequence (70%–80%)**: Stepwise accumulation of somatic mutations initiating with _APC_gene inactivation (chromosome 5q), followed by _K-ras_ proto-oncogene activation, and ultimate loss of tumor suppressor genes (_p53_ on 17p, _DCC/SMAD4_ on 18q).
- **Hereditary Non-Polyposis Colorectal Cancer (HNPCC / Lynch Syndrome)**: Autosomal dominant mutation in DNA mismatch repair (MMR) genes (_MSH2, MLH1, MSH6, PMS2_), leading to microsatellite instability (MSI-High).
- **Familial Adenomatous Polyposis (FAP)**: Germline mutation in the _APC_ gene leading to thousands of colorectal adenomas with a 100% lifetime risk of carcinoma if untreated.

#### 2. Environmental & Dietary Risk Factors

- **High Red & Processed Meat Intake**: Rich in heterocyclic amines and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons.
- **Low Dietary Fiber**: Prolongs intestinal transit time, increasing mucosal exposure to fecal carcinogens.
- **Obesity & Sedentary Lifestyle**: Increases circulating insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), promoting cellular proliferation.
- **Alcohol & Tobacco Abuse**: Synergistic carcinogenic effect on colorectal mucosa.

#### 3. Inflammatory & Pre-existing Conditions

- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**: Longstanding Ulcerative Colitis (pancolitis >8–10 years) and Crohn's colitis increase the risk of dysplasia and carcinoma.
- **Pelvic Radiation**: Previous pelvic irradiation for gynaecological or urological malignancies.

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Anatomical Classification of Rectal Cancer [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Upper 1/3rd Rectum (12–15 cm from anal verge)**: Intraperitoneal anteriorly/laterally; **above the peritoneal reflection**.
- **Middle 1/3rd Rectum (7–12 cm from anal verge)**: Peritoneum on anterior surface only.
- **Lower 1/3rd Rectum (<7 cm from anal verge)**: Extraperitoneal (below peritoneal reflection).

#### 2. Macroscopic (Gross) Morphology

- **Annular / Stenosing**: Encircles bowel lumen; common in upper rectum/rectosigmoid, causing subacute obstruction.
- **Ulcerating**: Central ulceration with raised, everted everted edges; prone to bleeding.
- **Polypoid / Fungating**: Exophytic mass projecting into lumen.
- **Infiltrative (Linitis Plastica-like)**: Diffuse submucosal infiltration.

#### 3. Histological Differentiation (WHO / Broders' System)

- **Well-differentiated (G1)**: >95% gland formation.
- **Moderately differentiated (G2)**: 50%–95% gland formation (most common).
- **Poorly differentiated (G3)**: <50% gland formation.
- **Special Variants**: Mucinous / Signet-ring cell adenocarcinoma (poor prognosis).

#### 4. Staging Systems (Dukes' vs. TNM 8th Edition) [⭐ High-Yield]

|Dukes' Stage|TNM Equivalent|Pathological Description|5-Year Survival|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Dukes A**|T1–T2, N0, M0|Tumor limited to rectal wall (submucosa or muscularis propria)|>90%|
|**Dukes B**|T3–T4, N0, M0|Penetrates muscularis propria into perirectal tissues/serosa; no lymph nodes|70%–80%|
|**Dukes C1**|Any T, N1/N2, M0|Regional pararectal lymph node involvement|40%–50%|
|**Dukes C2**|Any T, N2 (Apical), M0|Apical lymph nodes along vessel origin involved|20%–30%|
|**Dukes D**|Any T, Any N, M1|Distant metastasis (liver, lung, peritoneum)|<10%|

- **Local Circumferential Spread**: Growth spreads circumferentially around the rectal wall rather than longitudinally. Downward intramural spread exceeds 1–2 cm in <1% of cases.
- **Lymphatic Dissemination Pathway [⭐ High-Yield]**: Lymphatic drainage from the upper 1/3rd rectum (above the peritoneal reflection) occurs **almost exclusively in an upward (cephalad) direction** along the superior rectal vessels to the inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) origin and para-aortic nodes. Lateral pelvic wall lymphatic spread is virtually non-existent in upper rectal lesions (unlike low rectal cancer).
- **Hematogenous Dissemination**: Venous drainage via the superior rectal vein flows into the portal vein, making the **liver the primary site of distant blood-borne metastases (34%)**, followed by lungs (22%) and adrenals.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Altered Bowel Habits**: Progressive constipation alternating with loose diarrhea; feeling of incomplete evacuation.
- **Rectal Bleeding**: Painless, dark or bright red blood mixed with stool or mucus.
- **Colicky Abdominal Pain**: Stenosing annular lesions in the upper 1/3rd rectum cause postprandial colicky lower abdominal pain and subacute large bowel obstruction.
- **Tenesmus**: Persistent, painful desire to pass stool with minimal discharge (more prominent in mid/low rectal tumors, but seen in bulky upper lesions).
- **Constitutional Symptoms**: Weight loss, anorexia, malaise, and unexplained iron-deficiency anemia.

#### Signs

- **General Physical Exam**: Pallor (anemia), cachexia, jaundice (liver metastases), enlarged left supraclavicular node (Virchow's node).
- **Abdominal Examination**: May reveal lower abdominal distension, palpable mass in left iliac fossa/hypogastrium, hepatomegaly, or ascites.
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - Carcinoma of the upper 1/3rd rectum (12–15 cm from anal verge) is **typically NOT palpable on standard DRE** (DRE reaches up to 7–8 cm).
    - _Exam Value_: DRE assesses lower rectal mucosa, anal sphincter tone, and pelvic floor, confirming that the distal rectum is uninvolved.

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

```

                                   |
     +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
     |                                                           |
Primary Diagnostic Modality                             Staging & Preoperative Work-up
* Rigid / Flexible Sigmoidoscopy                      * CECT Thorax, Abdomen & Pelvis (IOC for Mets)
* Full Colonoscopy + Biopsy (Gold Standard)           * High-Resolution MRI Pelvis (Local T/N/CRM)
                                                      * Serum CEA Level (Baseline Marker)
```

#### 1. Endoscopic & Tissue Confirmation

- **Colonoscopy + Multiple Biopsies [Gold Standard Diagnostic Test]**: Direct visual identification of the upper rectal mass, histological biopsy confirmation, and complete evaluation of the proximal colon to rule out **synchronous adenomas/carcinomas (present in 3%–5% of cases)**.
- **Rigid / Flexible Sigmoidoscopy**: Precisely measures the exact distance of the lower tumor margin from the anal verge (crucial for anatomical localization at 12–15 cm).

#### 2. Staging Imaging [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Contrast-Enhanced CT of Thorax, Abdomen & Pelvis (CECT TAP) [Investigation of Choice for Systemic Staging]**: Evaluates distant liver/lung metastases, peritoneal carcinomatosis, and abdominal lymphadenopathy.
- **High-Resolution Pelvic MRI [Investigation of Choice for Local Staging]**:
    - Assesses depth of rectal wall invasion (T-stage), mesorectal lymph nodes (N-stage), and relationship of the tumor to the visceral peritoneal reflection.
    - Evaluates Circumferential Resection Margin (CRM) and extramural vascular invasion (EMVI).
- **FDG PET-CT**: Utilized in equivocal metastatic lesions or elevated CEA with negative conventional imaging.

#### 3. Laboratory Studies

- **Serum Carcinoembryonic Antigen (CEA)**: Baseline preoperative tumor marker level; essential for long-term postoperative surveillance and recurrence monitoring.
- **Complete Blood Count & Liver Function**: Assesses severity of anemia and liver metastatic function.

### Treatment / Management of Carcinoma Rectum Above Peritoneal Reflection [⭐ High-Yield]

Because the upper 1/3rd rectum lies above the peritoneal reflection, its management differs significantly from lower rectal cancer and follows principles similar to sigmoid colon cancer.

#### 1. Principles of Surgical Treatment (The Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

The primary definitive operation for carcinoma of the upper 1/3rd rectum is **High Anterior Resection (HAR)** via open, laparoscopic, or robotic approach.

##### Key Operative Steps of High Anterior Resection (HAR)

1. **Patient Position & Access**: Lloyd-Davies or supine Trendelenburg position; midline laparotomy or 4–5 port laparoscopic access.
2. **Abdominal Exploration**: Rule out unexpected peritoneal carcinomatosis or liver metastases.
3. **Vascular Ligation (High Tie)**: Ligation of the **Inferior Mesenteric Artery (IMA)** near its aortic origin (or just distal to the left colic artery) and superior rectal vein to clear high-lying apical lymph nodes.
4. **Mobilization**: Division of the lateral peritoneal reflection (white line of Monks/Toldt), mobilization of the sigmoid colon and splenic flexure to achieve tension-free length.
5. **Partial Mesorectal Excision (PME)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Full **Total Mesorectal Excision (TME) is NOT required** for upper 1/3rd rectal cancer.
    - The mesorectum is transected perpendicularly **at least 5 cm distal to the lower margin of the tumor** (Partial Mesorectal Excision / PME), preserving the lower mesorectal package and hypogastric autonomic nerves.
6. **Distal Bowel Clearance**: The rectum is transected with a distal clear margin of **at least 3 cm** (minimum 2 cm) below the tumor edge.
7. **Reconstruction**: Primary end-to-end colorectal anastomosis using a circular stapling device.
8. **Defunctioning Stoma**: **NOT routinely required** for upper 1/3rd rectal resections above the peritoneal reflection because the anastomosis sits high in the intraperitoneal cavity with excellent blood supply and low leak rates.

#### 2. Role of Neoadjuvant Therapy (Radiotherapy / Chemoradiotherapy) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **General Rule**: **Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) is ROUTINELY OMITTED** in upper 1/3rd rectal cancer.
- _Rationale_: Local recurrence rates post-HAR with clear margins in the upper 1/3rd rectum are extremely low (<5%). Pelvic radiotherapy in this intraperitoneal segment carries significant small bowel toxicity without survival benefit.
- **Exceptions**: Neoadjuvant therapy is reserved strictly for locally advanced **T4b tumors** invading adjacent structures or fixed bulky N2 disease to downstage the tumor prior to surgery.

#### 3. Role of Adjuvant Systemic Chemotherapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications**:
    - **Stage III (Node-positive disease, N1/N2)**.
    - **High-risk Stage II (T3/T4, N0)**: Tumors with lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, poorly differentiated histology, bowel obstruction, or <12 lymph nodes retrieved.
- **Standard Regimens**: 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) + Leucovorin + Oxaliplatin (**FOLFOX**) or Capecitabine + Oxaliplatin (**CAPOX**) administered for 6 months.

---

### Complications

- **Surgical / Anastomotic Complications**:
    - **Anastomotic Leak**: Lower incidence in upper rectal resections (<3%–5%) compared to low coloanal anastomoses.
    - **Intra-abdominal Abscess & Peritonitis**: Secondary to anastomotic disruption.
    - **Ureteric Injury**: Risk during mobilization of the left colon and IMA ligation; left ureter must be visualized.
    - **Autonomic Nerve Damage**: Injury to superior hypogastric plexus causing retrograde ejaculation or erectile dysfunction (rare in upper 1/3rd PME).
- **Oncological Complications**: Local recurrence, liver/lung metastases, bowel obstruction.

---

### Prognosis

- **5-Year Overall Survival**: Approximately 60%–70% across all stages; stage-for-stage survival is superior to lower rectal cancer.
- **Key Prognostic Factors**: Pathological nodal status (N-stage), depth of invasion (T-stage), histological grade, lymphovascular/perineural invasion, and achieving an **R0 resection with clear surgical margins**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Chemotherapy Regimens in Rectal Cancer)

#### 1. FOLFOX-6 Regimen (Adjuvant Chemotherapy)

- **Components**:
    - **Oxaliplatin**: 85 mg/m² IV infusion on Day 1.
    - **Leucovorin (Folinic Acid)**: 400 mg/m² IV infusion on Day 1.
    - **5-Fluorouracil (5-FU)**: 400 mg/m² IV bolus on Day 1, followed by 2400 mg/m² continuous IV infusion over 46 hours.
- **Frequency**: Repeated every 2 weeks for **12 cycles (6 months)**.
- **Indication**: Stage III and high-risk Stage II resected colorectal cancer.
- **Side Effects**: Peripheral sensory neuropathy, cold dysesthesia, neutropenia, diarrhea.

#### 2. Capecitabine (CAPOX Regimen)

- **Name**: Capecitabine (Oral prodrug of 5-FU).
- **Mechanism of Action**: Enzymatically converted to 5-fluorouracil in tumor tissue by thymidine phosphorylase, inhibiting thymidylate synthase.
- **Dose**: 1000 mg/m² PO BD.
- **Frequency**: Taken orally twice daily for 14 days of a 21-day cycle (for 8 cycles / 6 months), paired with Oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² IV on Day 1.
- **Side Effects**: Hand-foot syndrome (palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia), diarrhea, stomatitis.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In upper 1/3rd rectal cancer (above the peritoneal reflection), **do NOT perform a Total Mesorectal Excision (TME) down to the levator ani**. Performing TME unnecessarily for upper third lesions increases operative time, risk of autonomic nerve injury, and anterior resection syndrome without adding oncological benefit. A **Partial Mesorectal Excision (PME)** with a **5-cm mesorectal clearance below the lower edge of the tumor** is the gold standard.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Recommending routine preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) for all rectal cancers regardless of height.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: **Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy is standard for middle and lower 1/3rd rectal cancers (below peritoneal reflection) to prevent local recurrence, BUT IS NOT ROUTINELY INDICATED for upper 1/3rd rectal cancer (above peritoneal reflection)** unless T4b/threatened margins are present. Upper 1/3rd cancers undergo primary High Anterior Resection.

#### Topic Mnemonic: U-P-P-E-R R-E-C-T-U-M

- **U** – **U**pward lymphatic drainage exclusively along superior rectal and inferior mesenteric vessels.
- **P** – **P**artial Mesorectal Excision (PME) with 5-cm mesorectal clearance.
- **P** – **P**eritoneal reflection: upper 1/3rd lies completely above it (intraperitoneal).
- **E** – **E**nd-to-end colorectal stapled anastomosis without routine defunctioning stoma.
- **R** – **R**esected via High Anterior Resection (HAR).
- **R** – **R**adiotherapy (neoadjuvant nCRT) is NOT routinely required.
- **E** – **E**ndoscopic colonoscopy + biopsy is the gold standard diagnostic tool.
- **C** – **C**lear distal margin of ≥3 cm required.
- **T** – **T**NM / Dukes' staging guides adjuvant systemic chemotherapy.
- **U** – **U**nreachable on routine Digital Rectal Examination (located at 12–15 cm).
- **M** – **M**RI Pelvis + CECT TAP for comprehensive staging.

### Quick Revision

- **Anatomical Landmark**: Upper 1/3rd rectum lies 12–15 cm from anal verge, completely above the peritoneal reflection.
- **Lymphatic Behavior**: Drains almost exclusively in an upward direction along the superior rectal and IMA nodes.
- **Definitive Treatment**: High Anterior Resection (HAR) + Partial Mesorectal Excision (PME).
- **Surgical Clearance**: Distal bowel margin ≥3 cm; mesorectal clearance ≥5 cm below tumor.
- **Neoadjuvant Therapy**: NOT routinely indicated (unlike mid/low rectal cancer); reserved for T4b disease.
