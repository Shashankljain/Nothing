# Upper Gastrointestinal Bleed (Hematemesis) — Management

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Upper Gastrointestinal (UGI) Bleeding** is defined as intraluminal hemorrhage originating from any anatomical site proximal to the **Ligament of Treitz** (the suspensory muscle of the duodenum at the duodenojejunal flexure). Clinically, it presents as **hematemesis** (vomiting of frank red blood or dark "coffee-ground" material) and/or **melena** (passage of black, tarry, viscous, foul-smelling stools per rectum). It represents a surgical emergency with a global in-hospital mortality rate ranging from 5% to 10%.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Peptic Ulcer Disease (60% of cases) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Duodenal Ulcers (33%)**: Posterior duodenal wall ulcers eroding directly into the **gastroduodenal artery**.
- **Gastric Ulcers (21%)**: Lesser curvature ulcers eroding into the **left gastric artery**.
- **Esophageal Ulcers (6%)**.

#### 2. Mucosal Erosive Lesions (26% of cases)

- **Gastric Erosions (9%) & Esophageal Erosions (13%)**: Secondary to chronic NSAID ingestion, alcohol abuse, or severe physiological stress.

#### 3. Portal Hypertension & Variceal Bleeding (4%–20% of cases) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Esophageal Varices**: Dilated submucosal portosystemic collateral veins in the distal esophagus secondary to liver cirrhosis or extrahepatic portal vein thrombosis.
- **Gastric Varices & Portal Hypertensive Gastropathy**: Fundal varices and diffuse mucosal ectasia ("strawberry stomach" or "snake-skin" pattern).

#### 4. Mucosal Tears & Vascular Anomalies

- **Mallory-Weiss Tear (4%)**: Longitudinal mucosal-submucosal laceration at the gastroesophageal junction caused by severe, forceful retching or vomiting.
- **Dieulafoy's Lesion (0.5%)**: Abnormally large, tortuous submucosal arteriole that erodes through overlying gastric mucosa, causing recurrent massive arterial arterial hemorrhage.
- **Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia (GAVE / "Watermelon Stomach")**: Dilated mucosal venules in the antrum.
- **Aorto-enteric Fistula**: Erosion of a primary aortic aneurysm or synthetic aortic vascular graft into the third part of the duodenum; presents with a premonitory "herald bleed" prior to catastrophic exsanguination.

---

### Classification (Risk Stratification Scoring Systems) [⭐ High-Yield]

Prognostic risk scoring systems guide decisions regarding safe outpatient discharge versus mandatory ICU admission, urgent endoscopy, and overall mortality risk:

#### 1. Glasgow-Blatchford Bleeding Score (GBS) [Pre-Endoscopy Score] [⭐ High-Yield]

Calculated **prior to endoscopy** using clinical and laboratory parameters (blood urea, hemoglobin, systolic blood pressure, pulse rate, presence of melena, syncope, hepatic disease, or cardiac failure):

- **Score = 0 to 1**: Low risk; safe for outpatient management without emergency overnight endoscopy.
- **Score ≥2**: High risk; requires immediate hospital admission, fluid resuscitation, and urgent in-patient gastroscopy.

#### 2. Rockall Scoring System [⭐ High-Yield]

Integrates clinical pre-endoscopic risk criteria with diagnostic post-endoscopic findings to predict rebleeding risk and overall mortality:

|Variable|Score 0|Score 1|Score 2|Score 3|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Age (years)**|<60 years|60–79 years|≥80 years|—|
|**Shock Status**|No shock (HR <100 bpm, SBP >100 mmHg)|Tachycardia (HR >100 bpm, SBP >100 mmHg)|Hypotension (HR >100 bpm, SBP <100 mmHg)|—|
|**Comorbidities**|No major comorbidity|—|IHD, CHF, major comorbidities|Renal failure, liver failure, disseminated malignancy|
|**Endoscopic Diagnosis**|Mallory-Weiss tear / No lesion identified|All other diagnoses|Upper GI tract malignancy|—|
|**Endoscopic Stigmata of Bleeding**|None or dark spot only|—|—|Blood in lumen, adherent clot, active spurting vessel|

- **Total Rockall Score <3**: Excellent prognosis; low risk of rebleeding (<5%) and mortality (<1%).
- **Total Rockall Score ≥8**: High mortality (>30%) and high risk of rebleeding (>40%); mandates intensive care monitoring.

- **Vascular Disruption**: Deep tissue breakdown erodes muscular arteries (e.g., gastroduodenal artery in posterior duodenal ulcers) or ruptures high-pressure submucosal portosystemic varices.
- **Hemodynamic Cascade**: Rapid blood loss exceeding 15%–30% of total circulating blood volume triggers sympathetic nervous system activation, leading to compensatory tachycardia and peripheral vasoconstriction. Continued uncorrected hemorrhage produces profound hypovolemic shock, tissue hypoperfusion, lactic acidosis, and acute ischemic organ failure.
- **Intestinal Digestion**: Blood traversing the gastrointestinal tract undergoes bacterial breakdown of hemoglobin into hematin, resulting in characteristic black, tarry melena.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Hematemesis**: Vomiting of frank, bright red blood (indicating active, massive bleeding) or dark brown "coffee-ground" material (indicating blood denatured by gastric hydrochloric acid).
- **Melena**: Passage of foul-smelling, jet-black, tarry stools per rectum.
- **Syncope & Dizziness**: Postural lightheadedness, weakness, confusion, or sudden loss of consciousness due to acute cerebral hypoperfusion.
- **Dyspeptic / Cirrhotic History**: Chronic burning epigastric pain (PUD), heavy alcohol intake, jaundice, or known chronic liver disease.

#### Signs

- **Hemodynamic Instability / Shock**: Tachycardia (HR >100 bpm), postural hypotension, resting hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg), cold clammy extremities, delayed capillary refill time (>2 seconds), and oliguria (<0.5 mL/kg/hour).
- **Stigmata of Chronic Liver Disease**: Jaundice, palmar erythema, spider nevi, gynecomastia, caput medusae, splenomegaly, and ascites.
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Essential physical exam finding demonstrating black tarry stool or melenic blood on the examining glove.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Laboratory Diagnostic Work-up

- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Initial hemoglobin may be deceptively normal in acute sudden hemorrhage prior to hemodilution; serial Hb monitoring demonstrates microcytic anemia.
- **Renal Function Tests & Blood Urea**: **Markedly elevated Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) to Creatinine ratio (>20:1)** resulting from digestion and absorption of blood proteins in the small intestine combined with prerenal azotemia.
- **Coagulation Profile & Liver Function**: Prothrombin Time (PT/INR), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT), fibrinogen, and liver enzymes to detect underlying hepatic coagulopathy.
- **Type and Cross-Match**: Minimum 4 to 6 units of Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) cross-matched immediately.

#### 2. Diagnostic & Therapeutic Upper GI Endoscopy (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Timing**: Performed as soon as possible within 24 hours of admission once hemodynamic resuscitation is achieved (urgent <12 hours if ongoing massive hemorrhage).
- **Diagnostic Accuracy**: Identifies the exact bleeding source in >90% of cases, evaluates stigmata of recent hemorrhage (active spurting, non-bleeding visible vessel, adherent clot), and allows immediate dual endoscopic therapy.

#### 3. Radiologic Imaging & Angiography

- **Contrast-Enhanced CT Angiography (CTA)**: Detects active intraluminal arterial contrast extravasation at bleeding rates ≥0.3–0.5 mL/min; indicated when endoscopy fails to locate the bleeding site.
- **Catheter Selective Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA)**: Diagnostic and therapeutic; identifies vessel erosion and allows superselective transcatheter arterial embolization.

#### 1. Resuscitation & Initial Emergency Measures (cABCDE Protocol) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Airway Protection (A)**: Insert endotracheal tube in patients with massive ongoing hematemesis, severe shock, or hepatic encephalopathy to prevent fatal pulmonary blood aspiration.
- **Breathing & Oxygenation (B)**: Administer high-flow humidified oxygen via non-rebreather mask.
- **Circulatory Resuscitation & Transfusion Strategy (C)**:
    - Secure **two large-bore (14G or 16G) peripheral intravenous lines** immediately.
    - _Permissive Hypotension & Fluid Choice_: Resuscitate with small boluses of isotonic crystalloids (0.9% Normal Saline); avoid excessive crystalloids to prevent dilutional coagulopathy.
    - _Restrictive Transfusion Strategy_: Transfuse Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs) when **Hb drops below 7 to 8 g/dL**, targeting a post-transfusion Hb level of **7 to 9 g/dL** (over-transfusion elevates portal pressure and triggers recurrent variceal rebleeding).
    - _Coagulopathy Correction_: Correct deranged clotting profiles (INR >1.5) using Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) or Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC); administer platelets if platelet count is <50 × 10⁹/L.

#### 2. Specific Pharmacotherapy (Dosage Protocols) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### Non-Variceal Bleeding

- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)**: Neutralizes gastric acid (maintains intragastric pH >6.0), stabilizing blood clots over ulcer beds.
    - **Pantoprazole**: **80 mg IV bolus**, followed by **8 mg/hour continuous IV infusion for 72 hours**.

##### Variceal Bleeding

- **Splanchnic Vasoconstrictors**: Initiated immediately upon clinical suspicion of variceal bleeding prior to endoscopy.
    - **Terlipressin**: **2 mg IV every 4 hours** for 48 hours, reduced to 1 mg IV Q4H once bleeding is controlled (total duration 2 to 5 days).
    - **Octreotide**: **50 μg IV bolus**, followed by **50 μg/hour continuous IV infusion for 2 to 5 days**.
- **Prophylactic Antibiotics**: **Ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily** for 7 days (reduces bacterial translocation, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, and early rebleeding).

#### 3. Therapeutic Endoscopic Interventions [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Non-Variceal Peptic Ulcer Hemorrhage**:
    - **Dual Modality Therapy (Gold Standard)**: Submucosal injection of **1:10,000 Adrenaline (Epinephrine)** to produce local vasoconstriction, combined with a second mechanical or thermal modality—**Through-The-Scope (TTS) Hemoclips** or **Bipolar Electro-cautery / Heater Probe**.
- **Variceal Hemorrhage**:
    - **Esophageal Variceal Band Ligation (EVBL)**: Gold standard; rubber bands deployed over variceal trunks to induce thrombosis and ischemic necrosis.
    - **Gastric Variceal Injection**: Intra-variceal injection of **N-butyl-2-cyanoacrylate (tissue glue)** or thrombin.

#### 4. Rescue Interventions for Refractory / Re-bleeding Cases [⭐ High-Yield]

##### A. Balloon Tamponade (Bridge to Definitive Therapy) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Sengstaken-Blakemore Tube**: Features **3 lumens and 2 balloons** (gastric balloon inflated with **300 mL of air** and retracted against GEJ; esophageal balloon inflated to **30–40 mmHg**).
- **Minnesota Tube**: Features **4 lumens and 2 balloons** (includes an esophageal aspiration port).
- _Safety Rule_: Balloons must be deflated every **12 hours** to prevent esophageal pressure necrosis.

##### B. Interventional Radiology

- **Transcatheter Arterial Embolization (TAE)**: Indicated for refractory non-variceal peptic ulcer bleeding following failed endoscopic hemostasis.
- **Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Stent Shunt (TIPS)**: Gold standard rescue for refractory variceal bleeding; connects portal vein branch directly to hepatic vein via an expandable metallic stent, dropping HVPG <12 mmHg.

##### C. Emergency Surgical Intervention [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications**: Massive continuous bleeding requiring >6 units of PRBCs, failure of dual endoscopic therapy twice, or hemodynamic collapse.
- **Surgical Procedures**:
    - _Duodenal Ulcer_: Longitudinal duodenotomy/pyloroplasty, **three-point under-running suture ligation of the bleeding vessel in the ulcer base** (duodenal gastroduodenal artery), and Heineke-Mikulicz pyloroplasty closure.
    - _Gastric Ulcer_: Wedge excision of gastric ulcer + primary closure or distal gastrectomy.
    - _Variceal Bleeding_: **Sugiura Procedure** (esophagogastric devascularization + lower esophageal transection + splenectomy) or surgical shunts.

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hypovolemic Shock & Cardiac Ischemia**: Myocardial infarction triggered by acute anemia.
- **Pulmonary Aspiration Pneumonia**: Inhalation of blood into airway during hematemesis.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**: Prerenal azotemia progressing to acute tubular necrosis.
- **Hepatic Encephalopathy**: Precipitated in cirrhotic patients by absorption of nitrogenous blood products from the gut.

---

### Dosage Rule (Drugs in Upper GI Bleeding)

#### 1. Terlipressin

- **Name**: Terlipressin Acetate (Glypressin)
- **Mechanism of Action**: Synthetic analog of vasopressin; acts selectively on vascular V1 receptors causing splanchnic vasoconstriction, lowering portal venous pressure.
- **Dose**: 2 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous injection
- **Frequency**: Every 4 hours (Q4H) for 48 hours, then 1 mg Q4H for up to 5 days.
- **Features**: First-line vasoactive drug in acute variceal bleeding; significantly reduces mortality.
- **Side Effects**: Abdominal cramps, hyponatremia, peripheral limb ischemia, hypertension, myocardial ischemia.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately on clinical suspicion of variceal bleed; stop after 2 to 5 days.

#### 2. Octreotide

- **Name**: Octreotide Acetate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Long-acting synthetic somatostatin analog; inhibits release of vasodilatory peptides (e.g., glucagon), inducing selective splanchnic vasoconstriction and reducing portal blood flow.
- **Dose**: 50 μg bolus, followed by 50 μg/hour
- **Dosage / Route**: IV bolus followed by continuous IV infusion.
- **Frequency**: Continuous IV infusion for 2 to 5 days.
- **Features**: Safe alternative to Terlipressin with fewer ischemic side effects.
- **Side Effects**: Hyperglycemia, nausea, abdominal discomfort, bradycardia.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start on presentation; continue for 2 to 5 days post-endoscopy.

#### 3. Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Pantoprazole Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits H⁺/K⁺ ATPase proton pumps in gastric parietal cells, maintaining intragastric pH >6.0 to stabilize blood clots.
- **Dose**: 80 mg IV bolus, followed by 8 mg/hour infusion
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous
- **Frequency**: Continuous infusion over 72 hours.
- **Features**: Standard acid suppression for non-variceal peptic ulcer bleeding.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, thrombophlebitis.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately post-endoscopy; continue infusion for 72 hours, then step down to oral 40 mg daily.

### Clinical Pearl

When resuscitating a patient with an acute upper GI bleed, **never over-transfuse with blood products**. Target a **restrictive hemoglobin range of 7 to 9 g/dL**. In variceal bleeding, aggressive blood transfusion expands intravascular volume, elevates the Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG >12 mmHg), and triggers catastrophic early rebleeding.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Using a single endoscopic technique (such as adrenaline injection alone) to manage an actively spurting peptic ulcer.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Monotherapy with adrenaline injection achieves transient hemostasis but carries a high rebleeding rate. **Standard of care mandates Dual Endoscopic Therapy: Adrenaline injection (1:10,000) MUST be combined with a second mechanical (hemoclip) or thermal (heater probe) modality**.

- **B** – **B**lood transfusion (restrictive trigger Hb <7–8 g/dL; target 7–9 g/dL).
- **L** – **L**arge-bore IV access (two 14G/16G cannulae) + Fluid resuscitation.
- **E** – **E**ndoscopy within 24 hours (Investigation & Treatment of Choice).
- **E** – **E**VBL (Esophageal Variceal Band Ligation) for varices.
- **D** – **D**ual therapy for ulcers (Adrenaline + Hemoclips / Thermal cautery).
- **I** – **I**ntravenous PPI (Pantoprazole 80 mg bolus + 8 mg/h infusion x 72h).
- **N** – **N**on-responsive / Refractory cases: TIPS for varices; TAE or Surgery for ulcers.
- **G** – **G**lypressin (Terlipressin 2 mg Q4H) or Octreotide for splanchnic vasoconstriction.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Intraluminal bleeding proximal to the Ligament of Treitz; presents with hematemesis and melena.
- **Primary Causes**: Peptic Ulcer Disease (60%), Mucosal Erosions (26%), Esophageal Varices (4%–20%), Mallory-Weiss Tear (4%).
- **Risk Stratification**: Glasgow-Blatchford Score (pre-endoscopy) and Rockall Score (post-endoscopy).
- **Resuscitation Strategy**: Permissive hypotension + Restrictive blood transfusion (Hb target 7–9 g/dL).
- **Key Pharmacotherapy**: Pantoprazole IV infusion (non-variceal); Terlipressin/Octreotide IV + Ceftriaxone IV (variceal).
- **Endoscopic Gold Standard**: Dual therapy for ulcers (Adrenaline + Clips); Band Ligation (EVBL) for varices.
- **Rescue Options**: Sengstaken tube tamponade → TIPS (varices) or TAE / Vessel Ligation (ulcers).

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Upper gastrointestinal bleeding is a life-threatening surgical emergency arising proximal to the Ligament of Treitz, presenting as hematemesis or melena, evaluated by Rockall/Glasgow-Blatchford risk scores, resuscitated via a restrictive transfusion protocol, and managed definitively with dual endoscopic hemostasis or variceal band ligation paired with targeted pharmacotherapy._
