# Thromboangiitis Obliterans (Buerger's Disease) & Peripheral Vascular Disease

_(Defaulting to 10 Marks / Long Answer depth as mark weightage was not specified)_

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Vascular Disease**: Any structural or functional abnormality affecting the arterial, venous, or lymphatic circulations, resulting in altered vessel compliance, blood flow impairment, or tissue hypoperfusion.
- **Thromboangiitis Obliterans (Buerger's Disease)** [⭐ High-Yield]: A non-atherosclerotic, segmentally occlusive, inflammatory vasculopathy affecting small- and medium-sized arteries and veins of the distal extremities (most commonly the lower limbs), predominantly occurring in young male heavy tobacco smokers.

---

### Enumeration: Causes of Lower Limb Ischemia [⭐ High-Yield]

Lower limb ischemia is broadly divided into acute and chronic etiologies:

#### 1. Acute Limb Ischemia (ALI) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Arterial Embolism** (~40% of ALI):
    - _Cardiac Sources_: Atrial fibrillation (most common), post-myocardial infarction mural thrombus, prosthetic heart valve thrombi, infective endocarditis vegetations, left atrial myxoma.
    - _Arterial Sources_: Thrombi originating from aortic, iliac, or popliteal artery aneurysms or ulcerated atherosclerotic plaques.
- **Acute Arterial Thrombosis** (~40% of ALI): Thrombosis overlying a pre-existing atherosclerotic plaque, thrombosed popliteal artery aneurysm, or graft occlusion.
- **Trauma & Iatrogenic**: Direct arterial transection, intimal flap detachment, or compression secondary to bone fractures.
- **Compartment Syndrome**: Raised osseofascial compartment pressure impeding capillary microperfusion.
- **Rare Etiologies**: Popliteal artery entrapment syndrome, phlegmasia cerulea dolens (massive DVT causing venous gangrene).

#### 2. Chronic Limb-Threatening Ischemia (CLTI) & Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Atherosclerosis** (Accountable for >90% of chronic occlusive cases).
- **Thromboangiitis Obliterans (Buerger's Disease)**.
- **Arteritides & Autoimmune Vasculitides**: Takayasu's arteritis, temporal arteritis, polyarteritis nodosa, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and systemic sclerosis/scleroderma.
- **Extrinsic Compression & Degenerative Disorders**: Cystic myxomatous degeneration of the popliteal artery.
- **Miscellaneous**: Radiation-induced arteritis, ergotism, and inadvertent intra-arterial drug injection.

### Etiology & Risk Factors of Buerger's Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Heavy Tobacco Use (Primary Essential Trigger)** [⭐ High-Yield]: The presence of active tobacco use (cigarette, bidi smoking, or chewing tobacco) is the single non-negotiable etiological factor. Hypersensitivity to tobacco glycoproteins drives the inflammatory cascade.
- **Age & Gender**: Typically affects young males **<45 years of age** (male-to-female ratio >9:1, though incidence in females is rising due to increased tobacco usage).
- **Socioeconomic & Geographic Factors**: High prevalence in low- and middle-income socioeconomic strata, particularly in South Asia, the Middle East, Far East, and Eastern Europe.
- **Immunological & Genetic Predisposition**: Increased frequency of HLA-A9, HLA-B5, and anti-endothelial cell antibodies (AECA).

- **Pan-Vasculitis**: Affects all three layers of small- and medium-sized arteries, accompanying veins, and adjacent nerves (neurovascular bundle involvement).
- **Cellular Thrombus Formation**: Histologically characterized by a highly cellular intraluminal thrombus packed with polymorphonuclear leukocytes, microabscesses, and multinucleated giant cells, while preserving the internal elastic lamina and vessel architecture (unlike necrotizing arteritis).
- **Segmental "Skip" Pattern**: Normal, uninvolved arterial segments alternate with acutely diseased occluded segments.
- **Absence of Atherosclerosis**: No lipid deposition, calcification, or atheromatous plaque formation occurs.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Triad of Buerger's Disease** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    1. **Instep / Distal Claudication**: Severe cramping pain in the arch of the foot, calf, or hand during exertion.
    2. **Superficial Thrombophlebitis Migrans**: Recurrent, tender, inflamed subcutaneous cords in the upper or lower extremities.
    3. **Raynaud's Phenomenon**: Cold-induced triphasic color changes (blanching white → cyanotic blue → rubor red) in distal digits.
- **Ischemic Rest Pain** [⭐ High-Yield]: Severe, excruciating, burning pain in the toes or fingers, worsened by elevation or lying flat at night, causing the patient to sit up and hang their feet over the side of the bed for gravity-assisted perfusion.

#### Signs

- **Peripheral Arterial Pulses** [⭐ High-Yield]: Distal pedal pulses (dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial) are **absent or severely diminished**, whereas proximal pulses (femoral and popliteal) are characteristically **intact and normal**.
- **Allen's Test**: Demonstrates palmar arch occlusion due to radial or ulnar artery involvement in the upper extremity.
- **Trophic Changes & Sunset Foot**: Brittle nails, hair loss, thin shiny skin, slow capillary refill time (>3 seconds), and **dependent rubor (sunset foot sign)**—pallor on elevation transitioning to deep purple-red color on dependency.
- **Tissue Loss**: Shallow, painful, non-healing ulcers on the tips of digits progressing to dry black gangrene.

### Diagnosis & Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Bedside & Non-Invasive Vascular Testing

- **Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Normal ABI = **0.9 to 1.4**.
    - ABI **<0.9** indicates hemodynamically significant stenosis.
    - ABI **<0.4** indicates severe Chronic Limb-Threatening Ischemia (CLTI).
- **Toe-Brachial Index (TBI) & Digital Pressures** [⭐ High-Yield]: Digital pressure **<30–50 mmHg** or TBI **<0.6**confirms severe infrapopliteal / small-vessel distal occlusive disease.

#### 2. Imaging Modalities

- **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) / CT Angiography [Gold Standard]** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Demonstrates segmental occlusions of distal small-to-medium vessels (anterior tibial, posterior tibial, peroneal, plantar, and digital arteries).
    - Pathognomonic **"corkscrew" collaterals** (tortuous vasa vasorum bypassing occluded segments) around sites of obstruction.
    - Absence of atheromatous plaque or calcification in proximal vessels (aorta, iliac, femoral).
- **Duplex Ultrasonography (DUS)**: Maps distal arterial flow and evaluates superficial vein thrombosis.

#### 3. Laboratory Workup (Exclusion of Collagen Vascular Diseases)

- **Serological Screen**: Full blood count, fasting blood glucose, lipid profile, ESR, CRP, Antinuclear Antibodies (ANA), Rheumatoid Factor (RF), Antiphospholipid antibodies, and serum complement (C3, C4) to rule out SLE, scleroderma, and hypercoagulable states.

#### 1. Non-Pharmacological Management (Primary Basis) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Absolute Tobacco Cessation** [⭐ High-Yield]: The single most essential treatment. Complete abstinence from all forms of tobacco (cigarettes, bidis, chewing tobacco, e-cigarettes) halts disease progression and prevents major limb loss. Continued smoking results in relentless vascular occlusion and amputation.
- **General Protective Care**: Protection of extremities from cold exposure, wearing loose warm footwear, and meticulous hygiene to prevent minor skin trauma.

#### 2. Surgical & Interventional Procedures

- **Lumbar Sympathectomy** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Indication_: Severe rest pain or superficial non-healing digital ulcers when distal revascularization is impossible.
    - _Mechanism_: Chemical (phenol) or surgical ablation of the L2–L4 sympathetic ganglia abolishes cutaneous vasoconstrictor tone, increasing skin blood flow and relieving rest pain.
- **Revascularization (Bypass / Angioplasty)**: Rarely feasible because the disease process diffusely occludes distal infrapopliteal target outflow vessels.
- **Amputation**: Reserved for frank gangrene, spreading infection, or intractable rest pain in unreconstructable limbs.

### Special Note on Gangrene [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Definition

**Gangrene** is defined as the macroscopic death of tissue resulting from severe arterial ischemia, venous obstruction, or bacterial infection, accompanied by hemoglobin degradation and iron sulfide formation, which turns the affected tissue black.

#### 2. Pathological & Clinical Varieties of Gangrene

```
                            TYPES OF GANGRENE
                                    |
     +------------------------------+------------------------------+
     |                              |                              |
  Dry Gangrene                   Wet Gangrene                   Gas Gangrene
* Aseptic arterial occlusion   * Infected venous/arterial     * Anaerobic Clostridial
* Mummified, black tissue        necrosis                       infection
* Line of Demarcation PRESENT  * Swollen, foul-smelling,      * Severe pain, crepitus,
                                 spreading without clear line   sweetish odor, toxemia
```

- **Dry Gangrene** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Etiology_: Gradual, progressive arterial occlusion (e.g., atherosclerosis, Buerger's disease) in sterile conditions.
    - _Features_: Distal tissue becomes desiccated, shriveled, wrinkled, hard, and black. A clear **line of demarcation**(formed by a layer of granulation tissue) separates dead tissue from surrounding viable skin.
    - _Management_: Keep dry and sterile; allow autoamputation or perform elective formal amputation once the line of demarcation forms.
- **Wet Gangrene** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Etiology_: Sudden arterial or venous occlusion combined with superadded bacterial infection and putrefaction (e.g., infected diabetic foot, severe acute limb ischemia).
    - _Features_: Tissues are moist, edematous, foul-smelling, purulent, and rapidly dark, with **no clear line of demarcation** and high risk of spreading cellulitis and septic shock.
    - _Management_: **Surgical Emergency**; requires fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and urgent emergency debridement or amputation.
- **Gas Gangrene (Clostridial Myonecrosis)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - _Etiology_: Infection by anaerobic, spore-forming, Gram-positive bacilli—primarily _Clostridium perfringens_—following deep contaminated crush or missile wounds.
    - _Features_: Severe rapid muscle necrosis, gas production (soft-tissue crepitus on palpation/X-ray), thin sweet-smelling serosanguineous exudate, and rapid progression to systemic septic shock.
    - _Management_: High-dose IV Penicillin G + Clindamycin, emergency wide radical surgical excision/debridement of necrotic muscle groups, and hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) therapy.

### Complications of Buerger's Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Ischemic Complications**: Non-healing trophic digital ulcers, dry or wet gangrene, and secondary bacterial osteomyelitis.
- **Surgical & Psychological**: Loss of digits or limbs due to single or multiple major amputations, phantom limb pain, and severe impairment of quality of life.

### Dosage Rule (Pharmacotherapy for Peripheral Vascular Disease)

#### 1. Cilostazol

- **Name**: Cilostazol
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective Phosphodiesterase-3 (PDE-3) inhibitor; increases intracellular cAMP in platelets and vascular smooth muscle cells, providing potent antiplatelet activity and peripheral vasodilation.
- **Dose**: 100 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet.
- **Frequency**: 12-hourly (BD), taken 30 minutes before breakfast and dinner.
- **Features**: Improves pain-free walking distance in claudication and enhances microvascular perfusion.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, palpitations, diarrhea, dizziness.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start in symptomatic claudication or ischemic rest pain; **strictly contraindicated in patients with congestive heart failure**.

#### 2. Nifedipine

- **Name**: Nifedipine
- **Mechanism of Action**: Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker; inhibits voltage-gated L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle, relieving peripheral vasospasm.
- **Dose**: 10 mg to 20 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral modified-release tablet.
- **Frequency**: 12-hourly (BD).
- **Features**: First-line agent for vasospastic Raynaud's symptoms associated with Buerger's disease.
- **Side Effects**: Peripheral ankle edema, flushing, headache, reflex tachycardia.

#### 3. Iloprost

- **Name**: Iloprost
- **Mechanism of Action**: Synthetic prostacyclin (PGI₂) analog; induces potent vasodilation, inhibits platelet aggregation, and preserves endothelial integrity.
- **Dose**: 0.5 to 2.0 ng/kg/min titrating infusion.
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion over 6 hours daily for 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Side Effects**: Flushing, headache, nausea, hypotension.

### Clinical Pearl

In a young patient presenting with **digital gangrene or rest pain**, **always perform a thorough upper extremity vascular examination including Allen's test and check for a history of superficial thrombophlebitis**. Finding upper limb palmar arch occlusion alongside lower limb pedal pulse loss strongly points to Buerger's disease rather than routine atherosclerosis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Recommending **surgical bypass grafting** as the primary therapeutic strategy in a patient with advanced Buerger's disease.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Buerger's disease affects small- and medium-sized distal infrapopliteal vessels (tibial, peroneal, and digital arteries), creating diffuse microvascular occlusions without suitable distal target outflow vessels for a bypass graft. **Complete tobacco cessation is the only intervention proven to halt disease progression and prevent major amputation**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Non-atherosclerotic, inflammatory segmental occlusive disease of small/medium vessels in young tobacco smokers.
- **Classic Triad**: Intermittent claudication + Raynaud's phenomenon + Superficial thrombophlebitis migrans.
- **Diagnostic Hallmark**: Normal proximal pulses with absent pedal pulses; "corkscrew" collaterals on DSA.
- **Gangrene Types**: Dry gangrene has a clear line of demarcation; wet gangrene is a surgical emergency without a clear line of demarcation.
- **Cornerstone Management**: **Absolute and complete tobacco cessation**.
