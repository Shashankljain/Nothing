# Causes of Leg Ulcers & Thromboangiitis Obliterans (TAO / Buerger's Disease)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Leg Ulcer**: A break in the continuity of the covering skin/epithelium of the leg (typically below the knee) persisting for >3 weeks, resulting from chronic tissue hypoxia, venous hypertension, infection, neuropathy, trauma, or cellular necrosis.
- **Thromboangiitis Obliterans (TAO / Buerger's Disease)** [⭐ High-Yield]: A non-atherosclerotic, segmentally occlusive, inflammatory pan-vasculopathy affecting small- and medium-sized arteries and veins of the distal extremities (most commonly lower limbs), occurring predominantly in young male heavy tobacco smokers.

---

### Etiology / Causes of Leg Ulcers [⭐ High-Yield]

Leg ulcers are broadly categorized based on their underlying pathological mechanisms:

```
                               CAUSES OF LEG ULCERS
                                        |
     +-------------------+--------------+--------------+-------------------+
     |                   |                             |                   |
Vascular (~90-95%)    Neuropathic / Metabolic       Infective           Traumatic / Neoplastic
* Venous (80-85%)     * Diabetic foot               * Bacterial         * Pressure / Bedsores
* Arterial (5-10%)    * Hansen's (Leprosy)            * Tuberculous       * Marjolin's ulcer (SCC)
* Mixed (15-30%)      * Syringomyelia / Spina bifida * Fungal / Syphilis * Rodent ulcer (BCC)
```

#### 1. Vascular Causes (Accountable for >90% of Leg Ulcers)

- **Venous Ulcers (80–85%)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Caused by chronic **ambulatory venous hypertension** secondary to superficial venous reflux (great/small saphenous vein incompetence), deep venous valvular incompetence, or post-thrombotic syndrome (PTS). Characteristically located in the **gaiter region** (medial malleolus) over Cockett's perforator sites.
- **Arterial / Ischemic Ulcers (5–10%)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Caused by macrovascular or microvascular tissue hypoperfusion secondary to **atherosclerosis obliterans**, **Thromboangiitis Obliterans (TAO / Buerger's disease)**, Raynaud's syndrome, or acute arterial embolism/thrombosis. Characteristically located on distal pressure points (tips of toes, heel, lateral malleolus, shin).
- **Mixed Arterial-Venous Ulcers (15–30% of venous presentations)**: Coexisting peripheral arterial occlusive disease and venous insufficiency.

#### 2. Neuropathic & Trophic Causes

- **Diabetic Foot Ulcers**: Triad of peripheral sensorimotor neuropathy (loss of protective sensation), macro/microvascular arterial ischemia, and impaired resistance to infection leading to plantar pressure ulceration.
- **Trophic Ulcers of Leprosy**: Sensory loss secondary to posterior tibial nerve damage causing unperceived repetitive mechanical trauma and neurotrophic ulceration of the sole.
- **Other Neuropathies**: Tabes dorsalis, syringomyelia, spina bifida, and peripheral nerve transections.

#### 3. Infective Causes

- **Bacterial**: Pyogenic cellulitis, ecthyma, Meleney's synergistic gangrene, and necrotizing fasciitis.
- **Mycobacterial**: Cutaneous tuberculosis (lupus vulgaris, scrofuloderma, induratum of Bazin) and Buruli ulcer (_Mycobacterium ulcerans_).
- **Fungal & Spirochetal**: Syphilitic gumma (punched-out edge), Madura foot (mycetoma), and sporotrichosis.

#### 4. Autoimmune & Vasculitic Causes

- **Rheumatoid Arthritis & Systemic Vasculitis**: Cutaneous vasculitis, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma), and Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN).
- **Pyoderma Gangrenosum**: Painful, rapidly spreading ulceration with violaceous, undermined edges, strongly associated with Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Ulcerative Colitis, Crohn's) and rheumatoid arthritis.

#### 5. Traumatic & Physical Causes

- **Pressure Injuries / Bedsores**: Ischemic necrosis over bony prominences (calcaneus, lateral malleolus, sacrum) due to prolonged, unrelieved external pressure.
- **Thermal / Chemical / Radiation**: Frostbite, electrical/thermal burns, and radiation dermatitis.

#### 6. Neoplastic Causes

- **Primary Cutaneous Malignancies**: Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC), Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC / Rodent ulcer), Malignant Melanoma, and Kaposi Sarcoma.
- **Marjolin's Ulcer** [⭐ High-Yield]: A rare, aggressive **Squamous Cell Carcinoma** arising in a long-standing, non-healing chronic scar, burn wound, or chronic venous ulcer (characterized by everted edges, foul odor, bleeding, and rapid phenotypic change).

### Thromboangiitis Obliterans (TAO / Buerger's Disease)

### Etiology & Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Heavy Tobacco Exposure (Essential Factor)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Active consumption of tobacco (cigarettes, bidis, or chewing tobacco) is the primary non-negotiable etiological trigger. Cell-mediated hypersensitivity to tobacco glycoproteins drives vascular inflammation.
- **Age & Gender**: Predominantly affects young individuals (**<45 years of age**), historically with a strong male preponderance (male-to-female ratio >9:1).
- **Socioeconomic & Geographic Distribution**: Highest prevalence in low-income populations, South Asia, Middle East, Far East, and Eastern Europe.
- **Immunological Markers**: Increased prevalence of HLA-A9, HLA-B5, and circulating anti-endothelial cell antibodies (AECA).

- **Pan-Vasculitis**: Acute and chronic inflammatory cell infiltration involving all three layers of small- and medium-sized arteries, accompanying veins, and adjacent nerves within the neurovascular bundle.
- **Cellular Thrombus**: Intraluminal thrombosis characterized by dense cellular infiltration (neutrophils, microabscesses, and multinucleated giant cells) with strict preservation of the internal elastic lamina and vascular wall structure (unlike necrotizing arteritis).
- **Segmental "Skip" Pattern**: Diseased, occluded arterial segments alternate with completely normal intervening vessel segments.
- **Absence of Atherosclerosis**: Devoid of lipid deposition, foam cells, calcification, or atheromatous plaques.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Triad of Buerger's Disease** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    1. **Instep / Distal Claudication**: Pain in the arch of the foot, calf, or hand brought on by walking or exercise.
    2. **Superficial Thrombophlebitis Migrans**: Recurrent, tender, inflamed subcutaneous venous cords affecting different superficial vein segments at different times.
    3. **Raynaud's Phenomenon**: Cold-induced triphasic color changes (pallor white → cyanotic blue → rubor red) in distal digits.
- **Ischemic Rest Pain** [⭐ High-Yield]: Excruciating, burning pain in the toes or fingers, worsened by elevation or lying flat at night, causing the patient to sit up and hang their feet over the side of the bed for gravity-assisted blood flow.

#### Physical Signs

- **Peripheral Arterial Pulses** [⭐ High-Yield]: Distal pedal pulses (**dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial) are absent or severely diminished**, whereas proximal pulses (**femoral and popliteal) are characteristically intact and normal**.
- **Allen's Test**: Demonstrates palmar arterial arch occlusion due to concomitant radial or ulnar artery involvement in the hand.
- **Trophic & Cutaneous Changes**: Brittle nails, thin shiny skin, delayed capillary refill time (>3 seconds), **dependent rubor (sunset foot sign)** on dependency, painful shallow ulcers on toe tips, and dry mummified gangrene.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Non-Invasive Vascular Testing**:
    - _Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI)_: ABI <0.9 confirms peripheral arterial occlusion; ABI <0.4 indicates severe chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI).
    - _Toe-Brachial Index (TBI)_: Digital pressure <30–50 mmHg or TBI <0.6 confirms severe infrapopliteal small-vessel occlusive disease.
- **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) / CT Angiography [Gold Standard]** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Demonstrates abrupt, segmental occlusions of distal small-to-medium vessels (anterior tibial, posterior tibial, peroneal, plantar, and digital arteries).
    - Pathognomonic **"corkscrew" collaterals** (tortuous vasa vasorum bypassing occluded segments) around sites of obstruction.
    - Normal, smooth proximal arterial tree (aorta, iliac, femoral) without atheromatous plaques.
- **Autoimmune & Serological Screen**: Normal ESR, CRP, ANA, Rheumatoid Factor (RF), and complement levels, ruling out SLE, systemic sclerosis, and necrotizing vasculitis.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     MANAGEMENT OF BUERGER'S DISEASE
                                    |
     +------------------------------+------------------------------+
     |                                                             |
Strict Tobacco Cessation                               Pharmacological & Surgical Interventions
* ABSOLUTE CESSATION of all tobacco                     * Vasodilators (Cilostazol 100 mg BD)
  (Cigarettes, bidis, chewing tobacco)                  * Calcium Channel Blockers (Nifedipine)
* Single most vital therapy to halt                     * Lumbar Sympathectomy (L2–L4 ablation)
  disease progression & avoid amputation                * Minor / Major Amputation for gangrene
```

#### 1. Non-Pharmacological Management (Primary Basis) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Absolute & Complete Tobacco Cessation** [⭐ High-Yield]: The single non-negotiable intervention that halts disease progression and prevents major limb loss. Continued smoking results in relentless vascular occlusion and amputation.
- **General Protective Measures**: Cold protection (warm footwear/gloves), foot care hygiene, and avoidance of minor trauma.

#### 2. Surgical & Interventional Procedures

- **Lumbar Sympathectomy** [⭐ High-Yield]: Chemical (phenol) or surgical ablation of the L2–L4 sympathetic ganglia (or endoscopic thoracic sympathectomy for upper limb) to abolish cutaneous vasoconstrictor tone, increase skin blood flow, and relieve ischemic rest pain/heal superficial ulcers.
- **Revascularization (Bypass / Angioplasty)**: Rarely feasible due to diffuse occlusion of distal infrapopliteal target outflow vessels.
- **Amputation**: Digital ray excision, transmetatarsal, or below-knee amputation for frank gangrene, superadded wet infection, or unreconstructable limb-threatening ischemia.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- Trophic digital ulceration, dry or wet gangrene, secondary osteomyelitis, phantom limb pain, and single or multiple major limb amputations.

---

### Prognosis

- Excellent disease stabilization if absolute tobacco abstinence is maintained; poor prognosis with high major amputation rates if tobacco use continues.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In a young patient presenting with **digital gangrene or rest pain**, **always perform a upper extremity vascular examination including Allen's test and check for a history of superficial thrombophlebitis**. Finding upper limb palmar arch occlusion alongside pedal pulse loss with intact popliteal/femoral pulses strongly points to Buerger's disease rather than routine atherosclerosis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Recommending **surgical arterial bypass grafting** as the primary therapeutic strategy for a patient with advanced Buerger's disease.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Buerger's disease diffusely affects small- and medium-sized distal infrapopliteal vessels (tibial, peroneal, and digital arteries), creating occlusions without suitable distal target outflow vessels for a bypass graft. **Absolute tobacco cessation is the only intervention proven to halt disease progression and prevent major amputation**.

### Dosage Rule (Pharmacotherapy for Vasospastic & Ischemic Disorders)

#### 1. Cilostazol

- **Name**: Cilostazol
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective Phosphodiesterase-3 (PDE-3) inhibitor; increases intracellular cAMP in platelets and vascular smooth muscle cells, providing potent antiplatelet activity and peripheral vasodilation.
- **Dose**: 100 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet.
- **Frequency**: 12-hourly (BD), taken 30 minutes before breakfast and dinner.
- **Features**: Improves pain-free walking distance in claudication and enhances microvascular perfusion.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, palpitations, diarrhea, dizziness.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start in symptomatic claudication or ischemic rest pain; **strictly contraindicated in patients with congestive heart failure**.

#### 2. Nifedipine

- **Name**: Nifedipine
- **Mechanism of Action**: Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker; inhibits voltage-gated L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle, relieving peripheral vasospasm.
- **Dose**: 10 mg to 20 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral modified-release tablet.
- **Frequency**: 12-hourly (BD).
- **Features**: First-line pharmacological agent for vasospastic Raynaud's symptoms.
- **Side Effects**: Peripheral ankle edema, flushing, headache, reflex tachycardia.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start in severe vasospastic Raynaud's phenomenon; titrate to response.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: B-U-E-R-G-E-R

- **B** – **B**idi / Tobacco smoking is the essential etiological trigger.
- **U** – **U**pper and lower extremity small/medium vessel involvement.
- **E** – **E**xclusively non-atherosclerotic inflammatory pan-vasculitis.
- **R** – **R**aynaud's phenomenon + Rest pain + Recurrent thrombophlebitis (Triad).
- **G** – **G**angrene (dry, digital) resulting from severe distal ischemia.
- **E** – **E**ndarterectomy/Bypass rarely feasible due to loss of distal outflow tract.
- **R** – **R**estoration of tissue perfusion via absolute tobacco abstinence to prevent amputation.

### Quick Revision

- **Major Causes of Leg Ulcers**: Venous (~80–85%), Arterial (~5–10%), Mixed (15–30%), Diabetic/Neuropathic, Infective, Vasculitic, and Neoplastic (Marjolin's ulcer).
- **Buerger's Disease Definition**: Non-atherosclerotic, segmentally occlusive pan-vasculitis of small/medium distal vessels in young male tobacco smokers.
- **Classic Triad**: Instep claudication + Raynaud's phenomenon + Superficial thrombophlebitis migrans.
- **Angiographic Gold Standard**: Segmental occlusions with pathognomonic "corkscrew" collaterals and normal proximal vessels.
- **Cornerstone Therapy**: **Absolute and complete tobacco cessation**.
