# Peptic Ulcer Disease and Gastric Outlet Obstruction

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)** refers to a breach in the digestive mucosa extending through the muscularis mucosae into deeper tissue layers, occurring in areas exposed to gastric acid and pepsin. In the absence of gastric acid, peptic ulceration does not occur.

Common anatomical sites include:

- First part of the duodenum (D₁).
- Lesser curvature of the stomach (particularly near the incisura angularis).
- Gastrojejunal stoma following gastric surgery (stomal / anastomotic ulcer).
- Lower esophagus in gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD).
- Meckel's diverticulum containing ectopic gastric mucosa.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Peptic ulceration results primarily from an imbalance between aggressive luminal factors (acid, pepsin, infection) and protective mucosal defense mechanisms.

#### 1. Major Etiological Factors

- **_Helicobacter pylori_ Infection**: Responsible for 90%–95% of duodenal ulcers and 60%–65% of gastric ulcers. _H. pylori_ produces urease, which converts urea to ammonia, causing antral G-cell hyperstimulation and hypergastrinemia, leading to elevated acid output.
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)**: Inhibit the cyclo-oxygenase-1 (COX-1) enzyme, disrupting cytoprotective prostaglandin synthesis (PGE₂ and PGI₂), which impairs gastric mucus and bicarbonate secretion. Selective COX-2 inhibitors carry a lower incidence of ulceration.

#### 2. Secondary & Contributory Risk Factors

- **Cigarette Smoking**: Predisposes to peptic ulcer formation, impairs healing, and significantly increases relapse rates.
- **Gastric Acid Hypersecretion / Gastrinoma**: Severe acid hypersecretion seen in **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (ZES)** due to gastrin-secreting neuroendocrine tumors.
- **Stress Ulceration**: Occurs in critically ill patients (e.g., ICU patients, major burns [Curling's ulcer], severe head trauma [Cushing's ulcer]).
- **Rare Causes**: Autoimmune Immunoglobulin G4-related (IgG4) disease in refractory, culture-negative gastric ulcers.

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Anatomical / Site-based Classification

- **Duodenal Ulcer (DU)**: Most common type (DU > GU). Most frequently affects the first part of the duodenum (D₁).
    - _Anterior DU_: Tends to perforate into the peritoneal cavity.
    - _Posterior DU_: Erodes into the gastroduodenal artery, leading to massive upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage.
    - _"Kissing Ulcers"_: Coexisting anterior and posterior duodenal ulcers.
- **Gastric Ulcer (GU)**: Classified according to **Johnson's Classification**:

|Johnson Type|Location|Acid Secretion Status|Surgical Management [⭐ High-Yield]|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Type I**|Lesser curvature near incisura angularis (m/c type)|Normal or low acid|Distal gastrectomy + Billroth I / II reconstruction|
|**Type II**|Combination of Gastric + Duodenal ulcer|Acid hypersecretion (+)|Distal gastrectomy + Reconstruction + PPIs / Vagotomy|
|**Type III**|Prepyloric ulcer (within 2 cm of pylorus)|Acid hypersecretion (+)|Distal gastrectomy + Reconstruction + PPIs / Vagotomy|
|**Type IV**|High up on lesser curve near gastroesophageal junction|Normal or low acid (High bleeding risk)|Pauchet, Kelling-Madlener, Csendes, or Subtotal gastrectomy|
|**Type V**|Diffuse throughout stomach|Drug-induced (NSAIDs)|Stop NSAIDs + PPI medical therapy|

- **_H. pylori_ Mediated Pathophysiology**: _H. pylori_ colonizes antral mucosa and produces urease, hydrolyzing urea to ammonia and CO₂. Ammonia neutralizes local gastric acid, triggering a negative feedback loop that stimulates antral G-cells to release gastrin. Resulting hypergastrinemia causes parietal cell hyperplasia and acid hypersecretion. Increased acid load entering D₁ induces **duodenal gastric metaplasia**. Metaplastic gastric epithelium in D₁ is subsequently colonized by _H. pylori_, leading to active duodenitis and ulceration.
- **NSAID Mediated Pathophysiology**: Systemic COX-1 inhibition reduces mucosal blood flow, impairs bicarbonate/mucus secretion, and disrupts epithelial tight junctions, allowing acid-pepsin back-diffusion into the submucosa.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Epigastric Pain**: Gnawing, burning, or aching upper abdominal pain, often radiating to the back.
- **Pain-Food Relationship**:
    - _Duodenal Ulcer_: Pain occurs 2–3 hours after meals or at night ("hunger pain"), and is classically **relieved by food** or antacids.
    - _Gastric Ulcer_: Pain occurs early after eating and is **worsened by food**, often leading to fear of eating and weight loss.
- **Periodicity**: Symptom-free intervals lasting weeks to months followed by symptomatic relapses, corresponding to spontaneous healing and reactivation of ulcers.
- **Associated Dyspeptic Symptoms**: Epigastric fullness, nausea, bloating, and water brash.

#### Signs

- **Localized Epigastric Tenderness**: Point tenderness in the epigastrium (the "pointing sign").
- **Physical Examination in Uncomplicated PUD**: Frequently unremarkable between acute exacerbations.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Endoscopic Evaluation (Gold Standard)

- **Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (Gastroduodenoscopy)**: Investigation of choice.
    - Direct visualization of ulcer site, size, depth, and stigmata of recent hemorrhage.
    - Performs retroflexion ("U" or "J" maneuver) to inspect the incisura, lesser curve, and gastroesophageal junction.
    - **Gastric Ulcer Biopsy Rule [⭐ High-Yield]**: All gastric ulcers must be regarded as potentially malignant. Multiple biopsies (at least 6 to 10 well-targeted biopsies from ulcer edges and base) are mandatory to exclude ulcerated gastric adenocarcinoma. Repeat endoscopy and biopsy are required after a course of antisecretory therapy to confirm complete healing.

#### 2. Testing for _Helicobacter pylori_ [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Invasive Tests (Endoscopy-based)**:
    - _Rapid Urease Test (CLO Test)_: Biopsy placed in urea medium; color change indicates urease presence.
    - _Histology_: Gastric antral biopsy stained with modified Giemsa or Warthin-Starry silver stain.
    - _Culture_: Microbiological culture on selective media for antibiotic sensitivity testing in refractory cases.
- **Non-Invasive Tests**:
    - _Urea Breath Test (¹³C or ¹⁴C UBT)_: High sensitivity and specificity; preferred for confirming post-treatment eradication.
    - _Stool Antigen Assay_: Accurate for primary diagnosis and post-treatment monitoring.
    - _Serology_: Detects IgG antibodies; remains positive long after eradication, limiting its utility for post-treatment verification.

#### 3. Laboratory Findings

- **Full Blood Count**: Hemoglobin and hematocrit to evaluate microcytic hypochromic anemia from chronic occult blood loss.
- **Serum Gastrin Level**: Indicated in recurrent, refractory, or multiple atypical ulcers to screen for Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (fasting gastrin >10-fold elevated).

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Medical therapy forms the cornerstone of treatment for uncomplicated peptic ulcer disease.

#### 1. Medical Management

##### A. First-Line _H. pylori_ Eradication Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

Eradication of _H. pylori_ cures peptic ulcer disease in up to 90% of cases and dramatically reduces ulcer recurrence.

- **Triple Therapy Protocol (14 days)**:
    - Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) (standard dose) twice daily.
    - Amoxicillin 1 g twice daily.
    - Clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily.
    - _(Metronidazole 400 mg twice daily substituted if penicillin-allergic)_.
- **Quadruple Therapy (for areas with high clarithromycin resistance)**:
    - PPI twice daily + Bismuth subsalicylate + Metronidazole + Tetracycline for 14 days.

##### B. Acid Suppression Therapy

- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)**: Omeprazole 20–40 mg PO daily, Pantoprazole 40 mg PO daily, or Lansoprazole 30 mg PO daily for 4–8 weeks.
- **H₂-Receptor Antagonists**: Ranitidine 150 mg PO twice daily or Famotidine 20 mg PO twice daily.

##### C. Lifestyle Modifications

- Cessation of cigarette smoking.
- Complete withdrawal of NSAIDs and aspirin. If NSAID continuation is mandatory, co-prescribe daily PPI or misoprostol.

#### 2. Surgical Management for Refractory or Uncomplicated Ulcers

Elective surgery for uncomplicated PUD is now exceptionally rare due to effective PPIs and _H. pylori_ eradication.

- **Surgical Options for Gastric Ulcer (by Johnson Type)**:
    - _Type I_: Distal gastrectomy with Billroth I or Billroth II reconstruction.
    - _Type II & III_: Distal gastrectomy + Billroth reconstruction + PPIs / Truncal vagotomy (due to acid hypersecretion).
    - _Type IV_: Pauchet, Kelling-Madlener, Csendes procedure, or Subtotal gastrectomy with Roux-en-Y reconstruction.
- **Acid-Reducing Vagotomy Procedures for Duodenal Ulcer**:
    - _Highly Selective Vagotomy (HSV / Parietal Cell Vagotomy)_: Denervates parietal cell mass while preserving vagal innervation to the antrum and pylorus; drainage procedure not required.
    - _Truncal Vagotomy + Drainage_: Division of main anterior and posterior vagal trunks + drainage procedure (Heineke-Mikulicz pyloroplasty or Gastrojejunostomy) to prevent gastric stasis.

### Complications of Peptic Ulcer Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     PEPTIC ULCER COMPLICATIONS
                                  |
        +-------------------------+-------------------------+
        |                         |                         |
  Perforation               Hemorrhage                Stenosis / GOO
  - Anterior DU m/c         - Posterior DU m/c        - Cicatrization of D1
  - Peritonitis             - Gastroduodenal Artery   - Non-bilious vomiting
  - Omental Patch Repair    - Endoscopic Hemoclip     - Hypochloraemic alkalosis
```

#### 1. Perforated Peptic Ulcer [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Epidemiology**: Most common in elderly patients taking NSAIDs. Anterior DU is the most frequent site of perforation.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Sudden onset of excruciating, generalised abdominal pain. Board-like abdominal rigidity, severe tenderness, rebound tenderness, and absence of abdominal respiratory movements.
- **Imaging**: Erect chest radiograph demonstrates **free gas under the diaphragm** (pneumoperitoneum) in >50% of cases. Non-contrast or CECT abdominal scan is the most accurate diagnostic modality.
- **Management**:
    - Emergency resuscitation: NPO, IV fluids, nasogastric decompression, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and analgesia.
    - Emergency Surgery: **Graham's Omental Patch Repair** (or Cellan-Jones omental patch) via laparotomy or laparoscopy.

#### 2. Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Epidemiology**: Peptic ulceration is the most common cause of upper GI bleeding (60%). Posterior duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery**. Gastric ulcers erode into the **left gastric artery**.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Hematemesis ("coffee-ground" or frank blood) and melena. Hemodynamic instability, tachycardia, and shock in massive bleeding.
- **Management**:
    - Resuscitation (IV fluids, blood transfusion) + IV high-dose PPI infusion.
    - Emergency Upper GI Endoscopy: Hemostasis using dual modality (adrenaline injection + hemoclips / thermal coagulation).
    - Surgical Intervention: Indicated if endoscopic hemostasis fails twice or in massive refractory re-bleeding → Laparotomy, gastrotomy/duodenotomy, and **under-running/ligation of the bleeding vessel**.

### Special Note: Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO) [⭐ High-Yield]

Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO) refers to physical blockade or narrowing of the pyloric channel or duodenal bulb preventing normal gastric emptying.

#### 1. Etiology

- **Benign Causes**: Chronic scarring, fibrosis, and cicatrization from recurrent duodenal or prepyloric ulceration (Pyloric Stenosis).
- **Malignant Causes**: Gastric antral adenocarcinoma (most common cause in modern practice) and pancreatic carcinoma. _GOO should be considered malignant until proven otherwise_.

#### 2. Pathophysiology & Metabolic Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

```

A[Pyloric Channel Cicatrization / Tumor Obstruction]:::primary --> B[Persistent Vomiting of Gastric Juice HCl]:::danger
B --> C[Loss of H+, Cl- and Water]:::danger
C --> D[Initial State: Hypochloraemic Metabolic Alkalosis]:::warning
D --> E[Progressive Dehydration & Hyponatremia]:::danger
E --> F[Renal Compensatory Mechanism: Distal Tubule Na+ Reabsorption]:::primary
F --> G[Renal Excretion of K+ and H+ in Exchange for Na+]:::danger
G --> H[Hypokalemia + Paradoxical Aciduria Urine pH < 7.0]:::danger
D --> I[Lowering of Circulating Ionized Calcium Ca2+]:::warning
I --> J[Neuromuscular Hyperexcitability & Tetany]:::danger
```

- **Vomiting of Pure Gastric Juice**: Results in severe loss of water, H⁺, Cl⁻, Na⁺, and K⁺.
- **Hypochloraemic Metabolic Alkalosis**: Loss of gastric HCl produces primary hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis.
- **Development of Paradoxical Aciduria**:
    1. Dehydration activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), forcing the kidney to retain Na⁺.
    2. To retain Na⁺ at the distal renal tubule, the kidney excretes K⁺ and H⁺ ions into the urine.
    3. Excretion of H⁺ ions in exchange for Na⁺ results in **paradoxical aciduria** (acidic urine in the presence of systemic alkalosis).
    4. Concurrent loss of K⁺ leads to **hypokalemia**.
- **Tetany**: Alkalosis reduces circulating ionized calcium (Ca²⁺) concentrations, predisposing the patient to carpopedal spasms and tetany.

#### 3. Clinical Features of GOO

- **Vomiting**: Characteristic non-bilious, projectile vomiting of undigested food eaten 12–24 hours earlier.
- **Upper Abdominal Symptoms**: Severe epigastric fullness, bloating, and early satiety.
- **Physical Signs**:
    - Dehydration, dry mucous membranes, loss of skin turgor.
    - **Visible Gastric Peristalsis**: Waves traveling across the upper abdomen from left to right.
    - **Succussion Splash**: Audible splashing sound heard on shaking the patient's abdomen >3–4 hours after last meal/fluid intake.

#### 4. Diagnosis & Investigations of GOO

- **Laboratory Studies**: Serum electrolytes demonstrating hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypochloremia, and elevated blood urea/creatinine (prerenal azotemia). Arterial blood gas (ABG) shows metabolic alkalosis.
- **Upper GI Endoscopy (Diagnostic Gold Standard)**:
    - Performed after thorough gastric lavage with a wide-bore tube.
    - Confirms pyloric stenosis and allows **multiple biopsies** of the prepyloric area to exclude underlying malignancy.
- **Barium Meal / Contrast Radiography**: Demonstrates a grossly distended "bowl-shaped" stomach with delayed or absent contrast entry into the duodenum.

#### 5. Management of GOO [⭐ High-Yield]

##### Step 1: Resuscitation & Correction of Metabolic Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Intravenous Rehydration**: IV **0.9% Normal Saline** (or N/2 Normal Saline in 5% Dextrose). Normal Saline provides Cl⁻ and Na⁺, allowing the kidney to correct the acid-base disturbance.
- **Potassium Supplementation**: Add KCl (20–40 mEq/L) to IV fluids once adequate urine output is established to correct hypokalemia.

##### Step 2: Gastric Decompression & Lavage

- Insert a wide-bore orogastric / nasogastric tube to decompress the stomach.
- Perform daily saline gastric lavage until the stomach is completely emptied and normal muscular tone returns.

##### Step 3: Medical Therapy

- IV Proton Pump Inhibitors (Pantoprazole 40 mg IV twice daily) to heal active inflammation and reduce surrounding inflammatory edema.

##### Step 4: Definitive Interventions

- **Endoscopic Treatment**: **Through-the-scope (TTS) balloon dilatation** of benign pyloric strictures.
- **Surgical Options**:
    - _Benign Cicatrized Pyloric Stenosis_: **Gastroenterostomy** (loop gastrojejunostomy) or Truncal Vagotomy + Gastroenterostomy.
    - _Malignant GOO_: Distal gastrectomy with Roux-en-Y reconstruction, or endoscopic self-expanding metal stent (SEMS) placement for unresectable disease.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In a patient presenting with Gastric Outlet Obstruction, never administer Ringer's Lactate for initial fluid resuscitation. **0.9% Normal Saline with KCl supplementation is the fluid of choice** because Normal Saline supplies essential chloride ions (Cl⁻) needed by the kidney to retain H⁺ and correct hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Mistaking the urine pH in severe Gastric Outlet Obstruction for alkaline urine due to systemic metabolic alkalosis.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Severe GOO causes profound dehydration and hypokalemia, forcing the distal renal tubule to reabsorb Na⁺ in exchange for H⁺ ions. This produces **paradoxical aciduria (acidic urine despite systemic metabolic alkalosis)**.

### Dosage Rule (Drugs in Peptic Ulcer Disease)

#### 1. Omeprazole / Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Omeprazole / Pantoprazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase enzyme (proton pump) in parietal cells, suppressing final step of gastric acid secretion.
- **Dose**: Omeprazole 20–40 mg PO / Pantoprazole 40 mg IV/PO
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet / Intravenous infusion
- **Frequency**: Once or twice daily
- **Features**: Mainstay of acid suppression; heals >90% of ulcers in 4–8 weeks.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, long-term risk of hypomagnesemia and vitamin B₁₂ deficiency.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately on diagnosis; continue for 4–8 weeks (or lifelong if ongoing NSAID use).

#### 2. Amoxicillin

- **Name**: Amoxicillin Trihydrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Beta-lactam antibiotic; inhibits bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis in _H. pylori_.
- **Dose**: 1000 mg (1 g)
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule
- **Frequency**: Twice daily for 14 days
- **Side Effects**: Diarrhea, skin rash, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Component of 14-day triple eradication therapy.

#### 3. Clarithromycin

- **Name**: Clarithromycin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Macrolide antibiotic; binds 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis.
- **Dose**: 500 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Twice daily for 14 days
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, abdominal cramps, QT prolongation.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Component of 14-day triple eradication therapy.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Breach in digestive mucosa extending through muscularis mucosae in acid-exposed regions.
- **Primary Etiologies**: _H. pylori_ infection (90% DU, 60% GU) and NSAID consumption.
- **Diagnostic Gold Standard**: Upper GI endoscopy + minimum 6–10 biopsies for all gastric ulcers to exclude malignancy.
- **First-Line Medical Therapy**: 14-day _H. pylori_ triple eradication therapy (PPI + Amoxicillin 1 g + Clarithromycin 500 mg BD).
- **Complication Emergency**: Anterior DU perforates into peritoneal cavity (repaired via Graham's omental patch); Posterior DU bleeds from gastroduodenal artery.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction Metabolic Triad**: Hypochloraemic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria, treated with IV 0.9% Normal Saline + KCl.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Peptic ulcer disease is a chronic mucosal ulcerative breach extending beyond the muscularis mucosae caused predominantly by Helicobacter pylori infection or NSAID-mediated disruption of mucosal defense mechanisms, evaluated by upper GI endoscopy with mandatory biopsy of gastric ulcers, managed primarily by H. pylori triple eradication therapy, and complicated acutely by hemorrhage, omental-patch repaired perforation, or cicatrized gastric outlet obstruction._
