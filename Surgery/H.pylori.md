# Helicobacter pylori

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

_**Helicobacter pylori**_ is a microaerophilic, spiral-shaped, Gram-negative, flagellated bacillus that selectively colonizes the human gastric mucosa (predominantly the antrum). It produces a potent **urease enzyme** and specific cytotoxins (**CagA**, **VacA**) that disrupt the protective gastric mucus barrier, inducing an inflammatory cascade that serves as the primary cause of chronic Type B gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, gastric adenocarcinoma, and primary gastric MALToma (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 156_).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Epidemiology & Transmission**:
    - Infection is typically acquired during **early childhood** via oral-oral or fecal-oral transmission.
    - Prevalence is inversely related to socioeconomic status; it is significantly higher in resource-poor nations and overcrowded living conditions (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1156_).
- **Carcinogenic Designation**: Classified by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a **Class 1 Human Carcinogen**(_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Environmental & Lifestyle Interactions**: Combined infection with cigarette smoking or heavy alcohol intake synergistically increases the risk of peptic ulceration and gastric malignancy (_Bailey & Love p. 1157_).

---

### Classification & Virulence Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

The pathogenicity of _H. pylori_ relies on specific structural and enzymatic virulence factors (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 156_):

|Virulence Factor|Biological Function|Clinical / Pathological Significance|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Urease Enzyme**[⭐ High-Yield]|Hydrolyzes gastric urea into **ammonia (NH₃)** and carbon dioxide (CO₂)|Neutralizes acidic gastric juice in the immediate microenvironment, allowing bacterial survival at pH <2.0. Basis for CLO and Urea Breath Tests (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).|
|**Flagella**|4 to 6 polar sheath-covered flagella providing corkscrew motility|Enables rapid penetration through the thick, viscous gastric mucus layer to adhere to mucosal epithelial cells.|
|**CagA (Cytotoxin-Associated Gene A)**|Translocated into host gastric epithelial cells via a Type IV secretion system|Induces cytoskeletal rearrangement, chronic epithelial inflammation, cell proliferation, and oncogenesis (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 156_).|
|**VacA (Vacuolating Cytotoxin A)**|Exotoxin producing large cytoplasmic vacuoles in epithelial cells|Causes gastric epithelial apoptosis, cell junction breakdown, and tissue ulceration (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 156_).|

- **_H. pylori_ Urease Cascade**: _H. pylori_ colonizes the antral mucosa and secretes urease, which hydrolyzes urea into ammonia. Ammonia neutralizes gastric acid locally, removing normal negative feedback on antral G-cells. This produces **inappropriate hypergastrinemia**, which drives parietal cell hyperplasia and acid hypersecretion (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Duodenal Ulcerogenesis**: Excess acid entering the first part of the duodenum (D₁) induces **duodenal gastric metaplasia**. _H. pylori_ colonizes this metaplastic gastric tissue in D₁, causing active duodenitis and predisposing to **duodenal ulceration** (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **MALToma Induction**: Chronic inflammation stimulates T-cells, which recruit B-cells to form lymphoid follicles in the normally lymphoid-free gastric mucosa, giving rise to low-grade **MALT lymphoma** (_Bailey & Love p. 1179; Marrow p. 168_).
- **Inverse GERD Relationship**: _H. pylori_-induced pangastritis and parietal cell atrophy reduce overall gastric acid output, exerting a **slightly protective effect against GERD and esophageal adenocarcinoma** (_Marrow p. 156_).

### Clinical Features & Associated Diseases [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Symptom Spectrum

- Most individuals remain **asymptomatic chronic carriers**.
- **Dyspepsia**: Epigastric burning, postprandial fullness, early satiety, nausea, and bloating.
- **Peptic Ulcer Symptoms**: Epigastric pain occurring 2–3 hours post-meal or at night ("hunger pain" in DU, relieved by food/antacids; early postprandial pain in GU, aggravated by food) (_Bailey & Love p. 1157, 1158_).

#### 2. Associated Disease Entities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Duodenal Ulcer Disease**: Strongly associated (**90%–95%** of all duodenal ulcers) (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 153_).
- **Gastric Ulcer Disease**: Associated in **60%–65%** of cases (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 153_).
- **Chronic Type B Gastritis**: Predominantly antral-predominant gastritis or pangastritis (_Bailey & Love p. 1156_).
- **Gastric Adenocarcinoma**: Class 1 carcinogen linked to intestinal-type gastric carcinoma (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1170_).
- **Gastric MALToma**: Low-grade B-cell lymphoma; complete regression occurs in up to 80% of early-stage MALTomas following _H. pylori_ eradication therapy alone (_Bailey & Love p. 1179; Marrow p. 168_).

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

Diagnostic tests are divided into **non-invasive** (preferred in primary care/community) and **invasive** (endoscopy-based) modalities (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).

```
                      DIAGNOSTIC WORK-UP FOR H. PYLORI
                                    |
        +---------------------------+---------------------------+
        |                                                       |
 Non-Invasive Tests                                  Invasive (Endoscopy-Based)
 - 13C/14C Urea Breath Test (Gold Standard)          - Rapid Urease Test (CLO Test)
 - Stool Antigen Assay                              - Histopathology (Giemsa stain)
 - Serology (IgG antibodies)                        - Microbiological Culture
```

#### 1. Non-Invasive Diagnostic Modalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Urea Breath Test (¹³C or ¹⁴C UBT) [Gold Standard Non-Invasive Test]**:
    - _Principle_: Patient ingests radiolabeled urea (¹³C non-radioactive or ¹⁴C radioactive). Bacterial urease hydrolyzes urea, releasing labeled CO₂ exhaled in breath.
    - _Clinical Utility_: High sensitivity (>95%) and specificity (>95%). **Investigation of choice to confirm eradication** post-treatment (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Stool Antigen Test**:
    - Detects _H. pylori_ bacterial antigens in stool via enzyme immunoassay. Highly accurate for both primary diagnosis and post-treatment monitoring (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Serology (IgG Antibodies)**:
    - Detects circulating anti-_H. pylori_ IgG antibodies.
    - _Limitation_: Cannot distinguish active infection from past exposure because antibody titers remain elevated for up to 6 months post-eradication (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1160_).

#### 2. Invasive / Endoscopy-Based Modalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rapid Urease Test (CLO Test / Campylobacter-Like Organism Test)**:
    - _Principle_: Gastric antral mucosal biopsy is placed in a gel medium containing urea and a pH indicator (phenol red). Bacterial urease produces ammonia, elevating pH and causing a rapid color change from yellow to magenta/pink (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Histopathological Examination**:
    - Gastric mucosal biopsy stained with **Modified Giemsa stain** or **Warthin-Starry silver stain** demonstrates spiral organisms colonizing the gastric pits (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1156_).
- **Microbiological Culture**:
    - Cultured on selective media under microaerophilic conditions. Indicated primarily for evaluating antibiotic resistance in refractory cases failing multiple eradication attempts (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1160_).

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Medical eradication therapy forms the definitive treatment for _H. pylori_ infection (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1160_).

#### 1. Indications for Eradication Therapy [⭐ High-Yield]

- Proven **Duodenal or Gastric Ulcer Disease** (active or past history) (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- Low-grade **Gastric MALT Lymphoma** (_Bailey & Love p. 1179_).
- Post-resection for early gastric cancer.
- Uninvestigated or functional dyspepsia (test-and-treat strategy).
- Severe chronic atrophic gastritis or intestinal metaplasia.

#### 2. First-Line Eradication Regimen (14-Day Triple Therapy) [⭐ High-Yield]

Administered for **14 days** to achieve eradication rates of ~90% (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_):

- **Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)**: Omeprazole 20 mg PO BD (or Pantoprazole 40 mg PO BD).
- **Amoxicillin**: 1000 mg (1 g) PO BD.
- **Clarithromycin**: 500 mg PO BD.
- _(Note: In penicillin-allergic patients, Amoxicillin is replaced by Metronidazole 400 mg PO BD)_ (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).

#### 3. Second-Line Eradication Regimen (Bismuth-Based Quadruple Therapy)

Indicated for treatment failure or in areas with high clarithromycin resistance (>15%):

- **PPI**: Standard dose PO BD.
- **Bismuth Subsalicylate**: 525 mg PO QID.
- **Metronidazole**: 400 mg PO TDS.
- **Tetracycline**: 500 mg PO QID for 14 days.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Peptic Ulcer Complications**: Hemorrhage (erosion into gastroduodenal artery), Perforation (anterior D₁ breach), and Gastric Outlet Obstruction (cicatrized pyloric stenosis) (_Bailey & Love p. 1157, 1164, 1168_).
- **Gastric MALT Lymphoma**: Low-grade B-cell mucosal lymphoma (_Bailey & Love p. 1179_).
- **Gastric Adenocarcinoma**: Intestinal-type gastric carcinoma secondary to chronic atrophic pangastritis and dysplasia (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1170_).

---

### Clinical Pearl

In early-stage low-grade **Gastric MALToma** (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue Lymphoma), systemic multi-agent chemotherapy or surgical resection is **not** the first-line treatment. ** Eradication of _Helicobacter pylori_ alone leads to complete tumor regression and cure in up to 80% of patients** (_Bailey & Love p. 1179; Marrow p. 168_).

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Using Urea Breath Test (UBT) or Stool Antigen Test immediately after a patient completes a course of PPIs or antibiotics.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: PPIs and antibiotics suppress bacterial density, causing **false-negative results** on UBT and stool antigen tests. **All PPIs must be discontinued for at least 2 weeks, and antibiotics/bismuth stopped for at least 4 weeks, before performing post-treatment verification testing** (_Bailey & Love p. 1160_).

### Dosage Rule (Drugs in _H. pylori_ Eradication)

#### 1. Omeprazole / Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Omeprazole / Pantoprazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase enzyme (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells, suppressing gastric acid secretion and raising intragastric pH to optimize antibiotic activity (_Bailey & Love p. 1155, 1160_).
- **Dose**: Omeprazole 20 mg / Pantoprazole 40 mg.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule/tablet.
- **Frequency**: **Twice daily (BD)**.
- **Features**: Essential component of all _H. pylori_ eradication protocols.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, hypomagnesemia with long-term use.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start on Day 1 of eradication therapy; continue for 14 days (extend PPI for 4–8 weeks if active ulcer is present).

#### 2. Amoxicillin

- **Name**: Amoxicillin Trihydrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Beta-lactam antibiotic; binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), inhibiting cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis in _H. pylori_ (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Dose**: **1000 mg (1 g)**.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule/tablet.
- **Frequency**: **Twice daily (BD)**.
- **Features**: Low resistance rates (<2%); primary antibiotic component in standard triple therapy.
- **Side Effects**: Diarrhea, rash, anaphylaxis in penicillin-allergic patients.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Take for **14 days**.

#### 3. Clarithromycin

- **Name**: Clarithromycin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Macrolide antibiotic; binds 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Dose**: **500 mg**.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet.
- **Frequency**: **Twice daily (BD)**.
- **Features**: Potent bactericidal activity against _H. pylori_; high resistance limits efficacy in some regions.
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, nausea, abdominal pain, QT prolongation.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Take for **14 days**.

#### 4. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Reduced to toxic nitro-radicals that damage bacterial DNA strands (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Dose**: **400 mg**.
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet.
- **Frequency**: **Twice daily (BD)** (or 400 mg TDS in quadruple therapy).
- **Features**: Used in penicillin-allergic patients or as part of quadruple therapy.
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, nausea, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Take for **14 days**.

### Quick Revision

- **Pathogen**: Gram-negative, spiral, flagellated, microaerophilic bacillus; WHO Class 1 carcinogen (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Key Virulence Factors**: Urease (acid neutralization), CagA (oncogenesis), VacA (epithelial vacuolation) (_Bailey & Love p. 1155; Marrow p. 156_).
- **Major Disease Associations**: Duodenal ulcers (90%–95%), Gastric ulcers (60%–65%), Chronic Type B Gastritis, Gastric Cancer, MALToma (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **Gold Standard Non-Invasive Test**: ¹³C/¹⁴C Urea Breath Test (for primary diagnosis and confirming eradication) (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
- **First-Line Treatment**: 14-day Triple Therapy (PPI BD + Amoxicillin 1 g BD + Clarithromycin 500 mg BD) (_Bailey & Love p. 1155_).
