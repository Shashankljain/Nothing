# Sister Mary Joseph's Nodule

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Sister Mary Joseph's nodule** refers to a metastatic malignant nodule at the umbilicus resulting from transcoelomic, vascular, or direct lymphatic tracking of an advanced intra-abdominal or pelvic malignancy.

---

### Key Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primary Malignancy Sources**: Most commonly secondary to **gastric adenocarcinoma** (Stomach > Ovarian > Colorectal > Pancreatic).
- **Physical Presentation**: Appears as a firm, indurated, painless or tender, red-to-purple, nodular periumbilical mass, occasionally associated with ulceration, serous/bloody discharge, or surrounding skin induration.
- **Anatomical Routes of Dissemination**: Tumor cells reach the umbilicus via transcoelomic peritoneal seeding, vascular channels, or remnant embryonic structures (such as the falciform ligament, round ligament of the liver, remnant urachus, or vitelline duct).
- **Prognostic Significance**: Signifies **advanced, incurable Stage IV metastatic disease** with a grave prognosis; management is primarily palliative.
- **Historical Eponym**: First noted by Sister Mary Joseph (Julia Dempsey), nursing superintendent at St. Mary's Hospital (Mayo Clinic), and later coined by Hamilton Bailey in 1949.

---

### Clinical Pearl

The presence of Sister Mary Joseph's nodule is an unequivocal physical sign of widespread intra-abdominal dissemination. A fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) or core biopsy of the umbilical mass can establish a definitive tissue diagnosis and cell type, avoiding an unnecessary invasive diagnostic laparotomy.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Mistaking a Sister Mary Joseph's nodule for a primary umbilical lesion (such as an strangulated umbilical hernia, granuloma, or primary squamous cell carcinoma).
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Primary umbilical lesions are localized benign conditions or rare primary skin cancers, whereas **Sister Mary Joseph's nodule is an indurated metastatic deposit stemming from an advanced internal abdominopelvic malignancy (most commonly gastric cancer)**.

- **S** – **S**tomach adenocarcinoma is the most common primary source (Stomach > Ovarian).
- **I** – **I**ndurated, firm, red/purple metastatic periumbilical nodule.
- **S** – **S**tage IV advanced malignancy with grave, incurable prognosis.
- **T** – **T**ranscoelomic and falciform ligament routes of dissemination.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Metastatic malignant mass at the umbilicus from advanced intra-abdominal cancer.
- **Most Common Primary**: Gastric adenocarcinoma (Stomach > Ovarian).
- **Mechanism**: Spreads via peritoneal seeding, falciform/round ligament, or lymphatics.
- **Clinical Significance**: Indicates Stage IV advanced/terminal malignancy; warrants palliative care.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Sister Mary Joseph's nodule is a metastatic periumbilical skin lesion arising from advanced intra-abdominal malignancy—most commonly gastric adenocarcinoma—signifying Stage IV incurable disease and carrying a grave prognosis._
