# Breast Abscess and Bacterial Mastitis

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Bacterial mastitis** is an acute inflammation of the breast parenchyma resulting from bacterial invasion (most commonly during lactation), which, if untreated, progresses to tissue necrosis, fluid collection, and localized suppuration, forming a **breast abscess** (a localized collection of pus encased within an inflammatory wall in the breast tissue).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Bacterial mastitis and breast abscesses are broadly categorized into **lactational (puerperal)** and **non-lactational** types:

#### 1. Lactational (Puerperal) Mastitis & Abscess (Most Common: 3%–20%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Causative Organism**: **Staphylococcus aureus** is the most common pathogen. Hospital-acquired strains or recalcitrant cases are increasingly caused by Methicillin-Resistant _Staphylococcus aureus_ (MRSA).
- **Source of Infection**: Colonized **oropharynx of the infant**.
- **Portal of Entry**: **Cracked or fissured nipples** or skin abrasions around the nipple-areola complex.
- **Pathogenetic Factor**: Milk stasis due to incomplete emptying, blocked lactiferous ducts, or uncoordinated suckling.
- **Bimodal Timing Peaks**:
    1. _First Month Postpartum_: Inexperienced primiparous mothers with poor breastfeeding techniques.
    2. _At Weaning_: Sudden milk engorgement and trauma to the nipple from the infant's teeth.

#### 2. Non-Lactational Mastitis & Abscess

- **Periductal Mastitis / Subareolar Abscess**: Chronic inflammation around major subareolar milk ducts. Strongly associated with **cigarette smoking** and squamous metaplasia of lactiferous ducts. Organisms involved include _Staphylococcus_, _Bacteroides_, anaerobes, and _Enterococcus_.
- **Idiopathic Granulomatous Mastitis (IGM)**: Benign non-caseating granulomatous inflammation in young parous women, often associated with _Corynebacterium kroppenstedtii_.
- **Tubercular Mastitis**: Chronic secondary infection via retrograde axillary lymph node spread or hematogenous seeding, producing "cold" tuberculous abscesses and sinuses.

---

### Classification

1. **By Etiological Link**:
    - _Lactational (Puerperal) Breast Abscess_ (most common).
    - _Non-Lactational Breast Abscess_ (periductal, granulomatous, tuberculous).
2. **By Anatomical Location**:
    - _Premammary (Subcutaneous) Abscess_: Beneath the skin, superficial to breast tissue.
    - _Intramammary Abscess_ (most common): Within the glandular breast lobules.
    - _Retromammary Abscess_: Between the posterior pectoral fascia and pectoralis major muscle.
    - _Subareolar Abscess_: Directly beneath the nipple-areola complex (associated with periductal mastitis).

- **Milk Clotting & Multiplication**: Bacteria enter via a nipple crack and multiply within stagnant milk inside the ductal ampulla, inducing milk coagulation.
- **Cellulitic Phase**: Transmural invasion produces localized parenchymal edema, severe tissue hyperemia, and acute inflammatory leukocytic infiltration.
- **Suppurative Phase**: Unchecked neutrophil degranulation and bacterial exotoxins cause liquefactive tissue necrosis, forming hyperosmolar pus. The high osmotic pressure draws in extracellular fluid, increasing tissue tension and causing severe throbbing pain.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Pain**: Intense, localized, constant throbbing pain in the affected breast segment.
- **Swelling & Lump**: Rapidly expanding, painful, heavy mass in the breast.
- **Systemic Toxicity**: High-grade fever with chills/rigors, malaise, fatigue, and headache.
- **Difficulty in Breastfeeding**: Infant refuses to feed due to severe maternal pain or altered milk taste.

#### Signs

- **Signs of Inflammation**: Localized cutaneous erythema, marked warmth, edema, and exquisite tenderness over the affected quadrant.
- **Fluctuation (Late Sign) [⭐ High-Yield / Exam Trap]**: A fluctuant fluid wave is a **late sign** in breast, parotid, and deep tissue abscesses due to the thick overlying breast tissue and edema.
- **Nipple Changes**: Cracked, fissured, or excoriated nipple; subareolar abscesses cause a transverse slit-like ("fish mouth") nipple retraction.
- **Axillary Lymphadenopathy**: Tender, enlarged ipsilateral Level I and Level II axillary lymph nodes.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Targeted Imaging

- **Targeted Breast Ultrasound (USG) [Investigation of Choice - IOC] [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - _Cellulitic Stage_: Increased parenchymal echogenicity with subcutaneous edema.
    - _Abscess Stage_: Well-demarcated hypoechoic or anechoic collection containing floating internal debris that shifts with posture, showing posterior acoustic enhancement.
    - _Utility_: Confirms liquefaction, measures fluid volume, identifies loculations, and guides therapeutic needle/catheter placement.

#### 2. Microbiological Evaluation

- **Pus Aspiration Culture & Sensitivity**: Pus aspirated under sterile conditions is submitted for Gram stain and aerobic/anaerobic bacterial culture to guide targeted antibiotic modification.
- **GeneXpert MTB/RIF Test**: Mandatory in chronic, recurrent, or non-lactational abscesses to exclude _Mycobacterium tuberculosis_.

#### 1. Management of Cellulitic Phase (Pre-Suppurative) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Empirical Antibiotic Therapy**: Anti-staphylococcal antibiotics (Oral Cloxacillin 500 mg 6-hourly or Flucloxacillin 500 mg 6-hourly for 10–14 days).
- **Continued Breastfeeding / Milk Expression**: Breastfeeding from **both breasts** must be continued 2-hourly, followed by complete emptying using a mechanical breast pump. Milk is entirely safe for the infant.
- **Symptomatic Relief**: Cold compress applications, breast support garments, and NSAID analgesics.

#### 2. Management of Abscess Phase (Suppurative) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### Method A: Ultrasound-Guided Needle Aspiration (First-Line / Preferred) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Standard of Care**: For abscesses **≤3 cm in diameter or <30 mL in volume**.
- **Technique**: Serial USG-guided aspirations using an 18G needle under local anesthesia every 48 hours until complete resolution.
- **Advantages**: Superior cosmetic result, no disruption of lactiferous ducts, done as a day-care procedure, and allows uninterrupted breastfeeding.

##### Method B: Ultrasound-Guided Percutaneous Suction Catheter / Pigtail Drainage

- Indicated for large abscesses (**>3 cm in diameter or >30 mL in volume**).
- A vacuum suction catheter is inserted under USG guidance and irrigated daily with cold normal saline until resolution.

##### Method C: Surgical Incision and Drainage (I&D) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications**: Recurrent abscesses, multiloculated cavities with thick debris, skin necrosis/overlying ischemia, or failed percutaneous aspiration.
- **Incision Choice**:
    - _Intramammary Abscess_: **Radial incision** over the point of maximum fluctuation (parallel to lactiferous ducts to avoid ductal transection).
    - _Subareolar / Periareolar Abscess_: **Circumareolar / Circumferential incision** along the lower margin of the areola.
    - _Inframammary / Deep Abscess_: **Submammary crease incision (Gaillard Thomas incision)**.
- **Operative Steps (Hilton's Method)**:
    1. Incise skin and subcutaneous tissue over the abscess.
    2. Insert a blunt sinus forceps or finger into the cavity to **break all internal loculi**.
    3. Evacuate pus and take a specimen for culture and sensitivity.
    4. Irrigate cavity with Normal Saline and pack loosely with ribbon gauze or place a corrugated rubber drain.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mammary / Milk Duct Fistula**: Chronic, non-healing fistulous tract between lactiferous duct epithelium and skin, often resulting from aggressive incision near the areola.
- **Chronicity & Antibioma**: Partial sterilization of pus due to empirical antibiotics without complete drainage, forming a firm, painless indurated mass (antibioma).
- **Extensive Tissue Necrosis & Cosmetic Deformity**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Anti-Staphylococcal Antibiotics)

#### 1. Cloxacillin / Flucloxacillin

- **Name**: Cloxacillin Sodium / Flucloxacillin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Penicillinase-resistant beta-lactam antibiotic; binds penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), inhibiting bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis.
- **Dose**: 500 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule (or IV 1–2 g 6-hourly in severe systemic toxicity)
- **Frequency**: Every 6 hours (4 times daily) on an empty stomach (1 hour before meals)
- **Features**: Drug of choice for acute lactational mastitis caused by penicillinase-producing _S. aureus_.
- **Side Effects**: Nausea, diarrhea, cholestatic jaundice, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately upon clinical diagnosis of cellulitis/abscess; continue for **10 to 14 days**.

#### 2. Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Co-amoxiclav)

- **Name**: Amoxicillin + Clavulanic Acid
- **Mechanism of Action**: Beta-lactam combined with a suicide beta-lactamase inhibitor; broad-spectrum activity against aerobic and anaerobic pathogens.
- **Dose**: 625 mg (500 mg Amoxicillin + 125 mg Clavulanate)
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly or 12-hourly (1000 mg formulation) for 10–14 days.

### Clinical Pearl

Fluctuation is a **late and unreliable physical sign** in a breast abscess because thick overlying breast parenchyma and tense tissue edema obscure fluid wave perception. **Targeted Breast Ultrasound is the mandatory Investigation of Choice** to detect early liquefaction necrosis and guide needle aspiration before skin necrosis occurs.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Advising a lactating mother with a acute breast abscess to abruptly stop breastfeeding from the affected breast.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Stopping lactation causes severe milk stasis, engorgement, and rapid expansion of the abscess. **Breastfeeding from both breasts must be CONTINUED 2-hourly along with milk expression**, as ingested breast milk poses no harm to the infant and emptying the gland is essential for therapeutic resolution.

- **Type**: Topographical surgical schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Anterior view of female breast showing nipple-areola complex and quadrants.

    1. _Radial Incisions_ — oriented radiating outward from the areolar border over upper/outer quadrants (parallel to milk ducts).

    3. _Inframammary (Gaillard Thomas) Incision_ — curvilinear incision in lower submammary fold for deep/retromammary abscesses.
- **Annotation for Extra Marks**: _"Radial incisions prevent transection of lactiferous ducts, whereas circumareolar incisions are reserved for subareolar pathology."_

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: M-A-S-T-I-T-I-S (Lactational Abscess Features)

- **M** – **M**ilk stasis is the primary triggering event.
- **A** – _**A**ureus_ (_Staphylococcus aureus_) is the primary causative pathogen.
- **S** – **S**uction catheter / Needle aspiration is first-line management over open surgery.
- **T** – **T**hrobbing intense pain and systemic pyrexia.
- **I** – **I**nvestigation of choice is Ultrasound (USG) Breast.
- **T** – **T**wo-hourly breastfeeding/expression must be continued.
- **I** – **I**ncision radial (Hilton's method) if aspiration fails or skin is necrotic.
- **S** – **S**ource of infection is the infant's oropharynx via cracked nipples.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Primary Pathogen**: _Staphylococcus aureus_ entering via cracked nipples during lactation.
- **Diagnostic Gold Standard**: Ultrasound (USG) Breast; identifies fluid collection and guides drainage.
- **Fluctuation Sign**: Fluctuance is a **late sign** in breast abscesses.
- **First-Line Management**: USG-guided needle aspiration (for ≤3 cm / <30 mL) + Anti-staphylococcal antibiotics (Cloxacillin/Flucloxacillin) + Continued breastfeeding.
- **Surgical Drainage**: Reserved for failed aspiration/recurrence; done via **radial incision using Hilton's method**.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A breast abscess is a localized suppurative collection within the breast parenchyma caused predominantly by Staphylococcus aureus entry through a cracked nipple during lactation, diagnosed by targeted ultrasound, and managed primarily by ultrasound-guided needle aspiration and anti-staphylococcal antibiotics while continuing active breastfeeding._
