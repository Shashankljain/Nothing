# Ochsner-Sherren Regime

**Mark-Weightage: 5 Marks**

## 1. Definition & Rationale [⭐ High-Yield]

- The **Ochsner-Sherren regime** is a structured, conservative, non-operative protocol used for managing a localized **appendicular mass (appendiceal phlegmon)**.
- Named after **Albert John Ochsner** and **James Sherren**.
- **Surgical Rationale**:
    - Based on the principle that the acute inflammatory process is already safely localized by the **greater omentum** and adjacent bowel loops.
    - Immediate surgery on an acutely inflamed mass is technically difficult and dangerous.
    - Inadvertent dissection risks failing to identify the appendix, causing extensive damage to matted bowel, or producing an enterocutaneous / **faecal fistula**.

## 2. Indications & Prerequisites

- **Indications**:
    - Hemodynamically stable patients presenting with a palpable, localized **appendicular mass** without generalized peritonitis.
- **Prerequisites**:
    - Close inpatient monitoring capabilities in a surgical ward.
    - Absence of signs of generalized peritonitis, systemic sepsis, or intestinal obstruction.

## 3. Pathophysiology [⭐ High-Yield]

### Mechanism Sequence

- **Appendiceal Luminal Obstruction**: Triggered by a **faecolith** or **lymphoid hyperplasia**, causing intraluminal pressure buildup and mucosal ischemia.
- **Transmural Inflammation & Microperforation**: Bacterial invasion leads to transmural inflammation or localized perforation.
- **Host Containment**: The **greater omentum** ("abdominal policeman") and coils of terminal ileum migrate to wrap around the appendix, walling off contamination into a phlegmonous mass.
- **Conservative Resolution**: Under bowel rest and broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, inflammation regresses in **~90%** of cases.

## 4. Diagnostic Investigations

- **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) Scan**: Standard imaging modality; confirms the presence of a phlegmon, rules out free perforation, and identifies any drainable fluid collection / abscess.
- **Full Blood Count (FBC)**: Evaluates the degree of **leukocytosis** and neutrophilic shift.
- **C-Reactive Protein (CRP)**: Monitored serially as a non-invasive marker of inflammatory resolution.

## 5. Detailed Protocol of Ochsner-Sherren Regime [⭐ High-Yield]

### Non-Pharmacological Protocol

- **Strict NPO (Nil Per Os)** status initially to allow bowel rest.
- **Intravenous Crystalloid Resuscitation**: Maintains adequate hydration and urine output.
- **Skin Pencil Margin Marking**: The physical boundaries of the mass are outlined on the abdominal wall using a **skin pencil** upon admission to detect any expansion.
- **4-Hourly Vital Signs Charting**: Pulse rate and body temperature are recorded every **4 hours**.
- **Strict Fluid Balance Charting**: Accurate recording of intake and output.
- **Serial Abdominal Examination**: Performed regularly by the same surgical team to assess local tenderness and guarding.

### Pharmacological Management & Dosage Rule

- Therapeutic IV antibiotics targeting Gram-negative bacilli (_E. coli_) and anaerobes (_Bacteroides fragilis_).

|Drug|Dose|Route|Frequency|Duration|Key Caution|

|**Ceftriaxone**|**1–2 g**|IV|OD (q24h)|**5–7 days**|Monitor LFTs; check cephalosporin allergy|

|**Paracetamol**|**1 g**|IV|QID (q6h)|As needed|Maximum **4 g/day**; caution in hepatic insufficiency|

### Radiological Interventions

- If CT shows an associated periappendiceal abscess collection, **CT- or USG-guided percutaneous drainage** is performed as a bridge to resolution.

## 6. Criteria for Stopping Conservative Treatment (Emergency Surgery) [⭐ High-Yield]

### Summary Box 76.6 Criteria

- Clinical deterioration or failure to improve within **24–48 hours** mandates immediate abandonment of conservative treatment and performance of an emergency **laparotomy**.
- **The Three Mandatory Failure Criteria**:
    1. **A rising pulse rate**.
    2. **Increasing or spreading abdominal pain** (signifying peritonitis).
    3. **Increasing size of the mass** (extending beyond skin pencil marks).

## 7. Post-Resolution Protocol & Considerations in Patients >40 Years [⭐ High-Yield]

- Clinical improvement occurs within **24–48 hours** in approximately **90%** of cases.
- **Failure to Resolve**: Should raise immediate suspicion of **Carcinoma of the Caecum** or **Crohn's disease**.
- **Mandatory Workup for Patients >40 Years**:
    - Recent randomized trials demonstrated an unexpected **29%** incidence of underlying appendiceal neoplasms in patients aged **>40 years** presenting with periappendicular abscess/mass.
    - **Low-grade Appendiceal Mucinous Neoplasms (LAMNs)** account for **42%** of these unexpected tumors.
    - **Mandatory Follow-up**: Patients aged **>40 years** MUST undergo **Full Colonoscopy** and repeat **CECT / MRI scan** at **6–8 weeks** post-resolution to exclude underlying malignancy before deciding on interval appendicectomy.

## 8. Complications

- **Appendicular Abscess Formation**: Suppurative liquefaction requiring percutaneous drainage.
- **Generalized Peritonitis**: Secondary to uncontained rupture of the phlegmon.
- **Faecal Fistula**: Resulting from inadvertent early surgery or erosion of the appendiceal base.
- **Adhesive Small Bowel Obstruction**: Caused by persistent matted inflammatory bowel loops.

## 9. Prognosis

- High success rate: **~90%** of appendix masses resolve completely under the Ochsner-Sherren regime.

## 10. Mnemonic & Memory Palace

### Mnemonic for Stopping Criteria (P-A-M)

- **P**: **P**ulse rate rising (tachycardia)
- **A**: **A**bdominal pain increasing or spreading
- **M**: **M**ass increasing in size

3. **Bed 3 (Alarm Bell - P-A-M)**: An alarm rings displaying a **rising pulse rate**, expanding blue pencil lines, and spreading pain—triggering immediate transfer to the operating theater.
4. **Outpatient Clinic (6 Weeks Later)**: A **45-year-old patient** undergoes **colonoscopy** and **CECT scan** to check for **LAMN**.

### Clinical Pearl

- **The Skin Pencil Border Charting**: Outlining the exact physical margins of an appendicular mass with a **skin pencil** upon admission is the most effective bedside clinical tool for detecting early mass expansion and treatment failure.

### Exam Trap

- **The "29% Neoplasm Rule" in Patients >40 Years**:
    - **The Mix-up**: Assuming a resolved appendicular mass in a middle-aged or elderly patient is purely benign.
    - **Distinguishing Fact**: **29%** of patients aged **>40 years** presenting with periappendicular abscess/mass harbor an underlying appendiceal neoplasm (**LAMN** in **42%** of tumor cases). Never discharge these patients without scheduling a mandatory **colonoscopy** and **repeat CECT/MRI scan** at **6–8 weeks**.

### Quick Revision

- The **Ochsner-Sherren regime** is conservative management for a localized appendicular mass, preventing inadvertent surgical bowel injury or faecal fistula.
- Protocol includes strict NPO, IV fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum IV **ceftriaxone + metronidazole**, 4-hourly vitals, and **skin pencil mass marking**.
- Absolute criteria for stopping conservative treatment (**Summary Box 76.6**) are a **rising pulse rate**, **spreading abdominal pain**, and **increasing mass size**.
- Resolves successfully in **~90%** of cases within **24–48 hours**.
- Patients aged **>40 years** require mandatory **colonoscopy** and **repeat CECT/MRI** at **6–8 weeks** to exclude underlying **LAMN** or caecal carcinoma (**29% tumor incidence**).
