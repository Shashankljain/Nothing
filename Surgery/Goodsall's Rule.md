# Goodsall's Rule

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Goodsall's rule** is an empirical anatomical principle used in proctology to predict the subcutaneous course of a primary fistula-in-ano track and the location of its internal opening along the dentate line based on the anatomical position of its external opening relative to a transverse line bisecting the anus.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Transverse Anal Line**: A hypothetical line drawn horizontally through the center of the anal orifice divides the perianal region into anterior and posterior halves.
- **Anterior External Opening (<3 cm from verge)**: The fistula track runs in a straight, radial path directly into the anal canal at the corresponding clock position.
- **Posterior External Opening**: The fistula track follows a curved path to enter the anal canal in the **posterior midline (6 o'clock position)** at the dentate line.
- **Anterior Exception (>3 cm from verge)**: An anterior external opening situated >3 cm from the anal verge does NOT run radially; it curves posteriorly to open at the posterior midline.
- **Surgical Significance**: Guides the operating surgeon during examination under anesthesia (EUA) and fistulotomy to locate hidden internal cryptoglandular openings without creating false tracks.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Anterior (<3 cm)**: Straight, radial track to the same clock hour.
- **Posterior**: Curved track to the posterior midline (6 o'clock position).
- **Anterior (>3 cm Exception)**: Curves posteriorly to open at the posterior midline.
- **Utility**: Predicts the internal opening at the dentate line during anal fistula surgery.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Goodsall's rule is an anatomical guideline predicting that posterior or distant anterior (>3 cm) perianal fistula openings track along a curved path to the posterior midline dentate line, whereas close anterior (<3 cm) openings run in a straight, radial path._
