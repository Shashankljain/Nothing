# Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST)** is the most common primary mesenchymal neoplasm of the gastrointestinal tract, originating from the **Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICCA)**—the autonomic pacemaker cells located in the muscularis propria layer of the gut wall—and characterized pathologically by gain-of-function mutations in the **c-KIT (CD117)** or **PDGFRA** receptor tyrosine kinase genes.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Anatomical Distribution**:
    
    - **Stomach** (50%–60%; most common site).
    - **Small Intestine** (30%–35%; jejunum > ileum).
    - **Colon & Rectum** (5%).
    - **Esophagus & Omentum / Mesentery** (<5%).
- **Genetic & Molecular Drivers [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    
    - **c-KIT Gene Mutation** (85%–90%): Activating point mutation or deletion in the _c-KIT_ proto-oncogene (most commonly in **Exon 11**, followed by Exon 9, 13, and 17), causing ligand-independent constitutive autophosphorylation of the c-KIT tyrosine kinase receptor.
    - **PDGFRA Gene Mutation** (5%–10%): Activating mutation in Platelet-Derived Growth Factor Receptor Alpha.
    - **Wild-Type / SDH-Deficient GIST** (<5%): Lacks _c-KIT_ or _PDGFRA_ mutations; associated with defects in Succinate Dehydrogenase (SDH) subunit genes.
- **Inherited / Familial Syndromes [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    
    - **Carney's Triad** (Sporadic): Gastric GIST + Extra-adrenal Paraganglioma + Pulmonary Chondroma (SDH-B mutation; primary resistance to imatinib).
    - **Carney-Stratakis Syndrome** (Familial): Gastric GIST + Paraganglioma (germline SDH-B/C/D mutations).
    - **Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1)**: Multiple small-bowel GISTs.

---

### Classification (Fletcher / NIH Risk Stratification) [⭐ High-Yield]

The biological behavior of GIST is unpredictable. Malignant potential and risk of recurrence are classified using **Fletcher's Criteria** based on **Tumor Size** and **Mitotic Count** (per 50 High-Power Fields [HPF]):

|Risk Category|Tumor Size (cm)|Mitotic Count (per 50 HPF)|Malignant Risk Profile|

- **Intramural Growth**: Tumor originates deep within the muscularis propria and expands subepithetically or exophytically. As the fleshy mass expands, central ischemia causes mucosal erosion and ulceration, leading to gastrointestinal bleeding.
- **Patterns of Metastatic Spread**:
    - **Hematogenous Spread** (Most Common): Spreads via the portal circulation preferentially to the **liver**.
    - **Transcoelomic Spread**: Peritoneal seeding, mesenteric nodules, and omental cakes.
    - **Lymphatic Spread**: **Extremely rare (<10%)**; regional lymph node dissection is routinely **NOT required**during surgery.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage** (Most Common Presentation): Manifests as acute hematemesis, melena, or chronic occult fatigue and iron-deficiency anemia due to ulceration over the submucosal tumor peak.
- **Abdominal Pain & Fullness**: Dull ache, early satiety, or feeling of abdominal distension.
- **Palpable Abdominal Mass**: Large exophytic tumors present as smooth, firm, non-tender abdominal masses.
- **Acute Abdomen**: Caused by acute intraperitoneal rupture/perforation of the tumor or luminal intestinal obstruction.

#### Signs

- **General Examination**: Pallor secondary to chronic GI blood loss.
- **Abdominal Examination**: Smooth, firm, mobile mass in the epigastrium or RUQ (for gastric GISTs) that moves with respiration. Absence of significant peripheral lymphadenopathy or ascites in early stages.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Radiological Imaging

- **Contrast-Enhanced Computed Tomography (CECT Abdomen & Pelvis) [Investigation of Choice - IOC]**:
    - Demonstrates a well-circumscribed, hypervascular, smooth or lobulated submucosal/exophytic mass arising from the stomach or bowel wall with central areas of low-attenuation necrosis or hemorrhage.
- **18F-FDG PET-CT Scan**: High sensitivity for evaluating metabolic tumor response within 2–4 weeks of initiating tyrosine kinase inhibitor (imatinib) therapy and for detecting early recurrence.

#### 2. Endoscopy & Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)

- **Upper GI Endoscopy**: Shows a smooth, submucosal bulge with a central umbilical ulceration ("volcano lesion"). Standard mucosal biopsies are often uninformative because the tumor lies beneath normal mucosa.
- **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: Identifies a hypoechoic mass originating from the 4th layer (muscularis propria); allows **EUS-guided Fine-Needle Aspiration (EUS-FNA) or Core Biopsy**.

#### 3. Immunohistochemistry (IHC Biomarker Panel) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **CD117 / c-KIT (+)**: Positive in **>90%–95%** of GISTs (diffuse brown cytoplasmic staining).
- **DOG-1 (Discovered On GIST-1) (+)**: **Most sensitive and specific marker** for GIST, especially in c-KIT-negative cases.
- **CD34 (+)**: Positive in 60%–70% of cases.

#### 1. Surgical Management (Primary Treatment for Localized GIST) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Surgical Gold Standard**: **Wedge Resection** of the stomach or segmental bowel resection with an R0 margin (**2-cm clear surgical margin**).
- **Lymph Node Clearance Rule [⭐ High-Yield]**: Formal lymphadenectomy is **NOT required** because regional lymph node metastasis occurs in <10% of cases.
- **Technique Caution**: Careful handling is mandatory to prevent intraoperative tumor rupture, which causes intraperitoneal seeding and a near 100% recurrence rate.

#### 2. Targeted Medical Therapy (Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors - TKIs) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **First-Line Agent**: **Imatinib Mesylate** (oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting c-KIT and PDGFRA receptors).
- **Indications for Imatinib**:
    1. _Neoadjuvant Therapy_: Administered for 3 to 6 months prior to surgery for large, borderline-resectable tumors to reduce tumor volume and vascularity.
    2. _Adjuvant Therapy_: Given for 3 years (or indefinitely) in high-risk resected GISTs (tumor >5 cm or high mitotic index).
    3. _Unresectable / Metastatic Disease_: Primary treatment for liver or peritoneal metastases.
- **Second-Line Agent for Imatinib Resistance**: **Sunitinib** (multikinase inhibitor).
- **Third-Line Agents**: **Regorafenib** or **Sorafenib**.

---

### Complications

- **Massive Upper GI Hemorrhage**: Acute severe hematemesis or melena following mucosal erosion over the tumor peak.
- **Intraperitoneal Rupture**: Spontaneous or iatrogenic rupture causing acute peritonitis and diffuse peritoneal seeding.
- **Intestinal Obstruction**: Luminal occlusion by large intraluminal polypoid growth.

---

### Prognosis

Determined primarily by tumor size and mitotic count (Fletcher criteria). Complete R0 resection combined with targeted imatinib therapy yields 5-year survival rates exceeding **80%–90%** for localized GISTs.

---

### Dosage Rule (Imatinib Mesylate)

- **Name**: Imatinib Mesylate (Gleevec)
- **Mechanism of Action**: Competitive inhibitor of the ATP-binding site of the c-KIT and PDGFRA tyrosine kinase domains, blocking downstream oncogenic signal transduction and cell proliferation (_Bailey & Love p. 213, 1178_).
- **Dose**: **400 mg** once daily (increased to **800 mg/day** for Exon 9 mutations or disease progression).
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet (taken with food and a large glass of water).
- **Frequency**: Once or twice daily.
- **Features**: First-line targeted therapy achieving an 80% objective response rate in c-KIT-positive GISTs.
- **Side Effects**: Periorbital edema, fluid retention, nausea, diarrhea, muscle cramps, and myelosuppression.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start preoperatively for 3–6 months in bulky tumors, or postoperatively for 3 years in high-risk resected disease; continue indefinitely in unresectable or metastatic disease.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Unlike gastric carcinomas, GISTs spread almost exclusively via the bloodstream to the **liver** and across the **peritoneum**. Lymphatic metastasis occurs in **<10%** of cases; therefore, routine lymphadenectomy is strictly unnecessary during surgical wedge resection, sparing the patient extensive nodal clearance.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing a radical gastrectomy with D2 lymph node clearance for a localized gastric GIST.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Gastric adenocarcinoma spreads early to lymph nodes and requires D2 gastrectomy. GISTs rarely involve lymph nodes (<10%); hence, **complete surgical removal via simple wedge resection with 2-cm margins without lymphadenectomy is the procedure of choice**.

### Quick Revision

- **Origin**: Interstitial Cells of Cajal; most common site is the stomach (50%).
- **Genetics**: Gain-of-function mutation in _c-KIT_ (CD117) or _PDGFRA_.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Upper GI hemorrhage (hematemesis/melena) from mucosal ulceration.
- **IHC Markers**: CD117 (+), DOG-1 (+) (most specific), CD34 (+).
- **Surgical Rule**: Wedge resection with 2-cm clear margins; lymphadenectomy NOT needed.
- **Targeted Drug**: Imatinib mesylate 400 mg PO daily.
