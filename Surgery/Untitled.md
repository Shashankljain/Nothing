# Wound & Biology of Wound Healing

**Mark-Weightage: 10 Marks / Long Answer (Defaulted as not specified)**

## 1. Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- A **wound** is defined as a disruption of normal anatomical structure, cellular continuity, and functional integrity of living tissue, produced by physical, chemical, thermal, or surgical trauma.
- **Ulcer**: An ulcer is defined as a local break or discontinuity in the epithelial surface continuity of skin or mucous membrane, characterized by an inflamed or granulating base.

## 2. Etiology & CDC Classification of Surgical Wounds

- **Etiological Classification**:
    1. **Surgical Incised Wounds**: Clean cuts made by sharp instruments under aseptic conditions.
    2. **Traumatic Wounds**: Lacerations, contusions, abrasions, crush injuries, blast injuries, and degloving injuries.
    3. **Thermal / Chemical / Radiation Wounds**: Burns (scalds, flame, chemical) and cold injuries (frostbite).
    4. **Bite Wounds**: Human or animal bites carrying high bacterial contamination.
    5. **Chronic Wounds**: Ulcers that fail to progress through an orderly and timely sequence of repair (e.g., venous, arterial, neuropathic diabetic, and pressure ulcers).
- **CDC Surgical Wound Classification System**:

|Class|Classification|Description & Criteria|Estimated SSI Risk|Clinical Examples|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Class I**|**Clean**|Uninfected operative wound; no inflammation encountered; GI, respiratory, genital, or uninfected urinary tracts are NOT entered.|\(1-2%\)|Hernioplasty, Thyroidectomy, Mastectomy, CABG.|
|**Class II**|**Clean-Contaminated**|Respiratory, GI, genital, or urinary tract entered under controlled conditions without unusual contamination.|\(3-9%\)|Elective Cholecystectomy, Appendectomy, LSCS.|

## 3. Types of Wound Healing [⭐ High-Yield]

### 1. Primary Intention (Healing by First Intention)

- Occurs when wound edges are cleanly approximated and closed surgically (e.g., sutured clean surgical incisions).
- Minimal tissue loss, minimal granulation tissue formation, rapid re-epithelialization, and a hairline minimal scar.

### 2. Secondary Intention (Healing by Second Intention)

- Occurs in open wounds with significant tissue loss, devitalized tissue, or severe contamination where margins cannot be approximated.
- Wound is left open to heal gradually from the base upward through **granulation tissue formation**, active **wound contraction** (mediated by myofibroblasts), and **re-epithelialization**.
- Prolonged inflammatory and proliferative phases resulting in a broad, red, or contracted scar.

### 3. Tertiary Intention (Delayed Primary Closure)

- Employed in contaminated, untidy, or dirty traumatic wounds.
- The wound is initially left open, debrided, irrigated, and dressed for \(3-5\text{ days}\) to allow local infection and edema to subside.
- Once clean healthy granulation tissue appears without signs of infection, the wound margins are surgically approximated and sutured.

## 4. Biology & Phases of Wound Healing [⭐ High-Yield]

Normal wound healing is a continuous, dynamic biological process divided into four continuous, overlapping phases:

### Phase 1: Hemostasis (Immediate to Day \(1\))

- **Vascular Event**: Disruption of endothelium causes transient reflex vasoconstriction.
- **Platelet Plug**: Platelets adhere to exposed subendothelial collagen, aggregate, and degranulate.
- **Coagulation Cascade**: Extrinsic and intrinsic pathways converge to generate thrombin, converting fibrinogen to a **fibrin mesh clot**.
- **Mediator Release**: Platelets release vasoactive amines (histamine, serotonin) and key growth factors: **PDGF**(Platelet-Derived Growth Factor), **\(TGF-\beta\)** (Transforming Growth Factor-\(\beta\)), **FGF**, and **VEGF**.

### Phase 2: Inflammatory Phase (Days \(1-4\))

- **Early Inflammatory Phase (Days \(1-2\))**:
    - Neutrophils (polymorphonuclear leukocytes) are the first immune cells recruited into the wound bed via chemotaxis.
    - Neutrophils phagocytose bacteria, clear cell debris, and prevent infection.
- **Late Inflammatory Phase (Days \(2-4\))**:
    - Circulating blood monocytes enter the wound and differentiate into **tissue macrophages**.
    - **Master Orchestrators**: Macrophages act as the essential cell type for wound healing; they clear remaining debris via proteolytic enzymes and release pro-healing cytokines (\(IL-1\), \(IL-6\), \(TNF-\alpha\)) and growth factors (\(TGF-\beta1\), \(PDGF\), \(VEGF\)).
    - Clinical signs of inflammation: _rubor_ (redness), _tumor_ (swelling), _calor_ (heat), _dolor_ (pain), and _functio laesa_(loss of function).

### Phase 3: Proliferative Phase (Days \(4-21\) / Weeks \(2-4\))

- **Fibroplasia & Matrix Synthesis**: Fibroblasts proliferate and deposit ground substance (glycosaminoglycans, proteoglycans) and **Type III collagen** in a disorganized basket-weave arrangement.
- **Angiogenesis**: Sprouting of endothelial cells forms new blood capillary loops under the influence of **VEGF** and **bFGF**.
- **Granulation Tissue Formation**: Rich, pink, granular tissue composed of new capillaries, fibroblasts, macrophages, and collagen.
- **Wound Contraction**: Fibroblasts differentiate into contractile **myofibroblasts** (containing \(\alpha\text{-smooth muscle actin}\)) that actively pull wound edges together.
- **Re-Epithelialization**: Basal keratinocytes from wound edges and epidermal adnexa (hair follicles, sweat glands) migrate across the granulation tissue bed.

### Phase 4: Remodelling / Maturation Phase (Day \(21\) to \(1-2\text{ Years}\))

- **Collagen Maturation & Replacement**: Disorganized **Type III collagen** is degraded by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and replaced by organized **Type I collagen** until the normal skin ratio:

\[\frac{\text{Type I}}{\text{Type III}} = 4:1\]

- **Collagen Cross-Linking**: Collagen fibers align along lines of physical stress and undergo covalent cross-linking.
- **Tensile Strength Timeline**:
    - At \(1\text{ week}\): \(\approx 10%\) of uninjured tissue strength.
    - At \(3\text{ months}\) (\(12\text{ weeks}\)): Reaches a maximum plateau of \(\approx 70-80%\) of normal skin tensile strength.
    - Scar tissue **never regains \(100%\)** of the original uninjured tissue strength.

### Local Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Tissue Perfusion / Hypoxia & Ischemia**: Inadequate arterial supply or local venous congestion deprives tissue of \(O_2\), impairing neutrophil bactericidal killing and collagen synthesis.
2. **Infection / Contamination**: Bacterial burden \(>10^5\text{ organisms/g}\) of tissue or presence of virulent bacteria prolongs the inflammatory phase, releasing proteases that degrade matrix.
3. **Foreign Bodies & Devitalized Tissue**: Sutures, dirt, necrotic slough, or sequestra act as a persistent nidus for bacterial colonization.
4. **Excessive Skin Tension**: Incisions placed across lines of relaxed skin tension or flexor surfaces suffer from decreased local microvascular flow, leading to scar widening or dehiscence.
5. **Prior Radiation Therapy**: Induces endarteritis obliterans, rendering local tissue hypocellular, hypovascular, and hypoxic.
6. **Hematoma & Seroma**: Prevents physical apposition of tissue layers and provides a rich culture medium for bacterial infection.

### Systemic (General) Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Malnutrition & Protein Deficiency**: Serum albumin level \(<2\text{ g/dL}\) severely impairs fibroplasia, collagen synthesis, and immune response.
2. **Vitamin & Mineral Deficiencies**:
    - **Vitamin C Deficiency (Scurvy)**: Required for hydroxylation of proline and lysine in collagen synthesis; deficiency causes defective collagen cross-linking and wound breakdown.
    - **Vitamin A Deficiency**: Impairs epithelialization and lysosomal membrane stability.
    - **Zinc Deficiency**: Impairs DNA synthesis and cell division.
3. **Diabetes Mellitus**: Glycosylation of proteins, impaired neutrophil chemotaxis, microvascular disease, and tissue hyperglycemia delay repair and increase infection risk.
4. **Corticosteroids & Immunosuppressive Drugs**: Steroids inhibit early inflammatory response, macrophage recruitment, and fibroblast proliferation; should be restarted \(3-4\text{ days}\) post-injury if medically necessary.
5. **Anemia & Hypoxia**: Hemoglobin \(<10\text{ g/dL}\) reduces systemic oxygen transport capacity to healing tissues.
6. **Cigarette Smoking**: Nicotine causes potent peripheral vasoconstriction, while carbon monoxide reduces oxygen delivery.
7. **Jaundice & Uremia**: Elevated bilirubin and nitrogenous toxins impair protein synthesis and cell proliferation.
8. **Advancing Age & Obesity**: Slower cell turnover and poorly vascularized adipose tissue subject to fat necrosis and infection.

## 6. Complications of Abnormal Wound Healing

### Differential Diagnosis — Hypertrophic Scar vs. Keloid Scar [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Hypertrophic Scar|Keloid Scar|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Growth Boundary**|Elevated scar confined strictly **within**original wound margins.|Overgrows and extends **beyond** original wound boundaries into normal skin.|
|**Anatomical Site**|Flexor surfaces, area crossing tension lines, sternum.|Presternal region, earlobes, deltoid, upper back.|
|**Collagen Architecture**|Increased collagen arranged in **parallel bundles**.|Excess **Type III collagen** in thick, disorganized, chaotic whorls.|
|**Genetic Predisposition**|Minimal; related to local wound tension.|Strong familial & dark skin pigmentation predisposition.|
|**Spontaneous Regression**|**Yes**; flattens and regresses spontaneously over time.|**No**; persists indefinitely, rarely regresses.|
|**Treatment**|Silicone gel pads, pressure therapy.|Intralesional **Triamcinolone**, surgical excision \(+\) RT/laser.|

### Wound Dehiscence (Burst Abdomen)

- Disruption of any or all layers of a surgical wound, typically occurring between postoperative days \(5\) and \(8\) (when wound tensile strength is at its nadir).
- **Clinical Sign**: Sudden discharge of serosanguineous (**"salmon-pink"**) fluid from the wound, often preceded by coughing or straining.
- **Management**: Emergency cover with sterile moist saline pack \(\rightarrow\) return to OT for resuturing of fascial layer or temporary abdominal closure (e.g., Bogota bag / VAC).

### Marjolin's Ulcer

- Malignant transformation (**Squamous Cell Carcinoma**) arising in long-standing chronic non-healing wounds, burn scars, venous ulcers, or osteomyelitis sinuses.
- Features raised, everted, cauliflower-like margins; managed by wide local excision.

### Clinical Pearl

- **Vitamin C & Steroid Reversal**: Intravenous or oral Vitamin A (\(10,000-25,000\text{ IU/day}\)) can reverse the delayed wound healing effects of corticosteroids by restoring the early inflammatory response and macrophage activation.

### Exam Trap

- **Hypertrophic Scar vs. Keloid Scar**:
    - **The Mix-up**: Confusing growth boundaries and regression potential.
    - **Distinguishing Fact**: A hypertrophic scar stays strictly **within** the boundaries of the original wound and regresses spontaneously with time. A keloid extends **beyond** the original wound boundaries into normal surrounding skin, does not spontaneously regress, and has a high recurrence rate after simple surgical excision.

### Quick Revision

- **Wound**: Disruption of anatomical and functional continuity of tissue.
- **Phases of Healing**: Hemostasis (Immediate), Inflammatory (Days \(1-4\)), Proliferative (Days \(4-21\)), Remodelling (Day \(21\) to \(1-2\text{ years}\)).
- **Types of Healing**: Primary intention (sutured clean wound), Secondary intention (open wound healing via granulation, contraction, re-epithelialization), Tertiary intention (delayed primary closure after \(3-5\text{ days}\)).
- **Key Tensile Strength**: Reaches \(\approx 10%\) at \(1\text{ week}\), max \(70-80%\) at \(3\text{ months}\) (\(12\text{ weeks}\)); never reaches \(100%\).
- **Local & Systemic Factors**: Infection, ischemia, tension, foreign body, hypoalbuminemia (\(<2\text{ g/dL}\)), Vitamin C deficiency, diabetes, and steroids impair healing.

### One-Line Exam Opener

- **Wound healing is a tightly regulated, dynamic biological cascade progressing through overlapping phases of hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodelling, yielding a scar that achieves a maximum of 70–80% of uninjured tissue tensile strength by 3 months.**
