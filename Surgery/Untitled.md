# Complications of Peptic Ulcer Surgery

### Definition

**Complications of peptic ulcer surgery** (sequelae of vagotomy, gastrectomy, or drainage procedures) refer to a spectrum of early and late mechanical, metabolic, nutritional, and functional gastrointestinal disorders occurring in up to 30% of operated patients, with approximately 5% experiencing severe or intractable symptoms.

---

### Key Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Dumping Syndrome**:
    - _Early Dumping_: Rapid entry of hyperosmolar chyme into the small intestine causing fluid shifts from circulation into the gut, producing abdominal fullness, cramping, nausea, and vasomotor collapse (sweating, tachycardia) 10–15 minutes post-meal.
    - _Late Dumping_: Rapid gastric emptying causing a rapid spike in blood glucose, triggering an exaggerated insulin surge and secondary **rebound hypoglycemia** 30–40 minutes post-meal.
- **Postvagotomy Diarrhea**: Severe, explosive osmotic diarrhea seen predominantly after truncal vagotomy due to denervation of the biliary/gut system paired with rapid small bowel transit.
- **Nutritional Deficiencies**:
    - _Iron Deficiency Anemia_: Most common post-surgical anemia (microcytic hypochromic) due to reduced gastric acid and bypass of duodenal iron absorption sites.
    - _Vitamin B₁₂ Deficiency_: Megaloblastic anemia due to loss of parietal cell intrinsic factor (after gastrectomy) or bacterial overgrowth.
    - _Bone Disease_: Osteoporosis and osteomalacia secondary to calcium and vitamin D malabsorption.
- **Mechanical & Reconstructive Sequelae**:
    - _Duodenal Stump Blowout_: Acute leak typically occurring on postoperative day 4 following Polya gastrectomy or Roux-en-Y reconstruction.
    - _Afferent Loop Syndrome_: Mechanical twist or stasis in the afferent loop causing severe postprandial epigastric pain relieved by projectile bilious vomiting.
    - _Gallstone Formation_: Denervation of the biliary tree following truncal vagotomy causing gallbladder stasis and lithogenesis.
- **Malignant Transformation**: Chronic alkaline bile reflux gastritis predisposing to **gastric remnant carcinoma**(stump cancer) with a characteristic lag period ≥10 years.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Incidence**: Postoperative sequelae occur in ~30% of patients; ~5% experience intractable symptoms.
- **Dumping Triad**: Early dumping (fluid shift/vasomotor symptoms at 10–15 mins) vs. Late dumping (rebound hypoglycemia at 30–40 mins).
- **Vagotomy Specifics**: Postvagotomy diarrhea and gallstone formation due to denervation of upper GI visceral trunks.
- **Anemia Profiles**: Microcytic hypochromic (iron malabsorption) and Megaloblastic (vitamin B₁₂ / intrinsic factor deficiency).
- **Malignancy Risk**: Bile reflux gastritis increases the risk of gastric stump adenocarcinoma after ≥10 years.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Complications of peptic ulcer surgery encompass a distinct group of denervation and anatomical reconstructive sequelae—including early/late dumping syndrome, postvagotomy diarrhea, nutritional anemias, and gastric stump carcinoma—affecting up to 30% of patients following gastrectomy or vagotomy._
