# Peptic Ulcer Disease (Gastric and Duodenal Ulcers)

### Definition

**Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)** is defined as a circumscribed mucosal breach extending through the muscularis mucosae into the submucosa or deeper muscular layers, occurring in regions of the gastrointestinal tract exposed to acid-pepsin secretion.

Common anatomical sites include:

- First part of the duodenum (D₁)
- Lesser curvature of the stomach
- Gastrojejunal stoma following gastrectomy or gastroenterostomy (stomal ulcer)
- Lower esophagus in gastroesophageal reflux disease
- Meckel's diverticulum containing ectopic gastric mucosa

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

Peptic ulceration results from an imbalance between aggressive luminal factors (acid, pepsin, infection) and protective mucosal defense mechanisms.

#### 1. Primary Etiological Factors

- **_Helicobacter pylori_ Infection**: Present in 90%–95% of duodenal ulcers and 60%–65% of gastric ulcers. _H. pylori_produces urease, triggering antral inflammation, hypergastrinemia, parietal cell hyperplasia, and acid hypersecretion.
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)**: Inhibit COX-1 enzyme synthesis, depleting cytoprotective prostaglandins (PGE₂ and PGI₂), which impairs gastric mucus and bicarbonate secretion.

#### 2. Secondary & Environmental Risk Factors

- **Cigarette Smoking**: Directly predisposes to peptic ulceration, delays mucosal healing, and markedly increases relapse rates.
- **Gastric Acid Hypersecretion / Gastrinoma**: Uncontrolled acid production in Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (ZES).
- **Stress Ulceration**: Occurs in critically ill patients, major trauma, or severe burns (Curling's ulcer).

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Duodenal Ulcer (DU) vs. Gastric Ulcer (GU)

- **Duodenal Ulcer (DU)**: More common than GU; peak incidence in older age groups; equalizing gender ratio in modern practice. Located predominantly in D₁. Associated with acid hypersecretion.
- **Gastric Ulcer (GU)**: Less common than DU; equal gender incidence; more prevalent in lower socioeconomic groups. Must be thoroughly biopsied due to malignant potential.

#### 2. Johnson's Classification of Gastric Ulcers

- **Type I**: Single ulcer along lesser curvature near incisura angularis (most common; normal or low acid production).
- **Type II**: Combined gastric and duodenal ulcer (acid hypersecretion positive).
- **Type III**: Prepyloric ulcer within 2 cm of pylorus (acid hypersecretion positive).
- **Type IV**: High juxta-esophageal ulcer near gastroesophageal junction.
- **Type V**: Diffuse ulceration throughout stomach induced by NSAID ingestion.

- **_H. pylori_ Sequence**: _H. pylori_ urease converts urea to ammonia, neutralizing local gastric acid and triggering negative feedback that stimulates antral G-cells to release gastrin. Chronic hypergastrinemia increases parietal cell mass and acid load. Excess acid entering D₁ induces **duodenal gastric metaplasia**, which is subsequently colonized by _H. pylori_, resulting in active duodenitis and ulceration.
- **NSAID Sequence**: COX-1 inhibition blocks systemic prostaglandin synthesis, impairing mucosal bicarbonate secretion, decreasing mucosal blood flow, and allowing acid-pepsin back-diffusion into the submucosa.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Epigastric Pain**: Characteristic gnawing or burning sensation localized to the epigastrium, frequently radiating directly through to the back.
- **Pain-Food Relationship**:
    - _Duodenal Ulcer_: Pain occurs 2–3 hours after meals or during the night ("hunger pain"), classically **relieved by food** or antacids.
    - _Gastric Ulcer_: Pain occurs early after eating, is **worsened by food**, and leads to fear of eating and subsequent weight loss.
- **Periodicity**: Intermittent symptoms that disappear for weeks or months and return spontaneously, corresponding to cycles of mucosal healing and reactivation.
- **Associated Dyspepsia**: Epigastric fullness, bloating, nausea, and water brash.

#### Signs

- **Epigastric Point Tenderness**: Localized tenderness on deep palpation in the epigastrium ("pointing sign").
- **Pallor**: Microcytic hypochromic anemia from chronic occult blood loss.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (Gastroduodenoscopy) [Investigation of Choice]

- **Direct Visualization**: Identifies precise ulcer site, size, depth, and stigmata of recent hemorrhage.
- **Gastric Ulcer Biopsy Rule [⭐ High-Yield]**: Because ulcerated gastric adenocarcinoma can mimic benign gastric ulcers, **multiple biopsies (6 to 10 cores from edges and base)** must be obtained for all gastric ulcers to exclude malignancy.
- **Endoscopic Inspection**: Performs a 'U' maneuver (retroflexion) to view the incisura, lesser curve, and gastroesophageal junction.

#### 2. _Helicobacter pylori_ Diagnostic Panel

- **Invasive (Endoscopy-based)**:
    - _Rapid Urease Test (CLO Test)_: Antral biopsy placed in urea-containing medium; color change confirms urease presence.
    - _Histology_: Modified Giemsa or Warthin-Starry silver stain of gastric mucosal biopsy.
- **Non-Invasive**:
    - _Urea Breath Test (¹³C or ¹⁴C UBT)_: High sensitivity and specificity; gold standard for confirming post-treatment eradication.
    - _Stool Antigen Test_: Highly accurate for initial diagnosis and post-treatment monitoring.

#### 3. Laboratory Studies

- **Complete Blood Count**: Evaluates microcytic hypochromic anemia secondary to chronic occult GI blood loss.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. First-Line Medical Management

- **_H. pylori_ Eradication Therapy (14-Day Triple Protocol)**:
    - **Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI)**: Omeprazole 20 mg PO BD or Pantoprazole 40 mg PO BD
    - **Amoxicillin**: 1000 mg (1 g) PO BD
    - **Clarithromycin**: 500 mg PO BD _(Metronidazole 400 mg BD replaces Amoxicillin in penicillin-allergic patients)_.
- **Lifestyle Modification**: Mandatory cessation of cigarette smoking and complete withdrawal of NSAIDs.

#### 2. Surgical Management

Elective surgery is rarely performed today due to PPIs and _H. pylori_ eradication. Surgery is indicated primarily for refractory disease or acute life-threatening complications.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

The three cardinal complications of peptic ulcer disease are **bleeding, perforation, and stenosis (gastric outlet obstruction)**.

#### 1. Bleeding Peptic Ulcer [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pathology**: Most common complication of PUD and most common cause of upper GI hemorrhage. Posterior duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery**. Gastric ulcers erode into the left gastric artery.
- **Clinical Features**: Presents with hematemesis ("coffee-ground" or frank red blood) and melaena. Severe acute hemorrhage leads to tachycardia, hypotension, and hypovolemic shock.
- **Management**:
    - Resuscitation (IV fluids, blood transfusion) + IV PPI infusion.
    - Emergency Upper GI Endoscopy: Hemostasis using dual modality (adrenaline injection + hemoclips or thermal coagulation).
    - Surgical Intervention: Indicated if endoscopic hemostasis fails twice or >6 units of blood are required → Laparotomy, duodenotomy, and **ligation/under-running of the bleeding gastroduodenal artery**.

#### 2. Perforated Peptic Ulcer [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pathology**: Anterior duodenal ulcers most commonly perforate into the peritoneal cavity. Posterior ulcers rarely perforate into the retroperitoneum, causing **Valentino syndrome** (retroperitoneal fluid tracking down right paracolic gutter mimicking acute appendicitis) or gas under the right kidney.
- **Clinical Features**: Sudden onset of excruciating, generalized abdominal pain due to chemical peritonitis. Abdomen exhibits **board-like rigidity** and does not move with respiration.
- **Investigations**: Erect chest radiograph demonstrates **free gas under the diaphragm** (pneumoperitoneum) in >50% of cases. CT scan is diagnostic.
- **Management**:
    - Emergency Resuscitation: NPO, IV fluids, NG suction, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and analgesics.
    - Emergency Surgery: **Graham's Omental Patch Repair** (or Cellan-Jones omental patch repair) via laparotomy or laparoscopy.

#### 3. Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO) / Cicatrized Pyloric Stenosis [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pathology**: Chronic scarring and cicatrization of the first part of the duodenum (D₁) or pyloric channel secondary to recurrent ulceration.
- **Clinical Features**: Long history of PUD. Characterized by **non-bilious, projectile vomiting of undigested food**eaten 12–24 hours earlier. Physical exam reveals a distended stomach with visible gastric peristalsis and an audible **succussion splash**.
- **Metabolic Abnormalities [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. Persistent vomiting of pure HCl produces **hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis**.
    2. Progressive dehydration leads to hyponatremia and hypokalemia.
    3. Dehydration forces the distal renal tubule to reabsorb Na⁺ in exchange for H⁺ and K⁺ ions, resulting in **paradoxical aciduria** (acidic urine despite systemic alkalosis).
    4. Alkalosis lowers circulating ionized calcium (Ca²⁺), predisposing to **tetany**.
- **Management**:
    - Resuscitation: **IV N/2 Normal Saline in 5% Dextrose + KCl supplementation**. _(Normal Saline supplies Cl⁻ needed by kidneys to correct metabolic alkalosis)_.
    - Gastric Decompression: Wide-bore NG tube placement + daily normal saline gastric lavage.
    - Definitive Care: Endoscopic balloon dilatation or surgical **Gastroenterostomy**.

### Dosage Rule (Drugs in Peptic Ulcer Disease)

#### 1. Omeprazole / Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Omeprazole / Pantoprazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase enzyme (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells, suppressing gastric acid secretion.
- **Dose**: Omeprazole 20 mg / Pantoprazole 40 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule/tablet (or IV Pantoprazole 40 mg)
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Features**: Mainstay of acid suppression and _H. pylori_ eradication.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, long-term risk of hypomagnesemia and vitamin B₁₂ deficiency.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start immediately on diagnosis; continue for 14 days in eradication regimens, or 4–8 weeks for ulcer healing.

#### 2. Amoxicillin

- **Name**: Amoxicillin Trihydrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Beta-lactam antibiotic; inhibits bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis in _H. pylori_.
- **Dose**: 1000 mg (1 g)
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Features**: First-line component of _H. pylori_ triple eradication therapy.
- **Side Effects**: Diarrhea, skin rash, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administer for 14 days as part of eradication therapy.

#### 3. Clarithromycin

- **Name**: Clarithromycin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Macrolide antibiotic; binds 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis.
- **Dose**: 500 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Features**: Key antibiotic in first-line _H. pylori_ triple therapy.
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, abdominal cramps, nausea, QT prolongation.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administer for 14 days as part of eradication therapy.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In a patient presenting with Gastric Outlet Obstruction secondary to pyloric stenosis, **Ringer's Lactate is strictly contraindicated for initial fluid resuscitation**. The fluid of choice is **0.9% Normal Saline (or N/2 NS in 5% Dextrose) with KCl supplementation** because Normal Saline supplies chloride ions (Cl⁻) essential for the kidneys to retain H⁺ and correct hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis.

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Mistaking the location and complications of anterior vs. posterior duodenal ulcers.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: **Anterior duodenal ulcers tend to perforate** into the peritoneal cavity causing chemical peritonitis, whereas **posterior duodenal ulcers tend to bleed** by eroding directly into the gastroduodenal artery.

- **C** – **C**icatrization of D₁ leading to pyloric stenosis.
- **O** – **O**mental patch repair (Graham's repair) for perforated ulcers.
- **M** – **M**etabolic alkalosis (hypochloraemic, hypokalemic) in GOO.
- **P** – **P**osterior ulcers bleed (gastroduodenal artery); Anterior ulcers perforate.
- **L** – **L**oss of HCl via non-bilious projectile vomitus.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Mucosal breach extending beyond muscularis mucosae in acid-exposed regions.
- **Primary Causes**: _H. pylori_ infection (90% DU, 60% GU) and NSAID ingestion.
- **Pain Characteristics**: DU pain relieved by food ("hunger pain"); GU pain worsened by food.
- **Endoscopy Rule**: All gastric ulcers must undergo 6–10 biopsies to exclude malignancy.
- **Eradication Regimen**: 14-day Triple Therapy (PPI BD + Amoxicillin 1 g BD + Clarithromycin 500 mg BD).
- **Complication Triad**: Bleeding (posterior DU / gastroduodenal artery), Perforation (anterior DU / Graham's patch repair), and GOO (pyloric stenosis / hypochloraemic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Peptic ulcer disease is a chronic ulcerative breakdown of the gastrointestinal mucosa extending beyond the muscularis mucosae, caused predominantly by Helicobacter pylori infection or NSAID-induced prostaglandin depletion, diagnosed by upper GI endoscopy with mandatory biopsy of gastric ulcers, managed primarily with H. pylori triple eradication therapy, and complicated by gastroduodenal artery hemorrhage, anterior duodenal perforation, or cicatrized gastric outlet obstruction._
