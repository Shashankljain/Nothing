# Complications of Peptic (Gastric) Ulcer

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Complications of peptic (gastric) ulcer disease** refer to life-threatening structural or functional sequelae resulting from progressive acid-peptic erosion through the muscularis mucosae and gastric wall layers into underlying blood vessels, the peritoneal cavity, or adjacent visceral organs.

---

### Key Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemorrhage (Bleeding)** [⭐ High-Yield]: The most common acute complication; occurs when mucosal erosion penetrates underlying vessels (the **left gastric artery** in lesser curve gastric ulcers, or the **gastroduodenal artery** in posterior duodenal ulcers), presenting with hematemesis, melena, and hypovolemic shock.
- **Perforation**: Most commonly involves **anterior wall ulcers**, where transmural erosion breaches the visceral serosa into the peritoneal cavity, producing severe chemical peritonitis, board-like abdominal rigidity, and free subdiaphragmatic gas on erect X-ray.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (Pyloric Stenosis / Cicatrization)**: Occurs secondary to acute inflammatory edema or chronic fibrotic scarring near the pyloric channel, manifesting as persistent non-bilious vomiting of undigested food, succussion splash, and hypochloraemic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis.
- **Malignant Transformation**: Unlike duodenal ulcers (which are virtually always benign), chronic gastric ulcers carry a **1%–5% risk** of harboring or transforming into **gastric adenocarcinoma**, mandating mandatory endoscopic biopsies (6–10 cores) from the ulcer edges.
- **Penetration**: Deep ulceration eroding through the full thickness of the gastric wall to directly invade adjacent solid structures—most frequently the **pancreas**, presenting with constant, gnawing epigastric pain radiating to the back.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Triad of Core Complications**: Bleeding, perforation, and gastric outlet obstruction (stenosis).
- **Bleeding**: Most frequent complication; posterior ulcers erode into major underlying arteries.
- **Perforation**: Anterior ulcers breach the serosa, leading to pneumoperitoneum and acute peritonitis.
- **Malignancy Risk**: Unique to gastric ulcers (1%–5%), requiring routine endoscopic biopsy to exclude adenocarcinoma.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Complications of peptic gastric ulcers represent life-threatening surgical emergencies—predominantly hemorrhage, free peritoneal perforation, gastric outlet obstruction, penetration into adjacent organs, and malignant transformation—stemming from progressive acid-peptic transmural destruction of the gastric wall._
