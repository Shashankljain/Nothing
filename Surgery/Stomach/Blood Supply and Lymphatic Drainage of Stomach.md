# Blood Supply and Lymphatic Drainage of Stomach

### Anatomical Introduction [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Arterial Derivation**: The stomach is the dilated portion of the foregut, receiving an exceptionally rich arterial supply derived entirely, directly or indirectly, from the **Coeliac Trunk** (the artery of the foregut arising from the abdominal aorta at the T12 vertebral level).
- **Ischemic Resilience**: Extensive intramural submucosal anastomotic arcades exist throughout the gastric wall. Because of this rich collateral network, the stomach is remarkably resistant to ischemia, and up to 2–3 major arteries can be safely ligated during surgical procedures without causing gastric necrosis.
- **Surgical Lymphatics**: The lymphatic drainage of the stomach closely parallels its arterial supply. A thorough understanding of gastric lymphatic pathways and the **Japanese Gastric Cancer Association (JGCA)** nodal station classification (Stations 1 to 16) is the single most critical factor in performing radical curative surgery (D1/D2 gastrectomy) for gastric adenocarcinoma.

---

### Arterial Supply [⭐ High-Yield]

The stomach is supplied by **5 primary arterial sources** that form continuous anastomotic arcades along both the lesser and greater curvatures:

```
                         COELIAC TRUNK (T12)
                                  |
        +-------------------------+-------------------------+
        |                         |                         |
Left Gastric Artery      Common Hepatic Artery        Splenic Artery
        |                         |                         |
  (Lesser Curve)            +-----+-----+             +-----+-----+
                            |           |             |           |
                      Right Gastric  Gastroduodenal  Short     Left Gastro-
                         Artery        Artery      Gastrics     epiploic
                            |           |                         |
                      (Lesser Curve) Right Gastro-          (Greater Curve)
                                       epiploic
                                          |
                                    (Greater Curve)
```

#### 1. Left Gastric Artery [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Origin**: Direct branch of the coeliac trunk (smallest of the 3 coeliac branches).
- **Course**: Ascends behind the lesser sac in the gastropancreatic fold towards the cardiac orifice, giving off 1–2 **esophageal branches** that supply the lower abdominal esophagus. It then turns anteroinferiorly to run along the **lesser curvature** between the leaves of the lesser omentum.
- **Anastomosis**: Anastomoses with the right gastric artery in the region of the angular incisure (incisura angularis).
- **Surgical Relevance**: It is the dominant artery supplying the stomach. Involved in Type IV high lesser curve gastric ulcers, Mallory-Weiss tears, and upper GI hemorrhage. An aberrant or replaced left hepatic artery arises from the left gastric artery in up to 15%–20% of individuals.

#### 2. Right Gastric Artery

- **Origin**: Arises most commonly from the common hepatic artery (or hepatic artery proper / gastroduodenal artery).
- **Course**: Runs leftward within the lesser omentum along the **lesser curvature**.
- **Anastomosis**: Anastomoses directly with the left gastric artery.

#### 3. Left Gastroepiploic (Gastro-Omental) Artery

- **Origin**: Largest branch of the splenic artery, given off near the splenic hilum.
- **Course**: Passes between the layers of the gastrosplenic ligament into the upper gastrocolic omentum, running along the **greater curvature** from left to right.
- **Anastomosis**: Anastomoses with the right gastroepiploic artery.
- **Surgical Relevance**: Provides the main vascular supply when fashioning a **gastric tube/conduit** mobilized into the chest or neck for esophageal reconstruction following esophagectomy.

#### 4. Right Gastroepiploic (Gastro-Omental) Artery

- **Origin**: Terminal branch of the gastroduodenal artery (arising behind the first part of the duodenum).
- **Course**: Ascends to the greater curvature below the pylorus and runs right-to-left between the layers of the gastrocolic omentum about 1–2 cm from the gastric wall.
- **Anastomosis**: Anastomoses with the left gastroepiploic artery along the greater curvature.

#### 5. Short Gastric Arteries (Vasa Brevia) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Origin**: 5 to 7 small branches arising from the terminal portion of the splenic artery or its hilar branches.
- **Course**: Pass between the layers of the **gastrosplenic ligament** to supply the **fundus** and upper greater curvature of the stomach.
- **Surgical Relevance**: Divided during mobilization of the greater curvature in total/subtotal gastrectomy, splenectomy, or laparoscopic Nissen fundoplication.

#### 6. Posterior Gastric Artery (Variable)

- Arises from the mid-section of the splenic artery in ~30%–50% of individuals, ascending behind the lesser sac to supply the posterior wall of the gastric body and fundus.

### Venous Drainage [⭐ High-Yield]

The venous drainage of the stomach strictly mirrors its arterial supply and drains entirely into the **Portal Venous System**:

- **Left Gastric Vein (Coronary Vein)**: Runs along the lesser curvature; drains directly into the **portal vein** (or occasionally the splenic vein). Communicates with the lower esophageal submucosal venous plexus.
    - _Clinical Significance_: In portal hypertension (portal pressure >10–12 mmHg), retrograde flow through the left gastric vein leads to severe dilatation of these submucosal channels, producing **esophageal and gastric fundal varices** prone to catastrophic bleeding.
- **Right Gastric Vein**: Runs along the lesser curvature to drain directly into the portal vein.
    - _Pre-pyloric Vein of Mayo_: A constant vertical tributary of the right gastric vein that crosses anteriorly over the gastroduodenal junction, serving as an invaluable anatomical landmark to identify the pyloric sphincter during surgery.
- **Left Gastroepiploic Vein**: Runs in the gastrosplenic ligament to drain into the **splenic vein**.
- **Right Gastroepiploic Vein**: Runs in the gastrocolic omentum to drain into the **superior mesenteric vein (SMV)**. Frequently joins the right colic vein to form the **Gastrocolic Trunk of Henle** before entering the SMV.
- **Short Gastric Veins**: Drain the fundus via the gastrosplenic ligament into the **splenic vein**.

### Lymphatic Drainage & Japanese Nodal Stations [⭐ High-Yield]

Gastric lymphatic channels arise in intramural submucosal and subserosal plexuses and drain into regional lymph node basins that accompany the corresponding arteries.

To standardize radical surgery for gastric carcinoma, the **Japanese Gastric Cancer Association (JGCA)** has classified gastric lymph node drainage into **16 numbered Nodal Stations** grouped into distinct tier levels:

```
                       JAPANESE LYMPH NODE STATIONS
                                    |
        +---------------------------+---------------------------+
        |                           |                           |
  N1 Tier (Perigastric)       N2 Tier (Vascular Trunks)    N3 / Distant Tier
   (Stations 1 to 6)          (Stations 7 to 11)           (Stations 12 to 16)
        |                           |                           |
  1: Right paracardial        7: Left gastric artery       12: Hepatoduodenal lig.
  2: Left paracardial         8a: Common hepatic art.      13: Retropancreatic
  3: Lesser curvature         9: Coeliac axis              14: Mesenteric root
  4: Greater curvature        10: Splenic hilum            15: Middle colic art.
  5: Suprapyloric             11: Splenic artery           16: Para-aortic (M1)
  6: Infrapyloric
```

#### 1. Japanese Lymph Node Station Classification (Stations 1 to 16) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **N1 Tier (Perigastric Nodes - Stations 1 to 6)**:
    
    - **Station 1**: Right paracardial nodes.
    - **Station 2**: Left paracardial nodes.
    - **Station 3**: Nodes along the lesser curvature.
    - **Station 4**: Nodes along the greater curvature (_4sa_: short gastric; _4sb_: left gastroepiploic; _4d_: right gastroepiploic).
    - **Station 5**: Suprapyloric nodes.
    - **Station 6**: Infrapyloric nodes.
- **N2 Tier (Extra-perigastric / Major Vascular Trunk Nodes - Stations 7 to 11)**:
    
    - **Station 7**: Nodes along the left gastric artery.
    - **Station 8**: Nodes along the common hepatic artery (_8a_: anterior group).
    - **Station 9**: Nodes around the coeliac axis / trifurcation.
    - **Station 10**: Nodes at the splenic hilum.
    - **Station 11**: Nodes along the splenic artery (_11p_: proximal; _11d_: distal).
- **N3 / Distant Tier (Stations 12 to 16 & Beyond)**:
    
    - **Station 12**: Nodes in the hepatoduodenal ligament.
    - **Station 13**: Retropancreatic nodes.
    - **Station 14**: Nodes at the root of the superior mesenteric vessels.
    - **Station 15**: Nodes along the middle colic vessels.
    - **Station 16**: Para-aortic lymph nodes (_Involvement of Station 16 is pathologically categorized as M1 distant metastatic disease_).

#### 2. Extent of Surgical Lymphadenectomy (D1 vs D2 Dissection) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **D1 Lymphadenectomy**: En bloc removal of the affected stomach along with only the **perigastric lymph nodes (Stations 1 to 6)**.
- **D2 Lymphadenectomy (Standard Gold Standard)**: Complete clearance of all **N1 perigastric nodes (Stations 1–6) PLUS N2 extra-perigastric nodes along major vascular trunks (Stations 7–11)**. A minimum of **16 lymph nodes** must be pathologically retrieved for accurate TNM staging.
- **D3 Lymphadenectomy**: Extended clearance including Station 12–16 nodes; no longer routinely recommended due to significantly higher surgical morbidity without survival benefit.

- **Resistance to Ischemia**: Thanks to rich submucosal anastomoses, ischemic necrosis of the gastric wall is rare. During gastric mobilization for esophageal replacement, the stomach can remain completely viable based solely on the preserved **right gastroepiploic arcade**.
- **Peptic Ulcer Hemorrhage**: Posterior duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery** (or superior pancreaticoduodenal artery) causing massive bleeding, whereas lesser curve gastric ulcers erode into the **left gastric artery**.
- **Portal Hypertension**: Cirrhosis causes obstruction to portal flow, diverting blood retrogradely through the **left gastric vein** into submucosal veins of the lower esophagus, producing **esophageal varices**.

### Diagram 1: Arterial Supply of the Stomach

- **Type**: Labeled anatomical schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: Outline of the stomach from the lower esophagus to the duodenal cap, showing the lesser and greater curvatures.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. _Coeliac Trunk (T12)_ — central origin from the abdominal aorta.
    2. _Left Gastric Artery_ — arches along lesser curvature to anastomose with Right Gastric Artery.
    3. _Common Hepatic Artery_ — divides into Hepatic Artery Proper and Gastroduodenal Artery.
    4. _Splenic Artery_ — tortuous vessel along upper border of pancreas, giving off Short Gastric Arteries and Left Gastroepiploic Artery.
    5. _Right Gastroepiploic Artery_ — branch of Gastroduodenal Artery running along greater curvature.
- **Connections**: Coeliac Trunk → Left Gastric + Common Hepatic + Splenic Arteries → Anastomotic Arcades on Lesser and Greater Curvatures.
- **Annotation for Extra Marks**: _"Extensive submucosal arterial anastomoses between lesser and greater curvature arcades ensure high mucosal perfusion and prevent gastric necrosis even when multiple vessels are ligated."_

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: C-E-L-I-A-C (Arterial Supply & Nodal Flow)

- **C** – **C**oeliac trunk (T12) gives origin to all primary gastric arteries.
- **E** – **E**sophageal branches arise from the Left Gastric Artery to supply the lower esophagus.
- **L** – **L**eft Gastric Artery is the dominant arterial supply to the stomach.
- **I** – **I**nfrapyloric nodes (Station 6) drain the distal antrum and pylorus.
- **A** – **A**rterial arcades run continuously along both lesser and greater curvatures.
- **C** – **C**oeliac axis nodes (Station 9) form the main collecting center for N2 lymph drainage.

### Quick Revision

- **Arterial Origins**: Coeliac Trunk (T12) gives off Left Gastric, Common Hepatic, and Splenic arteries.
- **Major Arcades**: Lesser curvature (Left Gastric + Right Gastric); Greater curvature (Left Gastroepiploic + Right Gastroepiploic); Fundus (Short Gastric arteries).
- **Venous Drainage**: All gastric veins drain into the Portal System; Left Gastric vein forms portosystemic shunts causing esophageal varices in portal hypertension.
- **Japanese Nodal Stations**: Perigastric N1 (Stations 1–6); Extra-perigastric N2 (Stations 7–11); Distant N3 (Stations 12–16).
- **Minimum Node Harvest**: Minimum 16 lymph nodes required for accurate TNM pathological staging.
