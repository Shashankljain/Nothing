# Gastroscopy (Oesophagogastroduodenoscopy / OGD)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Gastroscopy**, formally termed **Oesophagogastroduodenoscopy (OGD)**, is a minimally invasive, direct diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopic procedure utilizing a flexible fiberoptic or digital video endoscope passed orally to visualize the luminal mucosa of the upper gastrointestinal tract—from the pharynx and esophagus, through the stomach, down to the second and third parts of the duodenum.

---

### Basic Surgical Anatomy & Anatomical Landmarks [⭐ High-Yield]

A standard forward-viewing endoscope measures 100–120 cm in working length. Direct endoluminal navigation relies on key distance landmarks measured from the upper incisor teeth:

1. **Upper Esophageal Sphincter (Cricopharyngeus)**: Situated at **15 cm** from incisors; represents the narrowest anatomical point of the upper GI tract and the highest risk site for iatrogenic perforation during intubation.
2. **Aortic & Left Bronchial Impression**: Located at **23–25 cm** from incisors; normal extrinsic pulsatile compression on the anterior/left esophageal wall.
3. **Gastroesophageal Junction (GEJ / Z-line)**: Located at **38–40 cm** from incisors; demarcates the squamocolumnar mucosal mucosal transition (whitish-pink esophageal squamous mucosa to salmon-pink gastric columnar mucosa).
4. **Gastric Regions**:
    - _Cardia & Fundus_: Inspected by executing the pathognomonic **"J" maneuver (Retroversion)**, bending the scope tip 180° back on itself within the gastric body.
    - _Incisura Angularis_: Notch along the lesser curvature separating the gastric body from the antrum.
    - _Pyloric Sphincter_: Smooth, circular muscular ring granting passage into the duodenum.
5. **Duodenum**:
    - _Duodenal Bulb (D₁)_: Smooth mucosal cap devoid of circular folds.
    - _Second Part of Duodenum (D₂)_: Characterized by prominent transverse mucosal folds (plicae circulares / valvulae conniventes) and the **Major Duodenal Papilla (Ampulla of Vater)** on its posteromedial wall.

---

### Classification (Types of Upper GI Endoscopes)

- **Forward-Viewing Gastroscope (Standard OGD Scope)**: Optics positioned at the distal tip facing straight ahead (140°–180° field of view); standard instrument for diagnostic and therapeutic upper GI procedures.
- **Side-Viewing Endoscope (Duodenoscope)**: Optics positioned laterally at 90°; essential for visualizing the Ampulla of Vater and performing **Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)**.
- **Ultra-Slim / Transnasal Endoscope**: Outer diameter <6 mm; passed transnasally without requiring pharyngeal local anesthesia or sedation.
- **Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS)**: Combines an endoscope with a high-frequency (5–12 MHz) ultrasound transducer at the tip; evaluates transmural tumor depth (T-staging) and guides Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) of pancreaticobiliary lesions.

### Clinical Indications & Contraindications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Diagnostic Indications

- **Red-Flag / Alarm Symptoms** [⭐ High-Yield]: Age >55 years with new-onset dyspepsia, progressive dysphagia, odynophagia, persistent intractable vomiting, unexplained weight loss (>10%), or unexplained iron deficiency anemia.
- **Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage**: Acute hematemesis, melena, or hemodynamic collapse secondary to suspected peptic ulcer disease, esophageal varices, or Mallory-Weiss tears.
- **Disease Surveillance**: Monitoring Barrett's esophagus (with Seattle biopsy protocol), chronic atrophic gastritis, post-gastric cancer resections, or inherited polyposis syndromes (e.g., FAP).
- **Tissue Sampling & Near-Patient Testing**: Obtaining mucosal biopsies for histopathology or performing the **Rapid Urease Test (CLO test)** for _Helicobacter pylori_ detection.

#### 2. Therapeutic Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hemostasis**: Dual-modality endoscopic treatment of bleeding peptic ulcers (1:10,000 adrenaline injection paired with hemoclip placement or bipolar electrocautery) and **Esophageal Variceal Band Ligation (EVBL)**.
- **Stricture Dilatation & Stenting**: Through-the-scope (TTS) balloon dilatation of benign pyloric or esophageal strictures, and Self-Expanding Metal Stent (SEMS) deployment for malignant obstructive tumors.
- **Enteral Nutrition Access**: Insertion of a **Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG)** or Nasojejunal (NJ) feeding tube.
- **Mucosal Resection**: Endoscopic Mucosal Resection (EMR) and Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection (ESD) for early mucosal cancers or large adenomatous polyps.
- **Foreign Body Extraction**: Removal of swallowed bones, coins, or food bolus impactions using retrieval nets or grasper forceps.

#### 3. Contraindications

- **Absolute**: Suspected or proven hollow viscus perforation, uncooperative patient refusing valid consent, or severe cardiovascular/respiratory collapse.
- **Relative**: Recent myocardial infarction (<6 weeks), severe uncorrected coagulopathy (INR >1.5 or platelets <50,000/μL for high-risk procedures), large Zenker's diverticulum, or unstable cervical spine trauma.

#### Pre-Procedure Preparation

1. **Fasting Protocol**: Minimum 6 hours NPO for solid foods and 2 hours for clear fluids to prevent pulmonary regurgitation and aspiration pneumonia.
2. **Anticoagulation Management**: Continue aspirin for diagnostic biopsy; withhold Warfarin (target INR <1.5) or Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs like Dabigatran/Rivaroxaban) 24–48 hours prior to high-risk therapeutic interventions (polypectomy, stricture dilatation, PEG insertion).

#### Operative Steps

1. **Positioning & Monitoring**: Patient positioned in the **Left Lateral Decubitus position** with the neck slightly flexed. A protective plastic bite-block (mouth gag) is secured between teeth. Pulse oximetry, continuous ECG, and non-invasive blood pressure monitoring are established.
2. **Pharyngeal Anesthesia & Sedation**: Administration of 10% Lignocaine spray to the posterior pharynx. Intravenous Midazolam (1–5 mg) administered incrementally for conscious sedation.
3. **Intubation & Esophageal Inspection**: The endoscope tip is guided over the tongue base into the hypopharynx. Gentle downward pressure synchronized with patient swallowing passes the scope through the cricopharyngeus (15 cm). The thoracic esophagus is examined during steady advancement down to the GEJ (40 cm).
4. **Gastric Inspection & Retroversion**: Upon entering the stomach, gentle air/CO₂ insufflation expands gastric folds. Suction clears pooled fluid. The antrum and pylorus are visualized. The endoscope tip is then fully angled upwards and rotated 180° (**Retroversion / "J" Maneuver**) to inspect the gastric cardia, fundus, and lesser curvature around the scope shaft.
5. **Duodenal Inspection**: The scope is straightened, advanced through the pyloric ring into the duodenal bulb (D₁), and turned rightward into the second part (D₂) to inspect the mucosa and ampullary region.
6. **Withdrawal**: Detailed repeat examination performed upon scope withdrawal under active air suctioning to prevent post-procedure abdominal distension.

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Diagnostic gastroscopy is safe with a serious complication rate of <1 in 10,000 procedures.

- **Cardiopulmonary Complications (Most Common)**: Medication-induced respiratory depression, hypoxemia, or cardiac arrhythmias secondary to sedation in elderly/comorbid patients.
- **Pulmonary Aspiration Pneumonia**: Resulting from regurgitation of gastric contents, especially in un-fasted emergency patients or those with gastric outlet obstruction.
- **Iatrogenic Perforation**: Rare in diagnostic procedures (~0.01%), but increases to 1%–2% during therapeutic stricture dilatation or EMR/ESD. Most common sites are the hypopharynx/cricopharyngeus and diseased esophagus.
- **Hemorrhage**: Usually minor post-biopsy oozing; severe bleeding may follow therapeutic polypectomy or EMR.
- **Bacteraemia & Infection**: Transient bacteraemia occurs in ~4% of diagnostic procedures; high-risk procedures (PEG insertion, sclerotherapy) require prophylactic intravenous antibiotics (e.g., Co-amoxiclav 1.2 g IV).

### Dosage Rule (Sedatives & Local Anesthetics for Gastroscopy)

#### 1. Midazolam

- **Name**: Midazolam Hydrochloride
- **Mechanism of Action**: Short-acting benzodiazepine; enhances inhibitory GABA-A receptor activity in the central nervous system, inducing sedation, anxiolysis, and anterograde amnesia.
- **Dose**: 1 mg to 2.5 mg (titrated incrementally; reduced to 0.5–1 mg in elderly/frail patients; maximum dose 5 mg).
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous injection slowly over 2 minutes.
- **Frequency**: Single procedural dose.
- **Features**: First-line agent for conscious sedation during upper GI endoscopy.
- **Side Effects**: Respiratory depression, hypotension, prolonged sedation.
- **Reversal Agent**: **Flumazenil** (0.2 mg IV over 30 seconds).
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administered 2–3 minutes immediately prior to endoscope intubation.

#### 2. Lignocaine 10% Topical Spray

- **Name**: Lignocaine (Lidocaine) Hydrochloride 10% Topical Spray
- **Mechanism of Action**: Reversible blockade of voltage-gated sodium channels along pharyngeal nerve fibers, preventing nerve impulse conduction and abolishing the gag reflex.
- **Dose**: 2 to 4 metered sprays (10 mg per dose spray; maximum 40 mg total).
- **Dosage / Route**: Topical spray applied to posterior oropharynx.
- **Frequency**: Single pre-procedural application.
- **Side Effects**: Transient loss of pharyngeal sensation, risk of aspiration if oral intake resumes prematurely, rare systemic toxicity (seizures, arrhythmias).
- **When to Start / Stop**: Applied 3–5 minutes prior to scope passage; patient kept NPO for 1 hour post-procedure until pharyngeal gag reflex fully recovers.

### Clinical Pearl

Execution of the **Retroversion ("J") Maneuver** is an indispensable mandatory step during gastroscopy. By angling the scope tip 180° backwards inside the gastric body, the endoscopist visualizes the cardiac orifice, gastric fundus, and high lesser curvature—the anatomical "blind spots" where early gastric cancers, hiatus hernias, and gastric varices frequently hide undetected during forward viewing.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Administering routine prophylactic antibiotics to all patients undergoing standard diagnostic gastroscopy with mucosal biopsy.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: According to BSG guidelines, **routine diagnostic upper GI endoscopy with or without mucosal biopsy does NOT require antibiotic prophylaxis** (bacteraemia risk is minimal ~4%). Prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., Co-amoxiclav 1.2 g IV) are strictly indicated ONLY for therapeutic procedures with high infection risk, specifically **Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG) insertion**, fine-needle aspiration of cystic lesions, or endoscopy in patients with severe neutropenia.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: G-A-S-T-R-O (Core Endoscopic Guidelines)

- **G** – **G**ag reflex abolished using 10% Lignocaine topical pharyngeal spray.
- **A** – **A**larm / Red-flag symptoms (dysphagia, weight loss, anemia, age >55) warrant urgent OGD.
- **S** – **S**edation with IV Midazolam (1–5 mg); monitor oximetry to prevent respiratory depression.
- **T** – **T**herapeutic capabilities: EVBL for varices, dual therapy for bleeding peptic ulcers, PEG insertion.
- **R** – **R**etroversion ("J" maneuver) mandatory to inspect cardiac orifice and gastric fundus.
- **O** – **O**rgan landmarks by distance: Cricopharyngeus (15 cm), Aortic arch (25 cm), GEJ Z-line (40 cm).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Minimally invasive optical visualization of upper GI tract mucosa from pharynx to D₂ duodenum.
- **Key Distance Landmarks**: Cricopharyngeus (15 cm), Aorta impression (25 cm), Gastroesophageal junction / Z-line (40 cm).
- **Primary Diagnostic Indications**: Alarm symptoms (dysphagia, weight loss, anemia, vomiting, age >55) and acute upper GI bleeding.
- **Mandatory Technical Step**: Retroversion ("J" maneuver) to view gastric cardia and fundus.
- **Sedation & Local Anesthetic**: IV Midazolam (1–5 mg; reversed with Flumazenil) + Topical 10% Lignocaine spray.
