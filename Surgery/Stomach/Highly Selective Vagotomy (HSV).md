# Highly Selective Vagotomy (HSV)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Highly Selective Vagotomy (HSV)**, also known as **Parietal Cell Vagotomy (PCV)** or **Proximal Gastric Vagotomy (PGV)**, is an acid-reducing surgical procedure in which only the vagal nerve branches supplying the parietal cell-bearing mass (fundus and body) of the stomach are divided, while preserving the vagal innervation to the antrum and pylorus (nerves of Latarjet) as well as extragastric visceral branches (coeliac and hepatic branches).

---

### Applied Anatomy & Indications [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Applied Surgical Anatomy

- **Anterior and Posterior Nerves of Latarjet**: Main gastric nerve trunks running along the lesser curvature.
- **Crow's Foot**: The terminal branches of the nerves of Latarjet supplying the antrum and pyloric sphincter, located **5–7 cm proximal to the pylorus**. Preserving these maintains normal antral grinding motility and pyloric relaxation, eliminating the need for a drainage procedure.
- **Criminal Nerve of Grassi**: The first branch of the posterior vagal trunk arising near the gastroesophageal junction, supplying the gastric fundus. Failure to divide this nerve is the leading cause of ulcer recurrence post-HSV.

#### 2. Indications

- Intractable or chronic **duodenal ulcer disease** refractory to medical therapy (_H. pylori_ eradication + PPIs).
- **Johnson Type II and Type III gastric ulcers** (associated with acid hypersecretion).

---

### Surgical Technique & Procedure Steps [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Identification of Latarjet Nerves**: The anterior and posterior main vagal trunks and their continuation as the nerves of Latarjet along the lesser curvature are identified.
2. **Preservation of Crow's Foot**: Dissection along the lesser curvature begins **5–7 cm proximal to the pylorus**, ensuring all antral and pyloric branches ("crow's foot") remain intact.
3. **Division of Gastric Branches**: All individual branches going from the lesser curvature to the anterior and posterior walls of the gastric body and fundus are sequentially ligated and divided.
4. **Esophageal Skeletonization**: The distal **5–7 cm of the abdominal esophagus** is completely skeletonized by dividing all nerve fibers and tissue connections, ensuring complete transection of the **criminal nerve of Grassi**.
5. **No Drainage Procedure Needed**: Because the pylorus and antrum remain fully innervated, **pyloroplasty or gastroenterostomy is NOT performed**.

### Complications & Sequelae [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Recurrent Ulceration (2%–10%)**: Most common long-term problem; caused by incomplete vagal denervation (e.g., failure to ligate the criminal nerve of Grassi).
- **Small Stomach Syndrome**: Early satiety caused by loss of vagally mediated **receptive relaxation** of the gastric fundus; resolves spontaneously over time.
- **Lesser Curve Necrosis**: Rare ischemic complication resulting from extensive devascularization of the lesser curvature during nerve division.
- **Postvagotomy Diarrhea & Dumping**: Exceptionally rare (<1%–2%) compared to truncal vagotomy because intestinal and biliary innervation is preserved.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Unlike truncal vagotomy, **Highly Selective Vagotomy never requires a gastric drainage procedure** (such as a Heineke-Mikulicz pyloroplasty or gastroenterostomy). Because the terminal "crow's foot" branches of the nerves of Latarjet are preserved, normal antral peristalsis and pyloric sphincter relaxation remain completely intact.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Omitting esophageal skeletonization during HSV because all branches along the lesser curvature have been cut.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Failure to skeletonize the distal 5–7 cm of the abdominal esophagus risks missing the **criminal nerve of Grassi** (a branch of the posterior vagal trunk), leading to persistent acid secretion and high rates of recurrent ulceration.

- **H** – **H**ighest recurrence rate (2%–10%) due to missed criminal nerve of Grassi.

- **V** – **V**ery low complications (<5% dumping/diarrhea) and lowest mortality (<0.2%).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Selective denervation of the parietal cell mass (body/fundus) sparing antral/pyloric and extragastric branches.
- **Key Anatomical Landmark**: Preserves the **crow's foot** (5–7 cm from pylorus) to maintain normal pyloric sphincter relaxation.
- **Critical Nerve**: Must divide the **criminal nerve of Grassi** on the distal esophagus to prevent ulcer recurrence.
- **Major Advantage**: No gastric drainage procedure needed; lowest mortality (<0.2%) and lowest risk of postvagotomy dumping/diarrhea (<5%).
- **Major Disadvantage**: Highest ulcer recurrence rate (2%–10%) among all vagotomy techniques.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Highly selective vagotomy (parietal cell vagotomy) is an acid-reducing surgical procedure that denervates only the parietal cell-bearing fundus and body of the stomach while preserving the crow's foot innervation to the antrum and pylorus, eliminating the need for a drainage procedure while achieving the lowest postoperative complication rate among vagotomies._
