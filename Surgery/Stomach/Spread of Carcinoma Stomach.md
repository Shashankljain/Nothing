# Spread of Carcinoma Stomach

### Definition

**Carcinoma of the stomach** (gastric adenocarcinoma) spreads via five distinct anatomical routes: direct extension, lymphatic dissemination, hematogenous metastasis, transcoelomic (peritoneal) seeding, and endoluminal / submucosal intramural spread.

---

### Routes of Spread [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Direct Extension

- Infiltrates through the layers of the gastric wall (mucosa → submucosa → muscularis propria → subserosa → serosa).
- Once the serosa is breached, it directly invades adjacent abdominal organs:
    - _Anterior wall tumors_: Invade the left lobe of the liver and anterior abdominal wall.
    - _Posterior wall tumors_: Invade the pancreas, lesser sac, transverse mesocolon, splenic artery, and spleen.
    - _Antral / Pyloric tumors_: Invade the duodenum and pancreas.
    - _Cardiac / GEJ tumors_: Invade the lower esophagus and diaphragm.

#### 2. Lymphatic Dissemination [⭐ High-Yield]

- Primary route of regional metastatic spread, following the Japanese Gastric Cancer Association (JGCA) nodal stations (Stations 1 to 16):
    - **N1 Tier (Stations 1–6)**: Perigastric nodes along lesser/greater curvatures and pyloric regions.
    - **N2 Tier (Stations 7–11)**: Extra-perigastric nodes along major coeliac arterial trunks (left gastric, common hepatic, coeliac axis, splenic hilum/artery).
- **Named Metastatic Signposts**:
    - **Virchow's Node (Troisier's Sign)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Enlargement of the left supraclavicular lymph node due to metastasis via the thoracic duct.
    - **Irish Node**: Enlargement of the left anterior axillary lymph node.

#### 3. Hematogenous (Blood-Borne) Metastasis

- Tumor cells enter the portal venous drainage to embolize primarily to the **liver** (most common site of distant solid organ metastasis).
- Systemic hematogenous spread to lungs, bones, brain, and adrenal glands occurs late via systemic venous channels.

#### 4. Transcoelomic (Peritoneal) Seeding [⭐ High-Yield]

- Occurs once the primary tumor breaches the serosal surface, shedding malignant cells into the peritoneal cavity:
    - **Krukenberg Tumor** [⭐ High-Yield]: Bilateral (or unilateral) ovarian metastases resulting from transcoelomic shedding of signet-ring cell gastric adenocarcinoma.
    - **Sister Mary Joseph's Nodule**: Metastatic nodule at the umbilicus due to transcoelomic tracking along the falciform ligament / round ligament.
    - **Blumer's Shelf**: Palpable rigid mass in the rectovesical or rectouterine (pouch of Douglas) pouch on digital rectal / vaginal examination.
    - **Malignant Ascites**: Diffuse peritoneal seeding causing severe proteinaceous abdominal fluid accumulation.

#### 5. Intramural / Submucosal Spread

- Infiltration along the submucosal lymphatics extending well beyond the visible gross tumor margin (up to 5 cm proximally into the esophagus and distally into the duodenum), mandating wide surgical resection margins.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Direct Extension**: Invades pancreas, liver, transverse colon, and esophagus.
- **Lymphatic Spread**: Perigastric (N1) → Coeliac trunks (N2); Virchow's node (left supraclavicular) and Irish node (left anterior axillary).
- **Transcoelomic Signs**: Krukenberg tumor (ovary), Sister Mary Joseph's nodule (umbilicus), Blumer's shelf (pelvic pouch).
- **Hematogenous**: Liver is the most common distant organ site.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Carcinoma of the stomach metastasizes through five distinct anatomical routes—direct extension into adjacent organs, lymphatic spread to perigastric and coeliac nodes (Virchow's node), transcoelomic seeding to the ovaries (Krukenberg tumor) and umbilicus (Sister Mary Joseph's nodule), hematogenous spread to the liver, and intramural submucosal lymphatic tracking._
