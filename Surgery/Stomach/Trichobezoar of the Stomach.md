# Trichobezoar of the Stomach

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **trichobezoar** (hairball) is an unusual, firm, compact mass of ingested, undigested hair trapped within the gastrointestinal tract, predominantly occupying the stomach lumen. It is virtually exclusively encountered in young females suffering from psychiatric disorders, specifically **trichotillomania** (compulsive hair pulling) and **trichophagia**(compulsive hair swallowing).

When a gastric trichobezoar extends past the pylorus into the duodenum, jejunum, or terminal ileum as a long tail of hair, it is designated as **Rapunzel Syndrome**.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Primary Behavioral & Psychiatric Etiologies

- **Trichotillomania & Trichophagia**: Recurrent, uncontrollable urge to pull out one's own hair (scalp, eyebrows, eyelashes) followed by swallowing it.
- **Associated Psychiatric Conditions**: Depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), severe anxiety, emotional neglect, low IQ, or pica.
- **Demographics**: Predominantly affects young females and adolescent girls (90% of cases occur in females <20 years of age).

#### 2. Gastric Anatomical & Physiological Factors

- **Indigestibility of Hair**: Keratinized hair fibers resist enzymatic hydrolysis by gastric hydrochloric acid and pepsin.
- **Mucosal Trapping**: Hair becomes trapped within the gastric mucosal folds (rugae) due to its high friction coefficient and slippery texture.
- **Peristaltic Compaction**: Continuous gastric peristaltic churning tangles the swallowed hair into a dense, solid, globular mass that grows continuously as more hair is ingested.

---

### Classification of Gastrointestinal Bezoars [⭐ High-Yield]

Bezoars are classified according to their primary constituent material:

|Type of Bezoar|Primary Constituent Material|Common Clinical Setting / Patient Population [⭐ High-Yield]|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Trichobezoar** [⭐ High-Yield]|Ingested hair / hair fibers|Young females with trichotillomania and trichophagia|
|**Phytobezoar**|Undigested vegetable / fruit fibers (e.g., persimmons, celery)|Post-gastrectomy, high-fiber diet, gastric stasis, hypochlorhydria|
|**Pharmacobezoar**|Ingested, undigested medication tablets / bulk laxatives|Overdose or slowed gut motility (e.g., enteric-coated aspirin, sucralfate)|
|**Lactobezoar**|Undigested milk curds (concentrated calcium-caseinate)|Premature neonates fed high-calorie / high-density formula milk|

- **Physical Evolution**: As ingested hair accumulates, gastric acid denatures the hair, turning it **pitch-black** regardless of the patient's original hair color.
- **Debris Entrapment**: The dense meshwork of hair traps decomposing food particles, mucus, and gas-producing bacteria, resulting in a characteristic **foul odor (halitosis)**.
- **Ischemic & Obstructive Sequelae**: As the mass expands to fill the entire gastric cavity (forming a perfect "J-shaped" cast of the stomach), it exerts continuous pressure on the gastric mucosa, causing pressure necrosis, ulceration, bleeding, and mechanical gastric outlet obstruction.
- **Rapunzel Syndrome Cascade**: A long tail of hair slips through the pylorus into the duodenum and jejunum, acting as a lead point for intussusception, mechanical bowel obstruction, or pressure perforation of the intestinal wall.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Epigastric Pain & Discomfort**: Dull, aching upper abdominal pain secondary to gastric distension and mucosal irritation.
- **Nausea & Vomiting**: Postprandial non-bilious vomiting resulting from gastric outlet obstruction.
- **Early Satiety & Anorexia**: Inability to eat normal meal volumes due to loss of gastric reservoir capacity.
- **Halitosis**: Extremely foul, putrid breath odor due to trapped decomposing organic material within the hairball.
- **Weight Loss & Malnutrition**: Progressive weight loss, cachexia, and protein-losing enteropathy.

#### Signs

- **Palpable Abdominal Mass**: A hard, smooth, non-tender, mobile epigastric mass that conforms to the J-shape of the stomach and can often be indented on deep pressure.
- **Alopecia**: Patchy or diffuse hair loss (alopecia areata-like) on the scalp, eyebrows, or eyelashes.
- **Pallor**: Microcytic hypochromic anemia secondary to chronic occult mucosal bleeding or iron malabsorption.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Diagnostic Accuracy**: Approaching **100%**.
- **Findings**: Direct visual confirmation of a large, dark-brown or black, foul-smelling, matted mass of hair occupying the gastric lumen. Allows assessment of underlying mucosal ulceration.

#### 2. Contrast-Enhanced Computed Tomography (CECT) Abdomen

- **Findings**: Demonstrates a well-defined, non-enhancing intraluminal gastric mass containing mottled gas bubbles ("honeycomb" or "concentric ring" appearance) that is completely separated from the gastric wall by a thin rim of fluid or air.
- **Value**: Highly effective for identifying duodenal or jejunal extension (**Rapunzel Syndrome**) and ruling out multi-site bowel obstruction.

#### 3. Barium Upper GI Contrast Study

- **Findings**: Shows a large, smooth, mobile intraluminal filling defect within the stomach, outlined by a thin band of barium contrast ("floating mass").

#### 4. Laboratory Studies

- **Complete Blood Count**: Microcytic hypochromic anemia (chronic occult blood loss).
- **Serum Proteins**: Hypoalbuminemia and hypoproteinemia secondary to protein-losing gastroenteropathy.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Surgical or endoscopic removal must always be paired with mandatory psychiatric intervention.

#### 1. Endoscopic Removal (For Small Bezoars)

- Indicated strictly for small, soft, or fragmentable trichobezoars.
- **Technique**: Fragmentation using mechanical lithotripters, endoclips, jet sprays, or electrocautery snares, followed by per-oral extraction. _(Note: Chemical dissolution using papain, acetylcysteine, or Coca-Cola is ineffective for trichobezoars due to the chemical stability of hair keratin)_.

#### 2. Surgical Removal (Gold Standard for Large Bezoars & Rapunzel Syndrome) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Large, dense trichobezoars filling the stomach or cases with intestinal extension (Rapunzel Syndrome).
- **Operative Steps**:
    1. Exploratory laparotomy via a upper midline incision (or laparoscopic approach).
    2. Anterior longitudinal gastrotomy between the lesser and greater curvatures.
    3. Extraction of the entire, intact J-shaped hair mass in a protective bag to prevent wound contamination.
    4. **Mandatory Intestinal Exploration**: Careful palpation of the entire small bowel from the ligament of Treitz to the ileocecal valve to locate and extract any broken-off satellite trichobezoars.
    5. Closure of gastrotomy in two layers (inner full-thickness, outer seromuscular).

#### 3. Psychiatric Evaluation & Long-Term Follow-up [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mandatory Psychiatric Referral**: Essential step to treat the underlying trichotillomania and trichophagia.
- **Modalities**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), habit-reversal training, and Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs).
- **Prevention**: Long-term psychiatric surveillance is mandatory to prevent recurrent hair ingestion and re-formation of the bezoar.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Gastric Ulceration & Hemorrhage**: Mucosal pressure erosion causing hematemesis or melena.
- **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO)**: Impaction of the mass into the pyloric channel.
- **Gastric Wall Necrosis & Perforation**: Transmural pressure ischemia leading to acute peritonitis.
- **Small Bowel Obstruction & Intussusception**: Caused by trailing hair tails in Rapunzel Syndrome.
- **Severe Malnutrition & Protein-Losing Enteropathy**.

---

### Clinical Pearl

During surgical gastrotomy for a trichobezoar, **never limit the operation to simply removing the gastric hairball**. Always perform a thorough manual run of the entire small intestine from the duodenojejunal flexure to the ileocecal valve; failure to extract a trailing satellite hairball in the distal ileum is the most common cause of early postoperative small bowel obstruction.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Attempting chemical dissolution (e.g., Coca-Cola lavage or papain enzymatic digestion) for a gastric trichobezoar.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Chemical dissolution is effective for **phytobezoars** (vegetable matter). **Trichobezoars (hairballs) consist of densely matted keratin that is completely resistant to chemical digestion**; they mandate endoscopic fragmentation (if small) or open/laparoscopic surgical extraction (if large).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Conglomerate mass of ingested hair trapped in the stomach.
- **Patient Profile**: Almost exclusively young females with trichotillomania and trichophagia.
- **Rapunzel Syndrome**: Extension of the gastric hairball tail past the pylorus into the small intestine.
- **Gold Standard Diagnosis**: Upper GI Endoscopy showing a black, foul-smelling, matted hair mass.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Open or laparoscopic anterior gastrotomy for large masses + mandatory psychiatric consultation.
