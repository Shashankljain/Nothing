# Bleeding Peptic Ulcer

### Definition

A **bleeding peptic ulcer** is an upper gastrointestinal hemorrhagic emergency resulting from acid-pepsin mucosal breakdown extending beyond the muscularis mucosae to erode underlying submucosal or muscular blood vessels (most commonly the gastroduodenal artery or left gastric artery).

---

### High-Yield Clinical Features & Management [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Epidemiology & Etiology**: Represents the single most common cause of upper gastrointestinal bleeding (~60%), driven predominantly by _Helicobacter pylori_ infection or chronic NSAID ingestion.
- **Implicated Vessels**: Posterior duodenal ulcers erode into the **gastroduodenal artery** (most common source of massive upper GI hemorrhage), whereas lesser curvature gastric ulcers erode into the **left gastric artery**.
- **Clinical Presentation**: Manifests acutely as **hematemesis** (bright red blood or "coffee-ground" vomitus) and/or **melena** (black, tarry stools per rectum), accompanied by signs of hypovolemic shock in severe cases.
- **First-Line Endoscopic Therapy**: Emergency upper GI endoscopy within 24 hours provides direct visual diagnosis and **dual-modality hemostasis** (1:10,000 adrenaline injection combined with hemoclips or thermal coagulation), followed by 72 hours of continuous IV proton pump inhibitor (PPI) infusion.
- **Surgical / Interventional Management**: Refractory bleeding failing endoscopic intervention mandates transcatheter arterial angioembolization or emergency surgery via duodenotomy/gastrotomy with **under-running suture ligation of the bleeding vessel**.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Most Common Cause**: Accounts for ~60% of all upper GI hemorrhage cases.
- **Anatomical Arteries**: Gastroduodenal artery (posterior D₁ ulcer) and Left gastric artery (gastric ulcer).
- **Gold Standard Management**: Dual endoscopic hemostasis (adrenaline injection + hemoclip/cautery) + 72-hour IV PPI infusion.
- **Refractory Rescue**: Angioembolization or surgical duodenotomy/gastrotomy with under-running vessel ligation.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A bleeding peptic ulcer is a life-threatening surgical emergency caused by acid-peptic erosion into a major underlying vessel (gastroduodenal or left gastric artery), presenting with hematemesis and melena, diagnosed and treated gold-standardly via dual endoscopic hemostasis, and managed surgically with under-running vessel ligation when endoscopic control fails._
