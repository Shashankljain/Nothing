# Perforated Peptic Ulcer

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **perforated peptic ulcer** is a life-threatening surgical emergency characterized by a full-thickness, transmural erosion in the gastric or duodenal wall, causing spillage of caustic acid-peptic contents and food debris into the peritoneal cavity, resulting sequentially in chemical and bacterial peritonitis.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)**: Most common causative factor in modern practice, especially among elderly females taking chronic analgesics or corticosteroids.
- **_Helicobacter pylori_ Infection**: Present in 70%–90% of duodenal ulcer perforations and 50%–60% of gastric ulcer perforations.
- **Cigarette Smoking & Alcohol Consumption**: Potent independent risk factors that impair mucosal blood flow, suppress bicarbonate secretion, and delay ulcer healing.
- **Stress Ulceration**: Occurs in critically ill patients, severe trauma, major burns (**Curling's ulcer**), or severe head injury (**Cushing's ulcer**).

---

### Classification

#### 1. By Anatomical Location

- **Anterior Ulcer Perforation (Most Common)**: Occurs on the anterior wall of the first part of the duodenum (D₁) or gastric antrum; spills directly into the greater peritoneal sac causing acute diffuse peritonitis.
- **Posterior Ulcer Perforation (Rare)**: Perforates posteriorly into the retroperitoneum or lesser sac; may present with gas behind the right kidney or track down the right paracolic gutter (**Valentino's syndrome**).

#### 2. By Size of Perforation

- **Small Perforation**: <0.5 cm in diameter.
- **Moderate Perforation**: 0.5 cm to 2.0 cm in diameter (standard size manageable via omental patch repair).
- **Giant / Massive Perforation**: >2.0 cm in diameter; simple suturing/omental patch fails, requiring resection or tube duodenostomy.

- **Phase 1: Chemical Peritonitis (0–6 Hours)**: Sudden breach of the gastric or duodenal wall releases acid and pepsin into the peritoneal cavity. Chemical irritation causes extreme, agonizing abdominal pain, reflex muscle spasm (**board-like rigidity**), and massive third-space fluid sequestration leading to hypovolemic shock.
- **Phase 2: Illusion Phase / Self-Limitation (6–12 Hours)**: Peritoneal exudate dilutes the acidic fluid. Pain may transiently ease, but fluid continues tracking down the right paracolic gutter to the right iliac fossa.
- **Phase 3: Bacterial Peritonitis & Sepsis (>12 Hours)**: Bacterial contamination supervenes, progressing rapidly to purulent peritonitis, paralytic ileus, multi-organ dysfunction, and septic shock.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Sudden-Onset Excruciating Pain**: Classical presentation is a sudden, explosive, severe generalized abdominal pain ("like a dagger blow").
- **Antecedent History**: History of chronic dyspepsia, epigastric pain, or heavy NSAID/corticosteroid ingestion.
- **Nausea & Vomiting**: Early reflex vomiting followed by absolute constipation as paralytic ileus develops.

#### Signs

- **General Signs**: Patient lies **completely still**, afraid to breathe or move; cold, clammy skin, tachycardia, and progressive hypotension.
- **Abdominal Signs**:
    - **Lack of Abdominal Respiration**: The abdominal wall remains immobile during respiration (**Hamilton Bailey's Sign**).
    - **Board-like Muscle Rigidity**: Involuntary, intense contraction of abdominal wall muscles ("wooden abdomen").
    - **Generalized Tenderness & Rebound Tenderness**: Present across all four quadrants.
    - **Obliteration of Liver Dullness**: Percussion over the right lower rib cage reveals hyperresonance due to subdiaphragmatic free gas.
    - **Silent Abdomen**: Total absence of bowel sounds on auscultation.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Imaging Studies

- **Erect Chest Radiograph (CXR) [Investigation of Choice / Initial Screening]**:
    - Demonstrates **subdiaphragmatic free gas (pneumoperitoneum)** under the right hemidiaphragm in >50%–75% of cases. (Patient must remain upright for 10 minutes prior to X-ray).
    - **Rigler's Sign**: Double wall sign on plain abdominal X-ray (gas visualizing both inner and outer lumen surfaces of bowel wall).
- **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT) Abdomen**: Most sensitive diagnostic modality; visualizes minute quantities of free intraperitoneal air, fluid collections, and exact site of perforation.

#### 2. Laboratory Investigations

- **Serum Amylase**: Mildly elevated due to peritoneal absorption of luminal amylase; must be distinguished from acute pancreatitis (levels in perforation rarely exceed 3 times normal).
- **Complete Blood Count**: Marked leukocytosis with neutrophilia.
- **Renal Function Tests & Electrolytes**: Elevated blood urea and creatinine secondary to severe dehydration (prerenal azotemia).

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Management requires rapid preoperative resuscitation followed by emergency operative repair.

#### 1. Initial Emergency Resuscitation (Preoperative Preparation)

- **NPO & Nasogastric Decompression**: Keep strictly Nil Per Os; insert a wide-bore Ryle's nasogastric tube for continuous suction to prevent further peritoneal contamination.
- **Aggressive Intravenous Fluid Resuscitation**: Rapid infusion of isotonic crystalloids (**0.9% Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate**) via two large-bore IV cannulae to restore intravascular volume.
- **Empirical Intravenous Antibiotics**: Broad-spectrum coverage against Gram-negative bacilli and anaerobes (e.g., **Ceftriaxone 1 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV**).
- **Analgesia & Monitoring**: Titrated IV opioids (Tramadol / Morphine) once diagnosis is established; insert Foley catheter to monitor urine output (target >0.5 mL/kg/hour).

- **Operative Approach**: Upper midline laparotomy or emergency laparoscopic repair.
- **Peritoneal Toilet (Most Critical Step)**: Copious peritoneal lavage using 4–6 liters of warm sterile 0.9% Normal Saline to remove all purulent exudate, food particles, and bilious fluid.
- **Surgical Repair Techniques**:
    - **Graham's Omental Patch Repair (Gold Standard)**:
        1. Interrupted full-thickness sutures (2-0 or 3-0 Silk/Vicryl) are placed across the perforation site parallel to the long axis of the duodenum without tying.
        2. A healthy, well-vascularized pedicled plug of greater omentum is laid over the perforation defect.
        3. Sutures are tied gently over the omental tongue to create an airtight seal without strangulating omental blood vessels.
    - **Primary Suture Closure with Omental Onlay (Cellan-Jones Repair)**: Ulcer closed transversely with interrupted sutures, and omentum tied over the suture line.
    - **Gastric Ulcer Biopsy Rule [⭐ High-Yield]**: All perforated gastric ulcers **must be edge-biopsied** prior to closure to exclude ulcerated gastric adenocarcinoma.
    - **Management of Giant Perforations (>2 cm)**: Distal gastrectomy with Roux-en-Y reconstruction or tube duodenostomy with feeding jejunostomy.

#### 3. Postoperative Care & _H. pylori_ Eradication

- Continue NG suction and IV fluids until bowel function returns.
- IV Proton Pump Inhibitor (**Pantoprazole 40 mg IV BD**).
- **Mandatory _H. pylori_ Eradication**: Initiate oral 14-day triple therapy once oral feeding resumes to prevent ulcer recurrence.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Intra-abdominal & Pelvic Abscesses**: Subphrenic or pelvic fluid collections requiring USG/CT-guided drainage.
- **Wound Infection & Abdominal Dehisence (Burst Abdomen)**: High incidence due to rampant peritoneal contamination.
- **Enterocutaneous / Duodenal Fistula**: Caused by breakdown of the omental patch suture line.
- **Systemic Sepsis, ARDS, & Multi-Organ Failure**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Oncological & Antisecretory Drugs)

#### 1. Pantoprazole

- **Name**: Pantoprazole Sodium
- **Mechanism of Action**: Irreversibly inhibits H⁺/K⁺ ATPase proton pump in parietal cells, suppressing gastric acid secretion.
- **Dose**: 40 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: IV injection / Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD)
- **Features**: First-line antisecretory drug to promote ulcer healing postoperatively.
- **Side Effects**: Headache, diarrhea, thrombophlebitis at IV site.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start IV immediately on admission; step down to oral once eating; continue for 4–8 weeks.

#### 2. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Generates nitro-reduction toxic intermediates that disrupt bacterial DNA in anaerobic organisms.
- **Dose**: 500 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion / Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Every 8 hours (TDS)
- **Side Effects**: Metallic taste, nausea, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start preoperatively; continue for 5–7 days postoperatively.

---

### Clinical Pearl

During Graham's omental patch repair for a perforated peptic ulcer, **never tie the sutures over the omental patch excessively tight**. Tying sutures with excessive force cuts through fragile, edematous ulcerated duodenal tissue and strangulates the omental blood supply, predisposing the patch to necrosis and postoperative duodenal fistulization.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Mistaking Valentino's syndrome (perforated duodenal ulcer) for acute appendicitis.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Gastric fluid from a perforated anterior duodenal ulcer can track down the right paracolic gutter to collect in the right iliac fossa. While both conditions present with RIF tenderness, **perforated duodenal ulcer exhibits maximum tenderness and board-like rigidity in the right hypochondrium alongside subdiaphragmatic free gas on CXR**.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: P-E-R-F-O-R-A-T-E

- **P** – **P**neumoperitoneum (crescentic subdiaphragmatic free gas on erect CXR).
- **E** – **E**xplosive sudden onset of severe generalized abdominal pain.
- **R** – **R**igidity (board-like "wooden" abdominal rigidity).
- **F** – **F**luid resuscitation + NG tube decompression + IV broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- **O** – **O**mental patch repair (Graham's repair) is the gold-standard surgical treatment.
- **R** – **R**igler's sign (double wall sign on plain abdominal X-ray).
- **A** – **A**moxicillin + Clarithromycin + PPI mandatory postoperatively for _H. pylori_ eradication.
- **T** – **T**oilet of peritoneal cavity (lavage with warm Normal Saline) is the crucial surgical step.
- **E** – **E**dge biopsy mandatory for all perforated gastric ulcers to rule out malignancy.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Surgical emergency due to transmural ulcer erosion causing chemical followed by bacterial peritonitis.
- **Classic Physical Sign**: Board-like abdominal rigidity with loss of liver dullness and absent bowel sounds.
- **Diagnostic Gold Standard**: Erect Chest Radiograph showing subdiaphragmatic free gas under the right hemidiaphragm.
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: Emergency peritoneal lavage + **Graham's Omental Patch Repair** (mandatory edge biopsy for gastric ulcers).
- **Postoperative Care**: IV Pantoprazole + 14-day oral _H. pylori_ triple eradication therapy.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A perforated peptic ulcer is a life-threatening acute abdominal emergency caused by transmural breach of the gastric or duodenal mucosa, presenting with sudden explosive pain, board-like abdominal rigidity, and subdiaphragmatic free gas on erect chest radiography, requiring urgent fluid resuscitation and emergency omental patch repair with thorough peritoneal lavage._
