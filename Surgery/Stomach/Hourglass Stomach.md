# Hourglass Stomach

### Definition

An **hourglass stomach** is an acquired structural deformity of the stomach characterized by a tight, central transverse constriction that divides the gastric lumen into two distinct communicating chambers (an upper proximal pouch and a lower distal pouch), connected by a narrow central channel.

---

### Etiology & Pathophysiology

- **Primary Cause**: Most commonly results as a late complication of a chronic, deep, fibrosing **lesser curvature gastric ulcer** (Johnson Type I gastric ulcer).
- **Mechanism**: As the chronic lesser curve ulcer undergoes repeated cycles of destruction, healing, and cicatrization, progressive fibrous tissue contraction pulls the greater curvature medially toward the lesser curvature, forming a permanent, tight transverse fibrous band that bilobes the stomach.
- **Secondary / Rare Causes**:
    - Extrinsic compression by tight abdominal bands or adhesions.
    - Rare congenital gastric duplications or true congenital muscular rings.
    - Functional spastic contraction (pseudo-hourglass stomach) secondary to acute peptic ulceration.

---

### Clinical Features

- **Symptoms**: Long history of peptic ulcer disease; early satiety; postprandial epigastric fullness and pain; nausea and non-bilious vomiting after meals as the upper pouch distends; progressive weight loss.
- **Diagnosis**:
    - **Barium Meal Contrast Radiography**: Classic diagnostic imaging showing two distinct contrast-filled gastric pouches connected by a narrow central communicates ("hourglass appearance").
    - **Upper GI Endoscopy**: Confirms the central lumen narrowing, evaluates the chronic lesser curve ulcer, and provides **mandatory biopsies (6–10 cores)** to exclude ulcerated gastric adenocarcinoma masquerading as a benign stricture.

---

### Management

- **Surgical Options**:
    - **Partial / Distal Gastrectomy (Billroth I or Billroth II)**: Procedure of choice; resects the diseased segment containing the chronic ulcer and stricture.
    - **Gastroenterostomy**: Bypass procedure performed in frail or high-surgical-risk patients.
    - **Gastroplasty (Sleeve resection)**: Rare option to excise the fibrous ring and reconstruct the gastric lumen.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: Cicatricial contraction of a chronic lesser curvature gastric ulcer.
- **Pathology**: Transverse fibrous band bilobing the stomach into upper and lower pouches.
- **Investigation**: Barium meal shows two pouches connected by a narrow channel; Endoscopy + mandatory biopsy rules out carcinoma.
- **Treatment**: Distal/Partial Gastrectomy (procedure of choice).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_An hourglass stomach is a structural gastric deformity characterized by a transverse fibrous constriction that bilobes the gastric lumen into upper and lower pouches, caused predominantly by cicatricial contraction of a chronic lesser curvature gastric ulcer and treated definitively by partial gastrectomy._
