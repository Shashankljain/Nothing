# Gynecomastia

### Definition

**Gynecomastia** is defined as a benign enlargement of the male breast tissue caused by proliferation of glandular ductal tissue and stroma, resulting from an altered balance between active circulating estrogens and androgens (increased estrogen to androgen ratio).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     ETIOLOGY OF GYNECOMASTIA
                                 |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
        |                                                 |
  Physiological (Trimodal)                            Pathological
  - Neonatal (maternal estrogens)                   - Drug-Induced (DISCKO)
  - Pubertal (12–15 years)                          - Hypogonadism (Klinefelter's)
  - Senile / Elderly (>50 years)                    - Liver Cirrhosis & CKD
                                                    - Neoplasms (Testicular, Adrenal)
```

#### 1. Physiological Causes (Trimodal Distribution) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Neonatal**: Seen in up to 60%–90% of newborns due to transplacental transfer of maternal estrogens; regresses spontaneously within a few weeks.
- **Pubertal**: Occurs in up to 60% of boys aged 12–15 years due to transient imbalance in testosterone and estrogen production; usually resolves spontaneously within 1 to 2 years.
- **Senile / Elderly**: Seen in aging males (>50–60 years) due to age-related decline in testicular Leydig cell testosterone secretion combined with increased peripheral aromatization of adrenal androgens in adipose tissue.

#### 2. Pathological Causes [⭐ High-Yield]

##### A. Drug-Induced Gynecomastia (Mnemonic: DISCKO) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **D** – **Digoxin** (inhibits sodium-potassium pump, exhibits estrogen-like activity).
- **I** – **Isoniazid** (antitubercular agent).
- **S** – **Spironolactone** (blocks androgen receptors & inhibits testosterone synthesis), **Steroids** (anabolic steroids).
- **C** – **Cimetidine** (H₂-receptor antagonist with anti-androgenic effect).
- **K** – **Ketoconazole** (inhibits cytochrome P450 enzymes involved in testosterone biosynthesis).
- **O** – **Oestrogens** / Exogenous hormones.
- _Other Drugs_: Calcium channel blockers, Metoclopramide, Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine, Methyldopa, Alcohol, and Marijuana.

##### B. Primary / Secondary Hypogonadism & Testicular Failure

- **Klinefelter's Syndrome (47,XXY)**: Characterized by small, firm testes, elevated gonadotropins, low testosterone, severe gynecomastia, and a significantly **increased risk of male breast cancer**.
- **Testicular Failure**: Lepromatous orchitis, post-mumps orchitis, viral orchitis, testicular trauma, or cryptorchidism.

##### C. Systemic & Endocrine Disorders

- **Hepatic Cirrhosis**: Impaired hepatic clearance of circulating estrogens and increased hepatic synthesis of Sex Hormone-Binding Globulin (SHBG), which binds testosterone preferentially over estrogen.
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**: Uremia-induced Leydig cell failure leading to low testosterone levels.
- **Hyperthyroidism**: Increased serum SHBG and accelerated peripheral aromatization.

##### D. Neoplasms & Paraneoplastic Conditions [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Testicular Tumors**: Leydig cell or Sertoli cell tumors (secrete estrogens directly) and non-seminomatous germ cell tumors (NSGCT / Choriocarcinoma producing human chorionic gonadotropin [hCG], which stimulates Leydig cell estrogen secretion).
- **Adrenocortical Tumors**: Estrogen-secreting adrenal adenomas/carcinomas.
- **Ectopic hCG Secretion**: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), Renal cell carcinoma (RCC), and Bronchogenic carcinoma.

##### E. Idiopathic

- Accounts for up to 25%–30% of clinical cases after exhaustive evaluation.

---

### Classification (Simon's Classification of Gynecomastia) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Grade I**: Minor breast enlargement without skin redundancy.
- **Grade IIA**: Moderate breast enlargement without skin redundancy.
- **Grade IIB**: Moderate breast enlargement with minor skin redundancy.
- **Grade III**: Severe breast enlargement with marked skin redundancy (resembling a ptotic female breast).

- **Hormonal Mechanism**: Unopposed estrogen action stimulates ductal epithelial hyperplasia, ductal elongation and branching, stromal edema, and increased vascularity (**Early Florid Phase <6–12 months**).
- **Fibrotic Progression**: Over time (>12 months), epithelial proliferation subsides, giving way to dense stromal hyalinization and irreversible fibrosis (**Late Fibrotic Phase >1 year**), which is largely unresponsive to medical therapy.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Breast Enlargement**: Unilateral or bilateral enlargement of the male breast.
- **Local Discomfort**: Dull ache or tenderness beneath the nipple-areola complex, especially during the early florid phase.
- **Psychological Distress**: Significant embarrassment, anxiety, or social isolation in young males and adolescents.

#### Signs

- **Inspection**: Symmetrical or asymmetrical enlargement of the breast mound with nipple-areola prominent.
- **Palpation (Pinch Test)**:
    - Soft to firm, rubbery, concentric disk of tissue centered directly beneath the nipple-areola complex.
    - _Pinch Test_: Grasping the breast between the thumb and index finger demonstrates a palpable disc of firm glandular tissue (>0.5 cm in diameter) beneath the areola, distinguishing true gynecomastia from pseudogynecomastia.
- **General Examination**: Check for signs of chronic liver disease (spider naevi, palmar erythema, jaundice), hyperthyroidism (tremors, goitre), and perform a **mandatory testicular examination** to rule out testicular atrophy or palpable Leydig/germ cell tumors.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Targeted Imaging

- **Targeted Breast Ultrasound (USG) [Investigation of Choice - IOC]**: Identifies a retroareolar disk of hypoechoic fibroglandular tissue (>0.5 cm in depth) and distinguishes true gynecomastia from pseudogynecomastia (lipomastia).
- **Bilateral Digital Mammography**: Reserved for males aged >50 years or those presenting with hard, eccentric, or asymmetric masses to rule out male breast cancer.

#### 2. Laboratory & Endocrine Panel

- **Serum Hormone Assays**: Serum Testosterone, Estradiol, LH, FSH, Prolactin (to exclude prolactinoma), and **Serum β-hCG** (to rule out an occult hCG-secreting testicular or extragonadal tumor).
- **Biochemical Profile**: Liver Function Tests (serum bilirubin, AST/ALT, albumin) and Renal Function Tests (serum urea and creatinine).

#### 3. Tissue Biopsy

- **Core Needle Biopsy / FNAC**: Indicated ONLY if the lesion is hard, eccentric, fixed to overlying skin/underlying muscle, or accompanied by nipple discharge/retraction suspicious for male breast carcinoma.

### Differential Diagnosis

|Feature|True Gynecomastia|Pseudogynecomastia (Lipomastia)|Male Breast Carcinoma|
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
|**Etiology**|Proliferation of ductal & stromal tissue|Subcutaneous adipose tissue deposition|Primary adenocarcinoma of male breast|
|**Anatomical Site**|Concentric, centered beneath nipple|Diffuse, extends across entire breast mound|Eccentric, asymmetric mass away from center|
|**Consistency**|Firm, rubbery disc of tissue|Soft, doughy, non-disc-like|Hard, irregular, ill-defined nodule|
|**Nipple Signs**|Normal / mild prominence|Normal|Nipple retraction, erosion, bloody discharge|
|**Ultrasound Finding**|Subareolar hypoechoic glandular disc (>0.5 cm)|Homogeneous subcutaneous fat tissue|Spiculated hypoechoic mass with shadowing|

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     MANAGEMENT OF GYNECOMASTIA
                                 |
        +------------------------+------------------------+
        |                                                 |
  Physiological / Mild                               Persistent / Severe
  - Reassurance & Observation                        - Medical: Tamoxifen (Early)
  - Stop offending drugs (DISCKO)                    - Surgical: Liposuction + Subareolar
  - Regresses in 1-2 years                             Excision (Webster Incision)
```

#### 1. Conservative Management & Reassurance

- **Physiological Cases**: Neonatal and pubertal gynecomastia require reassurance and observation, as >80%–90% resolve spontaneously within 12 to 24 months.
- **Drug Cessation**: Discontinue offending drugs (DISCKO) or treat underlying systemic conditions (cirrhosis, hypogonadism).

#### 2. Medical Therapy (Effective Only in Early Florid Phase <6–12 Months) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Tamoxifen**: Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM); blocks estrogen action on breast tissue; reduces pain and gland volume in >80% of early cases.
- **Danazol**: Synthetic androgen; reduces pituitary gonadotropin release.
- **Aromatase Inhibitors (Anastrozole)**: Blocks peripheral conversion of androgens to estrogens.

#### 3. Surgical Management (Mainstay for Chronic / Fibrotic / Severe Cases) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indications for Surgery [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. Failure of medical regression after >1 year of therapy (fibrotic stage).
    2. Severe psychological embarrassment or social distress.
    3. Persistent severe pain or tenderness.
    4. Moderate to severe breast enlargement (Simon Grade IIB or Grade III).
- **Surgical Modalities [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - **Subareolar Gland Excision (Webster Intra-areolar Incision) [⭐ High-Yield]**: Performed via a semi-circular intra-areolar incision along the inferior margin of the areola. The subareolar glandular tissue is excised while **leaving a thin 5-mm disc of tissue beneath the nipple-areola complex** to prevent a crater-like saucerization defect.
    - **Liposuction + Gland Excision [⭐ High-Yield]**: **Current Gold Standard treatment**. Suction-assisted lipectomy removes the surrounding fatty component, followed by direct excision of the dense subareolar fibrous core through a small periareolar incision.
    - **Reduction Mammoplasty / Skin Resection**: Reserved for Grade III cases with severe skin redundancy and ptosis.

---

### Complications

- **Psychological**: Persistent body image anxiety and depression.
- **Postoperative Complications**:
    - **Hematoma Formation**: Most common early surgical complication; reduced by meticulous hemostasis and suction drainage.
    - **Saucerization Defect**: Cosmetic crater-like depression beneath the nipple due to excessive radical excision of subareolar fat/tissue.
    - **Nipple-Areola Necrosis & Sensory Loss**: Due to compromised subdermal plexus blood supply or division of cutaneous nerves during excision.

---

### Dosage Rule (Tamoxifen)

- **Name**: Tamoxifen Citrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM); competitively blocks binding of estrogen to estrogen nuclear receptors in ductal epithelial and stromal cells (Bailey & Love p. 921).
- **Dose**: 10 mg to 20 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Once daily for 3 to 6 months
- **Features**: Most effective medical therapy when started during the early painful florid phase (<6 months duration).
- **Side Effects**: Nausea, hot flashes, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), muscle cramps.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start during the early painful phase; stop after 3–6 months if pain/gland size resolves or before planning surgical intervention.

---

### Clinical Pearl

When performing a subareolar gland excision (subcutaneous mastectomy) for gynecomastia, **always leave a 5-mm disc of breast tissue directly behind the nipple-areola complex**. Complete radical excision of all underlying tissue destroys the mechanical support of the areola, resulting in an unsightly, permanent "saucerization" (crater-like depression) defect and nipple inversion.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Mistaking **Male Breast Cancer** for simple gynecomastia in an elderly male presenting with a unilateral breast mass.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: True gynecomastia presents as a **concentric, smooth, firm disk centered directly beneath the nipple-areola complex**, whereas male breast cancer presents as an **eccentric, hard, irregular mass located away from the areola that may exhibit skin dimpling, nipple retraction, or bloody discharge**.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Benign enlargement of the male breast due to an increased estrogen to androgen ratio.
- **Physiological Phases**: Neonatal, pubertal (12–15 years), and senile (>50 years).
- **Drug Causes (DISCKO)**: Digoxin, Isoniazid, Spironolactone/Steroids, Cimetidine, Ketoconazole, Oestrogen.
- **Investigation of Choice**: Breast Ultrasound (USG) showing a subareolar hypoechoic glandular disc >0.5 cm.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Liposuction combined with Subareolar Gland Excision (Webster periareolar incision) leaving a 5-mm subareolar button.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Gynecomastia is a benign enlargement of the male breast caused by ductal and stromal proliferation secondary to an increased ratio of circulating estrogens to androgens, diagnosed by ultrasound, and managed conservatively, medically with tamoxifen, or surgically via liposuction and subareolar gland excision._
