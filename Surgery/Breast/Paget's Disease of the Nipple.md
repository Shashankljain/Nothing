# Paget's Disease of the Nipple (Paget's Disease of the Breast)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Paget's disease of the nipple** is a unique form of intraepithelial adenocarcinoma arising within the major lactiferous ducts and extending to involve the skin of the nipple-areola complex. Pathologically, it is defined by the presence of **Paget cells**—large, atypical intraepidermal epithelial cells with clear or vacuolated cytoplasm and prominent hyperchromatic nucleoli—situated within the Malpighian layer of the nipple epidermis.

---

### Etiology / Pathophysiology [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Epidermotropic Theory (Most Accepted)**: Malignant cells originate from an underlying intraepithelial Ductal Carcinoma In Situ (DCIS) or invasive ductal carcinoma within the major lactiferous ducts. These cells migrate upward along the basement membrane of the lactiferous ducts to infiltrate the epidermis of the nipple and areola.
- **In Situ Transformation Theory**: Less commonly, Paget cells are postulated to arise de novo as a primary intraepidermal carcinoma in situ within the skin of the nipple.
- **Association with Underlying Malignancy**: **>70% of patients** with Paget's disease have an underlying palpable or non-palpable parenchymal breast malignancy (DCIS > Invasive ductal carcinoma).

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- Persistent, unilateral itching, burning, tingling, or prickling sensation of the nipple.
- Pathological single-duct serous or sanguineous (bloody) nipple discharge.

#### Signs

- **Unilateral Eczematous Erosion**: Slowly spreading, erythematous, excoriated, crusted, or scaly lesion starting at the nipple tip and extending outward onto the areola.
- **Destruction of Nipple-Areola Complex**: Erosion, flattening, ulceration, and physical destruction of the nipple contour.
- **Underlying Palpable Mass**: A discrete breast mass is present in **>70% of cases** in the underlying breast tissue.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Histopathological Confirmation (Investigation of Choice)

- **Full-Thickness Punch Biopsy [⭐ High-Yield]**: A skin punch biopsy or wedge biopsy of the affected nipple skin provides the definitive histological diagnosis.
- **Microscopic Pathology**: Epidermis invaded by nests or single **Paget cells** (large cells with abundant clear cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei, and prominent nucleoli).
- **Immunohistochemistry (IHC)**: Paget cells stain **positive for CEA (Carcinoembryonic Antigen)**, **c-erbB-2 (HER2/neu)**, and **mucicarmine**, while typically testing **negative for ER/PR**.

#### 2. Triple Assessment & Imaging

- **Digital Mammography & Ultrasound (USG)**: Evaluates the underlying breast parenchyma for pleomorphic microcalcifications (DCIS) or a spiculated mass (invasive carcinoma).
- **Breast Contrast-Enhanced MRI**: High sensitivity for identifying occult DCIS or multifocal disease when mammography and ultrasound are negative.

---

### Differential Diagnosis — Paget's Disease vs. Eczema of Nipple [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Paget's Disease of Nipple|Eczema of Nipple|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Site / Laterality**|**Unilateral** (U/L)|Bilateral (B/L)|
|**Pruritus & Sensation**|Present (mild to moderate itching)|Intense itching, associated with generalized eczema|
|**Nipple-Areola Complex**|**Destruction (+) / Erosion of nipple**|No destruction of nipple structure|
|**Underlying Mass**|**Present in >70%** (DCIS > Invasive ductal cancer)|Absent (No underlying mass)|
|**Initial Site of Onset**|Starts at the **nipple** and spreads to areola|Starts on the **areola** and spreads to nipple|
|**Biopsy Findings**|**Paget cells** (CEA+, HER2/neu+, mucicarmine+)|Acanthosis, spongiosis; no malignant cells|
|**Response to Steroids**|**No response** to topical steroids|Responds well to 0.1% topical betamethasone|

---

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Surgical planning is guided by the presence and nature of any underlying parenchymal carcinoma:

#### 1. Paget's Disease Without Underlying Invasive Malignancy (Isolated DCIS)

- **Central Core Excision (Central Cone Resection)**: Complete resection of the nipple-areola complex along with a cone of major milk ducts down to the pectoralis major muscle, followed by **Whole Breast Radiotherapy**.
- **Breast Conservation Surgery (BCS)**: Wide excision of the central complex + underlying DCIS focus + Postoperative Radiotherapy.

#### 2. Paget's Disease With Underlying Invasive Carcinoma or Multifocal Disease

- **Modified Radical Mastectomy (MRM)** or Total Mastectomy.
- **Axillary Node Staging**: Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB) or Axillary Lymph Node Dissection (ALND) based on nodal involvement.
- **Adjuvant Systemic Therapy**: Chemotherapy, anti-HER2 targeted therapy (Trastuzumab), or endocrine therapy tailored according to biomarker status (ER, PR, HER2/neu) and overall stage.

---

### Complications

- Delayed diagnosis due to misdiagnosis and inappropriate trial of topical antieczematous creams.
- Progression of underlying occult DCIS to invasive ductal carcinoma with axillary lymph node metastasis.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Any chronic eczematous or erosive lesion of the nipple that fails to heal after **2 weeks of topical corticosteroid therapy**must undergo an immediate **full-thickness punch biopsy** to rule out Paget's disease of the nipple.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Misdiagnosing Paget's disease as benign nipple eczema and prescribing prolonged courses of topical steroids.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Benign eczema is usually **bilateral**, starts on the **areola**, and leaves the nipple intact. Paget's disease is strictly **unilateral**, originates directly on the **nipple**, and causes **erosion and destruction of the nipple-areola complex**.

- **A** – **A**ren't bilateral (strictly unilateral lesion starting at the nipple).

- **T** – **T**reatment: Central core excision + RT (if no mass) or Mastectomy (if underlying invasive cancer).

### Quick Revision

- **Pathology**: Intraepithelial adenocarcinoma of the nipple; Paget cells are large intraepidermal cells with clear cytoplasm, CEA+, and HER2/neu+.
- **Clinical Hallmark**: Unilateral eczematous erosion and destruction of the nipple-areola complex; >70% have an underlying breast mass.
- **Gold Standard Investigation**: Full-thickness punch biopsy of the nipple skin + Triple Assessment.
- **Key Distinguishing Feature**: Eczema is bilateral with intact nipple; Paget's is unilateral with nipple destruction.
- **Surgical Management**: Central core excision + Radiotherapy (isolated DCIS) or Mastectomy + SLNB/ALND (if underlying invasive cancer).
