# Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB)** is a minimally invasive diagnostic staging procedure designed to identify, harvest, and histologically analyze the **sentinel lymph node**—defined as the first-echelon lymph node receiving direct lymphatic drainage from a primary tumor—to accurately determine regional nodal metastasis.

---

### Key Clinical Features & Technique [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Target Indications**: Performed in clinically and radiologically node-negative (cN0) early malignancies, primarily **breast cancer**, **malignant melanoma**, **penile cancer**, vulvar carcinoma, and early head/neck cancers.
- **Dual-Tracer Gold Standard Technique**: Combines peritumoral/subareolar injection of **Blue Dye** (1–1.5 mL Methylene Blue / Patent Blue) with a **Radionuclide** (Technetium-99m tagged sulfur colloid).
- **Identification Criteria**: Sentinel nodes (most commonly located in Level I of the axilla in breast cancer) are identified visually as blue nodes or radioactively as "hot nodes" exhibiting >10% of the ex vivo gamma probe count of the hottest node.
- **Clinical Significance**: If the sentinel node is pathologically negative for malignancy, radical axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) is safely omitted, preventing chronic upper limb lymphedema, shoulder dysfunction, and nerve injuries.
- **Contraindications & Complications**: Contraindicated in inflammatory breast cancer, T4 disease, or prior breast/axillary radiation; key minor complications include skin tattooing, transient blue urine discoloration, and rare anaphylaxis.

### Quick Revision

- **Concept**: Originally described by Cabana; targets the first draining node (Level I axilla in breast cancer).
- **Gold Standard Tracers**: Dual mapping using Methylene Blue dye + Technetium-99m radiotracer.
- **Primary Objective**: Accurately stages the axilla while sparing node-negative (cN0) patients the morbidity of formal axillary dissection.
