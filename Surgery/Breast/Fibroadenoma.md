# Fibroadenoma of Breast (and Fibroadenosis)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fibroadenoma**: A benign, encapsulated, mixed epithelial-mesenchymal neoplasm arising from the lobular stroma and epithelium of the Terminal Duct Lobular Unit (TDLU). It represents an **Aberration of Normal Development and Involution (ANDI)** during the period of maximal lobular development (ages 15–25 years) and is the most common cause of a discrete breast lump in young women.
- **Fibroadenosis (Fibrocystic Disease / Cyclical Nodularity)**: A benign condition characterized by cyclical breast pain (mastalgia) and diffuse or localized lumpy nodularity, occurring due to an aberration of cyclical hormone-responsive changes in women aged 25–40 years.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Hormonal Sensitivity**: Driven primarily by an exaggerated local cellular response or hyper-responsiveness to circulating **estrogen** relative to progesterone during reproductive years.
- **Age Distribution**:
    - _Fibroadenoma_: Peak incidence between **15 and 25 years** of age.
    - _Fibroadenosis_: Peak incidence between **25 and 40 years** (premenopausal age group).
- **ANDI Classification (Cardiff / Lucknow Concept)**: Both conditions are derived from minor perturbations of normal physiological developmental and involutional processes rather than true destructive diseases.
- **Relative Risk of Malignancy**:
    - _Simple Fibroadenoma_: Minimal or no increased risk (RR = 1.5–1.7).
    - _Complex Fibroadenoma_ (containing cysts >3 mm, sclerosing adenosis, or apocrine metaplasia) or positive family history: Increased risk of breast cancer (RR = 3.1–3.7).

---

### Classification

#### 1. Variants of Fibroadenoma

- **Simple Fibroadenoma**: Classical well-circumscribed, encapsulated nodule measuring 2–3 cm.
- **Giant Fibroadenoma**: Measures **>5 cm** in diameter or weighs >500 g; typically occurs in pubertal girls and exhibits rapid growth.
- **Complex Fibroadenoma**: Contains histological features of sclerosing adenosis, epithelial calcifications, papillary apocrine metaplasia, or cysts >3 mm.
- **Juvenile Fibroadenoma**: Occurs in adolescents (10–18 years); rapidly growing, unencapsulated, hypercellular lesion.

#### 2. Fibroadenosis (Nodularity Grading - Lucknow-Cardiff Scale)

- Graded on an ordinal 5-point scale: **Grade 0** (Non-nodular) to **Grade 4** (Severe nodularity).

- **Fibroadenoma Formation**: Estrogen-driven proliferation of specialized intralobular stroma compresses adjacent lactiferous ductules. Depending on the histological pattern, stroma either compresses ducts into slit-like clefts (**intracanalicular pattern**) or surrounds open ductules (**pericanalicular pattern**).
- **Fibroadenosis Mechanism**: Repeated menstrual cycles induce incomplete involution of lobules, leading to intra-lobular fibrosis, retention of secretions in microcysts, and periductal inflammatory infiltration.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Fibroadenoma

- **Symptoms**: Painless, slow-growing, solitary lump in the breast (most commonly in the upper outer quadrant).
- **Signs**:
    - **Consistency & Surface**: Firm, smooth, rubbery, well-circumscribed, and lobulated.
    - **Mobility**: **Highly mobile** within the surrounding breast parenchyma in all directions (**"Breast Mouse"**).
    - **Skin & Nipple**: No skin dimpling, tethering, or nipple retraction.

#### 2. Fibroadenosis

- **Symptoms**: **Cyclical mastalgia** (breast pain appearing 1–2 weeks before menses, peaking on day 27–28, and abating with menstrual flow) and premenstrual fullness.
- **Signs**: Bilateral, ill-defined, tender, lumpy nodularity, most marked in the upper outer quadrants.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Triple Assessment Protocol

1. **Clinical Examination**: History taking and bilateral breast palpation using the **Dial Clock Method**.
2. **Imaging**:

- **Targeted Breast Ultrasound (IOC for Women <40 Years)**:
    - _Fibroadenoma_: Smooth, well-circumscribed, oval, hypoechoic mass with a **"wider-than-taller"** orientation and preserved fatty margins (BI-RADS 2/3).
    - _Fibroadenosis_: Diffuse microcysts with stromal echogenicity.
- **Mammography (Women ≥40 Years)**:
    - _Fibroadenoma_: Well-defined dense lesion showing coarse **"popcorn-like" macrocalcifications** in older postmenopausal women.

3. **Histopathology / Tissue Sampling**:

- **14-Gauge Core Needle Biopsy**: Indicated if age >25–30 years, atypical ultrasound features, or rapid size increase, to rule out phyllodes tumor or malignancy.

#### 1. Management of Fibroadenoma

- **Conservative Surveillance**: Simple fibroadenoma (<3 cm) in women aged <25–30 years with benign triple assessment requires reassurance and follow-up ultrasound at 6 months.
- **Indications for Surgical Excision [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    1. Patient age **>30 years**.
    2. Rapid increase in size or diameter **>3–5 cm**.
    3. Giant fibroadenoma (>5 cm).
    4. Suspicious features on ultrasound/mammogram (microlobulation, vertical orientation).
    5. Atypical histology on core biopsy.
    6. Family history of breast carcinoma.
    7. Severe anxiety or explicit patient preference.
- **Surgical Methods**:
    - **Enucleation (Open Surgery)**: Enucleated out of its capsule via a circumareolar incision or a submammary infra-mammary crease incision (**Gaillard Thomas incision**) for large/giant lesions.
    - **Vacuum-Assisted Breast Biopsy (VABB)**: Minimally invasive, scarless percutaneous resection using 8G–11G probes under real-time ultrasound guidance.

#### 2. Management of Fibroadenosis & Mastalgia

- **Non-Pharmacological**: Reassurance following normal triple assessment, firm supportive sports brassiere during daytime, and caffeine avoidance.
- **Pharmacotherapy for Severe Cyclical Mastalgia**:
    - First-line: Topical NSAID gel (Diclofenac cream 4 times daily) and oral essential fatty acids (Evening primrose oil / Flaxseed 30 g/day).
    - Second-line (Systemic Medical Therapy): **Ormeloxifene (Centchroman)** 30 mg twice weekly for 3 months, or **Tamoxifen** 10 mg daily for 3–6 months.

---

### Complications

- **Giant / Deforming Growth**: Ulceration of overlying skin due to pressure necrosis in giant lesions.
- **Infarction / Necrosis**: Spontaneous hemorrhagic infarction during pregnancy or lactation.
- **Malignant Transformation**: Rare (<0.1%); epithelial component may give rise to lobular or ductal carcinoma, or stromal element may transform into phyllodes tumor.

---

### Dosage Rule (Drugs for Mastalgia & Fibroadenosis)

#### 1. Ormeloxifene (Centchroman)

- **Name**: Ormeloxifene (Centchroman / Sevista)
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM); antagonizes estrogen action on TDLU breast epithelium and stroma (Bailey & Love p. 921).
- **Dose**: 30 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: **Twice weekly for 12 weeks**, followed by once weekly for 12 weeks.
- **Features**: First-line non-steroidal drug for cyclical mastalgia and nodularity; high efficacy with minimal side effects.
- **Side Effects**: Delayed menses, nausea, weight gain.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start on day 1 of menstrual cycle; stop after 3–6 months of complete symptom relief.

#### 2. Tamoxifen

- **Name**: Tamoxifen Citrate
- **Mechanism of Action**: Competitive inhibitor of estrogen nuclear receptors in breast tissue.
- **Dose**: 10 mg
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: Once daily for 3 to 6 months.
- **Features**: Highly effective for refractory cyclical mastalgia.
- **Side Effects**: Hot flashes, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), menstrual irregularity.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start after ruling out pregnancy; stop after 3–6 months.

---

### Clinical Pearl

A firm, smooth, painless, highly mobile breast mass in a woman under 25 years is clinically pathognomonic for a fibroadenoma (**"Breast Mouse"**). Reassurance and conservative surveillance are safe if triple assessment confirms benign BI-RADS 2/3 findings.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing an open surgical excision via an overlying radial skin incision for a giant fibroadenoma in a young female.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Overlying radial skin incisions leave prominent hypertrophic scars on the visible anterior breast mound. **Giant fibroadenomas must be enucleated via an inferior submammary crease incision (Gaillard Thomas incision)** to ensure an invisible cosmetic scar.

### Diagram Guide

### Diagram 1: Ultrasonographic Features of Fibroadenoma

- **Type**: Labeled ultrasound schematic diagram.
- **Outline shape to draw**: An oval, horizontal mass positioned parallel to the skin surface.
- **Labels in order**:
    1. _Skin and Subcutaneous Fat Layer_ — uppermost boundary.
    2. _Capsule_ — smooth, well-circumscribed, thin echogenic pseudocapsule.
    3. _Internal Matrix_ — homogeneous, hypoechoic solid tissue.
    4. _Orientation Dimension_ — **Wider-than-taller** (horizontal diameter > vertical diameter).
    5. _Posterior Acoustic Enhancement_ — normal sound transmission without posterior shadowing.
- **Annotation for Extra Marks**: _"Wider-than-taller orientation with well-defined margins is the characteristic ultrasonographic feature distinguishing fibroadenoma from carcinoma."_

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Most common benign breast tumor in females aged 15–25 years; derived from ANDI phase of lobular development.
- **Classic Sign**: Painless, firm, smooth, highly mobile lump (**"Breast Mouse"**).
- **Investigation of Choice**: Breast Ultrasound (<40 years) showing a smooth, **wider-than-taller** hypoechoic mass.
- **Indications for Excision**: Age >30 years, size >3–5 cm, giant fibroadenoma (>5 cm), or suspicious ultrasound/histology.
- **Surgical Approach**: Enucleation via **Gaillard Thomas (submammary) incision** or scarless VABB.
- **Fibroadenosis Care**: Reassurance + Sports Bra + Ormeloxifene (Centchroman) 30 mg twice weekly for 3 months.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A fibroadenoma is a benign, encapsulated, biphasic tumor arising from the terminal duct lobular unit during the lobular development phase of ANDI, clinically characterized by a painless, highly mobile "breast mouse" mass, and managed conservatively or via enucleation through a Gaillard Thomas incision._
