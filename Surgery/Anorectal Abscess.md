# Anorectal Abscess

### Definition

An **anorectal abscess** is an acute suppurative collection of pus in the potential tissue spaces surrounding the anus and rectum. It arises predominantly from an acute infection of the intersphincteric anal glands (the **cryptoglandular theory of Parks**).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Cryptoglandular Etiology (>90%) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Parks' Cryptoglandular Theory**: Intersphincteric anal glands (average 8–12 in number) open into the anal crypts along the dentate line. Infection and inflammatory obstruction of the gland duct lead to acute intersphincteric micro-abscess formation, which subsequently tracks along anatomical planes of least resistance.

#### 2. Non-Cryptoglandular & Secondary Causes

- **Pelvic Inflammatory Pathology**: Extension of intra-abdominal or pelvic sepsis (e.g., acute appendicitis, diverticulitis, gynaecological infection, or pelvic Crohn's disease) into the supralevator space.
- **Specific Local Pathology**: Inflammatory bowel disease (**Crohn's disease**), anorectal neoplasms, foreign body penetration, or deep skin infections.
- **Iatrogenic Causes**: Submucosal abscess formation following hemorrhoidal sclerotherapy injections.
- **Predisposing Risk Factors**: Male gender (significantly higher incidence in men), **Diabetes Mellitus**, HIV/AIDS, chemotherapy-induced neutropenia, and systemic immunosuppression.

1. **Perianal Abscess (60% - Most Common)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Confined to the subcutaneous space at the anal verge by terminal extensions of the conjoint longitudinal muscle.
2. **Ischiorectal Abscess (30%)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Involves the large, fat-filled ischiorectal fossa lateral to the external sphincter and inferior to the levator ani muscle.
3. **Submucosal / Intersphincteric Abscess (5%)**: Situated between the internal sphincter and mucosal layer or within the intersphincteric plane.
4. **Supralevator Abscess (5%)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Located superior to the levator ani muscle complex in the pelvirectal space.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Perianal Abscess**: Rapid presentation (2–3 day history) of severe, constant, well-localized, throbbing perianal pain aggravated by sitting, walking, or coughing.
- **Ischiorectal Abscess**: More insidious onset with less localized initial pain due to low tissue tension in the fat pad, but marked constitutional upset, high-grade fever, chills, and rigors.
- **Supralevator Abscess**: Deep, poorly localized pelvic/rectal pain, high fever, tenesmus, and micturition disturbances (dysuria or urinary retention) without visible external changes.

#### Signs

- **Perianal Abscess**: Tender, red, hot, indurated swelling visible at the anal margin.
- **Ischiorectal Abscess**: Diffuse gluteal asymmetry, widespread induration, and deep tenderness over the affected buttock.
- **Supralevator Abscess**: External inspection appears completely normal; Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) reveals a tender, boggy, fluctuating mass above the anorectal ring.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical & Examination Under Anesthesia (EUA)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Primary diagnostic approach; EUA with proctosigmoidoscopy allows full anatomical mapping and pus evacuation.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI Pelvis) [Gold Standard Imaging]**: Short Tau Inversion Recovery (STIR) fat-suppressed sequences accurately identify complex collections, horseshoe extensions, and supralevator involvement.
- **Endoanal Ultrasound (EAUS)**: High-resolution evaluation of intersphincteric and trans-sphincteric involvement.
- **Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT Pelvis)**: Diagnostic modality of choice to identify supralevator or pelvic source collections secondary to abdominal disease.

#### 1. Surgical Emergency Drainage [The Gold Standard] [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Operating Theater Setup**: Performed under general or spinal anesthesia in lithotomy position.
- **Incision & De-roofing**: A **cruciate skin incision** is made over the point of maximal fluctuance or induration, with excision of skin corners (**de-roofing**) to ensure uninhibited dependent drainage and prevent premature skin closure.
- **Tissue Preservation**: Healthy skin should be preserved; overaggressive excision of perianal skin must be avoided.
- **Fistula Management**: Injudicious probing during acute suppuration is dangerous and creates false tracks. If an obvious fistula track is identified by an experienced surgeon, a **loose draining seton** is passed. **Immediate fistulotomy is strictly avoided in the acute phase**.
- **Supralevator Abscess Drainage**: If arising from anal gland infection, it is drained **transanally through the rectal wall** (prevents an extrasphincteric fistula); if arising from pelvic pathology, the primary abdominal cause must be treated.

#### 2. Adjuvant Medical Therapy

- **Broad-Spectrum Intravenous Antibiotics**: Indicated in diabetic, immunocompromised, septic, or extensive phlegmonous infections.
- **Postoperative Care**: Daily warm sitz baths, analgesics, and stool softeners.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fistula-in-Ano Formation (30%–50%)**: The most frequent chronic sequela resulting from persistent cryptoglandular epithelialized tracks.
- **Horseshoe Extension**: Circumferential spread across the deep postanal space of Courtney into the contralateral ischiorectal fossa.
- **Necrotizing Fasciitis (Fournier's Gangrene)**: Rapid, life-threatening polymicrobial soft-tissue gangrene in diabetic or immunocompromised patients.
- **Recurrence**: Caused by incomplete locular drainage, missed secondary extensions, or underlying Crohn's disease.

---

### Clinical Pearl

**Fluctuance is a LATE sign in deep anorectal abscesses** (particularly ischiorectal and breast/parotid abscesses). In an ischiorectal abscess, large volumes of pus (>100 mL) accumulate within the low-resistance fatty space before surface fluctuance appears. **Do NOT wait for fluctuance before scheduling surgical drainage**—induration, heat, and deep tenderness are sufficient mandates for emergency operative intervention.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing an immediate, primary fistulotomy during the emergency incision and drainage of an acute perianal or ischiorectal abscess.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: **Immediate fistulotomy during acute abscess drainage is strictly contraindicated**. Acute, friable inflamed tissues prevent accurate identification of the sphincter complex, risking inadvertent sphincter division and permanent fecal incontinence. **Insert a loose draining seton or leave the cavity to drain freely instead**.

---

### Dosage Rule (Empiric Systemic Antibiotic Therapy)

#### 1. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Nitroimidazole antibiotic; forms reactive intermediates that cause DNA strand breakage in obligate anaerobic bacteria (_Bacteroides fragilis_).
- **Dose**: 500 mg IV / 400 mg PO
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion / Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS)
- **Features**: Essential coverage against colonic anaerobic organisms in anorectal sepsis.
- **Side Effects**: Nausea, metallic taste, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start preoperatively upon diagnosis; continue for 5–7 days postoperatively.

#### 2. Ciprofloxacin

- **Name**: Ciprofloxacin
- **Mechanism of Action**: Second-generation fluoroquinolone; inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, blocking Gram-negative bacterial DNA replication (_E. coli_).
- **Dose**: 400 mg IV / 500 mg PO
- **Dosage / Route**: Intravenous infusion / Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: 12-hourly (BD)
- **Side Effects**: Tendonitis/tendon rupture, QT prolongation, GI distress.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Combined with Metronidazole for 5–7 days postoperatively.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: P-A-R-K-S

- **P** – **P**erianal abscess is the most common type (60%).
- **A** – **A**nal gland intersphincteric cryptoglandular origin (Parks' theory).
- **R** – **R**adiological MRI (STIR sequence) is the gold standard for complex/supralevator cases.
- **K** – **K**ill acute sepsis via EUA, cruciate incision, de-roofing, and seton placement.
- **S** – **S**upralevator abscess requires origin-specific transanal or abdominal drainage.

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: Over 90% arise from infected intersphincteric anal glands at the dentate line (Parks' theory).
- **Classification**: Perianal (60%), Ischiorectal (30%), Submucosal (5%), Supralevator (5%).
- **Clinical Feature**: Severe throbbing pain; fluctuance is a late sign in deep collections.
- **Treatment**: Urgent EUA, cruciate incision, skin edge de-roofing, and pus evacuation.
- **Golden Rule**: Never perform an immediate fistulotomy during acute abscess drainage.
