# Imperforate Anus (Anorectal Malformations)

### Definition

**Imperforate anus** (more accurately termed **anal agenesis** or **anorectal malformation [ARM]**) encompasses a spectrum of congenital malformations characterized by the absence of a normal anal opening at the perineal dimple, occurring secondary to abnormal embryonic division of the cloaca and failure of resorption of the anal membrane.

---

### Basic Embryology & Surgical Anatomy

- **Cloacal Division**: Early in embryonic development, the hindgut and allantois open into a shared chamber known as the **cloaca**, which is separated from the surface ectoderm by the cloacal membrane.
- **Urorectal Septum**: Downgrowth of the urorectal septum divides the cloaca into a dorsal part (rectum) and a ventral part (urogenital sinus).
- **Anal Membrane Resorption**: Resorption of the anal membrane (composed of outer ectoderm and inner endoderm) by the 8th week of embryonic life creates the functional anal canal. Failure of complete division or membrane resorption leads to an imperforate anus.
- **Puborectalis / Levator Ani Relationship**: The key anatomical determinant for classification and prognosis is whether the rectal pouch terminates above or below the pelvic floor (puborectalis muscle complex).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Embryonic Arrest**: Developmental arrest or abnormal caudal migration of the urorectal septum between the 4th and 8th weeks of gestation.
- **Aneuploidies & Genetic Associations**: Higher incidence in chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Trisomy 21 / Down syndrome) where the rectum frequently ends blindly without a fistula.
- **VACTERL Association [⭐ High-Yield]**: Anorectal malformations serve as a critical indicator for other systemic congenital anomalies:
    - **V** – **V**ertebral defects (sacral agenesis, hemivertebrae).
    - **A** – **A**norectal malformations (Imperforate anus / Anal agenesis).
    - **C** – **C**ardiac anomalies (VSD, ASD, Tetralogy of Fallot).
    - **TE** – **T**racheo**E**sophageal fistula / Esophageal atresia.
    - **R** – **R**enal and urinary tract anomalies (renal agenesis, vesicoureteric reflux).
    - **L** – **L**imb defects (radial ray dysplasia).

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

Anorectal malformations are classified based on the level of termination of the rectum relative to the pelvic floor (puborectalis muscle):

```
                       ANORECTAL MALFORMATIONS (ARM)
                                     |
          +--------------------------+--------------------------+
          |                                                     |
  High Anomalies (Above Pelvic Floor)                  Low Anomalies (Below Pelvic Floor)
  * Rectum terminates above levator ani                * Rectum penetrates puborectalis
  * Males: Rectobulbar, Rectoprostatic,                * Males: Rectoperineal fistula,
    or Rectovesical (bladder neck) fistula               anoperineal stenosis, covered anus
  * Females: High Rectovaginal fistula,                * Females: Rectovestibular or
    or Persistent Cloaca                                 rectoperineal fistula
```

1. **High Anomalies (Above Levator Ani / Puborectalis)**:
    - **In Boys**: The rectum usually ends either blindly or with a fistulous communication to the **bulbar urethra**, **prostatic urethra**, or **bladder neck**.
    - **In Girls**: The rectum may open into a **high rectovaginal fistula** or join a common channel with the vagina and urethra, termed a **cloaca**.
2. **Low Anomalies (Below Levator Ani / Puborectalis)**:
    - **In Boys**: Rectoperineal fistula or stenotic anal orifice.
    - **In Girls**: **Rectovestibular fistula** or rectoperineal fistula.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Failure to Pass Meconium**: Absence of normal meconium passage within the first 24–48 hours of life.
- **Abdominal Distension & Bilious Vomiting**: Progressive features of neonatal low bowel obstruction as swallowed air accumulates.
- **Meconuria (in Boys)**: Passage of meconium-stained green urine or air bubbles per urethra (pathognomonic of a rectourethral or rectovesical fistula).
- **Meconium via Vulva / Vestibule (in Girls)**: Passage of meconium from the vaginal vestibule or a single common perineal orifice.

#### Signs

- **Perineal Inspection**: Absence of a normal anal opening in the center of the sphincter dimple.
- **Fistula Identification**:
    - _Boys_: Meconium specks along the median raphe or at the urethral meatus.
    - _Girls_: Meconium discharging from a **rectovestibular fistula** or a single perineal opening (**cloaca**) combining urethra, vagina, and rectum.
- **Flat Perineum**: Absence of normal gluteal fold indicates poor pelvic floor muscle development and sacral agenesis.

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                        DIAGNOSTIC WORK-UP PANEL
                                   |
     +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
     |                                                           |
Imaging for Level of Obstruction                       Systemic VACTERL Screening
* Prone Reverse Cross-Table Lateral Radiograph         * Abdominal USG (Renal anomalies)
  (performed after 18–24 hours)                        * Echocardiogram (Cardiac defects)
* Distal Colostogram (Pre-PSARP Contrast Study)        * Spinal X-Ray / MRI (Sacral dysraphism)
```

#### 1. Radiological Localization of Pouch Level

- **Prone Reverse Cross-Table Lateral Radiograph (or Invertogram)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Timing**: Performed **18 to 24 hours after birth** (allows swallowed air to reach the distal apex of the rectal pouch).
    - **Method**: Infant placed prone with hips elevated; a radio-opaque marker (lead dot) is placed on the perineal skin dimple.
    - **Interpretation**:
        - Distance from air pouch to perineal skin marker **>1 cm** = **High Anomaly (Rectal Agenesis)**.
        - Distance **<1 cm** = Low Anomaly.
- **Distal Colostogram (Mucogram)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Performed via the distal loop of a divided colostomy prior to definitive repair to clearly demonstrate the rectourethral or rectovaginal fistula.

#### 2. VACTERL Screening

- **Ultrasound Abdomen & Pelvis**: Evaluates renal agenesis, hydronephrosis, and vesicoureteric reflux.
- **Echocardiography & Spinal Radiographs**: Excludes cardiac defects (VSD/ASD) and sacral vertebral anomalies.

#### 1. Low Anomalies Management

- **Single-Stage Repair**: Most perineal and rectovestibular fistulae can be transposed directly into the muscle complex **without creating a stoma**.

#### 2. High Anomalies Management (3-Stage Surgical Protocol) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Stage 1: Neonatal Divided Sigmoid Colostomy** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - A completely **divided proximal sigmoid colostomy** is created in the newborn period.
    - _Rationale_: Decompresses the obstructed bowel, allows normal oral feeding, and defunctions the distal pouch to prevent urinary tract contamination.
- **Stage 2: Posterior Sagittal Anorectoplasty (PSARP / Peña Procedure)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - Performed at 3–6 months of age after a contrast study through the defunctioned stoma.
    - _High Prostatic / Bladder Neck Fistulae_: Managed via a combined **laparoscopic and perineal approach**.
    - _Prostatic & Bulbar Urethral Fistulae_: Approached directly via **Posterior Sagittal Anorectoplasty (PSARP)**, dividing the fistula and pulling the rectum through the center of the sphincter complex.
- **Stage 3: Stoma Closure**: The defunctioned colostomy is closed in a third stage once full anal dilatation is achieved and the anoplasty is fully healed.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Fecal & Urinary Incontinence**: The principal long-term concern; bowel and urinary control depend on the height of the lesion, sacral innervation, and sphincter complex development.
- **Urethral Stricture & Recurrent Fistula**: Secondary to breakdown of the fistular repair.
- **Anal Stenosis**: Prevented by strict adherence to a postoperative anal dilatation protocol using graduated dilators.

---

### Clinical Pearl

In female neonates with an imperforate anus, **meconium is frequently passed via a rectovestibular or rectoperineal fistula**. Because meconium is discharging externally, these high-risk anomalies are **frequently missed on cursory newborn examinations**, leading to delayed presentation with severe low bowel obstruction. Always carefully part the labia and verify the exact anatomical site of the anal orifice in every newborn female.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing an X-ray invertogram or cross-table radiograph immediately after birth (within 2–4 hours of life).
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Immediately after birth, normal colonic tone and viscous meconium prevent swallowed air from navigating down to the distal apex of the pouch. **Radiological evaluation (invertogram / cross-table lateral radiograph) MUST be delayed until 18 to 24 hours of life** to accurately measure the pouch-to-skin distance.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Congenital absence of a normal anal orifice due to abnormal cloacal division.
- **Classification**: High (above levator ani) vs Low (below levator ani).
- **Common Fistulae**: Rectobulbar/prostatic in boys; Rectovestibular/cloaca in girls.
- **Associated Anomalies**: Strongly associated with VACTERL syndrome (>50%).
- **High Anomaly Surgery**: 3-Stage Repair: (1) Divided Sigmoid Colostomy → (2) PSARP → (3) Stoma closure.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Imperforate anus is a congenital anorectal malformation resulting from defective cloacal development, classified into high or low anomalies relative to the levator ani muscle, associated with VACTERL defects, and managed in high cases via a three-stage repair comprising neonatal colostomy, Posterior Sagittal Anorectoplasty (PSARP), and stoma closure._
