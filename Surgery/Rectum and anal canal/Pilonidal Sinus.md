# Pilonidal Sinus

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **pilonidal sinus** (_pilus_ = hair, _nidus_ = nest) is an acquired chronic infective condition occurring predominantly in the natal cleft overlying the sacrococcygeal region, consisting of one or more midline blind primary skin openings (pits) that communicate with a subcutaneous fibrous tract containing a loose nidus of hair and granulation tissue.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Acquired Etiology (Widely Accepted)**:
    - **Hair Ingestion Theory**: Shed loose or broken hairs accumulate in the natal cleft.
    - **Frictional & Shearing Forces**: Buttock movement and deep natal cleft vacuum create a suction effect that "drills" sharp hair shafts into skin or dilated hair follicles.
    - **Subcutaneous Nidus**: Once embedded, hair acts as a foreign body, establishing a chronic granulomatous reaction and secondary lateral tracks.
- **Predisposing Risk Factors**:
    - **Age & Gender**: Males > Females (4:1 ratio); peak incidence post-puberty to 3rd decade.
    - **Hair Characteristics**: Hirsute individuals with coarse, dark hair.
    - **Anatomical**: Deep natal cleft, obesity, poor local hygiene.
    - **Occupational Hazard**: Prolonged sitting and local trauma (historically known as "Jeep seat disease"; interdigital pilonidal sinus in hairdressers).

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Asymptomatic Pilonidal Disease**: Presence of painless midline primary pits without discharge or inflammation.
2. **Acute Pilonidal Abscess**: Acute bacterial infection causing a painful, fluctuant, erythematous sacrococcygeal mass.
3. **Chronic Recurrent Pilonidal Sinus**: Persistent or episodic purulent/sanguineous discharge from midline pits and secondary off-midline lateral tracks.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Intermittent Discharge**: Recurrent purulent, serosanguineous, or foul-smelling discharge at the base of the spine.
- **Pain & Swelling**: Localized sacrococcygeal pain aggravated by sitting or stooping.
- **Recurrent Abscess History**: History of repeated boils in the gluteal cleft that burst spontaneously or required surgical drainage.

#### Signs

- **Primary Pits [⭐ High-Yield]**: One or multiple small, punctate openings located strictly in the midline natal cleft between the sacrococcygeal joint and tip of the coccyx.
- **Protruding Hair**: A visible tuft or "nest" of hair projecting from the primary sinus orifice.
- **Secondary Tracks**: Off-midline lateral openings (usually situated cephalad to the primary pits) lined by exuberant granulation tissue.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations

- **Clinical Diagnosis**: Primary pits strictly in the midline natal cleft containing loose hair.
- **Probe Test**: Careful insertion of a lacrimal probe demonstrates a subcutaneous track running cephalad away from the anus.
- **Histopathology / Biopsy**: Indicated for non-healing, recurrent, or atypical ulcerated sinuses to exclude **Squamous Cell Carcinoma (Marjolin's ulcer)**.

#### 1. Conservative Management (Asymptomatic / Mild Disease)

- **Hygiene & Hair Eradication**: Regular shaving, depilatory creams, or laser hair removal of the natal cleft.
- **Local Ablation**: Phenol injection, silver nitrate cautery, or laser coagulation of tracks.

#### 2. Management of Acute Pilonidal Abscess

- **Incision & Drainage**: Small longitudinal incision made **off the midline** over the site of maximal fluctuation.
- **Curettage**: Thorough evacuation of pus, debris, and the embedded hair nidus.

#### 3. Surgical Options for Chronic Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

Midline surgical scars in the natal cleft suffer from high mechanical tension and anaerobic depth, leading to high recurrence. **Off-midline flap techniques are preferred**.

- **Laying Open & Marsupialisation**: Tracks are laid open and curetted; healing occurs by secondary intention (low recurrence, slower healing).
- **Karydakis Operation [⭐ High-Yield]**: Asymmetric off-midline elliptical excision of the sinus complex; a mobilization flap shifts the scar laterally out of the midline natal cleft.
- **Bascom's Procedure**: Midline pits are excised individually; a lateral incision is made to curette the subcutaneous cavity and left open to heal.
- **Limberg (Rhomboid) Flap**: Rhomboid excision of the diseased tissue followed by rotation of a full-thickness transposition flap.

---

### Complications

- **Recurrence**: Leading complication (up to 10%–15%); caused by retained hair, midline scar placement, or inadequate hygiene.
- **Wound Dehiscence & Infection**: Secondary to tension or fluid accumulation in the natal cleft.
- **Malignant Transformation**: Extremely rare development of Squamous Cell Carcinoma (Marjolin's ulcer) in neglected chronic disease.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Always inspect the **exact anatomical location** of sacrococcygeal openings. True pilonidal sinus primary pits are **strictly located in the midline natal cleft**. Any discharging opening located lateral to the sacrum or close to the anal verge without midline pits must prompt investigation for a complex fistula-in-ano, hidradenitis suppurativa, or sacral osteomyelitis.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Performing a wide excision of a chronic pilonidal sinus with **primary midline suture closure**.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Primary midline closure creates a deep, avascular natal trench subject to high shearing forces and hair accumulation, leading to high failure and recurrence rates. **Surgical closure should ALWAYS be achieved off the midline (e.g., Karydakis, Bascom, or Limberg flap) to flatten the natal cleft and eliminate local suction forces**.

---

### Dosage Rule

#### Amoxicillin-Clavulanic Acid (Co-Amoxiclav)

- **Name**: Amoxicillin + Clavulanic Acid
- **Mechanism of Action**: Amoxicillin inhibits bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan synthesis; clavulanic acid inhibits beta-lactamase enzymes produced by staphylococcal and anaerobic organisms.
- **Dose**: 625 mg (500 mg amoxicillin + 125 mg clavulanate)
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS)
- **Features**: First-line empirical antibiotic for acute pilonidal cellulitis or post-drainage care.
- **Side Effects**: Diarrhea, nausea, cholestatic jaundice, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start during acute cellulitis/abscess; continue for 5–7 days post-drainage.

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: Acquired condition caused by friction and suction drilling loose hair into midline natal cleft skin.
- **Clinical Hallmark**: Midline primary skin pits containing hair tufts with off-midline secondary discharging tracks.
- **Acute Abscess Management**: Off-midline longitudinal incision, pus evacuation, and thorough hair curettage.
- **Definitive Surgery**: Off-midline flap closures (Karydakis, Bascom, Limberg) are superior to midline closure.
- **Prevention of Recurrence**: Postoperative hair depilation, strict natal cleft hygiene, and flattening of the cleft.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A pilonidal sinus is an acquired chronic inflammatory disease of the sacrococcygeal natal cleft caused by hair penetration due to gluteal friction and suction, characterized by midline primary pits containing a hair nidus, and managed definitively by off-midline surgical flap procedures such as the Karydakis or Bascom technique._
