# Rectal Prolapse (Prolapse of the Rectum)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Rectal prolapse** (prolapse of the rectum) is defined as the protrusion of rectal mucosal tissue or the entire thickness of the rectal wall through the anal sphincter mechanism. It ranges from a partial mucosal protrusion to complete circumferential full-thickness rectal wall exteriorization (procidentia).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

The etiology varies significantly according to age and anatomical extent:

#### 1. Pediatric Etiologies (Infants & Young Children)

- **Anatomical Factors**: The absence of a fully developed sacral curvature in infants leaves the rectum in a straight, vertical downward trajectory, predisposing to prolapse during defecation.
- **Loss of Supporting Fat**: Rapid loss of retrorectal and ischiorectal fossa adipose tissue following severe diarrhea, malnutrition, or Kwashiorkor.
- **Chronic Straining & Increased Intra-Abdominal Pressure**: Chronic constipation, pertussis (whooping cough), or phimosis/urethral obstruction.
- **Associated Systemic & Local Conditions**: **Cystic fibrosis** (up to 20% of children with CF present with rectal prolapse due to bulky, frequent stools), Hirschsprung's disease, spinal dysraphism, rectal polyps, and pelvic maldevelopment.

#### 2. Adult Etiologies

- **Demographics**: Pronounced **female predominance** (female to male ratio = 6:1), most commonly affecting elderly or postmenopausal women.
- **Pelvic Floor & Sphincter Weakness**: Generalized weakness or atrophy of the levator ani muscle complex, puborectalis, and anal sphincters.
- **Loss of Rectal Fixation**: Excessive mobility of the rectum due to attenuation or stretching of the lateral ligaments and mesorectum.
- **Deep Pouch of Douglas**: An abnormally deep, steep rectovaginal or rectovesical peritoneal pouch descending between the anterior rectal wall and the vagina/bladder.
- **Anorectal & Neurological Conditions**: Parity/obstetric perineal injury, chronic constipation with prolonged toilet straining, previous pelvic or sphincter surgery (e.g., fistula-in-ano repair), cauda equina lesions, or psychiatric disorders (e.g., anorexia nervosa).

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                             RECTAL PROLAPSE CLASSIFICATION
                                           |
     +-------------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
     |                                     |                                     |
Mucosal Prolapse                      Full-Thickness Prolapse               Internal Rectal Prolapse
(Partial Prolapse)                    (Complete / Procidentia)              (Rectoanal Intussusception)
* Involves mucosa & submucosa only    * Involves ALL layers of rectal wall  * Infolding of rectal wall without
* Protrusion length: 1–4 cm           * Protrusion length: >4–15 cm         * external protrusion per anus
* Radial mucosal grooves              * Concentric circular mucosal rings   * Causes obstructed defecation
```

1. **Mucosal Prolapse (Partial Prolapse)**: Protrusion limited strictly to the rectal mucosa and submucosa, usually extending **1–4 cm** beyond the anal verge. Palpation reveals a thin double-layer fold.
2. **Full-Thickness Prolapse (Complete Prolapse / Procidentia)**: Protrusion of all muscular layers of the rectal wall through the anal canal, extending **>4 cm (commonly 10–15 cm)**.
3. **Internal Rectal Prolapse (Rectoanal Intussusception)**: Intussusception / infolding of the upper or mid-rectal wall into the distal rectal lumen during defecation without protruding through the external anal verge.

- **Intussusception Theory**: Full-thickness prolapse begins as a circumferential sliding intussusception of the anterior wall of the mid-rectum (where peritoneal support is weakest). Driven by chronic straining, the intussusceptum descends into the lower rectal ampulla and eventually exits through the anal verge.
- **Sphincter Dysfunction**: Repeated passage of a large bowel mass through the anal canal mechanically stretches the internal and external anal sphincters, resulting in a patulous anal sphincter and **fecal incontinence in 50% of adult patients**.
- **Peritoneal Herniation**: In complete prolapses **>5 cm in length**, the deep pouch of Douglas herniates downward between the anterior and posterior walls of the prolapse as a true hernial sac, which may contain loops of small intestine (enterocele) or bladder.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Protruding Anal Mass**: Noticeable protrusion of a red mucosal mass per anus triggered initially by defecation/straining, which later prolapses upon walking, coughing, or standing.
- **Mucus Discharge & Perianal Wetness**: Continuous secretion of mucus from the exposed prolapsed rectal mucosa, causing skin excoriation and pruritus ani.
- **Rectal Bleeding**: Fresh rectal bleeding resulting from chronic mucosal friction, congestion, or ulceration.
- **Fecal Incontinence & Soiling**: Present in **~50% of adult patients** due to persistent stretching and neuropathic injury of the anal sphincters.
- **Obstructed Defecation & Tenesmus**: Constant sensation of incomplete rectal evacuation, tenesmus, and pelvic fullness.

#### Signs

- **Inspection**: Patient is examined in the left lateral or commode-squatting position while straining:
    - _Mucosal Prolapse_: Demonstrates **radial folds/grooves** radiating from the center of the anal orifice.
    - _Full-Thickness Prolapse_: Demonstrates **concentric circular rings of mucosa** surrounding a central lumen, extending 4–15 cm beyond the verge.
- **Palpation**: Feeling the mass between finger and thumb reveals a thick, bulky double layer of full-thickness bowel wall (in contrast to the thin double-mucosal layer of partial prolapse).
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Reveals a markedly **patulous, tone-less anal sphincter** that gapes widely on straining.

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     DIAGNOSTIC WORK-UP PANEL
                                       |
     +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
     |                                                                   |
Clinical & Endoscopic Evaluation                               Dynamic Functional Imaging
* Sitting/Squatting Straining Exam (Primary)                   * Defecating Proctogram (IOC for Internal Prolapse)
* Flexible Sigmoidoscopy / Colonoscopy                         * Dynamic Pelvic MRI / MR Defecography
  (Rule out polyp, SRUS, or malignancy)                        * Anorectal Manometry & Endoanal Ultrasound
```

#### 1. Clinical Evaluation

- **Straining Examination**: Patient is observed straining while sitting on a commode to fully demonstrate the maximal extent of prolapse.

#### 2. Endoscopic Work-up

- **Flexible Sigmoidoscopy / Colonoscopy**: Mandatory to evaluate the entire colon and rule out lead points such as **rectal polyps, solitary rectal ulcer syndrome (SRUS), inflammatory bowel disease, or rectal carcinoma**.

#### 3. Dynamic Functional Imaging [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Defecating Proctogram (Fluoroscopic Barium Defecography) [Investigation of Choice for Internal Prolapse]**: Serial lateral radiographs taken during rest, squeeze, and active evacuation to visualize rectoanal intussusception, rectocele, or enterocele.
- **Dynamic Pelvic Magnetic Resonance (MR) Proctography**: High-resolution multiplanar imaging assessing pelvic organ prolapse across anterior, middle, and posterior compartments without radiation exposure.
- **Anorectal Manometry**: Measures resting and squeeze anal canal pressures to quantify sphincter compromise prior to surgical repair.

#### 1. Pediatric Management Protocol (First-Line Conservative) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Digital Repositioning**: Parents are educated to manually reduce the protrusion immediately following defecation.
- **Bowel & Dietary Measures**: Elimination of underlying constipation via stool softeners, osmotic laxatives, and high-fiber intake.
- **Refractory Interventions**: If conservative trial fails after 6 weeks, **submucosal injection of 5% phenol in almond oil** or rubber band ligation under general anesthesia is performed. Laparoscopic rectopexy is reserved for exceptional refractory cases.

#### 2. Surgical Management in Adults (Full-Thickness Prolapse) [⭐ High-Yield]

Surgery is required for virtually all adult cases of full-thickness rectal prolapse. The choice depends on patient fitness, gender, and risk of recurrence.

##### A. Perineal Approach (Preferred in Elderly, Frail, or High-Risk Patients) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Advantages_: Can be performed under regional/spinal anesthesia, minimal surgical trauma, low morbidity.
- _Disadvantage_: Higher recurrence rates (**up to 20–30%**) compared to abdominal procedures.

1. **Delorme's Operation**:
    - **Technique**: The rectal mucosa is stripped circumferentially from the muscular gut tube over the entire length of the prolapse. The underlying denuded muscular tube is plicated with longitudinal concertina sutures to form a firm muscle ring above the anal canal, followed by mucosal re-anastomosis.
    - **Indication**: Short-segment full-thickness prolapse in elderly frail patients. Recurrence rate ≈30% over 5 years.
2. **Altemeier's Procedure (Perineal Rectosigmoidectomy)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Technique**: Full-thickness circumferential transection of the prolapsed rectum per anus, mobilization and resection of redundant rectum/sigmoid colon, followed by hand-sewn or stapled coloanal anastomosis.
    - **Indication**: **Procedure of choice for incarcerated or strangulated rectal prolapse**. Recurrence rate ranges from 0–20%.
3. **Thiersch's Operation**:
    - Subcutaneous placement of a prosthetic ring/tape around the anal canal. Obsolete due to high rate of infection and anal stenosis.

##### B. Abdominal Approach (Preferred in Fit Adults — Gold Standard) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Advantages_: Significantly lower recurrence rates (**<5–10%**).
- _Disadvantage_: Requires general anesthesia and transabdominal dissection.

1. **Laparoscopic Ventral Mesh Rectopexy (LVMR - D'Hoore Technique) [Gold Standard Operation]** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Technique**: The rectovaginal (or rectoprostatic) septum is dissected laparoscopically, and a strip of bioprosthetic or polypropylene mesh is sutured to the anterior rectal wall and resuspending the rectum by anchoring the upper end of the mesh to the sacral promontory.
    - **Key Advantage**: Avoids posterior mesorectal mobilization, **sparing autonomic pelvic nerves** and reducing post-rectopexy constipation. Success rate 70–80%.
2. **Sutured Rectopexy (Goldberg Procedure)**:
    - Mobilization of the posterior rectum down to the pelvic floor, followed by suturing the lateral rectal fascia to the presacral fascia with non-absorbable sutures.
3. **Resection Rectopexy (Frykman-Goldberg Procedure)**:
    - Combines sigmoid colon resection with posterior rectopexy; indicated specifically in patients with severe co-existing slow-transit constipation.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Incarceration & Strangulation**: Failure to reduce the prolapse leads to acute venous congestion, edema, ischemic gangrene, and necrosis.
- **Rectal Mucosal Ulceration & Hemorrhage**: Chronic mucosal friction causing bleeding and solitary rectal ulceration.
- **Persistent Fecal Incontinence**: Irreversible sphincter damage secondary to longstanding physical stretching.
- **Proctitis & Pelvic Sepsis**: Mucosal inflammation and mesh erosion/infection (2–4% following mesh rectopexy).

---

### Prognosis

- **Pediatric Outcome**: Excellent; >90% of childhood mucosal prolapses resolve spontaneously with conservative bowel management.
- **Adult Outcome**: Abdominal rectopexy (LVMR) achieves durable cure with recurrence rates <10%. Perineal repairs carry higher recurrence (20–30%) but offer a safe solution for high-risk elderly individuals.

### Dosage Rule (Sclerosing & Pharmacotherapy Agents)

#### 1. Phenol 5% in Almond Oil

- **Name**: 5% Phenol in Almond Oil Injectable Solution.
- **Mechanism of Action**: Chemical sclerosant that produces local aseptic submucosal inflammatory fibrosis, anchoring the loose rectal mucosa to the underlying muscularis coat.
- **Dose**: 1 to 3 mL injected per site (total dose 5–10 mL per session).
- **Dosage / Route**: Submucosal injection into the submucosal space at the apex of the prolapse above the dentate line via a rigid proctoscope using a submucosal needle.
- **Frequency**: Single application; may be repeated after 4–6 weeks if needed.
- **Features**: First-line interventional therapy for pediatric or adult mucosal prolapse failing conservative trial.
- **Side Effects**: Perianal pain, submucosal abscess formation, accidental prostatic/vaginal injection.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administer after a 6-week failure of digital repositioning and conservative measures.

### Clinical Pearl

In patients presenting with a large complete rectal prolapse **>5 cm in length**, the anterior layer contains a downward extension of the peritoneal pouch (pouch of Douglas). During manual reduction or surgical dissection (Altemeier's procedure), surgeons must be aware that **loops of small bowel (enterocele) or bladder may be trapped within this anterior peritoneal sac**, posing a risk of acute bowel obstruction or inadvertent visceral injury if cut unexpectedly.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing a complete (full-thickness) rectal prolapse with prolapsed 3rd or 4th-degree internal hemorrhoids.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Examine the mucosal orientation! **Full-thickness rectal prolapse presents with concentric, circular mucosal rings**, whereas **prolapsed hemorrhoids present with deep radial grooves/sulci**separating distinct mucosal cushions at 3, 7, and 11 o'clock positions.

- **P** – **P**rocidentia (full-thickness protrusion with concentric mucosal rings).

- **L** – **L**aparoscopic Ventral Mesh Rectopexy (LVMR) is the abdominal gold standard.

- **P** – **P**atulous anal sphincter resulting in 50% fecal incontinence.
- **S** – **S**acral curve underdevelopment predisposes infants to prolapse.
- **E** – **E**lderly females (6:1 F:M ratio) represent the classic adult demographic.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Protrusion of rectal mucosa (partial) or all wall layers (complete/procidentia) through the anal verge.
- **Diagnostic Hallmark**: Concentric circular rings in complete prolapse vs. radial grooves in hemorrhoids.
- **Pediatric Management**: Conservative (fiber + softeners + digital reduction); 5% phenol injection if refractory.
- **Perineal Surgery (Elderly/Frail)**: Delorme's (mucosal stripping + plication) or Altemeier's (perineal rectosigmoidectomy).
- **Abdominal Surgery (Fit Adults)**: Laparoscopic Ventral Mesh Rectopexy (LVMR) — lowest recurrence (<10%) and spares pelvic nerves.
