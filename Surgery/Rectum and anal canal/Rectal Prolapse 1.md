#  Rectal Prolapse

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Rectal prolapse** is defined as the protrusion of rectal mucosa or the entire thickness of the rectal wall through the anal sphincter mechanism. It ranges from a partial mucosal protrusion (1–4 cm) to complete full-thickness rectal wall exteriorization (procidentia, >4 cm up to 10–15 cm).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Pediatric Etiology**:
    - **Undeveloped Sacral Curve**: The straight downward course of the rectum in infants predisposes to prolapse.
    - **Loss of Ischiorectal Fat**: Rapid weight loss or diarrhea leading to depletion of supporting fat in the ischiorectal fossa.
    - **Systemic & Local Conditions**: Associated with cystic fibrosis, Hirschsprung's disease, rectal polyps, pelvic maldevelopment, and spina bifida / neurological disorders.
- **Adult Etiology**:
    - **Demographics**: Marked female predominance (females affected 6 times more often than males).
    - **Pelvic Floor & Sphincter Weakness**: Attenuation of pelvic floor muscles and anal sphincters in old age.
    - **Obstetric & Straining Damage**: Perineal trauma during childbirth in females and chronic straining from urethral obstruction or severe constipation in males.
    - **Psychiatric / Associated Conditions**: Associated with anorexia nervosa and other pelvic organ prolapse syndromes. Co-existing fecal incontinence is present in approximately 50% of adult patients.

---

### Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

1. **Mucosal Prolapse (Partial Prolapse)**: Protrusion limited to mucosa and submucosa extending 1–4 cm beyond the anal verge; palpation reveals a thin double layer of mucosa.
2. **Full-Thickness Prolapse (Complete Prolapse / Procidentia)**: Protrusion of all layers of the rectal wall extending >4 cm (commonly 10–15 cm); contains an anterior pouch of peritoneum when >5 cm in length.
3. **Internal Rectal Prolapse (Rectal Intussusception)**: Invagination/telescoping of the rectal tube during defecation without external protrusion, causing obstructed defecation syndrome.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Protruding Anal Mass**: Sensation or visual evidence of bowel protrusion per anus triggered by straining or defecation.
- **Mucus Discharge & Bleeding**: Secretion of rectal mucus causing perianal moisture, itching, and fresh rectal bleeding from mucosal ulceration.
- **Tenesmus & Obstructed Defecation**: Persistent feeling of incomplete evacuation ("I want to go but nothing happens").
- **Fecal Incontinence**: Coexists in ~50% of adult cases due to sphincter stretching.

#### Signs

- **Inspection**:
    - _Mucosal Prolapse_: Demonstrates radial mucosal folds/grooves.
    - _Full-Thickness Prolapse_: Demonstrates concentric circular mucosal rings surrounding the central lumen.
- **Palpation**: Feeling the prolapsed mass between finger and thumb reveals a thick double thickness of entire rectal wall in complete prolapse vs. a thin double layer of mucosa in partial prolapse.
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Characteristically demonstrates a patulous anal sphincter that gapes widely on straining.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Clinical Examination**: Inspection while the patient strains in a sitting/squatting position or on a commode.
- **Proctoscopy & Sigmoidoscopy**: Evaluates the mucosal state and rules out lead points like rectal polyps, solitary rectal ulcer syndrome (SRUS), or malignancy.
- **Defecating Proctography (Barium / Dynamic MR Defecography)**: Diagnostic gold standard for demonstrating internal rectal intussusception, rectocele, or enterocele in obstructed defecation.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Management in Infants and Young Children [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Conservative (First-Line)**: Digital repositioning by parents after defecation; treat underlying diarrhea/constipation. Most cases resolve spontaneously.
- **Sclerotherapy / Banding**: Submucosal injection of **5% phenol in almond oil** or rubber band ligation under general anesthesia if conservative trial fails after 6 weeks.

#### 2. Management in Adults [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Mucosal Prolapse**: Submucosal injection of 5% phenol in almond oil, rubber band ligation, mucosal excision, or endoluminal stapling (internal Delorme's).
- **Full-Thickness Prolapse (Procidentia)**: Surgery is almost always necessary.

##### A. Perineal Approach (Preferred in Elderly, Very Frail, or Young Men) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Advantages_: Lower operative morbidity, minimal pain, can be performed under regional anesthesia; avoids pelvic autonomic nerve damage (protects sexual/bladder function in young males).
- _Disadvantage_: Higher recurrence rate (up to 20–30%) compared to abdominal repairs.

1. **Delorme's Operation**: Circumferential stripping of rectal mucosa from the gut tube, concertina plication of underlying muscle tube with sutures, and mucosal re-anastomosis. Recurrence ~30% over 5 years.
2. **Altemeier's Operation (Perineal Rectosigmoidectomy)**: Full-thickness perineal mobilization and resection of prolapsed rectum/colon with hand-sewn or stapled coloanal anastomosis. **Procedure of choice for incarcerated or strangulated prolapse**. Recurrence 0–20%.
3. **Thiersch's Operation**: Subcutaneous placement of a wire or tape around the anal canal; largely obsolete due to infection, stenosis, and obstruction.

##### B. Abdominal Approach (Preferred in Fit Adults) [⭐ High-Yield]

- _Advantages_: Significantly lower recurrence rate (<10%).
- **Laparoscopic Ventral Mesh Rectopexy (LVMR - D'Hoore)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Dissection of the rectovaginal/rectoprostatic plane, suturing a mesh strip to the anterior rectum, and securing its proximal end to the sacral promontory. Success rate 70–80%, spares posterior autonomic nerves.
- **Sutured Rectopexy (Goldberg)**: Mobilization of rectum and suturing lateral fascia to the sacrum.
- **Resection Rectopexy**: Combines rectopexy with sigmoid colon resection in patients with severe constipation.

---

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Rectal Ulceration & Hemorrhage**: Chronic trauma causing bleeding and solitary rectal ulceration.
- **Incarceration & Strangulation**: Irreducible prolapse leading to ischemic necrosis.
- **Persistent Fecal Incontinence**: Longstanding stretching of anal sphincters.

---

### Dosage Rule

#### Phenol 5% in Almond Oil

- **Name**: 5% Phenol in Almond Oil Injectable Solution
- **Mechanism of Action**: Chemical sclerosant producing local submucosal inflammatory fibrosis to anchor mucosal layer to the underlying muscular coat.
- **Dose**: 1–3 mL per injection site (total 5–10 mL per session).
- **Dosage / Route**: Submucosal injection into the submucosa at the prolapse apex via proctoscope.
- **Frequency**: Single treatment session; reassessed after 4–6 weeks.
- **Features**: First-line interventional option for pediatric or adult mucosal prolapse failing conservative trial.
- **Side Effects**: Local pain, submucosal abscess, accidental prostatic injection.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administered after a 6-week failure of digital repositioning.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing full-thickness rectal prolapse with prolapsed 3rd/4th degree internal hemorrhoids.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Look at the mucosal fold pattern! **Full-thickness rectal prolapse exhibits concentric circular mucosal rings**, whereas **prolapsed hemorrhoids exhibit deep radial grooves** separating individual cushions at 3, 7, and 11 o'clock positions.
