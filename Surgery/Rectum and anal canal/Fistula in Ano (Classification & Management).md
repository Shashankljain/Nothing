# Fistula in Ano (Classification & Management)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **fistula-in-ano (anal fistula)** is a chronic, abnormal granulation tissue-lined communication extending from an internal opening within the anorectal lumen (predominantly at the dentate line) to an external opening on the skin of the perineum or buttock.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Cryptoglandular Theory (Parks)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Over 90% of anal fistulae arise secondary to acute infection and abscess formation in the **intersphincteric anal glands** (which open into the anal crypts at the dentate line).
- **Secondary / Non-Cryptoglandular Causes**:
    - **Inflammatory Bowel Disease**: Crohn's disease (frequently complex, multiple, or recurrent fistulae).
    - **Specific Granulomatous Infections**: Tuberculosis, Actinomycosis, Lymphogranuloma venereum.
    - **Malignancy & Radiation**: Rectal or anal carcinoma, post-pelvic radiation necrosis.
    - **Iatrogenic & Trauma**: Injudicious drainage of anorectal abscesses, obstetric perineal trauma, foreign body trauma.

#### 1. Parks' Anatomical Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

Parks' classification categorizes anal fistulae based on the relationship of the primary track to the internal and external anal sphincter muscles:

1. **Intersphincteric Fistula (45% - Most Common)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Course**: The track stays entirely within the intersphincteric plane between the internal and external anal sphincters.
    - **Behavior**: It traverses the internal sphincter at the dentate line to reach the perianal skin without crossing the main external anal sphincter.
2. **Trans-sphincteric Fistula (40%)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Course**: The primary track passes from the intersphincteric space directly through both the internal and external anal sphincters (at a variable level) into the ischioanal fossa to reach the buttock skin.
    - **Extensions**: May give rise to high secondary extensions into the roof of the ischioanal fossa or horseshoe tracks.
3. **Suprasphincteric Fistula (10%)**:
    - **Course**: The track passes upward in the intersphincteric plane to a level above the puborectalis muscle, loops over the puborectalis, and passes downward through the levator ani muscle and ischioanal fossa to open onto the skin.
4. **Extrasphincteric Fistula (5%)**:
    - **Course**: Runs completely outside the sphincter mechanism, extending directly from the rectal lumen down through the levator ani muscle to the perineal skin.
    - **Etiology**: Usually results from pelvic disease (e.g., diverticulitis, Crohn's), penetrating pelvic trauma, or iatrogenic injury during examination.

#### 2. American Gastroenterological Association (AGA) Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

|Parameter|Simple Anal Fistula|Complex Anal Fistula|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Track Level**|Low (superficial, low intersphincteric, or low trans-sphincteric <30% sphincter involvement)|High (high trans-sphincteric, suprasphincteric, or extrasphincteric)|
|**External Openings**|Single external opening|Multiple external openings|
|**Associated Pathology**|No associated abscess, stricture, or rectovaginal track|Associated with abscess, ano-vaginal fistula, or rectal stricture|
|**Etiology**|Idiopathic / Cryptoglandular|Active Crohn's disease, radiation, or malignancy|

#### 3. Anatomical Level Classification (Low vs. High)

- **Low Anal Fistula**: The main track opens into the anal canal below the level of the anorectal ring (puborectalis muscle). Can be safely treated with one-stage fistulotomy without risking fecal incontinence.
- **High Anal Fistula**: The track or internal opening lies at or above the level of the anorectal ring. Division of the enclosed muscle mass during fistulotomy carries a high risk of permanent fecal incontinence.

#### 4. Goodsall's Rule (Topographical Direction Classification) [⭐ High-Yield]

Goodsall's rule predicts the course of the primary fistula track and the location of its internal opening based on the anatomical position of the external opening relative to a transverse line drawn through the center of the anus:

```
                                  ANTERIOR
                                     |
                Radial, straight     |     Radial, straight
                short track          |     short track
                                     |
      -------------------------------+------------------------------- Transverse Anal Line
                                     |
                Curved track to      |     Curved track to
                Posterior Midline    |     Posterior Midline
                                     |
                                 POSTERIOR
```

- **Anterior External Opening (<3 cm from verge)**: The track runs in a straight, radial line directly into the anal canal at the same clock hour.
- **Posterior External Opening (or Anterior >3 cm from verge)**: The track curves backward to enter the anal canal in the **posterior midline (6 o'clock position)** at the dentate line.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Intermittent Purulent Discharge**: Chronic, persistent, or episodic pus (often blood-stained or foul-smelling) discharging onto underwear.
- **Pain & Swelling**: Perianal throbbing pain that builds up as pus accumulates inside an obstructed track and subsides temporarily following spontaneous drainage.
- **Pruritus Ani**: Secondary perianal skin excoriation and itching from persistent discharge.

#### Signs

- **Inspection**: External opening visible as a small red papula or pouting granulation button discharging pus on compression.
- **Palpation**: An indurated, cord-like fibrous track can be palpated subcutaneously running from the external opening toward the anal margin.
- **Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)**: Internal opening felt as a small indurated pit or nodule along the dentate line (most commonly posterior midline).

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Clinical & Bedside Tests

- **Hydrogen Peroxide Instillation**: Injection of dilute H₂O₂ through the external opening during anoscopy visualizes bubbles escaping from the internal opening at the dentate line.
- **Probing**: Gentle passage of a malleable metallic probe along the track establishes the direction and height of the primary track.

#### 2. Advanced Imaging Modalities [⭐ High-Yield]

- **High-Resolution Pelvic Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) [Gold Standard Imaging Modality]**:
    - Accurate evaluation of primary tracks, secondary Extensions, horseshoe spreading, and relation to the puborectalis muscle.
- **Three-Dimensional Endoanal Ultrasound (3D-EAUS)**: High accuracy for defining intersphincteric tracks and assessing internal/external sphincter muscle integrity.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

The surgical objective is to eradicate the fistula tract while preserving anal sphincter continuity and preventing fecal incontinence.

#### 1. Low / Simple Fistulae Management

- **Fistulotomy (Laying Open)** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Indication**: Intersphincteric and low trans-sphincteric fistulae involving <30% of the external sphincter.
    - **Technique**: A probe is passed through the track, all overlying skin and muscle are divided, granulation tissue is curetted, and wound edges are marsupialized.

#### 2. High / Complex Fistulae Management (Sphincter-Preserving Options) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Ligation of Intersphincteric Fistula Tract (LIFT) [Gold Standard Sphincter-Preserving Surgery]** [⭐ High-Yield]:
    - **Technique**: Dissection in the intersphincteric groove, identification, ligation, and division of the intersphincteric tract, followed by curettage of the external track. Preserves sphincter integrity with zero risk of incontinence.
- **Seton Placement**:
    - _Loose Drainage Seton_: Placed loosely around the sphincter complex to maintain drainage and clear local sepsis prior to definitive repair.
    - _Cutting Seton_: Gradually cuts through the enclosed sphincter muscle via slow pressure, inducing fibrosis behind the cutting line to prevent muscle retraction.
- **Endorectal Advancement Flap**: Closure of the internal opening using a broad-based mucosal/submucosal flap.
- **Video-Assisted Anal Fistula Treatment (VAAFT) / Laser Closure (FiLaC)**: Minimal access diagnostic and ablative techniques.

---

### Complications

- **Fecal / Flatus Incontinence**: Resulting from inadvertent or excessive division of the external anal sphincter or puborectalis muscle.
- **Recurrence**: Caused by missed secondary tracks, incomplete internal opening division, or underlying Crohn's disease.

---

### Clinical Pearl

Before performing a fistulotomy, **always palpate the anorectal ring (puborectalis muscle) with your index finger in the anal canal**. If the fistula probe passes deep to or above the puborectalis muscle, **NEVER perform a one-stage fistulotomy**, as dividing the anorectal ring results in complete, permanent fecal incontinence. Place a loose draining seton or perform a LIFT procedure instead.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing Goodsall's rule for anterior openings located **greater than 3 cm** from the anal verge.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Anterior external openings within 3 cm follow a straight radial path; **however, an anterior external opening situated >3 cm from the anal verge DOES NOT open radially—it curves posteriorly to enter at the posterior midline (6 o'clock position)**.

### Quick Revision

- **Etiology**: Over 90% cryptoglandular in origin arising from infected intersphincteric anal glands at the dentate line.
- **Parks' Tiers**: Intersphincteric (45%), Trans-sphincteric (40%), Suprasphincteric (10%), Extrasphincteric (5%).
- **Goodsall's Rule**: Anterior <3 cm = straight radial track; Posterior (or anterior >3 cm) = curves to posterior midline.
- **Treatment**: Simple low fistulae = Fistulotomy; Complex high fistulae = LIFT procedure or Seton placement.
