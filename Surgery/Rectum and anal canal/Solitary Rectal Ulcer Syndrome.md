# Solitary Rectal Ulcer Syndrome

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Solitary rectal ulcer syndrome (SRUS)** is a chronic, benign evacuatory disorder associated with obstructed defecation syndrome and internal rectal prolapse (intussusception), characterized histologically by fibromuscular obliteration of the lamina propria and mucosal ulceration classically located on the anterior rectal wall, 6–8 cm from the anal verge.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Etiology & Pathophysiology**: Caused by uncoordinated pelvic floor contraction (dyssynergic defecation), excessive toilet straining, and repeated anterior rectal wall intussusception, leading to mucosal trauma and ischemic necrosis.
- **Anatomical Site & Clinical Presentation**: Classically located on the anterior rectal wall 6–8 cm above the anal verge; presents with fresh rectal bleeding, mucopurulent discharge, tenesmus, and a constant feeling of incomplete evacuation.
- **Diagnostic Trap**: Endoscopically and macroscopically mimics **rectal adenocarcinoma** or **Crohn's disease**(especially when healing leaves a nodular/polypoid mass); diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy showing characteristic fibromuscular lamina propria hyperplasia.
- **Management**: First-line therapy consists of conservative measures (high-fiber diet, stool softeners) and **biofeedback therapy** to retrain defecation dynamics; surgical interventions (e.g., STARR procedure or laparoscopic ventral mesh rectopexy) are reserved for refractory internal prolapse.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Classic Location**: Anterior rectal wall, 6–8 cm from the anal verge.
- **Etiology**: Obstructed defecation, excessive straining, and internal rectal intussusception.
- **Clinical Mimic**: Frequently misdiagnosed as rectal carcinoma or Crohn's disease.
- **First-Line Treatment**: Biofeedback re-coordination therapy and bowel retraining.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener
