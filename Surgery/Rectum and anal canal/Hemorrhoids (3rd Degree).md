# Hemorrhoids (3rd Degree)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Hemorrhoids (piles)** are symptomatic, pathological enlargements and caudal (downward) displacements of the normal **anal cushions**—which consist of subepithelial vascular plexuses, fibroelastic connective tissue, and smooth muscle (Treitz's muscle). **Third-degree hemorrhoids** are defined as hemorrhoidal masses that prolapse through the anal canal during defecation or straining and **require manual reduction** back into the anal canal.

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Increased Intra-Abdominal Pressure**: Chronic constipation with prolonged straining, pregnancy, pelvic tumors, and chronic coughing or heavy lifting.
- **Anatomical & Age-Related Degeneration**: Fragmentation and loss of elasticity of the supporting fibroelastic connective tissue framework (Parks' ligament), accelerating with aging.
- **Venous Factors**: Absence of valves in the portal venous system, causing high hydrostatic back-pressure in the superior rectal venous plexus during straining.
- **Lifestyle & Dietary Factors**: Low-fiber diet leading to hard, scybalous stools and shearing forces acting on the anoderm during evacuation.

---

### Classification (Goligher's Degrees of Internal Hemorrhoids) [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                             GRADES OF INTERNAL HEMORRHOIDS
                                           |
     +-------------------+-----------------+-------------------+-------------------+
     |                   |                                     |                   |
  First Degree        Second Degree                         Third Degree        Fourth Degree
  * Bleed only        * Prolapse on straining               * Prolapse on       * Permanently prolapsed
  * NO prolapse       * Reduce SPONTANEOUSLY                  straining         * Irreducible / Incarcerated
                      * Require MANUAL reduction
```

- **1st Degree**: Bleed only; no mucosal prolapse beyond the anal verge.
- **2nd Degree**: Prolapse during defecation or straining but **reduce spontaneously**.
- **3rd Degree [⭐ High-Yield]**: Prolapse outside the anal canal during straining and **require manual reduction** by the patient.
- **4th Degree**: Permanently prolapsed, irreducible externalized masses; frequently associated with a significant cutaneous skin component ("mixed" hemorrhoids).
- **Anatomical Clock Positions**: Classically located at the **3, 7, and 11 o'clock positions** in the lithotomy position (corresponding to the main terminal branches of the superior rectal artery).

- **Etiopathogenetic Cascade**: Repeated straining and raised intra-abdominal pressure cause engorgement of the internal hemorrhoidal venous plexus. Over time, shearing forces fragment the underlying fibroelastic tissue anchoring the cushions to the internal sphincter. The cushions slide downward (caudal displacement) through the anal canal, leading to venous stasis, mucosal edema, bright-red bleeding from arteriovenous communications, and manual prolapse.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Manual Prolapse (The Cardinal Feature)**: History of lumps ("piles") protruding per anus during micturition/defecation that **must be pushed back manually**.
- **Painless Rectal Bleeding**: Bright red blood seen as a "splash in the pan" or on wiping, characteristically separate from the stool.
- **Mucus Discharge & Pruritus Ani**: Irritation, perianal wetness, and severe itching resulting from persistent rectal mucus transudation over perianal skin.
- **Anal Discomfort / Fullness**: Constant sensation of incomplete evacuation or a foreign mass in the anal canal.
- **Pain**: Uncomplicated internal hemorrhoids are **painless** (above the somatically innervated dentate line); pain indicates a complication (strangulation, thrombosis, fissure).

#### Signs

- **Inspection**: On parting the buttocks or asking the patient to strain, purplish, mucosal-covered masses protrude through the anal verge at the 3, 7, and/or 11 o'clock positions.
- **Palpation & Digital Rectal Exam (DRE)**: Uncomplicated internal hemorrhoids are soft, compressible venous cushions and are **NOT palpable** on DRE unless thrombosed. DRE excludes rectal tumors or low fecal impaction.
- **Proctoscopy**: Essential diagnostic tool; visualizes purple vascular bulges collapsing into the lumen of the proctoscope upon withdrawal.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations

- **Proctoscopy / Anoscopy [Gold Standard Bedside Diagnostic Test]**: Directly visualizes the position, size, mucosal state, and degree of prolapse of internal hemorrhoids.
- **Rigid Sigmoidoscopy / Colonoscopy**: Mandatory in all patients >40 years or those with atypical symptoms to exclude co-existing colorectal malignancy or inflammatory bowel disease.
- **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**: Assesses microcytic hypochromic anemia from chronic, severe occult or overt bleeding.

### Treatment / Management [⭐ High-Yield]

Management of 3rd-degree hemorrhoids transitions from conservative measures to non-excisional procedures or formal surgical hemorrhoidectomy.

```
                  MANAGEMENT OF 3RD DEGREE HEMORRHOIDS
                                    |
     +------------------------------+------------------------------+
     |                                                             |
Non-Operative / Office Interventions               Surgical Operations (Gold Standard)
* Dietary Fiber + Stool Softeners                  * Open Haemorrhoidectomy (Milligan-Morgan)
* Rubber Band Ligation (Barron's Bander)           * Closed Haemorrhoidectomy (Ferguson)
* Doppler-Guided HAL / Mucopexy                    * Stapled Haemorrhoidopexy (PPH / Longo)
```

#### 1. Conservative & Non-Operative Measures

- **Dietary & Lifestyle Modification**: High-fiber diet (25–30 g/day), adequate fluid intake, stool bulking agents (psyllium husk), and avoidance of straining on the toilet.
- **Phlebotonics / Flavonoids**: Flavonoid extracts (calcium dobesilate, micronized purified flavonoid fraction) to improve lymphatic drainage and reduce capillary fragility.

#### 2. Non-Excisional / Office Procedures

- **Rubber Band Ligation (Barron's Bander) [⭐ High-Yield]**:
    - _Technique_: Using a Barron bander through a proctoscope, a tight elastic band is applied to the pedicle/apex of the hemorrhoid strictly **at least 1 cm above the dentate line**.
    - _Mechanism_: Induces ischemic necrosis; the pile sloughs off within 7–10 days, leaving an ulcer that heals with fibrosis, anchoring the mucosa to the underlying sphincter.
    - _Role in 3rd Degree_: Effective for early 3rd-degree hemorrhoids; can be repeated across sessions.
- **Transanal Hemorrhoidal Dearterialization (HAL / THD)**:
    - Doppler-guided transanal ligation of feeding branches of the superior rectal artery combined with suture mucopexy to elevate prolapsed mucosa. Lower postoperative pain than formal surgery.

#### 3. Surgical Operations (The Gold Standard for 3rd & 4th Degree) [⭐ High-Yield]

##### A. Open Haemorrhoidectomy (Milligan-Morgan Operation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Third- and fourth-degree hemorrhoids, failed non-operative therapy, or mixed hemorrhoids with well-defined skin tags.
- **Technique**:
    1. Performed under spinal/general anesthesia in lithotomy or prone jack-knife position.
    2. Artery forceps are applied to the cutaneous external component and internal mucosal component of the pile.
    3. A V-shaped skin incision is made; dissection proceeds proximally, separating the pile from the underlying internal anal sphincter.
    4. The apex/pedicle is transfixed with a strong absorbable ligature (e.g., Vicryl) and excised.
    5. **Mucocutaneous Bridges**: Essential to leave adequate mucocutaneous skin bridges between the three excision sites to prevent postoperative anal stricture.
    6. **Wound Healing**: The raw surgical wounds are left **open to heal by secondary intention**.

##### B. Closed Haemorrhoidectomy (Ferguson Operation)

- Popular in North America; identical ligation and excision of the hemorrhoid, but the mucosal and anoderm defects are **sutured closed** with a continuous absorbable suture.

##### C. Stapled Haemorrhoidopexy (Procedure for Prolapse and Haemorrhoids - PPH / Longo's Operation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Technique**: A specialized circular stapling device is inserted transanally. A purse-string suture placed 3–4 cm above the dentate line pulls a circumferential strip of submucosa and mucosa into the stapler.
- **Mechanism**: Excises a ring of redundant mucosa/submucosa, interrupting arterial blood supply and stapling the mucosal edges together, thereby **resuspending the prolapsed anal cushions** back into their anatomical position.
- **Advantages**: **Significantly less postoperative pain** and faster recovery compared to open surgery.
- **Disadvantages**: Higher long-term recurrence rate, risk of staple line dehiscence, rectovaginal fistula, or persistent tenesmus.

### Complications [⭐ High-Yield]

Complications are divided into disease-related complications and postoperative surgical complications:

```
                            COMPLICATIONS OF HEMORRHOIDS
                                          |
     +------------------------------------+------------------------------------+
     |                                                                         |
Complications of the Disease Itself                                Postoperative Complications
* Strangulation & Acute Thrombosis                                 * Early: Severe Pain, Urinary Retention,
* Ulceration & Mucosal Necrosis                                             Reactionary Hemorrhage
* Gangrene & Portal Pyaemia (Septic Emboli)                        * Late: Secondary Hemorrhage, Anal Stricture,
* Severe Iron-Deficiency Anemia                                             Fissure, Incontinence
```

#### 1. Complications of Untreated / Acute Disease [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Strangulation & Acute Thrombosis** [⭐ High-Yield]: Occurs when 3rd or 4th-degree hemorrhoids prolapse and become gripped tightly by the spasm of the internal anal sphincter, impairing venous and lymphatic return. Presents as a dark purple, exquisitely tender, irreducible perianal mass with impending mucosa necrosis.
- **Ulceration & Gangrene**: Ischemic compromise leads to mucosal ulceration, tissue breakdown, and frank gangrene.
- **Portal Pyaemia (Suppurative Phlebitis)**: Rare, catastrophic septic embolization into the portal venous system secondary to infected, gangrenous hemorrhoidal thrombosis.

#### 2. Postoperative Complications of Haemorrhoidectomy [⭐ High-Yield]

##### A. Early Postoperative Complications (Within 24–48 Hours) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Postoperative Pain**: Severe pain secondary to surgical trauma to the somatically innervated anoderm and internal sphincter spasm. Managed with multimodal analgesia, GTN ointment, and oral metronidazole.
- **Acute Urinary Retention**: The **most common early complication** (especially in males), triggered by pelvic floor muscle spasm, pain, and perioperative IV fluids. Managed with analgesia, warm baths, or catheterization.
- **Reactionary Hemorrhage**: Occurs within 24 hours post-op; results from slippage of a primary pedicle ligature or uncoagulated vessel as blood pressure normalizes. Requires urgent return to the operating theater for under-running ligation or packing under anesthesia.

##### B. Late Postoperative Complications (Days to Weeks) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Secondary Hemorrhage**: Occurs **7 to 14 days postoperatively**; caused by infection and sloughing of the vascular pedicle wall. Treated with bed rest, antibiotics, or examination under anesthesia with suture ligation if severe.
- **Anal Stricture / Stenosis**: Results from overaggressive resection of anoderm without leaving adequate mucocutaneous skin bridges. Prevented by regular post-op digital rectal examination and treated with anal dilators or Y-V flap anoplasty.
- **Fecal Incontinence**: Results from inadvertent thermal or mechanical division of the internal anal sphincter muscle during deep pedicle dissection.
- **Anal Fissure & Submucous Abscess**: Unhealed surgical wounds predisposing to chronic fissuring or local infection.

### Dosage Rule (Drugs Used in Hemorrhoidal Management)

#### 1. Phenol 5% in Almond / Arachis Oil

- **Name**: 5% Phenol in Almond / Arachis Oil Injectable Solution.
- **Mechanism of Action**: Sclerosant causing local aseptic chemical inflammation, submucosal fibrosis, and obliteration of vascular channels.
- **Dose**: 3 to 5 mL injected into the apex/pedicle of each hemorrhoid.
- **Dosage / Route**: Submucosal injection via a dedicated hemorrhoidal syringe/needle above the dentate line.
- **Frequency**: Single treatment session; reassessed after 8 weeks.
- **Side Effects**: Prostatitis, pelvic sepsis (if injected too deeply), superficial mucosal necrosis/ulceration (if too superficial).
- **When to Start / Stop**: Used for 1st and 2nd degree (or early 3rd degree) failing conservative therapy.

#### 2. Metronidazole

- **Name**: Metronidazole
- **Mechanism of Action**: Nitroimidazole antimicrobial; generates reactive intermediates that disrupt anaerobic bacterial DNA synthesis, reducing postoperative wound bacterial counts and inflammatory pain.
- **Dose**: 400 mg PO
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral tablet
- **Frequency**: 8-hourly (TDS) for 5 days postoperatively.
- **Features**: Proven to significantly reduce postoperative pain following open hemorrhoidectomy.
- **Side Effects**: Nausea, metallic taste, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Start on Postoperative Day 1; continue for 5 days.

#### 3. Calcium Dobesilate / Flavonoids (Phlebotonics)

- **Name**: Calcium Dobesilate / Micronized Purified Flavonoid Fraction.
- **Mechanism of Action**: Reduces capillary permeability, enhances venous tone, and stimulates lymphatic drainage.
- **Dose**: 500 mg PO
- **Dosage / Route**: Oral capsule
- **Frequency**: Twice daily (BD) with meals.
- **Features**: Symptomatic relief of acute bleeding and edema.
- **Side Effects**: Mild gastrointestinal upset, skin rash.

---

### Clinical Pearl

When performing rubber band ligation (Barron's bander) or sclerosant injection, **ALWAYS apply the band or needle at least 1 cm ABOVE the dentate line**. The mucosa above the dentate line receives autonomic innervation (insensitive to sharp pain), whereas the anoderm below receives somatic inferior rectal innervation; banding below the dentate line causes immediate, intolerable, severe agony requiring emergency band removal.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Excising all circumferential hemorrhoidal tissue completely during an open Milligan-Morgan hemorrhoidectomy.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Overaggressive mucosal excision destroys the anoderm and causes **severe, disabling anal stricture/stenosis**. **Surgeons MUST preserve adequate intact mucocutaneous skin bridges between each excised primary pile (3, 7, and 11 o'clock)** to allow normal epithelial re-growth.

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: 3rd-degree hemorrhoids prolapse during defecation and require manual reduction.
- **Classic Locations**: 3, 7, and 11 o'clock positions in lithotomy position.
- **Non-Operative Gold Standard**: Rubber band ligation (Barron's bander) applied ≥1 cm above dentate line.
- **Surgical Gold Standard**: Open Milligan-Morgan or Closed Ferguson Haemorrhoidectomy.
- **Key Surgical Rule**: Preserve intact mucocutaneous skin bridges to prevent anal stricture.
- **Major Early Complications**: Severe pain, acute urinary retention, reactionary hemorrhage.
- **Major Late Complications**: Secondary hemorrhage (days 7–14), anal stricture, incontinence.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Third-degree hemorrhoids are symptomatic, pathologically prolapsed anal cushions situated classically at the 3, 7, and 11 o'clock positions that require manual reduction, diagnosed via proctoscopy, and managed definitively via Barron's rubber band ligation or open Milligan-Morgan / closed Ferguson haemorrhoidectomy._
