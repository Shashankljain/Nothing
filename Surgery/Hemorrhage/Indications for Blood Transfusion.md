# Indications for Blood Transfusion

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Blood transfusion** involves the intravenous administration of whole blood or specific blood components (Packed Red Blood Cells [PRBCs], Fresh Frozen Plasma [FFP], Platelet Concentrates, Cryoprecipitate) to restore oxygen-carrying capacity, expand intravascular volume, or correct severe coagulopathies.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

#### 1. Indications for Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBCs)

- **Acute Exsanguinating Hemorrhage**: Active hemorrhagic shock secondary to trauma, major surgical bleeding, or severe gastrointestinal bleeding (Class III or Class IV shock with >30% blood loss).
- **Severe Symptomatic Anemia**:
    - **Surgical / Perioperative**: Hemoglobin (Hb) <7 g/dL in stable, non-cardiac patients (or Hb <8 g/dL in patients with underlying ischemic heart disease, active acute coronary syndrome, or elderly patients undergoing major surgery).
    - **Medical**: Severe chronic symptomatic anemia refractory to iron/vitamin supplementation (e.g., Hb <6 g/dL with dyspnea, confusion, or high-output cardiac failure).
- **Exchange Transfusion**: Sickle cell disease crises (e.g., severe acute chest syndrome or stroke) and severe neonatal hyperbilirubinemia/erythroblastosis fetalis.

#### 2. Indications for Blood Components (FFP, Platelets, Cryoprecipitate)

- **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)**: Coagulopathic bleeding in massive transfusion protocols (1:1:1 ratio with PRBCs and platelets), emergency reversal of Warfarin anticoagulation, acute liver failure coagulopathy, and Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
- **Platelet Concentrates**:
    - _Therapeutic_: Active bleeding with severe thrombocytopenia (platelets <50,000/μL) or neurosurgical/ophthalmic bleeding (platelets <100,000/μL).
    - _Prophylactic_: Asymptomatic severe bone marrow failure (platelets <10,000/μL) or prior to invasive procedures/surgery (platelets <50,000/μL).
- **Cryoprecipitate**: Severe hypofibrinogenemia (plasma fibrinogen <1.5 g/L) in massive hemorrhage, DIC, or hereditary fibrinogen deficiencies.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Trigger Threshold for PRBCs**: Hb <7 g/dL in stable patients; Hb <8 g/dL in cardiac disease or perioperative settings.
- **Massive Hemorrhage**: PRBCs + FFP + Platelets in a balanced **1:1:1 ratio** (Damage Control Resuscitation).
- **Platelet Trigger**: <10,000/μL for prophylaxis; <50,000/μL for general surgery/bleeding; <100,000/μL for neurosurgery.
- **Cryoprecipitate Trigger**: Plasma Fibrinogen <1.5 g/L.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Indications for blood transfusion encompass restoring tissue oxygenation via PRBCs in severe acute anemia (Hb <7 g/dL) or hemorrhagic shock, and correcting coagulopathies using specialized components (FFP, platelets, cryoprecipitate) during major hemorrhage or invasive surgical procedures._
