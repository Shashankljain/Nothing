# Reactionary Hemorrhage

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Reactionary hemorrhage** is a form of delayed postoperative or post-traumatic bleeding that occurs within **24 hours**(typically within 24–48 hours) of surgery or primary injury, resulting from the disruption of initial physiological or surgical hemostasis as blood pressure normalizes.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Underlying Etiology & Mechanisms**:
    - **Slippage of Ligatures / Knots**: Insecurely tied ligatures (e.g., a "Granny's knot") slipping off vascular pedicles as arterial pressure and pulsations increase postoperatively.
    - **Clot Dislodgement**: Displacement of temporary intravascular micro-thrombi caused by fluid resuscitation, rising blood pressure, or coughing/straining.
    - **Vasodilation**: Re-bleeding from small vasoconricted cut vessels as core body temperature warms up and perioperative vasoplegia wears off.
- **Classic Clinical Examples**:
    - **Post-Tonsillectomy**: Bleeding from the tonsillar bed requiring re-exploration.
    - **Post-Prostatectomy / TURP**: Venous or arterial bleeding into the urinary bladder causing clot retention.
    - **Post-Haemorrhoidectomy**: Bleeding into the anal canal or rectum post-excision.
    - **Post-Thyroidectomy**: Rapidly expanding neck hematoma causing airway compromise.
- **Clinical Significance & Monitoring**: Serves as an early surgical emergency; slow or concealed leaks may go undetected until the patient suddenly decompensates with tachycardia (↑ HR), falling blood pressure (↓ BP), and low urine output (<0.5 mL/kg/h).
- **Management Protocol**:
    - **Resuscitation**: Secure airway, establish large-bore IV access, draw blood for crossmatching, and initiate fluid/blood replacement.
    - **Definitive Surgical Control**: Immediate return to the operating theater under anesthesia for wound re-exploration, hematoma evacuation, direct vessel re-ligation, diathermy coagulation, or packing.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Timing**: Occurs within **24 hours** post-surgery/trauma.
- **Primary Causes**: Ligature slippage, clot dislodgement via rising BP, and vasodilation of cut vessels.
- **Classic Sites**: Post-tonsillectomy, post-TURP, post-haemorrhoidectomy, post-thyroidectomy.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Immediate return to the operating theater for surgical re-ligation or diathermy under anesthesia.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Reactionary hemorrhage is a delayed postoperative bleeding complication occurring within 24 hours of surgery due to ligature slippage or clot dislodgement secondary to rising blood pressure, requiring prompt resuscitation and surgical re-exploration._
