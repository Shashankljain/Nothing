# Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)** is a blood component separated from fresh whole blood and stored at -40 °C to -50 °C (shelf life up to 2 years), containing normal physiological levels of all plasma proteins and coagulation factors.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primary Indications**: First-line therapy in coagulopathic hemorrhage during damage control resuscitation (administered in a 1:1:1 balanced ratio with PRBCs and platelets), emergency reversal of warfarin anticoagulation, acute liver failure coagulopathy, and massive trauma-induced coagulopathy (TIC).
- **Dosage & Administration**: Administered intravenously at a standard dose of 10–20 mL/kg. Rh-D positive FFP can be administered to Rh-D negative patients, though Rh-D immunisation may rarely occur owing to residual red cell fragments.
- **Therapeutic Mechanism**: Provides immediate replacement of essential procoagulant factors (factors II, V, VII, VIII, IX, X, XI, and XIII) to arrest active hyperfibrinolysis and prevent dilutional coagulopathy.
- **Major Complications**: **Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)** (non-cardiogenic ARDS within 6 hours caused by donor anti-HLA antibodies in plasma from multiparous female donors), Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO), and citrate-induced hypocalcemia.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Storage**: Stored at -40 °C to -50 °C with a 2-year shelf life.
- **Indication**: First-line therapy for coagulopathic hemorrhage and 1:1:1 balanced resuscitation in major hemorrhage protocols.
- **Dose**: 10–20 mL/kg IV infusion.
- **Major Hazard**: Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) is a coagulation factor-rich blood product stored at -40 °C to -50 °C, indicated as first-line therapy for coagulopathic hemorrhage in a 1:1:1 ratio during massive transfusion protocols, and administered at a dose of 10–20 mL/kg._
