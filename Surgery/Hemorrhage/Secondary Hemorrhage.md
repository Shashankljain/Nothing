# Secondary Hemorrhage

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Secondary hemorrhage** is a form of delayed postoperative or post-traumatic bleeding occurring **7 to 14 days** after initial surgery or trauma, resulting from erosion or sloughing of a vessel wall.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Precipitating Factors**: Commonly precipitated by local or deep-seated **wound infection**, pressure necrosis (e.g., from a rigid surgical drain eroding an adjacent vessel), or malignant tissue invasion.
- **Pathophysiology**: Infection-induced inflammatory necrosis causes progressive digestion and sloughing of the vascular wall, leading to sudden vascular disruption.
- **Classic Examples**: Secondary post-tonsillectomy hemorrhage, bleeding from an infected arterial graft anastomosis, or erosion of a major vessel by a surgical drain.
- **Management**: Resuscitation with blood products, systemic intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics to treat the underlying infection, and urgent surgical exploration for vessel re-ligation, bypass, or interventional angioembolisation.

---

### Quick Revision

- **Timing**: Occurs **7–14 days** post-surgery/trauma.
- **Etiology**: Wall sloughing secondary to deep infection, pressure necrosis (drains), or tumor erosion.
- **Management**: IV broad-spectrum antibiotics + surgical re-exploration/ligation or angioembolisation.

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Secondary hemorrhage is a delayed bleeding complication occurring 7 to 14 days after surgery or trauma caused by infection-induced sloughing and erosion of a blood vessel wall, requiring urgent antibiotic therapy and surgical or radiological control._
