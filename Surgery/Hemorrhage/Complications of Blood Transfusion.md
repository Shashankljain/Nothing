# Complications of Blood Transfusion (Transfusion Reactions)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

A **blood transfusion reaction** refers to any adverse or noxious clinical response occurring in a recipient during or following the intravenous administration of whole blood or blood components (packed red blood cells [PRBCs], fresh frozen plasma [FFP], platelets, or cryoprecipitate). These complications range from mild, self-limiting febrile episodes to life-threatening acute intravascular hemolysis, severe respiratory failure, or anaphylactic shock.

---

### Etiology & Classification [⭐ High-Yield]

Complications of blood transfusion are broadly categorized based on **onset timing** (acute vs. delayed) and **underlying mechanism** (immunological vs. non-immunological).

```

                                     |
         +---------------------------+---------------------------+
         |                                                       |
Acute / Immediate Complications (<24 Hours)            Delayed Complications (>24 Hours to Months)

  - Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (ABO Mismatch)   - Delayed Hemolytic Reaction (Rh / minor antibodies)
  - Febrile Non-Hemolytic Reaction (FNHTR)                - Transfusion-Associated Graft-vs-Host Disease (TA-GVHD)
  - Allergic / Anaphylactic Reaction                      - Post-Transfusion Purpura
  - Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)         - Alloimmunization (HLA / RBC antigens)
* Non-Immunological:                                   * Non-Immunological:
  - Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO)    - Transfusion-Transmitted Infections (HBV, HCV, HIV)
  - Bacterial Contamination / Septic Shock                - Hemosiderosis / Iron Overload (chronic transfusions)
  - Massive Transfusion Complications:
    * Hypothermia, Citrate Toxicity (Hypocalcemia),
      Hyperkalemia, Dilutional Coagulopathy
```

#### 1. Immunological Reactions

- **Acute Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Caused by **ABO incompatibility** due to clerical or identification errors. Pre-formed recipient IgM antibodies bind donor RBC antigens, activating the complement cascade and inducing catastrophic intravascular hemolysis, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), acute kidney injury, and shock.
- **Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (FNHTR)** [⭐ High-Yield]: The **most common overall transfusion reaction** (~1%–2%). Mediated by recipient antibodies reacting against donor leukocytes or cytokine release (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α) during blood storage. Prevented by using **leukodepleted (leukoreduced) blood**.
- **Allergic & Anaphylactic Reactions**: Caused by recipient IgE antibodies reacting against soluble donor plasma proteins. Severe anaphylaxis occurs in **IgA-deficient recipients** with anti-IgA antibodies upon exposure to IgA-containing plasma.
- **Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Mediated by donor anti-HLA or anti-neutrophil antibodies (most commonly from **multiparous female donors**) that activate recipient neutrophils in the pulmonary microvasculature, leading to capillary leak and non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema.

#### 2. Non-Immunological & Physical Hazards

- **Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO)** [⭐ High-Yield]: Hydrostatic volume overload caused by rapid or excessive blood administration, particularly in elderly patients or those with underlying cardiac/renal dysfunction.
- **Bacterial Contamination / Septic Shock**: Proliferation of Gram-negative or Gram-positive bacteria in stored blood components (platelets stored at room temperature [20–24 °C] pose the highest risk).

#### 3. Hazards of Massive Blood Transfusion [⭐ High-Yield]

_(Massive Transfusion is defined as replacement of total blood volume in 24 hours, >10 units PRBCs in 24 hours, or >4 units PRBCs within 1 hour)._

- **Hypothermia**: Infusion of cold stored blood (2–6 °C) drops core body temperature, reducing coagulation factor activity by 10% per 1 °C fall, provoking cardiac arrhythmias and worsening trauma-induced coagulopathy.
- **Dilutional Coagulopathy**: Rapid administration of stored red cells and crystalloids dilutes endogenous platelets and coagulation factors.
- **Citrate Toxicity (Hypocalcemia & Hypomagnesemia)**: Citrate anticoagulant in blood bags chelates ionized serum calcium (Ca²⁺) and magnesium (Mg²⁺), leading to neuromuscular excitability, perioral numbness, tetany, and prolonged QT interval.
- **Hyperkalemia**: Extracellular leakage of potassium (K⁺) from lysed, aging RBCs during prolonged storage causes elevated serum K⁺ levels, risking lethal cardiac arrest.
- **Metabolic Alkalosis**: Hepatic metabolism of transfused citrate generates systemic bicarbonate.

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Acute Hemolytic Reaction**: Chills, rigors, fever, severe **flank / lumbar back pain** (from renal capsular stretch secondary to hemoglobinuria), dyspnea, anxiety, and feeling of impending doom.
- **TRALI & TACO**: Acute breathlessness, chest tightness, cyanosis, and cough.
- **Anaphylaxis**: Pruritus, urticaria, wheezing, abdominal cramps, and lightheadedness.
- **Citrate Toxicity**: Perioral paresthesias, muscle cramps, and carpopedal spasm.

#### Signs

- **General Examination**: Fever (rise of >1 °C), tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension / shock, jaundice (delayed), and petechiae/purpura (DIC or Post-Transfusion Purpura).
- **Hemolytic Signpost**: Dark red / cola-colored urine (hemoglobinuria) and ooze from IV sites (DIC).
- **Auscultation & Hemodynamic Signs**:
    - _TACO_: Hypertension (↑ BP), raised JVP, bilateral basilar crepitations, facial puffiness, pedal edema, S3 gallop.
    - _TRALI_: Hypotension (↓ BP) or normal BP, normal JVP, diffuse bilateral lung crepitations.

### Diagnosis & Differential Diagnosis [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Diagnostic Evaluation

- **Immediate Bedside Check**: Stop transfusion, re-verify patient name, hospital number, donor bag serial number, and blood group compatibility label.
- **Direct Antiglobulin (Coombs) Test**: Positive in acute and delayed hemolytic reactions.
- **Laboratory Panels**:
    - _Hemolytic Work-up_: Plasma free hemoglobin, serum bilirubin, plasma haptoglobin (decreased), blood urea nitrogen/creatinine, DIC panel (PT/aPTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer).
    - _Urinalysis_: Dipstick positive for blood with microscopy showing absence of intact RBCs (confirming hemoglobinuria).
    - _Microbiology_: Gram stain and bacterial culture of remaining donor blood bag and patient blood.

#### Differential Diagnosis: TRALI vs. TACO [⭐ High-Yield]

|Feature|Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO)|Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI)|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**Primary Mechanism** [⭐ High-Yield]|**Hydrostatic volume overload**|**Immune-mediated neutrophil activation & capillary leak**|
|**Implicated Products**|Any blood component administered rapidly/excessively|Plasma-rich components (FFP, Platelets) from multiparous women|
|**Blood Pressure**|**Hypertension (Elevated BP)**|**Hypotension (Reduced BP)** or normal BP|
|**Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)**|**Elevated JVP (Distended neck veins)**|**Normal or low JVP**|
|**Edema Features**|Facial puffiness and pedal edema present|Absent peripheral edema|
|**Chest X-Ray Findings**|Cardiomegaly with vascular congestion|Normal heart size; bilateral diffuse patchy infiltrates (ARDS)|
|**Serum BNP / NT-proBNP**|**Markedly Elevated (↑↑)**|Normal or low|
|**Response to Diuretics**|**Prompt improvement with Furosemide**|**No improvement / Diuretics contraindicated**(worsens shock)|

### Treatment / Management Protocol [⭐ High-Yield]

```
                     MANAGEMENT OF ACUTE TRANSFUSION REACTION
                                        |
     +----------------------------------+----------------------------------+
     |                                                                     |
Immediate Bedside Protocol                                 Targeted Medical & Supportive Care
1. STOP the transfusion immediately!                       * Anaphylaxis: Epinephrine (0.3–0.5 mg IM) + IV Hydrocortisone
2. Maintain IV access with 0.9% Normal Saline              * Acute Hemolysis: IV Fluids + Furosemide + Osmotic Diuresis
3. Re-check patient identity & blood bag label             * TACO: IV Furosemide (20–40 mg) + Oxygen + Sitting Position
4. Send blood bag, tubing, & fresh samples to blood bank  * TRALI: High-flow Oxygen + Mechanical Ventilation (ARDS)
```

1. **Immediate Resuscitative Steps**:
    - **STOP THE TRANSFUSION IMMEDIATELY** and disconnect the administration set.
    - Maintain IV line with a new administration set using **0.9% Normal Saline** (never dextrose or hypotonic solutions, which cause ex vivo hemolysis).
    - Monitor vital signs (HR, BP, SpO₂, temperature) and urine output hourly.
2. **Investigation & Clerical Verification**:
    - Inspect blood bag, tubing, and patient label for clerical error.
    - Send the remaining donor bag, administration set, and fresh EDTA/clot-tube patient blood samples + urine sample to the blood bank.
3. **Specific Pharmacotherapy & Interventions**:
    - **Acute Hemolytic Reaction**: Vigorous IV fluid hydration with 0.9% Normal Saline (maintaining urine output >100 mL/h) ± forced alkaline diuresis (Sodium Bicarbonate) to prevent renal tubular precipitation of acid hematin.
    - **Anaphylaxis**: Intramuscular **Epinephrine (0.3–0.5 mg of 1:1000)**, IV Chlorpheniramine, IV Hydrocortisone, and nebulized bronchodilators.
    - **TACO**: Sit patient upright, administer high-flow oxygen, and give **IV Furosemide (20–40 mg)**.
    - **TRALI**: Oxygenation, supportive care, low-tidal-volume mechanical ventilation for ARDS; **avoid diuretics**as systemic intravascular volume is normal or reduced.
    - **Febrile Non-Hemolytic Reaction**: Antipyretics (Paracetamol); avoid aspirin in bleeding patients.

### Clinical Pearl

To drastically reduce the incidence of **Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reactions (FNHTR)**, **HLA alloimmunization**, and **Cytomegalovirus (CMV) transmission**, modern blood banks routinely perform **pre-storage leukodepletion (leukoreduction filtering)**. Transfusing leukodepleted blood products eliminates >99.9% of donor white cells, rendering febrile non-hemolytic reactions exceedingly rare.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Treating a patient with **TRALI** using aggressive IV loop diuretics (Furosemide) because the chest X-ray shows pulmonary edema.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: Pulmonary edema in **TACO** is hydrostatic (cardiogenic); it presents with hypertension and raised JVP, and **responds dramatically to IV Furosemide**. Pulmonary edema in **TRALI** is non-cardiogenic (permeability pulmonary edema from capillary leak); it presents with hypotension and low/normal JVP. **Giving diuretics in TRALI is contraindicated and dangerous**, as it worsens systemic hypovolemia and shock.

### Mnemonic / Memory Palace

#### Topic Mnemonic: T-R-A-N-S-F-U-S-E

- **T** – **T**RALI (non-cardiogenic ARDS within 6 hours from multiparous female donor FFP).
- **R** – **R**ealize clerical errors cause ABO Acute Hemolytic Reactions (lumbar pain + hemoglobinuria).
- **A** – **A**naphylaxis in IgA-deficient recipients.
- **N** – **N**on-hemolytic febrile reaction (FNHTR) prevented by leukodepleted blood.
- **S** – **S**top transfusion immediately as the first management step.
- **F** – **F**luid overload (TACO) presenting with hypertension and raised JVP.
- **U** – **U**rinary output monitoring (>100 mL/h) to protect against acute tubular necrosis.
- **S** – **S**eptitis / Bacterial contamination (most common in room-temperature stored platelets).
- **E** – **E**lectrolyte hazards of massive transfusion: Citrate toxicity (Hypocalcemia) & Hyperkalemia.

### Quick Revision

- **Acute Hemolytic Reaction**: ABO mismatch due to clerical error → Intravascular hemolysis, DIC, shock, and lumbar back pain.
- **Febrile Non-Hemolytic Reaction**: Most common reaction; prevented by pre-storage leukodepletion filters.
- **TRALI vs. TACO**: TRALI = Anti-HLA antibodies → Capillary leak, hypotension, ARDS (no diuretics); TACO = Volume overload → Hypertension, raised JVP, pulmonary edema (treat with Furosemide).
- **Massive Transfusion Hazards**: Hypothermia, Citrate toxicity (hypocalcemia), Hyperkalemia, and Dilutional coagulopathy.
- **First Management Step**: STOP transfusion immediately, keep IV open with 0.9% Normal Saline, and return blood bag/samples to blood bank.

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Complications of blood transfusion represent immunological or non-immunological hazards resulting from blood component administration, ranging from acute ABO-mismatch intravascular hemolysis and TRALI to volume-overload TACO and massive transfusion electrolyte derangements, managed universally by immediately halting the transfusion and instituting targeted organ support._
