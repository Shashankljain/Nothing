# Free Gas Under Diaphragm (Pneumoperitoneum)

### Definition [⭐ High-Yield]

**Free gas under the diaphragm (pneumoperitoneum)** refers to the presence of free air or gas within the peritoneal cavity, seen classically as a radiolucent crescent beneath the diaphragmatic domes on an upright chest radiograph, serving as the cardinal radiological indicator of a perforated hollow abdominal viscus.

---

### Key Exam Bullets [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Primary Etiology**: Perforation of a peptic ulcer (predominantly anterior duodenal ulcer), followed by perforated colonic diverticulitis, gangrenous appendicitis, or intestinal ischemia.
- **Radiological Investigation of Choice**: **Erect Chest Radiograph (CXR)**, which can detect as little as 10–20 mL of free intraperitoneal air; the patient must remain seated upright for 10 minutes prior to taking the radiograph to allow air to collect beneath the right hemidiaphragm.
- **Pathognomonic Radiological Signs**:
    - _Rigler's Sign (Double Wall Sign)_: Visualization of both the inner mucosal and outer serosal surfaces of the bowel wall due to gas present both inside the lumen and outside in the peritoneal cavity.
    - _Football Sign_: Large oval gas collection outlining the falciform ligament and peritoneal cavity.
- **Important Differential Diagnoses / Pitfalls**:
    - _Chilaiditi's Syndrome_: Harmless, asymptomatic colonic interposition between the liver and right hemidiaphragm mimicking pneumoperitoneum.
    - _Postoperative Air_: Benign residual air persisting in the peritoneal cavity for 5–7 days following open or laparoscopic surgery.
- **Initial Management**: Emergency resuscitation with IV fluids, nasogastric tube decompression, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (aerobic + anaerobic cover), followed by emergency laparotomy (e.g., **Graham's omental patch repair**for perforated duodenal ulcer).

---

### Quick Revision

- **Classic Landmark**: Subdiaphragmatic crescentic radiolucency beneath the right hemidiaphragm on erect CXR.
- **Primary Cause**: Perforated hollow viscus (most commonly anterior duodenal ulcer).
- **Diagnostic Sensitivity**: Erect CXR detects 10–20 mL of air after 10 minutes of upright positioning; CECT is the most sensitive modality.
- **Exam Trap**: Chilaiditi's syndrome (colonic interposition) and normal post-laparotomy gas (persists 5–7 days).

---

### One-Line Exam Opener

_Free gas under the diaphragm is a pathognomonic radiological hallmark of pneumoperitoneum, most commonly caused by a perforated hollow viscus such as an anterior peptic ulcer, visualized as a subdiaphragmatic crescent on an erect chest radiograph and requiring urgent fluid resuscitation and emergency surgical intervention._
