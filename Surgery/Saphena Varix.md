[[C15- Venous disease]]


### Definition

A **saphena varix** is a localized saccular or aneurysmal dilatation of the terminal portion of the Great Saphenous Vein (GSV) at the Saphenofemoral Junction (SFJ) in the groin (femoral triangle).

---

### Etiology / Causes / Risk Factors

- **Primary Cause**: Valvular incompetence at the saphenofemoral junction (SFJ) causing chronic retrograde venous reflux.
- **Hemodynamic Factor**: Persistent ambulatory venous hypertension in the lower limb transmission to the terminal GSV segment.
- **Structural Failure**: Congenital or acquired weakness in the smooth muscle and elastic matrix of the terminal GSV wall.
- **Risk Factors**: Prolonged standing occupations, female gender, multiparity, increasing age, obesity, and positive family history of varicose veins.

---

### Surgical Anatomy [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Location**: Situated in the saphenous opening (fossa ovalis) of the cribriform fascia/fascia lata, approximately **2.5 to 4 cm below and lateral to the pubic tubercle**.
- **Vascular Relationships**: Lies in the femoral triangle, anterior and medial to the Common Femoral Vein (CFV) and Common Femoral Artery (CFA).
- **SFJ Tributaries [⭐ High-Yield]**: Surrounding the saphena varix at the SFJ are the **6 classical tributaries**:
    1. _Superficial Inferior Epigastric Vein_ (lateral / superior).
    2. _Superficial Circumflex Iliac Vein_ (lateral).
    3. _Superficial External Pudendal Vein_ (medial).
    4. _Deep External Pudendal Vein_ (medial).
    5. _Anterior Accessory Great Saphenous Vein (AAGSV)_ (distal).
    6. _Posteromedial Thigh Vein_ (distal).

- **Retrograde Reflux**: High-pressure blood column surges backwards from the Common Femoral Vein (CFV) through the incompetent SFJ valve during standing or Valsalva.
- **Wall Distension**: The terminal segment of the GSV, subjected to localized high pressure, undergoes progressive dilatation and loss of muscular tone, forming a thin-walled saccular varix.

---

### Clinical Features [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Symptoms

- **Groin Swelling**: Painless or mildly uncomfortable lump in the groin (femoral triangle).
- **Lower Limb Symptoms**: Associated dull aching pain, bursting sensation, heaviness, or cosmetic concern due to lower limb varicose veins.

#### Signs

- **Inspection**: Smooth, soft, bluish-tinted swelling located in the femoral triangle below and lateral to the pubic tubercle.
- **Postural Variation**: Prominent and soft when standing; **disappears completely when the patient lies supine / recumbent**.
- **Morrissey's Cough Impulse Test / Kelly's Sign [⭐ High-Yield]**: A distinct expansile fluid thrill / cough impulse is felt over the swelling when the patient coughs or performs a Valsalva maneuver.
- **Auscultation**: A continuous venous hum / bruit may be heard over the varix when the patient stands.
- **Trendelenburg Test (SFJ Component)**: Shows rapid, immediate filling of the groin mass and trunk from above when the patient stands up.

---

### Diagnosis / Investigations [⭐ High-Yield]

#### Duplex Ultrasound Scanning (Investigation of Choice / Gold Standard)

- **Patient Positioning**: Performed in the **standing position**.
- **B-Mode Findings**: Identifies the saccular dilatation of the terminal GSV and demonstrates its direct anatomical continuity with the Common Femoral Vein (CFV).
- **"Mickey Mouse" Sign**: Transverse B-mode view at the groin showing the Common Femoral Artery (CFA), Common Femoral Vein (CFV), and the expanded Great Saphenous Vein (GSV).
- **Spectral Doppler Findings**: Demonstrates retrograde venous flow / reflux lasting **≥0.5 seconds** across the SFJ on calf release or Valsalva maneuver.

#### 1. Trendelenburg Procedure (Flush Saphenofemoral Ligation) [⭐ High-Yield]

- **Indication**: Definitive surgical cure for SFJ incompetence with saphena varix.
- **Incision**: Oblique groin incision placed in/above the groin crease, lateral to the pubic tubercle.
- **Flush Ligation**: The GSV is traced up to its confluence with the Common Femoral Vein (CFV). Ligation is performed **flush with the CFV wall**.
- **Division of All 6 Tributaries**: Every tributary meeting the SFJ (superficial epigastric, superficial circumflex iliac, superficial external pudendal, deep external pudendal, AAGSV, and posteromedial thigh vein) must be individually ligated and divided.
- **Prevention of Stump Varix**: Leaving a residual GSV stump causes recurrence and stump varix formation due to persistent flow from unligated tributaries.
- **Venous Stripping**: GSV is retrogradely stripped **down to knee level only** (prevents saphenous nerve injury).

#### 2. Endovenous Thermal Ablation (EVLT / RFA)

- **Technique**: Percutaneous cannulation of GSV under ultrasound guidance.
- **Catheter Tip Placement**: Positioned just distal to the SFJ under direct real-time ultrasound visualization to ablate the truncal reflux while protecting the CFV.

---

### Complications

- **Thrombosis within Varix**: Acute superficial thrombophlebitis within the saphena varix, presenting as a painful, hard, tender groin mass.
- **Haemorrhage / Rupture**: Spontaneous or traumatic rupture of the thin-walled varix causing profuse venous bleeding.
- **Surgical Misdiagnosis Hazard [⭐ High-Yield]**: Mistaking a saphena varix for a femoral hernia and attempting open herniorrhaphy can lead to inadvertent incision or suture transfixion of the Common Femoral Vein (CFV), causing catastrophic hemorrhage or deep vein thrombosis.
- **Groin Recurrence**: Recurrent varicosities/stump varix due to incomplete flush ligation or missed SFJ tributaries.

---

### Dosage Rule (Sclerotherapeutic Agent)

#### Sodium Tetradecyl Sulphate (STS)

- **Name**: Sodium Tetradecyl Sulphate (Fibrovein)
- **Mechanism of Action**: Anionic detergent sclerosant; causes endothelial desquamation, vein spasm, microthrombosis, and fibrous occlusion.
- **Dose**: 0.2 to 1 mL per injection site (maximum total session volume = 10 mL of foam).
- **Dosage / Route**: Direct intravenous injection as Tessari foam (1:4 ratio with air) under ultrasound guidance.
- **Frequency**: Single treatment session; repeatable after 4–6 weeks.
- **Features**: Used as adjunctive ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy (UGFS) for residual tributary varicosities after treating SFJ incompetence.
- **Side Effects**: Localized phlebitis, skin hyperpigmentation, transient visual scotomas.
- **When to Start / Stop**: Administered in outpatient setting; stop immediately if chest pain or visual disturbances occur.

---

### Clinical Pearl

To clinically differentiate a saphena varix from a femoral hernia on examination, **ask the patient to lie down**. A saphena varix empties instantly into the femoral vein and disappears completely without manual pressure, whereas a femoral hernia requires manual reduction or remains persistently irreducible due to its narrow neck.

---

### Exam Trap

- **The Mix-up**: Confusing a saphena varix with an irreducible femoral hernia and attempting an emergency repair or needle aspiration in the groin.
- **The Distinguishing Fact**: A saphena varix exhibits a distinct **expansile fluid thrill on coughing (Morrissey's cough impulse)** and disappears completely on lying supine, whereas a femoral hernia is a firm mass below the inguinal ligament that lacks a fluid thrill. Attempting needle aspiration or blind suturing will puncture the Common Femoral Vein (CFV).

### Quick Revision

- **Definition**: Saccular/aneurysmal dilatation of the terminal Great Saphenous Vein at the Saphenofemoral Junction.
- **Anatomical Site**: Located at the saphenous opening, 2.5 to 4 cm below and lateral to the pubic tubercle.
- **Key Clinical Signs**: Soft, bluish groin swelling; disappears on lying down; distinct expansile cough impulse (fluid thrill).
- **Gold Standard Investigation**: Duplex Ultrasound in standing position showing SFJ reflux ≥0.5 seconds and "Mickey Mouse" B-mode view.
- **Definitive Treatment**: Trendelenburg procedure (flush ligation at CFV junction + division of all 6 SFJ tributaries + GSV stripping to knee).

### One-Line Exam Opener

_A saphena varix is a saccular or aneurysmal dilatation of the terminal Great Saphenous Vein at the saphenofemoral junction caused by primary valvular incompetence and ambulatory venous hypertension, presenting as a compressible groin mass below the pubic tubercle that exhibits a fluid thrill on coughing and disappears on lying supine._
